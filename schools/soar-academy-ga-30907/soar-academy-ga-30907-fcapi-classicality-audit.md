## Executive Summary

Soar Academy GA 30907 presents a distinctive blend of private, non-traditional education focused on neurodivergent learners, individualized learning plans, and project-based modalities. Public materials emphasize a mission-driven approach to education that centers on tailoring instruction to each child’s learning style, leveraging research and discovery, and preparing students for higher learning and life-long creativity. While these strengths align with several values commonly associated with classical education (e.g., formation of character, emphasis on student-centered formation, community and family engagement), the site also foregrounds contemporary, experiential, and multi-modal methods (lecture-style teaching, multi-sensory learning, and exploration Fridays) and explicit project-based learning approaches that are not typically central to classical pedagogy as defined by the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale:
- Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Not Classical. Public materials do not demonstrate grounded theological commitments or liturgical practices. The site emphasizes individualized learning and neurodivergent supports rather than explicit transcendental or liturgical foundations. Evidence cited shows a private, non-traditional learning environment and accredited status, with no explicit theological program described. 
- Element 2 (Purpose & Goal): Somewhat Classical. The school emphasizes formation and preparing students for higher learning, with a focus on tailoring education to individual students and fostering life-long learners. However, explicit reference to truth, goodness, and beauty as real transcendent aims or a teleological framework is not clearly articulated in the materials. The mission/vision language points toward formation and excellence rather than purely job-readiness. 
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Not Classical. Public materials emphasize multiple instructional modalities (lecture-style, multi-sensory, computer/video learning) and a programmatic emphasis on exploration rather than the classical triad of grammar-logic-rhetoric and Great Books literacy. The explicit Great Books/Great Conversation cadence and Seven Liberal Arts foundation are not described in public pages. 
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Not Classical (leaning toward contemporary, experiential methods). While there is evidence of some contemplative/mentoring aspects (e.g., small classes, individualized support), the predominant methods highlighted are project-based, hands-on, and exploratory rather than Socratic seminars and structured classical pedagogy. 
- Element 5 (Faculty): Somewhat Classical. The site frames teachers as dedicated professionals and mentors; it also references a living curriculum and strong staff involvement. However, there is also explicit note that not all teachers are SPED-certified or licensed, suggesting a mixture of formal credentialing and on-the-job learning. This combination aligns with “model teachers” and “living curriculum” ideas but lacks consistent classical credentialing norms. 
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Classical. The school actively partners with families (PTO, volunteer sign-up, parent portals, staff pages, public mentions of family involvement) and demonstrates open channels for parental engagement, which aligns well with classical expectations for parent partnerships and community formation.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy): Introduce classical core elements as anchors
  - Explicitly incorporate Seven Liberal Arts foundations (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as a curricular spine; build a program around Great Books and Great Conversation, and integrate historical texts that illuminate the development of Western civilization.
  - Adopt Socratic dialogue as a formal pedagogy in upper grades (e.g., regular Socratic seminars or Paideia-style practice) to cultivate dialectical reasoning and virtuous inquiry.
  - Restore and emphasize memory-work and recitation (Scholé practices) to support retention and moral formation.
  - Reduce early-stage reliance on lecture-style and constant multi-modal delivery as the sole mode; balance with disciplined, classical study routines that emphasize contemplative learning and intellectual formation.
- For Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Clarify or affirm metaphysical commitments if desired
  - If a classical/metaphysical dimension is intended, articulate a clear stance on truth, goodness, beauty, and whether theology or sacred traditions are integrated into the curriculum as a governing principle.
  - If non-sectarian, be explicit about the value framework (e.g., natural law, teleology, or a universalist tradition) to align with transcendental ideals.
- For Element 2 (Purpose & Goal): Make telos explicit
  - Use language around telos, truth, goodness, and beauty to orient the curriculum toward objective standards and perennial questions, rather than solely toward personalized learning outcomes.
- For Element 5 (Faculty): Strengthen the classical faculty model
  - Invest in a target profile for teachers that mirrors classical masters (living curriculum, mentorship, mastery of classical pedagogy) and pursue targeted professional development to deepen classical-methodology skills across the faculty.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and deepen partnerships
  - Expand parental education and classical book clubs to deepen family formation and ensure alignment between home and school in the classical project.

ABSTRACT

Soar Academy GA 30907 demonstrates a mixed, non-traditional approach with several classical-aligned features (formation of students, strong parent/community engagement, and a living faculty culture) but also prominent contemporary modalities (lecture-style instruction, multi-sensory learning, and project-based experiences). The keyword analysis against the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School indicates a primary alignment with classical ideals in Element 6 (Parents & Community) and a partial alignment in Element 5 (Faculty). Categories 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), 3 (Curriculum), and 4 (Pedagogy) show non-classical traits due to a lack of explicit metaphysical/theological grounding, absence of classical Great Books infrastructure, and emphasis on modern pedagogies. Element 2 (Purpose & Goal) reflects a nuanced blend: formation and lifelong learning are evident, but explicit classical telos language remains underdeveloped in public materials.

Overall, Soar Academy GA 30907 is BEST DESCRIBED AS SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with substantial strengths in parental engagement and a living-faculty approach, but with clear opportunities to integrate classical curriculum anchors (Great Books, Trivium), traditional pedagogical methods (Socratic inquiry, memorization, and disciplined contemplative study), and explicit metaphysical framing to move toward full CLASSICAL status.
