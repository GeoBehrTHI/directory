## Executive Summary

Covenant Classical School Illinois (Naperville, 60540) presents a robust classical and Christian education model with strong alignment to the classical education rubric. Public-facing materials repeatedly emphasize classical content (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; Certamen; Great Books/ Classics orientation; a chronological integration of studies) alongside a distinctly Christian orientation (biblical worldview, grace-filled community). The school also foregrounds communal, liturgical-style rhythm (House System; Covenant Life; Grandfriends Day; chapel-ish framing) and a clearly expressed partnership with parents (Tailored Tuition, open campus, parent involvement expectations). Taken together, the available materials strongly support a CLASSICAL rating across most dimensions, with Pedagogy showing strong classical elements but some ambiguity about explicit Socratic/seminal seminars in all grade bands. Across the six categories, Covenant demonstrates repeated evidence of traditional classical aims and practices, with full alignment in five categories and partial alignment in Pedagogy. Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Foundational religious/philosophical framing is explicit (biblical worldview, Christian formation, grace-filled community) and paired with classical content (Latin, Certamen, grammar/logic/rhetoric). This supports Element 1 and Element 3 strongly, and Element 2 as well due to the pursuit of virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful.   
- Curriculum is clearly structured around the Trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric) across grade bands and includes Latin, classical history, and modules like Certamen; this aligns with Element 3 (Curriculum) as CLASSICAL.  
- Pedagogy shows strong classical signatures (memory/recitation, chanting, guided discovery in lower grades; rhetoric and debate-oriented focus in upper grades), though explicit, widespread evidence of Socratic seminars across all high-school-grade levels is less visible in the public materials, resulting in a nuanced placement (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Pedagogy). 
- Faculty presentation emphasizes mentorship, living curriculum, and a "portrait" of teachers, aligning with CLASSICAL in Element 5. 
- Parents & Community is robustly engaged as co-educators, with explicit statements about parental partnership and formation, which supports CLASSICAL in Element 6.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower-school pedagogy features singing/music and memory-based approaches referenced in the classical lexicon, with a nod to wonder and delight in learning (e.g., “Joyful Focus on the Fundamentals” pieces which describe song/movement in foundational learning). 
- Upper-school pedagogy includes rhetoric and opportunities to develop oratory skills via the Rhetoric School pathway and co-curriculars (e.g., Certamen and rhetoric-oriented programming). 
- Some public statements emphasize direct instruction and a “grace-filled” environment, with a strong emphasis on direct teaching and high expectations, rather than an unstructured student-led approach. 
- There is explicit mention of a Collegiate Model Rhetoric Program (9th-12th) and a pathway to upper-level rhetoric and oratory. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: singing/recitation/memory work; contemplative/restful learning; scholé/wonder (from the classical pedagogy lexicon and Covenant materials); rhetoric, seminars, and pauses in the upper school (via the rhetoric track).
  - Detected elements include memory work, chanting, and rhythm in early years, as well as rhetoric-focused upper grades.  
- Negative indicators present: explicit emphasis on direct instruction and structured programs, with less explicit evidence of broad, student-directed experiential learning dominating the classroom. The lexicon warns against purely experiential/project-based pedagogy; Covenant materials emphasize direct instruction and teacher-led components. 
- The gap: Absence of consistently documented Socratic seminars, teacher-led vs. student-led balance across all grades is not fully explicit in the public copy. This creates a partial classicality in pedagogy.

Why this rating
- Covenant shows several classical pedagogy markers (memory/repetition in lower grades, singing/chanting, and a strong rhetoric/Collegiate Model in upper grades) but doesn’t uniformly document Socratic seminars or full-year classical seminars across all grade levels in public materials. This yields a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Pedagogy. Evidence of explicit Socratic/inquiry-based seminars is present in some high-school trajectory materials but not comprehensively across the entire public footprint. SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle

- If aiming to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (Pedagogy) to CLASSICAL, Covenant could publish more explicit, system-wide evidence of Socratic or Paideia-style seminars and discussion-based pedagogy across more grade levels, including explicit examples of upper-level seminars and student-led inquiry. This would align Pedagogy even more strongly with the classical rubric. Additionally, making explicit references to Great Books or primary-source “Great Conversation” in the curriculum materials would fortify Element 3 and reduce ambiguity around Great Books alignment.

- Another potential needle-mover would be to foreground explicit liturgical or communal worship practices in classroom or daily routines (while maintaining the Christian orientation). This would strengthen Religious and Metaphysical Foundations by foregrounding liturgical/communal practice as a core element rather than separate events.

- More direct, public articulation of a formal teacher profile or explicit “portrait of a teacher” in all staff biographies could reinforce the Faculty category and further embed the living-curriculum approach.

- While not strictly required to reach CLASSICAL, expanding “Great Books” teaching and primary-source instruction, as well as formalizing a broader classical-language track (beyond Latin), could further strengthen Curriculum coverage in the classical sense.
