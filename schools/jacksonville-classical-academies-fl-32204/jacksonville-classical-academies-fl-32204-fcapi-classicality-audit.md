## Executive Summary

Jacksonville Classical Academy (JCA) demonstrates a predominantly classical orientation in its public materials, with clear commitments to a liberal arts, content-rich education rooted in Western civilization, and a mission centered on virtue, wisdom, and character formation. The campus explicitly describes a classical curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a liberal-arts framework, and a Great-Books-oriented ethos, reinforced by Core Knowledge, Latin, and classical literacy programs. There are strong signs of virtuous character formation, civic responsibility, and a parent-community partnership, all of which align with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric.

However, certain elements typically associated with a robust classical program—explicit liturgical/theological grounding, and a more explicit articulation of contemplative schooling (Scholé) and Socratic pedagogy as core teaching methods—are less evident in the public-facing materials. This yields a mostly CLASSICAL profile with a few somewhat Classical aspects still visible. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL, with category-by-category notes below and concrete needle-movers outlined in the “What would move the needle” sections.

Key takeaways:

- Strengths: strong curriculum core (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Core Knowledge integration, Great-Books/western-civilization framing, emphasis on virtue and civic virtue, and active parent/community engagement (Parent Group, campus tours, public information). Evidence of a living-faculty ethos and staff expectations aligned to classical aims.
    - Evidence snippets include motto and virtue framing, curricular focus on the trivium, Core Knowledge, and Western-civilizational heritage, and explicit parent partnerships. See motto and virtue language (Learn the Truth. Do the Good. Love the Beautiful.) and the program description (virtuous living, civic responsibility, Western civilization) as core signals.
    - Curriculum signals include grammar/logic/rhetoric, Core Knowledge Sequence, Latin, explicit phonics, Orton-Gillingham literacy, Singapore Math, and a content-rich classical narrative.
    - Public-parent engagement signals are strong (Parent Group, open-campus mentality, parent involvement).

- Areas to strengthen (needle-movers): provide more explicit references to contemplative pedagogy (Scholé), formal Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in the high school, and a clearer note on any liturgical/theological grounding or communal practices beyond civic virtue and moral formation. Also consider more explicit articulation of the “Great Conversation” through published Great-Books statements or course sequences. See the gaps identified in the Six Elements lexicon and match to observed materials.

- Overall: CLASSICAL, with some SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL facets in certain categories that lack explicit liturgical/theological grounding and explicit Socratic pedagogy in public-facing materials.

This report is grounded in the six-category classical rubric, including keyword analyses against the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School documents, and triangulated with the public-facing materials from the school’s crawl content.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public materials emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty as transcendent ideals via motto language: “LEARN THE TRUTH. DO THE GOOD. LOVE THE BEAUTIFUL.”
- The description highlights virtue, moral character, and civic virtue as core aims, alongside a Western-civilization frame.
- The school’s mission centers on virtuous living and civic responsibility within a liberal-arts framework.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: truth; goodness; beauty; virtue; moral character; civic virtue; Western civilization. (Multiple explicit occurrences across the motto and mission text.)
- Negative keywords: Not prominently present in public materials (no explicit secularism-only or value-neutral framing detected in the accessible materials).

Why this rating

- The school foregrounds transcendent values (truth, goodness, beauty) and virtue-focused formation, but there is no explicit theology, liturgical practice, or formal doctrinal program described in public-facing content. That keeps this from a full, explicit theological grounding, hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL for this category.

Notes for documentation:

- The “Great-Conversation” and explicit theological grounding are not clearly stated in the site copy, though a Western-civilization frame exists. The lexicon and Six Elements documents point to liturgical/theological signals as strong signals in fully classical institutions, which are not evidenced publicly here.

What would move the needle

- Add explicit theology or liturgical/community practices (even if ecumenical) to ground metaphysical claims in a theological frame.
- Include a clearly stated liturgical calendar or communal worship practices, or a formal statement about philosophical/theological aims that align with the “Mistress Science” approach.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public materials emphasize formation of virtue and wisdom, not merely job-readiness (virtue, wisdom, moral character; civic virtue; formation over information).
- The school frames education as a liberal, content-rich, classical mission focused on human excellence, virtue, and stewardship rather than only workforce prep.
- Academics page states high standards of ethics and personal discipline, and that graduates should be highly literate and ethical citizens.
- Motto-language and Great-Conversation-oriented framing (Western civ, classical mission) reinforce the aim toward truth, goodness, beauty, and formation.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: virtue; wisdom; formation; liberal education; human flourishing; truth/beauty/goodness; great conversation (implied in the classical aim); telos; classical mission.
- Negative keywords: None prominent in public materials (no explicit “career-readiness/workforce-first” framing observed in the accessible site text).

Why this rating

- The site consistently emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and the cultivation of human excellence as the aim of education, aligning strongly with the Classical goal category. While AP and dual-enrollment exist, they are situated within a broader formation framework rather than a primary job-prep focus.

Notes for documentation:

- Core elements in the Six Elements material include formation and pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty as ends of education; the school text aligns with that emphasis. See the core goals in the Six Elements materials and lexicon.

What would move the needle

- More explicit articulation of “formation over information” in public materials (beyond general statements) and clearer articulation of telos and human excellences as consistently pursued outcomes across grades.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The site explicitly describes the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as the framework for the curriculum.
- Core Knowledge Sequence is referenced as a central curricular backbone; Singapore Math, explicit phonics, Latin, and a literature/arts emphasis are highlighted.
- History is taught as a throughline across disciplines, rooted in Western civilization; the curriculum emphasizes Great Books and classic texts, with a focus on cultural literacy (Core Knowledge, Great Books framing).
- Curriculum maps show Core Knowledge Sequence; explicit mention of “Great Books/Great Conversation” framing and classical literacy programs.
- The public-facing tour and academics pages emphasize a content-rich, historically anchored curriculum with a liberal-arts orientation.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: grammar; logic; rhetoric; Great Books/Great Conversation (implied by Great-Works and classical canon language); Core Knowledge Sequence; Western civilization; Latin; explicit phonics; Orton-Gillingham; Singapore Math; history through the curriculum.
- Negative keywords: None strongly observed in public content (no major modern, skills-based, or project-based-only framing observed in the core materials).

Why this rating

- The curriculum is squarely anchored in the classical framework (grammar/logic/rhetoric), with a robust liberal-arts emphasis, and explicit tie-ins to Great Books and a Western-civilization narrative. The mix of Core Knowledge and classical content supports a robust, historically grounded curriculum, matching classical criteria.

Notes for documentation:

- Core Knowledge references, grammar/logic/rhetoric language, and Western-civilization framing are all present in multiple crawl results.

What would move the needle

- If the school can publish a formal, explicit Great Books list and a formal Great Conversation sequence across grades (with sample texts and a published reading plan), and show more direct integration of a true Trivium/Quadrivium continuum across K-12, it would strengthen this category further.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The public-facing materials emphasize a classical curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and a focus on critical thinking and eloquent expression; however, explicit pedagogy signals such as ongoing Socratic seminars, Harkness-style discussions, or a rest-focused “Scholé” cadence are not clearly spelled out in public copy. The Upper School content references deep reading of foundational texts and the role of logic; explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy is implied but not strongly evidenced in the public pages.
- The presence of a strong emphasis on classical rhetoric and debate in upper levels is implied by the curriculum focus and public descriptions; the site notes a high regard for logic and argument in the upper levels.
- Some lower-level pedagogy elements (e.g., singing/chanting in lower grades, restfulness, or “Scholé” days) are not clearly documented in the public copy; the six-element materials describe these pedagogical signals, but they are not overtly claimed by the school in the visible site text.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue/dialectic (not clearly evidenced on-site but implied by classical pedagogy); memorization/recitation (not clearly evidenced on-site); formation through imitation and practice (implied through living-language curriculum and mastery emphasis); scholé (not explicitly named in public copy).
- Negative keywords observed: none explicit in the site copy; however, explicit references to “restful learning” or dedicated Socratic seminars are not clearly documented in public content.

Why this rating

- The school demonstrates a clear classical curricular orientation but lacks explicit public documentation of core pedagogical methods (Socratic seminars, Scholé days, or explicit memory work in classroom practice). This places Pedagogy in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than full CLASSICAL, pending stronger public articulation of the pedagogical methods used.

Notes for documentation:

- The Six Elements lexicon and the “Pedagogy” section describe a set of potential methods (Socratic dialogue, Scholé, memorization/recitation, imitation/formation). The current public-facing JCA materials show strong curricular alignment but less explicit pedagogy language. See the pedagogical keywords in the lexicon.

What would move the needle

- Publish a clear, school-wide pedagogy charter that documents:
    - Regular Socratic seminars/seminars (Harkness-style or similar) across grades (including sample prompts and assessment rubrics).
    - Explicit incorporation of Scholé-restful days or routines in the schedule.
    - Memorization/recitation practices (e.g., selected memory work, chants, poetry recitation, or canonical passages) with published examples and performance assessments.
    - A formal “living curriculum” framework linking models of excellence to classroom practice (i.e., imitation and practice with masters’ works).

Evidence

The staff pages articulate traditional roles (faculty/staff) and public leadership.
- The site references a “Portrait of a teacher” and classical-teaching standards in the Six Elements lexicon, which aligns with the expected living-curriculum approach; public copy confirms a strong faculty focus on classical aims and ongoing professional expectations.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: virtuous educators; mentorship; master-teacher model; portrait of a teacher; living curriculum; faculty as a core; professional development; fidelity to classical pedagogy.
- Negative keywords observed: none clearly stated in public content; the explicit “teacher-as-guide” or “faculty collaboration” language seen in the lexicon is not overtly demonstrated in public copy, but the Careers and Faculty pages indicate a strong teacher-centered classical mission.

Why this rating

- The materials show a clear emphasis on faculty alignment with classical aims and expectations for teachers to model classical virtues and pedagogy. Even without a fully explicit “living curriculum” phrase in the site copy, the visible content aligns with a CLASSICAL evaluation for Faculty.

Notes for documentation:

- The Careers page provides explicit teacher expectations; governance pages show leadership continuity. The “living curriculum” concept is supported by the Six Elements materials, and the site’s presentation aligns with that in practice.

What would move the needle

- Publish a formal “Faculty Portrait” and “Teacher as Mentor” statement with explicit examples of faculty development, mentorship structures, and living-curriculum practices visible in classroom practice and professional development offerings.

## What Would Move the Needle (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL categories)

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations)
    - Add explicit theology or liturgical/community grounding statements to public materials (even if ecumenical) and publish a small canon or creed section showing how truth/goodness/beauty translate into a metaphysical framework.
    - Provide a public-facing description of communal practices (morning/evening routines, liturgical calendar, or sacred music) to strengthen liturgical signaling.

- Element 4 (Pedagogy)
    - Publish a formal pedagogy charter detailing:
        - Regular, school-wide Socratic seminars or Harkness-style discussions with sample prompts and rubrics.
        - Explicit incorporation of Scholé (restful learning) days and routines in the daily/weekly schedule.
        - A published plan for memorization/recitation practices (e.g., memorized passages, chanted texts) with example units.
        - A clear articulation of imitation/practice as a core learning mechanism, including how students study models of excellence in literature, history, and mathematics.

- Element 1/Element 4 cross-cutters
    - Provide a simple crosswalk document showing how the school’s published practices connect to the Six Elements’ positive/negative keyword signals (with explicit text references on the school site).

- Element 5 (Faculty)
    - Publish a formal “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and a teacher-professional-development framework that shows mentorship, collaboration, and living-curriculum practices, with examples of faculty collaboration and seminars.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community)
    - Publish a yearlong calendar of parent-education events (book studies, Great Conversation-oriented sessions) and a clear outline of “Parent Formation” activities with measurable outcomes, to strengthen the parent-education dimension beyond events.
