## Executive Summary

Providence Preparatory School (Belton, TX) presents a robust Classical Christian program that aligns strongly with the core criteria of classical education as defined by the knowledge base. Across the six rubric categories, the school demonstrates explicit Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, a purpose oriented toward virtue and wisdom, a curriculum steeped in Great Books and the Trivium, a pedagogy that balances contemplative/memory work with Socratic/seminar-style inquiry, a faculty culture described as mentors and living exemplars, and an active partnership with and formation of parents and community. Public materials consistently reference God-centered aims, a classical curriculum (including Great Books, Latin, and classical literature), collaborative home-school models, and a high regard for virtue, wisdom, and the formation of character.

- The public-facing materials explicitly identify Providence as a Christian, classical school with a mission to glorify God through a collaborative, home-inclusive model and a curriculum anchored in the classical tradition (Great Books, paideia, the trivium, and rhetoric).
- Foundational catechetical language, creedal/Scripture emphasis, and terms such as “paideia,” “discipleship,” and “God-shaped wonder” appear throughout core-distinctive pages and admissions content, signaling a coherent metaphysical basis.
- The curriculum highlights explicitly promote classic literature, Great Books, cross-disciplinary integration, Latin instruction, and a four-year, trivium-informed progression, with references to Shakespeare and “100 Great Texts” as core components of the Grammar School to Rhetoric School progression.
- Pedagogical practices include memory-narration, recitation, chant, and contemplative elements (scholé) in early years, balanced with Socratic discussions, seminars, and rhetoric-focused speech in higher grades; the home/onsite collaborative model is repeatedly described as central to Providence’s pedagogy.
- Faculty and staff materials frame teachers as living exemplars, mentors, and co-educators with a clear emphasis on character formation and virtuous leadership; parent-teacher collaboration is highlighted as central to the model.
- Parent involvement is foregrounded as co-educators and co-leaders in a “collaborative school culture,” with open invitations for parent participation, training, and on-campus/off-campus learning rhythms that integrate family life with schooling.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL
