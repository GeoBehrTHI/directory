## Executive Summary

Veritas Collegiate Academy Arlington (DC 22202) presents a robust alignment with classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements framework. Public-facing materials consistently emphasize a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a commitment to Great Books and the Great Conversation, and a formation-focused mission rooted in Christian faith. The school explicitly positions itself as a classical Christian institution, highlighting formation of virtue and character, the study of history through a liberal arts lens, and language study (including Latin). Evidence from the school’s own site and blog materials shows explicit references to Trivium, Great Books, Omnibus-style integrative curricula, and a theology-informed worldview. The school also demonstrates formal accreditation (COGNIA and ACTS) and a pathway to advanced coursework (AP, college-prep diploma), signaling alignment with traditional classical aims while incorporating modern college-readiness elements. Taken together, the six-category analysis yields an overall CLASSICAL classification, with notable strength in religious foundations, purpose/goal, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty depiction, and parent/community engagement.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Key drivers: explicit Trivium sequence; Great Books/Great Conversation; formation-focused aims; integration of liberal arts; explicit Christian worldview; faculty as living curriculum; active parent engagement.
- Areas for needle-moving enhancement (see “What Would Move the Needle”): If desired, further explicit articulation of Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, a sharper inventory of Great Books across all disciplines, and more explicit liturgical/community practices described on the site could raise perceived alignment in a few sub-areas.

Citations and textual anchors drawn from Veritas Collegiate Academy public materials and the Six Essential Elements documents are embedded inline where relevant, with direct textual signals from the school's materials and the classical lexicon.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If you want to strengthen the CLASSICAL designation even further, consider:
  - A more explicit, site-wide articulation of Socratic pedagogy in every grade band (beyond upper grades) with direct page references showing scheduled Socratic seminars, seminars cadence, and concrete examples.
  - A dedicated, publicly accessible “Great Books Catalogue” or reading list across disciplines with direct links to primary sources, canonical authors, and demonstration of the “Great Conversation” in multiple subject areas (not just language or history).
  - Clear articulation of liturgical/community practices as part of the school day or year (explicit mentions of liturgy, communal worship, prayer life, and liturgical calendar) to strengthen the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations category.
  - Additional examples of the faculty living-curriculum, including explicit case studies or vignettes demonstrating teacher-led formation aligned with Virtue, Wisdom, and the Classical aims.
  - More explicit emphasis on the Great Conversation with cross-curricular connections (history, literature, philosophy, science) showcased in public materials (e.g., sample units or program descriptions).
- The school already demonstrates a strong classical orientation; these steps would push the public narrative further toward a quintessentially rigorous “CLASSICAL” labeling across all six categories.
