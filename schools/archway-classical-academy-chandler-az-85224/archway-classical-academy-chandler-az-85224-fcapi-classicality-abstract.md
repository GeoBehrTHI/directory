## ABSTRACT

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- 6-category synthesis:
  - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Strong transcendental language (truth, goodness, beauty; virtue), but less explicit theology and liturgical/community practices in public materials.
  - School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Formation and virtue are central, but college-prep outcomes and “Great Books/virtue” formation sit alongside college-readiness language, creating a balance that is not purely formation-first.
  - Curriculum: CLASSICAL — Clear emphasis on liberal arts, Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium concepts through public materials, with a strong Great Books/Western-canon orientation.
  - Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Strong Socratic/doctoral pedagogy signals and rhetoric emphasis; however, resting/restful pedagogical routines are not always explicitly spelled out in public content.
  - Faculty: CLASSICAL — Living curriculum, model teachers, two-teacher homeroom models, mentor culture, and a collaborative faculty ethos reflect classical faculty ideals.
  - Parents & Community: CLASSICAL — Explicit partnership with families, parent formation opportunities, and open-campus culture indicate a classical community orientation.

- Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL

- What would move the needle (summary): If more explicit theological grounding and liturgical practices were foregrounded publicly, and if a more explicit, school-wide scholé/restful learning rhythm were published, the school would move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in some categories to a stricter CLASSICAL alignment. However, as currently composed, Archway Chandler demonstrates a robust classical profile with a majority CLASSICAL orientation across categories and a strong emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty, Great Books, and virtue-led formation, with faculty and parent-community engagement that reinforce the classical mission.
