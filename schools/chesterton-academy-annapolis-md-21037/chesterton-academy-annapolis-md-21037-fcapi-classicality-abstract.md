## ABSTRACT

Chesterton Academy of Annapolis demonstrates a clearly identifiable classical-Catholic orientation in its public-facing materials, with strong emphasis on formation in virtue, Catholic faith, and liturgical life. The school presents a classical pedagogy via Socratic method and seminar-style exploration, and a humanities-centered curriculum (literature/history/philosophy/theology, with Latin and debate). Parental partnership and community life are central, including open-visit policies and parent involvement in the life of the school. However, explicit articulation of certain canonical classical elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric as a formalized Trivium; explicit Great Books/Great Conversation; and the precise language of Scholé as a pedagogical aim) is less visible in public materials, creating some tension with a full, canonical Classical Education reading. The result is a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL standing overall, with two areas showing clear CLASSICAL alignment (Religious Foundations and Parents/Community) and four areas showing partial alignment (Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty).

Summary of the 6 primary categories:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
- School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
