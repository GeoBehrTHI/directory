## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of Annapolis (CA Annapolis) presents a strongly Catholic, classical-leaning vision within a tuition-conscious private school setting. Public-facing materials consistently foreground a classical, Catholic framework, with explicit references to formation in virtue, the truths of the Catholic faith, and liturgical life (notably Mass). The pedagogy centers on Socratic methods and a classical curricular mood, with emphasis on virtue, character formation, and parental partnership. While CA Annapolis clearly leans classical in spirit and aims at formation beyond mere information, several pieces of public material also signal college/professional life preparation and pragmatic affordability, introducing some tension with a fully “formation-first” ideal in the strict classical rubric.

Overall classification by the Six Essential Elements rubric: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Strengths across multiple categories include: clear religious/theological grounding (Catholic identity, liturgical life, daily Mass) and a family-centric community ethos (parent involvement, open-campus posture). Evidence of classical methods (Socratic method, debate, literature/history/philosophy/theology focus) is present, albeit not always described with the explicit grammar-logic-rhetoric framing of a canonical Trivium-focused program. The Great Books/Great Conversation element is not overtly documented in CA Annapolis public materials, though the curriculum is described as rigorous, classical, and literature/history/philosophy/theology-centered. Community and family partnerships are highlighted as central to the model.

- What would move CA Annapolis toward full CLASSICAL status: explicit articulation of the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as core, explicit mention of Great Books and Great Conversation, broader incorporation of reformulation toward scholé (restful contemplative learning) and memory/recitation practices if not already present, and a more explicit living-curriculum portrait of teachers as mentors in the classical tradition.

What would move the needle

- Explicitly articulate grammar, logic, and rhetoric as core, map them to a formal Trivium structure, and provide public-facing details of where these elements appear in the CA Annapolis curriculum.
- Make explicit mention of Great Books or Great Conversation as a regular curricular component to reinforce the classical hallmarks.
- strengthen explicit “Scholé” language: mention contemplative/restful learning days, memory work, and chanting/memorization practices as part of daily pedagogy.
- Expand the living-curriculum narrative for faculty: publish a formal faculty-staff portrait and a description of teachers as mentors and living exemplars of classical pedagogy.
- Maintain and deepen parental formation programs (book studies, seminars) and publish a clear, regular cadence of parent-education opportunities on the public site.
- Provide explicit historical synthesis across subjects (interdisciplinary integration) and articulate a clear "formation-first" telos beyond college/workforce readiness to further align with classical aims.
