## Executive Summary

Nolan Catholic High School (Fort Worth, TX 76103) demonstrates a robust Catholic, formation-centered mission aligned with many core elements of classical education. Public-facing materials emphasize a strong religious foundation (Education in Faith, Formation in Hope, Perseverance in Charity), liturgical life (weekly Mass), and a catechetical framework anchored in Catholic theology, virtue formation, and character development. The curriculum shows intentional engagement with liberal-arts elements via a Core curriculum (mathematics, science, theology, literature, history) and a Trivium-inspired pathway (Grammar & Composition, Logic & Composition) within a broader plan that includes strong reading lists and classical-education-oriented programming. Community and parental partnership are clearly foregrounded through family engagement, fundraising, campus visitation, and participation in school planning.

Overall, Nolan Catholic is CLASSICAL in orientation, with some indications of strengthening consistency toward the full classical rubric (notably in explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and broader Socratic pedagogy in upper grades). The school exhibits clear strengths in religious/metaphysical foundations, a formation-centered purpose, a liberal-arts–leaning curriculum, and a vocationally balanced but formation-forward pedagogy. Areas that could move toward fuller classical alignment include more explicit emphasis on Great Books/Great Conversation and broader Socratic pedagogy across more grade levels.

- Evidence drawn from Nolan Catholic site materials, including mission statements, curriculum pages, and strategic planning content. See: Education in Faith, Formation in Hope, Perseverance in Charity; weekly Mass; Core curriculum; Trivium elements; reading lists; Marianist/Catholic identity; partnerships with families; and long-range plans for facilities and academic programs.

Notes on rubric alignment

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence

Why this rating

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Core structure explicitly lists five subjects plus 30+ electives, with Mathematics, Science, Theology, Literature, and History as the core disciplines. This reflects liberal-arts thinking at a high level. 
- The curriculum page references a Core and a course guide, and notes a reading list, indicating engagement with substantial texts beyond standard core courses. 
- There is explicit alignment with Trivium components in course offerings: Grammar and Composition; Logic and Composition (X. TRIVIUM). This demonstrates a Trivium-centered approach within the high school curriculum. 
- The school signals a move toward classical education through “CLASSICAL EDUCATION RESOURCES” and related programming (e.g., classical education series). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) explicitly present in course tracks; Great Books/Great Conversation language not explicitly present in public-facing materials; mention of a reading list and classical-education resources. Wonder is implied through outdoor learning initiatives but not explicitly described as a curricular mechanism in all publications. 
- Negative: Explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language is not clearly claimed in public materials (no direct statement about reading Great Books or joining the Great Conversation). History is treated as a core discipline, but the public-facing materials do not clearly label history as “Western civilization” or “Great Conversation.” The explicit Quadrivium is not shown in the public materials. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum demonstrates strong liberal-arts elements (core subjects; Trivium) and a published reading-list/classical-learning orientation, but it does not consistently and explicitly articulate Great Books/Great Conversation or a fully explicit Quadrivium framework in the public materials. This aligns with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL in this category. 

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Trivium-oriented pedagogy appears in course listings (Grammar and Composition; Logic and Composition), with potential for rhetoric/argumentation emphasis as students progress (the high school rhetoric track is embedded in the curriculum documentation). 
- Public-facing content references contemplative and wonder-oriented approaches (e.g., outdoor learning, IDEA Building, and emphasis on arts and sciences), as well as a long-standing plan to integrate faith, formation, and leadership within the learning experience. 
- Some elements parallel classical pedagogy (e.g., memory-focused engagement through recital-like and disciplined study in classical traditions; the site references classical education resources and seminars). 
- However, explicit statements about Socratic seminars, frequent upper-school seminars, or regular public-speaking opportunities across the entire range (K–12 or 9–12) are not uniformly present in the public-facing materials; there are references to rhetoric and potential public-speaking opportunities, but not a comprehensive, stated pattern across all grades. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: Socratic-style inquiry and seminars are implied by the presence of rhetoric-focused coursework and classical-learning resources; contemplation/scholé and wonder appear in the classical-education framing, and there is explicit emphasis on a balanced approach (contemplative/restful elements alongside rigorous study). 
- Negative: No universal, explicit, school-wide articulation of a pure Socratic, seminar-based pedagogy across all grades; limited explicit references to daily, systematic Socratic discussions or memorization/narration pedagogy in all publications. 

Why this rating
- The presence of Trivium elements and classical-education language supports a largely classical pedagogy, but the public materials do not consistently articulate a full, uniform implementation of classical pedagogy (e.g., wide-scale Socratic seminars, routine narrative/narration, or chant/memorization). This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school aimed to push toward fuller CLASSICAL in Curriculum (Element 3) and Pedagogy (Element 4), it could:
  - Explicitly articulate a Great Books/Great Conversation program and publish a clear Great Books reading list as part of the curriculum, including primary sources and classical texts across grades. This would strengthen the “Great Books and Great Conversation” criterion and show a more complete Classical alignment in history/literature integration.推薦
  - Expand explicit Socratic seminars and narrative/narration-based pedagogy across upper and lower grades, with documented facilitation approaches and example units to demonstrate a uniform classical pedagogy (beyond the current references to Trivium elements). 
  - Provide more explicit details on the Quadrivium integration (e.g., in math/science/music/astronomy sequences) to map public materials to the Quadrivium dimension of classical education.

- For Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 2 (Purpose and Goal), Nolan Catholic is already strong; additional needle-moving steps could include:
  - Publicly codifying liturgical rhythms or communal prayer practices in curriculum-wide statements (beyond weekly Mass) to further emphasize liturgical and communal practices as a central educational rhythm.
  - Publishing a concise formulation of the telos and the “Pursuit of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty” in school-facing materials to reinforce transcendental aims even more explicitly for prospective families.

- For Element 5 (Faculty) and Element 6 (Parents & Community), the school could:
  - Provide more explicit, public-facing articulation of “portrait of a teacher” and “portrait of a student” in the classical sense (the living-curriculum idea) with concrete exemplars and testimonials from students and families, reinforcing the model-teacher concept.
  - Expand structured parent-education programs on classical pedagogy and the Great Conversation to deepen parent formation and alignment with the school’s classical mission.

- These needle-moving steps would deepen alignment with the classical rubric and help translate strong Catholic formation into a fully articulated classical framework in public-facing materials. See the classical-education resources and the “Great Books/Great Conversation” emphasis in Nolan Catholic materials as starting points.
