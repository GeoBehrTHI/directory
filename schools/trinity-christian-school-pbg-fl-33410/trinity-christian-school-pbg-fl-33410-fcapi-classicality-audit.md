# Classicality Audit: Berean Christian Academy (Katy, TX)

Website used: https://bereanchristianacademy.org 

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Berean Christian Academy presents strong classical Christian markers: an explicit Christ-centered mission grounded in Truth, Goodness, and Beauty; a curriculum structured around the seven liberal arts with the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Latin, Great Books, and a senior thesis; and upper‑school pedagogy that includes Harkness discussion, formal logic, debate, and rhetoric. These align closely with the positive indicators in the Six Essential Elements framework and lexicon (e.g., truth/goodness/beauty, theology, Great Books, Trivium, Harkness/seminar, rhetoric)    . 

Gaps: The public materials reviewed do not explicitly reference liturgy/chapel, lower‑school singing/chanting/memory work, or restful learning (scholé); faculty are not described as mentors/living curriculum; and parent formation opportunities are not specified. These omissions reduce category coverage in Religious Foundations, Pedagogy (K–8), Faculty, and Parents & Community relative to the Six Elements criteria and keyword sets   .

Highlights from the school site:
- Mission anchors to “Truth, Goodness, and Beauty” within a “Christ-centered worldview,” and emphasizes a parent–faculty “partnership” .
- Curriculum claims “the time tested learning model of the seven liberal arts,” details the Trivium (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric), Latin, Great Books, original texts, theology, philosophy, oratory, and a 12th‑grade Senior Thesis .
- Upper‑school pedagogy includes formal logic, debate, and Harkness Table conversation, culminating in rhetorical performance and thesis defense .

Aggregate result across the six categories: 2 Classical, 4 Somewhat Classical → Overall SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## Overall Judgment
- Category ratings: Religious Foundations (Somewhat), Purpose/Goal (Classical), Curriculum (Classical), Pedagogy (Somewhat), Faculty (Somewhat), Parents & Community (Somewhat).
- Aggregate rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale: Strong classical core in mission (TGB), curriculum (Trivium, Latin, Great Books, theology/philosophy), and upper‑school pedagogy (Harkness, debate, logic, rhetoric/thesis). To reach a confident “CLASSICAL” overall, the school’s public materials should more clearly evidence liturgical life, restful learning/wonder, lower‑school chanting/memory practices, and explicit faculty and parent‑formation philosophy as articulated in the Six Elements guidance  .

## What would move the needle (to CLASSICAL overall)
Targeted, text‑level additions to the website or handbook would substantively improve coverage of the Six Elements:

- Religious Foundations
  - Publish liturgical/communal practices: daily prayer, chapel, hymnody, feast days, or liturgical calendar rhythms (keywords: liturgy, morning prayer, daily chapel, sacred music, hymnody) .

- Pedagogy (K–8 and whole‑school)
  - Name restful learning explicitly (scholé, contemplation, wonder, deep reading) and show it in schedules or program descriptions .
  - Describe lower‑school memory arts: chanting, singing, recitation, catechism, and poetry memorization (keywords: chanting, recitation, memory work) .

- Faculty
  - Add a “Portrait of a Teacher” (teachers as mentors/model teachers, “living curriculum,” collegial culture) and classical formation/training for faculty  .

- Parents & Community
  - Offer (and publicize) parent formation: classical book clubs, seminars, parent education nights; state an open‑campus/hospitality posture (keywords: parent partnership, co‑educators, parent formation) .

- Curriculum
  - State explicitly that students study “history” (not “social studies”), and add a line about cultivating “wonder” and the “unity of truth” across disciplines, as recommended in the curriculum element .
