## Abstract

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

The Ascent Classical Academy of Northern Denver demonstrates strong classical signals across multiple categories, particularly in School Purpose and Goal (formation of virtue, wisdom, and character) and Pedagogy (Socratic/dialectic methods, teacher-led instruction, Great Books orientation). The Curriculum category also shows solid classical elements (Great Books, liberal-arts framing, primary sources, explicit core-knowledge components). The Parents & Community signals are robust (partnering with families, parental engagement). The Religious and Metaphysical Foundations signal shows virtuous formation and transcendent aims (truth, beauty, virtue, wisdom, wonder) but lacks explicit theology and liturgical/community practices in public-facing materials. The Faculty category signals are present (leadership, mentor-like pedagogy) but would benefit from a more explicit codified “living curriculum” portrayal. The aggregate assessment across categories lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear strengths and a few gaps that, if addressed, could elevate the school to CLASSICAL.

Key category takeaways:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (virtue and transcendent aims are present, theology and liturgy are not explicit in public materials). Evidence: truth/beauty/virtue, core virtues, mission framing.   
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL (formation-focused, virtue-centered, wisdom-oriented). Evidence: formation language, civil virtue, happiness through classical education.  
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (Great Books, liberal-arts framing, core knowledge; some Seven Liberal Arts signals not fully explicit). Evidence: Core Knowledge, Great Books, primary sources, integrated curriculum. 
- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL (Socratic methods, teacher-led instruction, memory/recitation, great-books orientation). Evidence: Socratic techniques, classical hallmarks.  
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (leadership visible; mentorship signals; living-curriculum language is strong in theory but less explicit publicly). Evidence: Headmaster presence, mission to form minds/hearts, mentor-like signals. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL (strong parent partnership, engagement opportunities, welcome for visits). Evidence: parent meetings, engagement language, community guidelines. 

This evaluation uses the knowledge-base Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) as the rubric framework and maps the school’s public materials to the Positive/Negative Keyword indicators (e.g., truth, beauty, virtue, wisdom, wonder, Great Books, primary sources, Socratic methods, formation, etc.). The method systematically compared the school’s public messaging with the classical-school lexicon and rated each category accordingly, then provided a composite assessment and concrete recommendations to move toward a fuller CLASSICAL alignment.

Cited sources include the Northern Denver public materials (evidence of mission, pedagogy, curriculum, virtues, and parental engagement) and the classical-school keyword framework derived from the Six Essential Elements documents.

## Abstract (End)

- Aggregate Classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Key Strengths: formation-oriented goals; virtue-centered mission; Socratic and teacher-led pedagogy; Great Books/primary sources; parental partnership.
- Key Gaps: explicit theology or liturgical/community practices; complete Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) scaffolding; codified faculty portrait as “living curriculum.”
- Needle-Movers Suggested: articulate theology and liturgical practices; strengthen Seven Liberal Arts/Curricular scaffolding; publish a formal living-curriculum faculty portrait; expand explicit Great Books across grades.
