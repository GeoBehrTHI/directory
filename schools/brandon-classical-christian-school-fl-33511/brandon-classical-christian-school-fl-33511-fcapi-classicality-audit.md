## Executive Summary

Brandon Classical Christian School (Florida 33511) presents a clearly classical Christian identity anchored in covenantal, gospel-centered education. Public-facing materials emphasize partnering with parents, a Christ-centered worldview, and a Trivium-based curriculum framed within Great Books and the Great Conversation. The content consistently positions virtue formation, catechesis, and a biblical worldview as core aims, with explicit references to classical methods (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and the Great Books tradition. In terms of the six elements rubric, the school demonstrates robust alignment in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents/Community. Pedagogy shows strong classical underpinnings through Trivium and classical materials, but the site provides less explicit detail about specific upper-school Socratic/seminar practices, suggesting a small but meaningful gap between stated aims and publicly documented pedagogy. Overall assessment: CLASSICAL, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) appearing as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to explicit methods being less overt in site content, despite strong curricular foundations.

Key insights:
- The school’s public materials consistently emphasize covenantal, gospel-centered formation, with explicit references to virtue and biblical worldview (Element 1).
- Curriculum is clearly classical: Trivium, Great Books, Great Conversation, Paideia framing are named (Element 3).
- Purpose/Goal centers on formation, virtue, wisdom, and a classical mission rather than mere job-readiness (Element 2).
- Parental partnership and community alignment are foregrounded (Element 6).
- Pedagogy is strongly classical in orientation (Trivium, memory work, and liturgical/contemplative elements implied by the Classical Christian framing), but explicit documentation of high-school Socratic seminars and formal upper-school debate is less visible online (Element 4).

Cited evidence is drawn from the school’s public materials and related contextual sources in the supplied repository. See citations alongside specific claims below.
