## Executive Summary

Saint Raphael Catholic School (Crystal, MN 55428) presents a strongly classical Catholic program across its public materials. The school identifies itself explicitly as a Catholic, classical institution focused on formation in virtue, wisdom, and the love of truth, goodness, and beauty. Public materials emphasize a Trivium-centered curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a liberal-arts orientation, and a family-centered partnership with parents. Religion instruction is integrated with the Magisterium and sacramental practice, and the school highlights liturgical life, catechesis, and the sacramental life as core to formation. The faculty pages and mission statements frame teachers as living exemplars and part of a “living curriculum,” with clear attention to character formation and a collaborative community with parents. Overall, the school aligns with the six classical-element criteria as CLASSICAL, with strong signals in religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose and goals, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement.

Key evidentiary signals include: the mission to form students in a faithfully Catholic education and to cultivate truth, beauty, and goodness; explicit references to the Magisterium and CGS in religion; a Core Content that foregrounds the Mass, liturgy, sacraments, and virtue; detailed articulation of the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and classical curriculum; lower- and upper-school pedagogy featuring contemplative and Socratic/seminar-based approaches; faculty described as living exemplars and part of a living curriculum; and an emphasis on parents as primary educators and on family partnership. See sources describing mission and Catholic/ liturgical formation, the Core Content religion program, classical curriculum and Trivium, and parent-community collaboration. 

Note: The analysis below uses a keyword-based rubric drawn from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) and its associated lexicon, cross-checked against the public-facing content of the Saint Raphael site. Positive keyword indicators (e.g., truth, virtue, wisdom, liturgy, great books, Trivium, formation) are used to gauge alignment; negative indicators (e.g., secularism, values-neutrality, project-based dominance without classical grounding) are checked where present. The school’s materials show strong presence of positive classical indicators with minimal signals of negative indicators in the public materials reviewed.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

For example, religion is taught “in full accord with the Magisterium of the Catholic Church,” and catechesis (CGS) is described as part of preschool and kindergarten religion pedagogy. 
- The school highlights liturgical life, sacraments, and prayer as integral to formation, including Mass and liturgy, and the sacramental life. 
- The mission statement explicitly cites transcendental values of truth, beauty, and goodness as foundational to education. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: truth, goodness, beauty, liturgy, Mass, liturgical life, Mass, catechesis, Magisterium, sacraments, sacred, imago Dei, theology (implicit in Catholic formation). The site frequently uses terms such as “transcendental values of truth, beauty and goodness” and “faithfully Catholic,” signaling robust positive alignment with religious metaphysical foundations. Approximate positive keyword hits include: truth, beauty, goodness, transcendentals, liturgy, Mass, sacraments, Magisterium, catechesis, sacred, theology. Negative indicators: none apparent in the public materials reviewed.
- Why this rating: The school’s own framing—explicit Catholic identity, liturgical life, sacramental education, and the Magisterium-based theology—maps directly to the classical-element criterion for a grounded religious/metaphysical orientation.

Citations: Catholic/Catholic-family framing, Magisterium, CGS, liturgy, sacraments, and transcendental values are evidenced in the Saint Raphael materials cited above. 

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school presents a formation-focused purpose: “Our mission is to cultivate a family-centered community that classically forms students in a faithfully Catholic education, bringing forth joyful witnesses to the love of Jesus Christ.” This frames virtue and formation as the aim of education rather than mere job preparation. 
- The public content stresses truth, beauty, and goodness as guiding transcendental aims, and cites formation of character, virtue, and wisdom as central outcomes. The core language emphasizes formation over mere information and a philosophical orientation toward higher goods. 
- Classical pedagogy language appears in mission/style—“classically forms students,” “love of wisdom and personal formation,” and explicit reference to a Western classical tradition. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: formation, virtue, wisdom, truth, goodness, beauty, human flourishing, moral imagination, contemplation, lifelong learning, great conversation (the Great Conversation language appears in classical framing and is echoed in the public materials through explicit emphasis on classical formation). The materials show multiple references to formation and the classical mission. Approximate positive keyword hits: formation, virtue, wisdom, truth, goodness, beauty, contemplation, great conversation (conceptual), sacred tradition, imago Dei, Logos.
- Negative: none evident; the materials strongly reject a purely utilitarian or secular framing.
- Why this rating: The school’s stated purpose aligns with classical paideia aims: formation toward virtue and wisdom, with transcendental ideals guiding education.

Citations: Mission and formation language; transcendental values; and formation emphasis are evidenced in the cited mission and core content materials. 

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Liberal arts core is explicitly described in public materials as classical, with emphasis on grammar, logic, and rhetoric. The site highlights a classical curriculum and a focus on the liberal arts, history, and the classics. 
- The Trivium is presented as the framework for instruction (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), with references to memorization, historical study, and classical literature. The public materials discuss the School’s focus on classical texts and logic, rhetoric, and a Western-tradition-based curriculum. 
- There are explicit mentions of a history-centric curriculum and a reading/engagement with classic texts, including middle/high-school literature that emphasizes classic works. The materials describe Sacred history and Western traditions as foundational, and reference the “great books/classical texts” approach in spirit if not always with the exact phrase “Great Books.” 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: grammar, logic, rhetoric, classical, liberal arts, great books (conceptually present through “classical texts” and “great conversation” framing), history (as a classical discipline rather than social studies), wonder, and a global classical canon. Approximate positive keyword hits: grammar, logic, rhetoric, classical, liberal arts, history (as classical history), great books (conceptually referenced), wonder.
- Negative: Montessori in preschool is not inherently negative for a classical assessment, but it is a non-classical pedagogy signal at the earliest stage. If measured strictly, Montessori could be viewed as a potential non-classical element in a pure K–8 classical curriculum, but the site’s overall classical framing and subsequent Trivium emphasis outweigh this at the public level. Positive-to-negative balance remains strongly favorable for CLASSICAL in this category.
- Why this rating: The curriculum is squarely grounded in classical pedagogy, with explicit references to grammar/logic/rhetoric, a Trivium-centered approach, and emphasis on liberal arts and history. The Montessori element is limited to preschool and does not undermine the overall classification.

Citations: Classical curriculum language, Trivium emphasis, and classic-text orientation are evidenced in core curriculum pages. 

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The site describes both contemplative/restful elements and rigorous inquiry, including slower, memory-building routines (e.g., “memory work,” recitation, singing in lower grades) and more Socratic/seminal discussions in upper grades. This aligns with the classic scholé (restful study) approach coupled with Socratic pedagogy. 
- Lower-school pedagogy includes singing, chanting, and memory work; upper grades emphasize Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetorical training (speeches). The public materials explicitly mention these modalities. 
- The public materials describe the upper school as training in rhetoric and giving speeches, consistent with classical pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive: contemplative/restful learning (scholé), memorization/recitation, chanting, singing, memory work, Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, rhetoric, public speaking, masterful discourse, and living-books-inspired pedagogy (as reflected in “classical education” language). Positive keyword hits include: scholé, memorization, recitation, singing/chanting, Socratic, seminars, debates, rhetoric, public speaking. Approximate hits: 7–8.
- Negative: the lexicon’s negative indicator for Pedagogy would flag an over-emphasis on project-based learning (experiential learning) or purely student-directed approaches if present as dominant; the Saint Raphael materials show a balanced mixture rather than an exclusively project-based approach. No explicit project-based dominance is described in public materials.
- Why this rating: The teaching methods align with classical pedagogy across lower-to-upper grades, balancing memory work and contemplation with Socratic discussion and rhetoric instruction.

Citations: Pedagogy signals are evidenced in core content (religion pedagogy and classical curriculum), upper-school Socratic/seminar language, and lower-school memory/chanting practices.
