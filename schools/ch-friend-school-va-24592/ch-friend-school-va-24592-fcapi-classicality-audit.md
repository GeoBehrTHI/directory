## Executive Summary

CH Friend School (CHFS), located in South Boston, VA 24592, presents a distinctly Christian K3–12 private school model that emphasizes character formation, Christ-centered education, and small-class, instructor-led learning. Public-facing materials consistently describe a Christ-centered environment, values-based mission, and a strong partnership with families. At the same time, the publicly available curriculum and pedagogy emphasize traditional instruction, hands-on learning, and college/career pathways, with limited explicit evidence of Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, or Socratic inquiry in the public materials. Taken together, the school demonstrates substantial classical-affinity in its religious foundations, formation-oriented aims, and family-community alignment, but falls short of a full classical profile in curriculum and pedagogy as defined by the standard Six Essential Elements framework. Overall, CHFS is BEST described as CLASSICAL in several core respects, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in governance of formation and community, and NOT FULLY CLASSICAL in curriculum and pedagogy.

Key takeaways:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are clearly present through explicit Christ-centered language, Christ as a primary axis of mission, and Christian values permeating leadership and student formation. Positive signals far outweigh explicit secular framing (with a notable exception that the site also markets itself as non-church-affiliated in one place), which the school frames in its public materials as a distinguishing context rather than a negation of faith identity. Evidence: Christ-centered mission and values; Head Mistress notes; explicit Christ-centered environment in multiple pages.   
- School purpose and goal show emphasis on formation, virtue, and lifelong learning, with clear references to forming responsible citizens and lifelong learners within a Christ-centered frame, alongside college/career pathways. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL standing for this category, given the formation emphasis but also the explicit emphasis on college-prep and career-readiness. Evidence: mission statements and lifelong learning framing; Christ-centered environment; college prep/Career & Tech pathways.  
- Curriculum signals lean toward a traditional, instructor-led approach with strong emphasis on foundational English/reading programs (UFLI) and later curricular tracks (Wit & Wisdom, DE/CTA). There is no direct public emphasis on Great Books, the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), or explicit Great Conversation. This points to a NOT CLASSICAL classification for Curriculum, in tension with the classical ideal. Evidence: UFLI Foundations in early grades; Wit & Wisdom; DE/CTA; no explicit Great Books framework. 
- Pedagogy described publicly is explicitly traditional/instructor-led with hands-on learning, rather than Socratic seminars, scholé (restful learning), or formalization of memorization/recitation in the public-facing materials. Classrooms are described as traditional with small class sizes; this supports a NOT CLASSICAL designation for Pedagogy, though there is some activation of hands-on learning and memory work in the program descriptions. Evidence: “traditional instructor led method” with “hands on learning.” 
- Faculty presentation aligns with classical expectations: the site portrays the Head Mistress and staff as active mentors/model teachers, with a “living curriculum” ethos and a family-like school community. Evidence: Head Mistress message; staff intent to help each student grow; “the living curriculum” framing in public content. 
- Parents and community emphasis is strong, featuring explicit partnerships with families, parent involvement, and open access to school life (visits, communications, and events). This aligns well with classical expectations around family partnership and community formation. Evidence: Parent-focused language, “Partner with families” framing, and explicit parent engagement materials.

What Would Move the Needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category improvements)

- Curriculum
  - Introduce explicit Great Books reading lists and the Great Conversation framework; identify cognitive goals aligned with the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) in core grades.
  - Integrate a cross-disciplinary approach that weaves literature, history, philosophy, and sciences around thematic questions and primary texts.
  - Publicly publish a curriculum map showing connections to the Seven Liberal Arts and Quadrivium elements to signal a fuller classical orientation.

- Pedagogy
  - Adopt a classical-discourse model (Socratic Seminars, Harkness-style discussions) in upper grades to cultivate dialectic reasoning and virtuous inquiry.
  - Incorporate Scholé-inspired pacing and contemplative moments in daily routines to balance rigorous study with reflective time.
  - Expand memorization/recitation and structured oral work (poetic memory, classical chants) as a formal practice within lower and middle grades.

- Public Communication
  - Clarify and publicly separate the church-affiliated identity from the school’s Christian formation language to reduce ambiguity and strengthen the classical-faith alignment, while maintaining faith-based formation.
  - Publish a formal statement detailing how faith informs the curriculum, pedagogy, and assessment in alignment with classical education principles.

- Faculty Development
  - Provide professional development focused on classical pedagogy (Socratic method, living books, classical memory work) to deepen classroom practice and ensure consistent implementation across grades.

- Community/Parent Engagement
  - Expand parent formation offerings (reading groups on classical education, lectures on virtue and literature) to deepen parent understanding of classical aims and their co-educator role.
