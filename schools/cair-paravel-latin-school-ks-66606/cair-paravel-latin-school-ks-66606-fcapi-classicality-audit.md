## Executive Summary

Cair Paravel Latin School (CPLS) presents a robust Classical Christian program. Public materials from CPLS consistently frame education as formation in truth, goodness, and beauty within a Christian and classical framework. The school foregrounds the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a strong Great Books/Great Conversation orientation, and explicit engagement with Latin and Ancient Greek (including a language option for Ancient Greek). Public materials frequently articulate a mission built around virtue, character, and a teleological aim toward Christ-centered human flourishing, rather than solely workforce preparation. Evidence from CPLS communications shows an intentional partnership with families and a community orientation around shared faith and classical formation. Based on the publicly available content, CPLS aligns with a CLASSICAL orientation across all six evaluated categories.

Key evidentiary anchors include CPLS’s mission statement linking education to the Lordship of Jesus Christ and community formation; explicit articulation of the Great Conversation and Great Books in the curriculum and culture; the presence of grammar, logic, and rhetoric across grade levels; a strong emphasis on virtue and formation; visible faculty exemplars and leadership; and a family/community orientation with parent involvement and open-campus expectations. See supporting citations throughout the category sections.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

ABSTRACT

Abstract
Cair Paravel Latin School (CPLS) demonstrates a robust Classical Christian orientation across six evaluation categories: Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community. Evidence from CPLS’s public-facing materials shows a teleological, virtue-centered mission anchored in Christian theism; a curriculum built on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Great Books (Great Conversation); languages (Latin and Ancient Greek) as foundational elements; classic pedagogy combining memory work, dialogue, and rhetorical practice; faculty as virtuous mentors; and an engaged parents/community partnership.

- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: CPLS presents a Christ-centered telos with references to the Lordship of Jesus Christ, as well as faith-based formation and truth/goodness/beauty. Positive cues emphasize transcendentals and worship-like community alignment; no negative signals apparent in public materials. Evidence: mission statements and staff testimonies.  
- School Purpose and Goal: Formation and virtue-centered aims are foregrounded, with the Good Life and timeless truth guiding aims rather than solely workforce outcomes. Evidence: mission statements and byproduct-of-education statements about college readiness as a byproduct. 
- Curriculum: Strong emphasis on trivium (grammar/logical/rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin/Greek, and history/Western civilization, with explicit language about the Great Conversation and primary sources. Evidence: Rhetoric School page; Great Conversation references; JCL page; language offerings.  
- Pedagogy: A mix of contemplative and rigorous approaches, including Socratic/seminal discussion elements in higher grades and rhetoric training; formal memory work and recitation described. Evidence: Rhetoric School description; grammar/logic progression; Great Ideas references.  
- Faculty: Public materials portray model teachers and mentors who integrate faith with classical practice, often highlighting personal testimony and long-tenured staff. Evidence: staff bios and faculty spotlights. 
- Parents & Community: Public messaging consistently frames parents as partners/co-teachers and audiences for formation, with visible open-tour pathways and engagement opportunities. Evidence: admissions/contact materials and parent-facing resources. 

Overall, CPLS aligns with a Classical framework across all six categories, with explicit signals in theology, virtue formation, grammar-logic-rhetoric pedagogy, Great Books orientation, classical languages, and parent/community partnership. The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL.
