## ABSTRACT

John Paul the Great Academy (Lafayette, LA) presents a clearly Classical-Catholic profile. The school situates its mission within a Catholic, domestic-church framework, emphasizing truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and wisdom as real, knowable transcendentals. It explicitly anchors its curriculum in the liberal-arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music) and aims for Great Books-inspired engagement with Western civilization. The school foregrounds liturgical and communal practices (Mass, Chapel), a family-centered governance and partnership model (Domestic Church, Parent Advisory Committee), and a faculty culture described as mentor-like, with teachers as the living curriculum and master teachers. Across Categories 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6, JPG demonstrates CLASSICAL alignment, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) showing SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals due to less explicit public documentation of Socratic/ seminar-based pedagogy in higher grades, though memory-work, poetry, and musical training reflect classical pedagogy in practice.

Key evidence from the school’s public materials:
- Catholic, classical identity and liturgical life: “a small Catholic college preparatory school... classical curriculum”; Catholic liturgical/traditional formation; Mass schedule and chapel life.  
- Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Great Conversation, and Western Civilization in curriculum framing; integration of faith, reason, and culture.  
- Domestic Church framing and family primacy; parent governance and PAC as liaison.  
- Faculty as role models and “living curriculum”; Headmaster leadership and faculty emphasis on virtue.  

The knowledge-base elements (Six Essential Elements and classical-lexicon) align with these findings, confirming that JPG’s public materials emphasize the core Positive Keywords associated with a classical education (truth, virtue, wisdom, Great Books, Great Conversation, liturgy, formation, etc.) and minimize Negative Keywords (e.g., no overt emphasis on purely skills-based, relativistic, or value-neutral approaches).
