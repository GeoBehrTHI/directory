## Executive Summary

St. John Paul II Classical School (Green Bay area, ZIP 54304) presents a strong Catholic, faith-based identity and a clear formation-oriented ethos. Public-facing materials emphasize Catholic liturgy, catechesis, and incarnational formation, with demonstrable emphasis on character formation and sacred practice (e.g., All-school Mass, Advent reflection, catechesis). However, explicit, public articulation of core classical curriculum elements (e.g., grammar–logic–rhetoric, Great Books/Great Conversation, traditional liberal arts sequence) and core classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, disciplined memory work) is less evident in the site content itself, though the school is named to signal classical aims and is connected with Chesterton Academy within the GRACE system. Taken together, the school demonstrates substantial classical-influenced orientation but with gaps in explicit public detail about key classical curriculum and pedagogy markers. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations are clearly present and reinforced via Catholic identity, liturgical life, and incarnational theology. Evidence includes references to Mass, Advent, catechesis, and incarnational education.  
- School Purpose/Goal emphasizes formation, virtue, and human flourishing in a Christian/Catholic frame, including the refrain “Together we form saints.” 
- Curriculum signals are implied through the classical branding (and Chesterton affiliation) but explicit Great Books/Trivium-type components are not clearly enumerated on the public site content reviewed. Element 3 is therefore incomplete in explicitness. 
- Pedagogy content is not overtly described in terms of classical methods (e.g., Socratic seminars, memory work, or scholé) in the accessible site text; no explicit procedural pedagogy is described in the public materials we reviewed. 
- Faculty narrative is not deeply articulated in the public materials as a formal “living curriculum” or mentor-model framework; some references to leadership (e.g., new headmaster) exist, but not a robust faculty portrait. 
- Parents & Community are touched by the GRACE affiliation, worship life, and public activities (e.g., Walk for Virtues), but explicit statements about parents as co-teachers or parent formation programs are not strongly visible in the sample content.
