## Executive Summary

Classical School of Wichita (CSW) is strongly aligned with classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements. Public-facing CSW materials repeatedly emphasize the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), the Great Books/Great Conversation through Omnibus history and literature, a Christ-centered formation, reverence for Truth-Goodness-Beauty, and a family-centered, prayerful, liturgical rhythm in learning. The school presents a coherent classical formation across curriculum, pedagogy, faculty role, and parent partnership, and it maintains explicit references to virtue, character formation, and a “living curriculum” through its faculty and community ethos. Evidence from CSW’s site materials shows explicit integration of theology, biblical foundations, liturgical practice (hymn singing, liturgical rhythm), a K-12 Trivium-based progression, Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin, and conscious formation of a Portrait of a Graduate guided by Christian virtue. The school also foregrounds parental partnership as a core principle, aligning with the classical model of formation that involves families as co-educators.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- Most evident strengths: explicit Trivium framework, Great Books/Great Conversation, integrated liberal arts approach (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin), and a clearly stated teleology focused on virtue, wisdom, and God-centered formation. The public materials consistently foreground Truth-Goodness-Beauty, theological framing, baptismal/catechetical vocabulary, and a communal liturgical rhythm. These features map strongly to the Positive Keyword indicators in the classical lexicon and to the Six Essential Elements’ recommended practices.
- Notable alignment features: (a) curricular structure around the Trivium and Great Books; (b) emphasis on formation, virtue, and wisdom over mere career-focused outcomes; (c) ongoing parent partnership; (d) a “living curriculum” concept via faculty modeling virtue and humanities mastery.

Citations anchor points for the above general assessment include CSW mission and faith language, Trivium emphasis, Great Books/Great Conversation references, and explicit parent partnership.

- Mission/Foundations: Truth-Goodness-Beauty; in Christ; biblical/theological framing.
- Curriculum and Great Books: Omnibus/Great Conversation; grammar-logic-rhetoric progression; Latin; history across curricula with focus on classical civilizations.
- Pedagogy: lower-school singing/memorization with a contemplative (Scholé) thread; upper-school Socratic discussion and rhetoric-focused work (thesis, defense).
- Faculty: living curriculum and faculty-as-mentors; explicit emphasis on virtue and modeling.
- Parents & Community: formalized parent partnership and co-teaching stance; opportunities for parental formation and open campus visits.

Key CSW-cited sources illustrating these features include mission statements, Trivium pedagogy, Great Books/Omnibus references, Fine Arts and liturgical elements, and explicit teacher-student/family partnerships. ## Executive Summary — Category-by-Category Snapshot

### Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Mission statements and faith-centered framing explicitly foreground Truth, Goodness, and Beauty and Christ-centered formation. Texts describe education as formation in a Christian context, with integration of theology and biblical instruction throughout the program.  
- Communal liturgical practices and worship language, and statements about The Bible, Gospels, and a theologically grounded worldview appear in multiple CSW materials (e.g., “Distinctively Christian,” Statement of Faith elements in governance/public materials).  
- The Great Conversation and theological framing are connected to the Trivium and classical formation (grammar/logic/rhetoric) with historical/theological anchors. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: truth, goodness, beauty; Jesus Christ, gospel, Bible/scripture; theology; liturgy; worship; reverence; wonder; imago Dei; logos; virtue; wisdom; virtue formation; sacred music; chant. These map to the core Positive Keyword set for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations.
- Negative indicators: virtually none evident in public CSW materials (no explicit relativism or secular-humanist framing observed).

Why this rating
- The school explicitly treats education as formation in a Christian-theological frame and integrates theology into curricular and communal life. The presence of liturgical/classical practices and a theology-grounded mission aligns strongly with a CLASSICAL orientation for this category.

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school’s mission centers on cultivation of wisdom and virtue, not merely job preparation; formation of character and human excellence is foregrounded. The mission states nourishing hearts and minds on Truth, Goodness, and Beauty so that in Christ they can glorify God. 
- Reference to permanence of truth and beauty and the teleology of forming virtuous and wise human beings; emphasis on formation over information. 
- The public materials consistently frame education as “formation” and the cultivation of virtue (e.g., Portrait of a Graduate, habit formation, and moral imagination) within a classical-Christian frame. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, truth, beauty, the true/good/beautiful; liberal education/telos; scholé/ contemplation; Great Books/Great Conversation as a humanistic telos.
- Negative keywords observed: none (no explicit “career-readiness” or job-prep framing). The materials emphasize formation over workforce preparation.

Why this rating
- The CSW materials demonstrate a consistent aim toward virtue, wisdom, and humane excellence rather than vocational metrics, aligning with CLASSICAL expectations for purpose and goal.

### Category 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Core curricular framework explicitly centers on the Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Trivium, with a Quadrivium extension (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as foundational elements. The school presents Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric stages (K-12) and discusses their roles within Omnibus Ancient/Medieval history and literature, i.e., the Great Conversation. 
- History is studied throughout the curriculum through Omnibus (Ancient/Medieval History and Literature), not labeled as “Social Studies.” This is explicit in the Trivium/GREAT BOOKS framing. 
- Great Books/Great Conversation is embedded through Omnibus and public-facing statements about the Great Conversation. Latin, logic, rhetoric, and classical literary study appear across stages. 
- Education aims to cultivate wonder; the lower grades emphasize memory, recitation, and devotional/wonderful experiences, while upper grades emphasize rhetoric and thesis work. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), Great Books/Great Conversation, Omnibus, Latin, history as a canonical discipline, memory/recitation, wonder, and integration of arts and sciences.
- Negative keywords observed: minimal to none; there is no explicit emphasis on project-based-only or purely experiential learning displacing liberal arts or Great Books, which would be a negative signal per the Six Elements lexicon.

Why this rating
- The curriculum language and structure align squarely with classical pedagogy and content: Trivium, Great Books, and the integrated liberal arts. The public materials emphasize a Great Conversation through Omnibus and explicit classical progression, fulfilling Category 3 criteria.

### Category 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower-grade pedagogy includes singing, memorization, and restfulness in learning (recitation, scholé-like elements). Upper grades emphasize Socratic discussion, seminars, and debates, with students producing theses and defending arguments (senior thesis). The school explicitly connects the Trivium to pedagogy (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric). 
- The Great Conversation is framed as a central pedagogical and curricular device; upper-level rhetoric and thesis work align with classical rhetorical training. 
- The division of stages (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and the emphasis on memory, recitation, and structured discussion reflect a balanced pedagogy that supports both contemplative (Scholé) and rigorous discourse (Socratic/seminar-style).

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: scholé/restful learning, recitation/memorization/chanting; singing; memory-based learning; Socratic discussion/seminars; rhetoric training; public speaking; senior thesis; defended theses; learning through dialogue.
- Negative keywords observed: limited presence of purely project-based or constructivist approaches; the site emphasizes classical forms of pedagogy rather than primarily project-based or student-directed paradigms. This maps to a lack of the modern “project-based learning” emphasis in public materials (which would be a negative signal in this rubric).

Why this rating
- Pedagogy aligns with classical expectations: restfulness in parts (Scholé), memorization and chant in lower grades, Socratic discourse and rhetoric at higher levels, and a thesis-based culminative capstone in high school. This combination supports CLASSICAL status in pedagogy.

### Category 5: School Faculty — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Faculty are described as models for students, mentors, and living exemplars; there is emphasis on virtue, skill, and knowledge in teacher role, with Luke 6:40 as a guiding verse. The faculty are presented as the “living curriculum” and as colleagues who share in the classical mission. 
- Portraits of faculty and staff are used to communicate the expected character and professional standards; teachers are expected to be committed to Christ, excellent teachers, and lovers of learning. 
- Public materials emphasize a collegial, virtuous, mentor-like faculty culture, aligned with classical education expectations of the teacher as master-mentor. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: living curriculum; portrait of a faculty/staff member; mentor; virtuous exemplars; master-teacher model; collegial culture; teachers as mentors; Christ-centered professionalism.
- Negative keywords observed: none that signal a departure from classical faculty norms.

Why this rating
- The faculty framing emphasizes virtue, lifelong learning, and mentorship, aligning with CLASSICAL expectations for the faculty category.

### Category 6: School Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Parent Partnership materials state that CSW does not function above parental authority but partners with parents in education; parents have delegated responsibility and are co-educators, with opportunities for involvement and formation. This is a core classical-education dynamic. 
- The school indicates it educates parents through the website, book studies, and gatherings so they can learn more about classical education; there are open invitations for visits and involvement. 
- The plan for parent engagement includes explicit parental formation and a family-centered approach; the community is described as activated through parent participation and shared mission. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: partnership with parents; co-teachers; parental formation; book studies; parent involvement; open campus, visits; family-centered education.
- Negative keywords observed: none; no consumerist/low-engagement cues; the content emphasizes active partnership.

Why this rating
- The CSW materials firmly place parents as co-educators and emphasize ongoing parental formation and involvement in classical education, fulfilling Category 6’s criteria.
