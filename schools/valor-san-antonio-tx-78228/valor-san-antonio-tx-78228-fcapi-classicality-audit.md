## Executive Summary

Valor San Antonio, TX (78228) presents a robust alignment with classical education through its explicit emphasis on a classical curriculum, memorization, Great Books, Latin, and a deliberately virtue- and beauty-centered vision. Public materials from Valor loudly foreground a formation-oriented mission, a reverence for truth, goodness, and beauty, and a culture of community that includes families and extended stakeholders. The school’s rhetoric and curricular signals indicate a strong classical orientation, particularly in its curriculum (Great Books, poetry memorization, Latin, nature study, fine arts) and its stated aims (cultivation of virtue and wisdom; formation over information). However, several hallmark features of a fully classical program—explicit, widespread emphasis on Trivium/Quadrivium disciplines (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as foundational liberal arts; a consistently Socratic/Harkness pedagogy; explicit liturgical/theological integration; and a fully explicit, cohesive "living curriculum" manifested in pedagogy across all grade bands—are less clearly articulated in the public-facing materials. As a result, Valor San Antonio is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with strong strands in curriculum and purpose that anchor it toward CLASSICAL, and with some missing or less explicit marks in theology-liturgical integration, explicit Trivium/Quadrivium framing, and pedagogy that foreground Socratic discourse and scholé-style contemplative practice.

What moves Valor San Antonio toward full CLASSICAL are concrete shifts that make its classical commitments more explicit and systematically implemented at every category: articulate and publish an explicit Trivium/Quadrivium framing, integrate theology or a robust Christian worldview within the Great Books and history sequence, expand and document a consistent Socratic/Harkness pedagogy across upper grades, and foreground liturgical or communal practices that align with traditional classical patterns of formation. Strengthening family partnerships with clearly defined roles in formation, and documenting faculty development as a living curriculum anchored in classical methods, would further reinforce the classical identity.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate and publish a formal Trivium/Quadrivium framing, including explicit grammar, logic, rhetoric, and the quadrivium disciplines as foundational across the grade bands; ensure history is taught as liberal-arts history rather than solely as social studies; emphasize and document Great Books as a central spine with primary sources.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Implement and publicize a consistent Socratic/Harkness-based pedagogy for upper grades; bolster the role of scholé (restful learning) and contemplative activities in the daily schedule; demonstrate how memory work, recitation, and disciplined dialogue integrate with inquiry-based learning.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Deepen the living-curriculum model by clearly documenting teacher development in classical pedagogy; publish profiles of exemplary teachers and a teacher-formation plan aligned with classical aims; elevate teacher-student mentorship as a visible, ongoing program.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand parent-formation offerings (book studies, lectures, seminars) with a published calendar and outcomes; maintain open-visit policies with structured parent-involvement roles that align with classical formation and family catechesis where appropriate.
