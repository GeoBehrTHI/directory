## ABSTRACT

San Marcos Academy (SMA) demonstrates CLASSICAL leanings in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and in School Philosophy (Categories 1 and 2), supported by explicit Christ-centered formation, worship/discipleship, mission/vision statements, and virtue-focused leadership development. The pedagogical and community dimensions show signs of classical influence (lower-school memory work and contemplative elements; leadership and community-building in upper grades; strong teacher mentorship). However, explicit canonical classical curriculum components—such as a public-facing Great Books/Great Conversation program and a formal grammar-logic-rhetoric (GLR) progression—are not consistently documented in SMA’s public-facing materials; likewise, explicit Socratic seminars and rhetoric training across the upper grades are not clearly evidenced. This places SMA in the realm of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall.

Category-by-category snapshot:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL — Christ-centered, worship/discipleship, theology-grounded formation are central, supported by mission and core-values language.  
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL — Formation, virtue, integrity, lifelong learning, and service framed as primary aims; leadership rooted in moral formation. 
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — TfT-based, Bible-led curriculum with Deep Roots; no publicly documented Great Books or explicit GLR sequence in public materials. 
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Contemplative, memory-rich lower grades; dialogic elements in upper grades, but lacking explicit Socratic/Great-Books-based pedagogy in public materials. 
- Faculty: CLASSICAL — Faculty as living exemplars, mentors, and core drivers of the curriculum; strong emphasis on virtue and character formation. 
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Visible parent engagement, testimonials, and community events; however, explicit “parents as co-teachers” structures are not clearly evidenced in public materials. 

Overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. SMA clearly embodies classical virtues and a strong formation ethos within a Christian TfT framework, yet it lacks some of the explicit public-facing elements (Great Books/Great Conversation, GLR sequencing, and clear Socratic pedagogy) that typify a fully classical program. The needle would move toward CLASSICAL with targeted public articulation and implementation of the classical curriculum primitives, structured Socratic and rhetorical pedagogy, and formal parent-education/co-teaching initiatives.

End of REPORT.
