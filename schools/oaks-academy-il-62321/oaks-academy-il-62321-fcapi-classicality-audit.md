## Executive Summary

The Oaks Academy IL 62321 presents a distinctly Classical Christian education model, anchored in a Christ-centered, classical framework that emphasizes virtue, wisdom, and a robust, historically rooted curriculum. Public-facing materials consistently describe the school as “Christ-centered, classical education,” with explicit references to the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Great Books, and the Great Conversation, all within a Paideia/Christian-theology framework. Evidence from the school’s web materials highlights soul formation, virtue cultivation, and a biblical worldview as core aims, alongside a strong emphasis on parental partnership and engaged community.

Overall, the Oaks site demonstrates a Classical orientation across all six rubric categories, with explicit language and programmatic references that align closely with classical education criteria in the Six Essential Elements framework. The public materials emphasize truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and theological grounding; a Trivium-centered curriculum; a Great Books/Great Conversation approach; contemplative and Socratic-era pedagogy implicit in Paideia-traditions; faculty as living mentors within a community; and strong parent involvement as co-educators. In short, Oaks Academy IL 62321 aligns with Classical to a high degree.

- Evidence of pedagogy: Paideia-style terminologies (Great Conversation, seminars implied through classical pedagogy); emphasis on memory, contemplation, and restoration of scholé-like learning in the public materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school desires to further lock in a fully explicit “Socratic seminars” branding in pedagogy, the school could publish explicit statements or pages describing upper-school seminars, Socratic dialogues, and formal seminars as a core modality. The existing Paideia/Great Books/C Trivium framework supports this direction; public communications could explicitly name “Socratic seminars” and provide examples of student-led debates to clarify pedagogy for external stakeholders. (Potential corroboration: classical pedagogy literature in the Six Essential Elements corpus; not a required shift, but a potential needle-mover for transparency.)

- The school already demonstrates strong parent partnership; to push toward “stronger formation of affections” and “cultural transmission” as explicit daily practice, the school could publish a public-facing “Parent Formation” program (e.g., book studies, liturgical/seasonal family activities) with a calendar and sample materials to amplify parent formation beyond visits and occasional gatherings.

- A formal, published “Living Curriculum Portrait” for Faculty (as recommended in the Six Essential Elements) could be added to website content to further illuminate the intended profile of teachers as virtuous exemplars, mentors, and co-educators in a classical sense.
