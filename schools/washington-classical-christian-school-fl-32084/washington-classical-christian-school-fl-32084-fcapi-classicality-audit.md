## Executive Summary

Washington Classical Christian School Florida (St. Augustine) presents a robust classical Christian program that centers on the Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), integration of biblical faith with all disciplines, deliberate Great Books/primary-source study, and explicit parental partnership. Public materials repeatedly describe a Christian worldview anchored in God’s Word, theology, and a culture of virtuous formation, memory-work, and rhetorical expression. Across the public content, the school foregrounds a classical orientation (grammar-dialectic-rhetoric; Latin; Great Books; historical-literary study) coupled with a clearly Christian framework (Bible, theology, biblical worldview). Based on the six-category classical rubric and keyword indicators, Washington CCS demonstrates CLASSICAL alignment in all six categories, with explicit evidence of religious-metaphysical foundations, formation-focused purpose, a liberal-arts curriculum, Trivium-based pedagogy, a faculty modeled as the living curriculum, and strong parent-community partnership.

Key highlights from the source materials include:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations: explicit references to God’s Word, Bible, theology, biblical worldview, and a Christian approach embedded in learning (evidence cited).
- Purpose and goals: emphasis on formation, virtue, wisdom, and lifelong learning; engagement with Great Books in the curriculum (evidence cited).
- Curriculum: clear Trivium framing (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), Great Books/primary sources, Latin, Bible/theology/history/ literature integration (evidence cited).
- Pedagogy: emphasis on the Trivium across levels, rhetoric, public-speaking opportunities, and a structured progression (evidence cited).
- Faculty: descriptions of godly teachers, living curriculum, and a faculty-centered culture; explicit narrative of teacher roles within the program (evidence cited).
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with parents, parental formation opportunities, and open-campus/visit culture (evidence cited).

- The Trivium approach is explicitly framed as serving God’s truth and a biblical worldview (e.g., “God’s word as the basis and center of all learning; teaching students to think and live biblically”).
- The public materials articulate a Christian, biblical worldview and a goal of forming character through religious foundations.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: God’s Word, Bible, Theology, biblical worldview, Christian, worship-oriented references (e.g., glorify the Lord). These align with the Lexicon’s Religious Foundations (truth/virtue/theology/liturgy/worship).
- Negative keywords: none of the standard negative indicators (relativism, secular humanism, morally neutral) appear in these explicit materials.

Why this rating

- The school’s materials foreground transcendental moral realism via a theologically grounded, explicitly Christian framework (God’s Word and biblical worldview), with liturgical/congregationally oriented language (e.g., unity of faith and learning). This matches the Classical Elements’ emphasis on grounded theologies and liturgical/cultural practice rather than secular, relativistic schooling. Therefore: CLASSICAL.

Executive Summary — Final Classification

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle

- If you want to move toward an even more explicit “fully Classical” posture (in the sense of strengthening the Six Elements rubric beyond current practice), consider:
    - Explicitly naming and documenting the Great Conversation across grades (e.g., a published, grade-aligned list of Great Books for each year, with a cross-reference to primary sources and supplemental readings).
    - Broadening liturgical/community practices in public-facing materials (e.g., a clearly articulated liturgical calendar, chapel/mass or liturgical song choices, and reflections on wonder as a core pedagogy).
    - Expanding a formal “living-curriculum” portrait that showcases faculty development tied to classical pedagogy (e.g., structured professional development focused on progrades in the Trivium across grade bands).
    - Increasing explicit articulation of virtue formation in a measurable way (e.g., virtue-based assessments that align with the positive keywords in the Six Elements lexicon, including explicit references to truth, goodness, and beauty in student work and assessments).
    - More direct paraphrasing of the Seven Liberal Arts as core frame for the curriculum (e.g., a published map showing how all disciplines map onto grammar/dialectic/rhetoric and quadrivium, with examples).
