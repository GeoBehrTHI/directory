## Executive Summary

Clarksburg Classical Academy (WV 26301) presents a clearly classical orientation anchored in the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and a mission to form virtuous, capable students. Public materials emphasize character formation, virtue, and a civically engaged, well-rounded education, with concrete references to the Trivium, classical texts in ELA, and a four-part instructional model (ACCEL Instructional Model) that centers on teacher quality, extended instructional time, formative assessments, and individualized learning. However, explicit public commitments to Great Books/Great Conversation, liturgical/theological grounding, and full Socratic pedagogy across all grades are less visible. On balance, the school is best classified as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with meaningful classical elements in place but room for fuller alignment with all six classical elements in the public-facing materials.

Key takeaways:
- Core classical elements are present: Trivium framing, grammar/logic/rhetoric focus, character education, and a robust teacher-development and assessment framework through ACCEL. Public pages describe the four-part ACCEL Instructional Model (AIM) and a heavy emphasis on higher-order thinking and writing. Evidence includes explicit mention of the Trivium and the AIM components.  
- Strength in school purpose and culture: mission statements emphasize transformation, virtue, and a well-rounded formation of students, with explicit language about shaping character and educating the whole person. This supports a CLASSICAL orientation in spirit, albeit not always in the precise vocabulary of the canonical six elements. 
- Curriculum and pedagogy show strong classical signals (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; classical texts in ELA; emphasis on formation). However, there is less explicit public articulation of Great Books/the Great Conversation, liturgical/theological grounding, or full Socratic seminar practice across all grades in the publicly accessible materials. This keeps the rating in the “Somewhat Classical” range rather than “Classical” for several categories.  
- Parent and community engagement is foregrounded (family partnership, community integration, open campus language, calendar of events, etc.), aligning with the classical ideal of parents as co-educators and active community participants. This supports a CLASSICAL rating in category 6.

Aggregate assessment: Across the six categories, Clarksburg Classical Academy WV 26301 exhibits a strong classical orientation in several categories (Faculty; Parents & Community) and clear classical elements in Curriculum and Pedagogy, with formation-focused aims evident in School Purpose and Goal and some Religious/Metaphysical signals. Taken together, the school lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with solid CLASSICAL signals in at least two categories (Faculty and Parents & Community) and meaningful but not always fully explicit classical commitments in the other categories.

What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)

- Explicitly foreground Great Books and the Great Conversation in the Curriculum category, including a stated plan to engage primary sources and canonical works across grades, and how these intersect with history, philosophy, and literature.
- Add explicit references to Socratic seminars or dialectic in upper grades, and describe concrete Pedagogy practices that align with scholé/restful learning and contemplative study, when appropriate.
- Strengthen Religious/Metaphysical Foundations in public statements (even for non-religious but philosophically oriented classical programs) by clearly naming transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty) and, where relevant, liturgical or communal practices that support formation of character and virtue.
- Expand explicit articulation of the Seven Liberal Arts (including Quadrivium and Latin/Greek where applicable) in Curriculum, showing how these disciplines structure the program beyond grammar/logic/rhetoric.
- Maintain and enhance Parent & Community programming by adding more structured parent-education offerings (classical book studies, parent seminars on classical education, etc.) to deepen the co-teacher model.
