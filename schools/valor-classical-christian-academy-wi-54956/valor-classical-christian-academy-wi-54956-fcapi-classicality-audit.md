## Executive Summary

Valor Classical Christian Academy (Neenah, WI 54956) presents a robust classical Christian program that is largely CLASSICAL across the core dimensions of a classical education. The public materials emphasize a theology-infused, virtue-centered formation model, continuous integration of biblical truth, and a structure built around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with explicit attention to Great Books / Great Conversation and a liberal-arts rhythm. The school places strong emphasis on virtue, formation, wisdom, and character, with communal Christian life integrated into learning and school life. Pedagogy includes concrete lower-school practices (singing, memorization, memory work) and upper-school opportunities for reasoning, debate, and rhetoric, aligning with classical expectations. The governance, faculty descriptions, and parent/community engagement also echo classical norms (living curriculum, partnership with parents, and a curriculum map aligned with the Great Conversation). Overall, Valor is CLASSICAL with a clearly stated formation-centric aims and a public presence that emphasizes virtue, truth, and Christian formation.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Lower grades emphasize memory work, chants, singing, and recitation as instructional supports (typical of the grammar stage). Valor explicitly depicts younger students learning through singing, memorization, and memory-based review.
- Upper grades emphasize logic, questioning, and guided inquiry; the Logic School description highlights questions, debate, and pursuing truth, with an emphasis on reasoning and clear communication.
- There are references to seminars and rhetoric training as part of the upper-grade experience (e.g., “logic school,” and rhetoric-focused language in some staff bios). However, explicit, consistent public documentation of formal, repeated Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars across all upper grades is not uniformly explicit in the public content. The materials do reference Socratic-style activities in the broader classical tradition (public lexicon and contextual materials) but do not always present a tight, current-year, across-grade schedule for Socratic seminars on Valor’s site.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Explicitly document and publicly showcase a consistent, school-wide, Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across upper grades (including 9–12 if/when added). The rubric emphasizes Socratic discussion in higher grades; providing concrete, public evidence of regular seminars and a formal, gradated Paideia trajectory would push Element 4 to CLASSICAL.
- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Consider more explicit liturgical practices or prayer/chapel rhythms tied to a liturgical calendar, documented publicly (e.g., morning prayers, liturgical celebrations, or chapel schedules). The Lexicon highlights liturgical and communal practices; public examples would strengthen the score.
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Maintain and publicly publish explicit Great Books selections and a more formal listing of primary sources across grades, with a stated rationale aligned to Christian truth. A visible cross-subject integration map that showcases Great Books within grammar, logic, and rhetoric would reinforce the CLASSICAL status.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Continue to grow public-facing “living-curriculum” narratives that show teachers as exemplars of virtue and pedagogy in action, including teacher professional development grounded in classical pedagogy (e.g., seminars for faculty on classical pedagogy, or a formal teacher-formation section).

These are concrete steps that would move Element 4 toward CLASSICAL and further reinforce the other categories, especially Element 1 and Element 3.

Citations (for needle-moving suggestions): references to the public approach to Trivium pedagogy and liturgical integration in Valor’s materials; Great Books / Great Conversation language; and the public governance/teacher formation language.
