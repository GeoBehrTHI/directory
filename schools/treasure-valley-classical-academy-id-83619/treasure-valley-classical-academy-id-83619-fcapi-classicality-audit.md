## Executive Summary

Treasure Valley Classical Academy (TVCA) presents a coherent, public-charter expression of the American classical education tradition. Its public-facing materials consistently foreground a content-rich, classical curriculum, a focus on virtue and civic formation, and explicit engagement with the Great Books through Socratic inquiry and memorization practices. The school emphasizes virtues (e.g., Courage, Courtesy, Honesty, Perseverance, Self-Government, Service), the Great Books/Great Conversation framework, and memory-based pedagogy (recitation, singing) along with Socratic seminars in the upper grades. There is clear alignment with the classical pedagogy toolkit (grammar/logic/rhetoric; memory work; Socratic discourse; great books; liberal arts framing) and with Hillsdale College’s Barney Charter School Initiative. The religious or liturgical foundations are not explicit in the public materials, which places TVCA solidly within a secular/classical-education frame rather than a theologically grounded frame.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key supporting signals
- Mission and curricular framing center on virtue, truth, goodness, and beauty within a classical, content-rich approach (e.g., “classical, content-rich curriculum that emphasizes virtuous living, traditional learning, and civic responsibility”) and a Great Books/Great Conversation orientation. 
- Curriculum and program notes repeatedly emphasize Great Books, Socratic discussion (especially in upper grades), memorization/recitation, and upper-level rhetoric/public speaking, with a clear Trivium/Quadrivium-inspired rhythm (grammar/logic/rhetoric concepts appear in the classical discussion of the model).  
- The school is affiliated with Hillsdale College and the Barney Charter School Initiative, aligning with a national classical-education framework. 
- Core virtues and the Great Conversation are explicit in mission and vision statements (e.g., virtues as a core formation aim, and a vision of the Great Books curriculum engaging the deepest questions of truth, justice, virtue, and beauty). 

Note: In Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations), explicit theology, liturgical practices, or worship are not foregrounded in TVCA’s public materials. This weakens alignment with the religious-metaphysical foundations of classical schooling, which the Six Essential Elements describe as a potential component. The remaining five categories show strong classical alignment.

What Would Move the Needle

- The TVCA profile is strongly CLASSICAL across four categories, with Element 2–6 solidly CLASSICAL. Element 1 lags due to a lack of explicit theological, liturgical, or metaphysical grounding in public materials. To move the needle toward a more robust, fully CLASSICAL standing in Element 1, TVCA could:
  - Add explicit statements about theological or metaphysical foundations within public materials (e.g., a stated integration of sacred traditions or explicit transcendent aims, where appropriate for the school’s mission).
  - Include explicit references to liturgical or communal practices (even if non-denominational) as part of the school’s culture, or clearly articulate how virtues and transcendental ideals are cultivated in a way that aligns with classical education’s metaphysical dimension.
  - Include a brief statement about how the school conceptualizes truth, goodness, and beauty as objective, knowable, and integral to the formation of character, with a clear rationale for how this is integrated into the curriculum and daily life.

- These changes would bring Element 1 into closer alignment with the Positive Keywords in the Six Essential Elements framework and push Element 1 toward CLASSICAL.
