## ABSTRACT

Treasure Valley Classical Academy largely embodies CLASSICAL education across four of the six classical categories (Purpose and Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community) and shows a strong classical approach in its mission to cultivate virtue and wisdom through a Great Books/Great Conversation framework, a disciplined memory-based pedagogy, and a Hillsdale/Barney Charter backbone. The Public Materials reveal a robust classical curriculum (Great Books, primary sources in history, grammar/logic/rhetoric framing, Latin/Greek roots, senior thesis), Socratic dialogue in upper grades, and a heavy emphasis on virtues (Courage, Courtesy, Honesty, Perseverance, Self-Government, Service). Parental involvement and community partnership are also emphasized, aligning with the classical model of parent as co-educator.

Category-by-category summary:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: NOT CLASSICAL — TVCA does not publicly foreground theology, liturgy, or explicit religious metaphysics in its public-facing materials.
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL — formation, virtue, and knowledge of truth/goodness/beauty are central.
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL — explicit emphasis on liberal arts framing, Great Books, primary sources, and integrated history/literature/science.
- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL — mix of contemplative (Scholé) and rigorous Socratic/seminar-based methods; memorization/recitation; rhetoric and public speaking.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL — living curriculum; masterful teachers; mentorship; subject-matter mastery.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL — active partnership with families, parent sessions, and community engagement.

This combination yields an overall CLASSICAL classification with a potential needle toward improvement primarily in making explicit any metaphysical/theological foundations (Element 1). The school already demonstrates a strong and coherent classical education program through content, pedagogy, and community integration.
