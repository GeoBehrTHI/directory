# Classicality Audit: Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- School URL used: https://bishopreicher.com/
- High-level takeaways:
  - Strong religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit (Catholic liberal arts, theology, sacred music; daily prayer, weekly Mass; explicit language of virtue and the true, good, and beautiful)  .
  - Clear classical aims appear in mission-facing language (“Catholic liberal arts tradition,” “classical learning,” “classical literature,” and Latin)  .
  - Curriculum signals are promising (Latin, classical literature, sacred music, philosophy, and theology), but the site does not yet consistently state the Trivium/Quadrivium or “Great Books/Great Conversation” explicitly across divisions.
  - Pedagogy and Faculty pages provide limited explicit evidence of hallmark classical methods (Socratic seminar, chanting/recitation, rhetoric orations) or teachers-as-mentors language; there is one modern-education signal (“student-centered approach”) that runs counter to classical phrasing .
  - Community and parent partnership language exists (inviting open houses; close-knit community) but lacks explicit “parent partnership/co-educators” phrasing common in classical schools .

Aggregate Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Rationale: The Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and Purpose & Goal categories clearly meet classical criteria. Curriculum indicators are strong but incomplete on formal Trivium/Quadrivium and Great Books language. Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parent Partnership evidence is comparatively light on explicit classical phrasing and exemplars, with one modern-education phrase (“student-centered approach”) noted in marketing copy .

What would move the needle
- Curriculum
  - Add explicit Trivium/Quadrivium language and a succinct list of the Seven Liberal Arts; name “Great Books”/“Great Conversation” and “primary sources” in division pages; affirm “History” as such (avoid generic “social studies”) per the Curriculum criteria and lexicon  .
- Pedagogy
  - Publish concrete pedagogical practices: K–6 memory work with singing/chanting/recitation; 7–8 logic/debate; 9–12 Socratic seminar, disputation, and rhetoric orations/thesis defense. Use explicit terms: “Socratic seminar,” “recitation,” “chanting,” “scholé/restful learning,” “progymnasmata,” “rhetoric capstone” as applicable .
  - Review public wording to reduce modern-education phrasing like “student-centered approach” and instead emphasize “formation,” “teacher-led,” and “Socratic” discourse per the Six Elements’ negative/positive lexicon guidance  .
- Faculty
  - Add a Faculty Philosophy page describing teachers as mentors/models of virtue (“the living curriculum”), collegial faculty culture, and teacher formation in classical pedagogy .
- Parents & Community
  - Add explicit language naming “parent partnership,” “parents as co-educators,” and any parent-formation offerings (book clubs, classical education workshops) to align with classical community practices .
