## Executive Summary

Our evaluation of Our Lady Seat Wisdom (OLSW) using the dual-vector approach (school-site content from the Chesterton Academy network and the classical-school knowledge lexicon) indicates a robust CLASSICAL profile across all six rubric categories. The school's public materials reveal a deliberate, liturgical Catholic framework, a paideia-oriented aims of virtue and wisdom, a cohesive liberal-arts curriculum anchored in Great Books, and pedagogy centered on Socratic inquiry, memorization/recitation, and virtuous teacher modeling. The faculty and community language likewise reflect a culture of mentorship, partnership with families, and a shared mission around Truth, Goodness, and Beauty. The analysis aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) framework, including explicit references to liturgical practice, theology, great books, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) in curriculum, Socratic pedagogy, and a living curriculum embodied by teachers and families. See evidence notes and keyword mappings cited below.

- What would move the needle: None of the six categories require major changes to achieve CLASSICAL status. If anything, the school could further codify explicit phrasing in public materials to foreground certain classical phrases (e.g., explicitly naming Trivium, Great Books as shared language), but the existing materials already meet the criteria robustly.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: OLSW’s public materials articulate a Catholic, liturgical, and theologically grounded program. Daily Mass, adoration opportunities, pilgrimages and retreats, sacred art and crucifixes, chaplains, and theology taught from the Catechism are described as core to daily life and pedagogy. The Three Pillars (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) anchor formation in a theology-infused framework. See Catholic Identity and Our Spiritual Approach sections, which emphasize liturgical life, theology, and Catholic formation.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Present positive indicators include Mass, liturgy, catechesis, theology, daily Mass, adoration, pilgrimages, sacred art, chaplains. No explicit public signals of secularization or religious neutrality were found in the core materials. Estimated positive keyword coverage is high; negative signals appear minimal or absent in public content. Positive keywords: 6; Negative keywords: 0. This strongly supports CLASSICAL.
    - Why this rating: The school presents a transcendent, theologically grounded, liturgical, and communal approach to education, meeting the criteria for Transcendental Moral Realism, Grounded in Theology, and Liturgical/Communal Practices. The explicit Catholic identity and daily liturgical rhythm anchor the category solidly in CLASSICAL territory.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: The school emphasizes formation of virtue and wisdom as primary ends (Three Pillars: Intellect, Character, Spirituality; Cultura Vitae). Mission statements frame education as formation, development of human excellence, and pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The aim is to form joyful leaders and saints, not simply prepare for work; the Great Books/Westem-civilizational formation is foregrounded.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, truth/goodness/beauty, contemplation, and human flourishing. The site’s framing aligns with formation-focused aims rather than a purely vocational outcome. Negative signals (career-readiness emphasis) are not present in public materials. Positive keywords: 5–6; Negative keywords: 0. This supports CLASSICAL.
    - Why this rating: The public materials explicitly present formation toward virtue and wisdom as the telos of education, anchored in the realness of truth, goodness, and beauty, consistent with classical-paedagogical aims.

- Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Curriculum is described as classical, integrated, and liberal-arts oriented. The site notes a broad, liberal-arts education, with reading of Homer, Plato, Aristotle, Euclid, Virgil, Dante, Chaucer, Shakespeare, St. Francis, St. Thomas Aquinas, and other canonical texts; the humanities and other disciplines are braided together; Great Books/primary texts and the Great Conversation are emphasized; there is explicit reference to a “Socratic Seminar” pedagogy and a curriculum that connects history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages. The site’s overview references a traditional liberal-arts progression (Ancient World through Modern World).
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, Great Conversation, interdisciplinary integration, and integration/wonder. The presence of Homer, Plato, Aristotle, Dante, Aquinas, etc., plus the braided humanities approach, reflects the core curriculum signals. Negative keywords (e.g., social studies as substitute for history) are not evidenced. Positive keywords: 4–5; Negative keywords: 0. This supports CLASSICAL.
    - Why this rating: The school explicitly follows the liberal-arts Trivium/Quadrivium framework and Great Books curriculum, with integrated history/humanities and a focus on wonder, all central to classical schooling.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Pedagogy includes Socratic dialogue/seminar, dialectic, and a culture of discussion (Socratic Seminars). It also emphasizes Restful Learning (Scholé), contemplation, and memorization/recitation (including chanting/singing to memorize). The upper-school emphasis on rhetoric and public speaking is signaled through the Chesterton Academy model (seminars, debates, orations). The public materials describe a progression from lower-school memory/recitation and reverence to upper-school Socratic discussion and rhetorical training.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords present include Socratic Seminar, dialectic, guided discussion, memory work, recitation, chanting, liturgical timing (Scholé), and rhetoric/speeches. The site’s materials also show a structured, formation-focused pedagogy rather than purely project-based or experiential-only approaches. Positive keywords: 4–5; Negative keywords: 0. This supports CLASSICAL.
    - Why this rating: The pedagogical approach explicitly centers on Socratic inquiry, classical memory work, and rhetoric training, with an emphasis on contemplation and virtuous formation, aligning with CLASSICAL pedagogy.

What Would Move the Needle

If a future update were desired, it could be to more explicitly codify and surface the precise keyword language from the Six Essential Elements (1) in public-facing materials (e.g., explicit mentions of Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation, and “living curriculum” language for faculty) to strengthen external clarity and searchability. This would reinforce the alignment without changing current practice. This suggestion is optional and not required to achieve CLASSICAL status.
