## Executive Summary

 Valor Christian High School ( Highlands Ranch, CO; 80126) presents a clearly Christian, Christ-centered educational environment with emphasis on character formation, virtue, and spiritual formation. Public materials emphasize Christ-centered identity, prayer, Bible study, and discipleship as central to student development, alongside college-preparatory academics and mentorship. These religious foundations align with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Parents/Community dimensions of the classical rubric. However, explicit classical elements such as Great Books, the Great Conversation, grammar/logic/rhetoric as a curricular spine, and overt Socratic pedagogy are not prominently evidenced in available public materials. Pedagogical approaches (e.g., Socratic seminars, Scholé-style contemplative learning) are not clearly articulated on the site, and the curriculum is presented in a broader Christian curriculum framework with emphasis on college preparation and disciplinary offerings rather than a formal classical curriculum. Faculty are described as skilled, mission-focused mentors, which aligns with the Faculty criterion. Parent involvement and community engagement are evident through campus visits, family involvement initiatives, and parent-facing offerings.

 Overall, Valor Christian High School exhibits substantial classical-adjacent strengths in Religious Foundations, Faculty, and Parents/Community, moderate alignment in School Purpose/Goal, and weaker alignment in Curriculum and Pedagogy. Aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

 Evidence and synthesis draw on Valor’s published materials, including campus history statements about a Christian mission, Bible and theology emphasis, and role of mentors; plus admissions and campus pages describing family involvement and community life. Key sources include Valor’s site pages discussing the Christian mission and discipleship (e.g., History and Bible department language), campus life, and family/community focus. See citations to the school’s public materials for specifics.

## What Would Move the Needle

- To push Valor toward a FULLY CLASSICAL profile, implement explicit classical curriculum and pedagogy signals:
  - Adopt and publish a formal classical curriculum spine (grammar, logic, rhetoric), with a stated plan for Great Books exploration and a “Great Conversation” framework across relevant grade levels.
  - Introduce explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades (and clearly document the use of Socratic seminars and dialectic discussions) along with opportunities for students to deliver rhetorical speeches and capstone projects.
  - Restore, emphasize, and publicize formal memory work, recitation, and chanting where appropriate (e.g., poetry, catechesis, or classical texts) as part of routine instruction.
  - Elevate and articulate a more explicit telos-focused statement that centers virtue, wisdom, and human excellence as the primary aims of education (over and above college-prep in a broad sense).
  - Expand the Great Books/Great Conversation framing in curriculum materials, course catalogs, and parent communications to demonstrate a canonical classical approach.
  - Where possible, implement a more explicit liberal-arts course sequence (grammar‑stage, logic, rhetoric), and highlight Latin/Greek study if applicable to the school’s program.
