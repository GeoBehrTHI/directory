## Executive Summary

Hope Christian Academy ND 58601 presents a robust Classical Christian program that aligns strongly with core elements of classical education, particularly in its religious/metaphysical foundations, its emphasis on formation (virtue, wisdom, character), and its partnership with families. Public-facing materials consistently foreground a Christ-centered frame, Word-of-God pedagogy, and a stated aim of forming virtuous, wise students through classical methods. The school also embraces the liberal-arts orientation in its rhetoric around rhetoric, history, and character formation, and it pursues a community model in which parents are actively engaged as partners.

Overall, based on the public materials analyzed and mapped to the Six Essential Elements rubric, the school is CLASSICAL. There are strong signals in Categories 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), 2 (Purpose & Goal), 5 (Faculty), and 6 (Parents & Community). Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) show substantial classical alignment but with some gaps or less explicit articulation (for example, explicit reference to the Seven Liberal Arts and Great Books in the school’s public language is not always explicit, though the presence of rhetoric, history-focused integration, and contemplative elements are evident). Taken together, the aggregate picture supports a CLASSICAL designation, with modest room for deeper public articulation in Curriculum and Pedagogy to move toward full canonical classical explicitness.

    - Evidence: Education framed as formation of virtue and wisdom; aims beyond information transfer; focus on human flourishing and the formation of character; explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive terms such as formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education, paideia, great conversation, classical mission surface in public materials; negative signals (e.g., job-prep framing) are not evident.
    - Why this rating: The public materials position education as formation and character formation within a classical framework rather than solely job preparation.

- Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Content references to classical language of education (classical education rhetoric), integration of history with other subject areas, and a focus on literature/rhetoric; upper-school emphasis on biblical worldview, apologetics, and capstone thesis. However, explicit, consistent articulation of Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a formal Great Books/Great Conversation framework is not uniformly explicit in the public-facing materials.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include rhetoric, history, biblical worldview, Great Conversation-like framing in some pages, and a history-centric approach; negative indicators include less explicit mention of the full Seven Liberal Arts sequence and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language.
    - Why this rating: The school demonstrates strong classical tendencies (rhetoric, integrated history, classical language of education, and formation through literature and Biblical worldview), but it does not consistently market or certify explicit adherence to all canonical elements (e.g., explicit Great Books program or comprehensive Seven Liberal Arts framework) in its public materials.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Mention of contemplative/restful learning (Scholé), memory work, singing/recitation in lower grades, and public-speaking/rhetoric in upper grades. Some reference to Socratic discussion or seminars is present indirectly through emphasis on rhetoric and debate in the upper grades, though explicit Socratic seminars are not consistently named across all materials.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include chanting, singing, memory work, recitation, restfulness, and rhetoric; negative indicators such as exclusive reliance on experiential/project-based learning or student-directed constructivism are not supported by public materials.
    - Why this rating: Pedagogy includes core classical elements (memory work, chant, recitation, rhetoric, Socratic-style discussion in principle, and bulk emphasis on formation through conversation and lectures) but may not consistently foreground a full spectrum of classical pedagogies (e.g., every category explicitly naming Socratic seminars or scholé days across grades). This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

- Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Materials present staff as central to education (staff, faculty, head of school, master-teacher-type language in social copy). The public materials describe faculty as mentors and living curriculum; there is emphasis on character formation and community.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include "living curriculum," "staff committed to growing character," "portrait of a teacher," and "collegial" faculty culture; negative indicators are not evident in public content.
    - Why this rating: The school emphasizes the faculty as a core driver of the classical mission, with language that aligns with the classical model of teachers as mentors and living curriculum.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Clear emphasis on partnership with parents; paideia approach in partnership with parents; parental formation through book studies and parent gatherings; open campus and frequent parent visits; family partnership language.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include "Partnership with Parents," "paideia," "family partnership," "open campus," and "parent formation"; no significant negative signals show up in public materials.
    - Why this rating: The school frames parents as co-educators and integrates them into the classical education project, aligning with the classical model’s emphasis on community and parental partnership.

Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL

- The school demonstrates strong alignment across four categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Faculty, Parents & Community) with Element 3 and Element 4 showing substantial classical alignment but with some gaps in explicit canonical labeling. Taken together, the school meets the core criteria of classical education and earns an overall CLASSICAL designation.

What would move the needle

- If the school intends to move toward a fully explicit, canonical classical education label, it could:
    - Explicitly publicize and document the full Seven Liberal Arts framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium) as the curriculum backbone.
    - En rich the public articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation and demonstrate a clear, structured Great Books program across grades.
    - Expand explicit description of Socratic dialogue/seminars as a regular pedagogical practice in multiple grade bands, with examples of seminars and student-led discourse across the upper grades.
    - Provide visible, public alignment between specific classical pedagogy elements (scholé days,Dialogic seminars, recitation schedules) and measurable outcomes.

Note: The above needle-moving suggestions apply only to the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL areas; given the overall CLASSICAL designation, these are incremental actions that could further strengthen canonical alignment.

# ABSTRACT

Hope Christian Academy ND 58601 demonstrates a CLASSICAL orientation in its public-facing materials. It centers a Christ-centered, Word-of-God framework, with a strong emphasis on formation (virtue, wisdom, character) and a partnership with families. The school highlights religious/metaphysical commitments, a formation-focused purpose, and a community-based approach to parents and faculty that align with classical education norms. Curriculum and pedagogy show clear classical influence—such as focus on rhetoric, history-informed study, and contemplative elements (Scholé) and memory work—though explicit canonical labelling of all Seven Liberal Arts and Great Books/Great Conversation across the entire curriculum is not uniformly explicit in the published materials. The faculty are portrayed as living curriculum and mentors, and parents are actively engaged as co-educators, which reinforces the classical tradition. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL, with room for more explicit articulation in Curriculum and Pedagogy to achieve full canonical clarity.
