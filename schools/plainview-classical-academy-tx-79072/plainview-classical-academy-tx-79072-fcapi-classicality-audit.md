## Executive Summary

Plainview Classical Academy (Plainview, TX 79072) presents a robust classical framework in public-school form. The public materials consistently emphasize virtue, knowledge, and beauty as core aims, and explicitly describe a classical curriculum (liberal arts, Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Greek/Latin) alongside a community-centered ethos (partnership with families, parent involvement, volunteer culture). However, while the school foregrounds formation, virtue, and the Great Books tradition, explicit references to theological grounding, liturgical practice, or clearly articulated doctrinal commitments are minimal in the public content. Taken together, the school demonstrates strong classical characteristics in intent and content, but certain religious/metaphysical foundations and explicit liturgical/community practices are not overtly foregrounded in the site materials.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (with substantial classical elements evident across curriculum, pedagogy, and community, but lacking explicit theological or liturgical articulation in public-facing materials). The strongest supports come from Curriculum (Great Books, liberal arts, Trivium/Quadrivium, Western canon) and from explicit aims around virtue, formation, and partnership with families. Gaps appear in explicit religious/theological framing and liturgical/community practices on the public site.

While direct textual claims of singing or chanting in lower school are not strongly visible in the public content, the presence of a memory-oriented, classical approach is suggested. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis

These items align with the public materials referencing classical pedagogy and Great Books.

This creates some tension with a full-strength CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy. 
- Why this rating

This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make religious/metaphysical foundations explicit and public
  - Add a clear, public articulation of theological/theistic grounding (if applicable) or a transparent statement about the school’s philosophical-religious orientation, including whether spiritual formation or faith-based aims underpin the curriculum.
  - Include explicit liturgical or communal practices if present (or clarify their absence) to reinforce liturgical or communal dimensions of education.

- Strengthen explicit pedagogical language
  - Publicly describe concrete upper-school Socratic/ dialogue-based seminars, as well as lower-school chant/memory activities if they occur, with examples and sample units.
  - Provide explicit language about Schole-like contemplative pace or “restful learning” in the lower grades, and how it is balanced with rigorous inquiry.

- Fortify the faculty as “living curriculum”
  - Articulate in public materials how faculty serve as mentors and how teachers collectively embody the school’s classical aims, including a visible portrayal of teacher formation, mentorship programs, and professional development aligned with classical pedagogy.

- Expand explicit Great Conversation/Great Books commitments
  - Provide a public outline of Great Books selections, weekly discussion formats, and assessment methods for upper grades to demonstrate ongoing engagement with canonical texts.

- Deepen parent-education channels
  - Expand offerings like parent book studies, lectures, and family-oriented events to further emphasize parents as co-teachers and to institutionalize lifelong partnership with the school community.
