## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy - Tyler (FCA Tyler) presents a clearly identified classical教育 orientation, with core commitments to virtue, truth, beauty, and liberal-arts learning. Public materials emphasize formation of character, virtuous citizenship, and a classical curricular framing, along with a visible community/parent-partnership ethos (e.g., parent reading programs and family involvement). However, while FCA Tyler uses classical language and signals, several hallmark elements commonly associated with a robust classical program—such as explicit reference to the Great Books/Great Conversation, the grammar–logic–rhetoric trifecta, and explicit Socratic pedagogy across grade levels—are not consistently foregrounded in the public-facing materials. On balance, FCA Tyler leans toward a Classical orientation, but with meaningful gaps in explicit articulation of the full Classical School rubric.

Aggregate ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key insights:
- Religious/metaphysical foundations: Emphasizes truth, goodness, and beauty and virtue, but does not foreground explicit theology, liturgical practice, or sacral rituals in public messaging. Expected to be strengthened with explicit theological framing or liturgical/community practices to reach a full Classical rating. Evidence points to a strong moral-philosophical orientation rather than a doctrinal program.  
- Purpose and goal: Strong formation narrative; emphasis on forming virtuous citizens and human flourishing, not job-readiness. This strongly supports a Classical orientation. 
- Curriculum: Uses “traditional liberal arts and sciences” language; explicit grammar/logic/rhetoric and Great Books/Great Conversation are not consistently named in public content, tempering a full Classical rating. 
- Pedagogy: Public materials do not consistently foreground Socratic seminars, scholé, or chant/memorization methods; thus, pedagogy is not fully aligned with the core Classical pedagogy rubric as presented in the Six Essential Elements. 
- Faculty: Public messaging centers on leadership and partnership; explicit “living curriculum” or teacher-portrait constructs are not prominent, yielding a middling fit on this category. 
- Parents & Community: Strong parent/teacher partnership signals (Reading At Home, parent resources, family involvement), aligning well with classical-community expectations.

What Would Move the Needle

- Category 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Add explicit theological framing and liturgical/community practices to public materials (even if non-sectarian in governance, a stated approach to reverence, contemplative life, or liturgical rhythms would raise alignment). 
  - Concrete steps: Publish a short, explicit statement tying virtue/truth/beauty to a theological/metaphysical framework and include at least one liturgical or communal practice described publicly.
- Category 3 (Curriculum): Elevate explicit mention of the full classical curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books and Great Conversation) and Great-Books-based readings.
  - Concrete steps: Add a publicly accessible curriculum map detailing Trivium components (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Quadrivium coverage, and a Great Books/GX list with sample units and a plan for “Great Conversation” style discussions across upper grades.
- Category 4 (Pedagogy): Incorporate and publicly describe classical pedagogical methods (Socratic dialogue, scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation).
  - Concrete steps: Publish a Pedagogy section that explains how Socratic seminars are used, how memory work and recitation are integrated, and how rest/pondering time (Scholé) is embedded in daily routines.
- Category 5 (Faculty): Strengthen and publicize a portrait of the faculty as the “living curriculum” and identify teacher-formation practices.
  - Concrete steps: Add a “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and “What a Classical Teacher Does” page, plus descriptions of ongoing teacher development in classical pedagogy.
- Category 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and deepen public-facing parent-education content to reinforce the co-teacher model, possibly including structured parent book clubs, and more formal family-involvement programming.
  - Concrete steps: Publish a yearly calendar of parent-education events and book studies; provide summaries of parent-facing resources and measurable outcomes of parent involvement.

ABSTRACT

Founders Classical Academy - Tyler presents a publicly accessible Classical orientation: a strong virtue-formation narrative, liberal-arts curriculum framing, and explicit parent/community partnership. Category-by-category analysis shows strong Classical alignment in School Purpose & Goal and Parents & Community, with moderate alignment in Curriculum and Faculty, partial alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, and weaker alignment in Pedagogy. The aggregate rating is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reflecting notable strengths but notable gaps in explicit reference to canonical Classical elements (Great Books/Great Conversation, grammar–logic–rhetoric, and Socratic pedagogy) and explicit theological or liturgical practices. Targeted enhancements—explicitly naming Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric, and Socratic pedagogy; clarifying theological or liturgical framing; and detailing the faculty living-curriculum model—would move FCA Tyler toward a full CLASSICAL designation.
