## Executive Summary

Acacia Classical Academy CA 95628 presents a robust Classical Christian orientation, with explicit emphasis on Christ-centered, covenantal education, virtue formation, and a liberal arts-inflected curriculum that includes Latin study. Public-facing materials repeatedly frame education as formation in truth, goodness, and beauty, with strong partnerships with homeschooling families and a two-days-on-campus / three-days-at-home model. The school clearly situates itself within classical education through phrases like “Classical Christian Education,” “liberal arts tradition,” and the Nicene Creed alignment. However, some six-element criteria—most notably explicit articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation, and explicit Socratic pedagogy for upper grades—are not clearly stated in the public materials. In sum, Acacia Classical Academy is CLASSICAL, with some areas that are clearly classical and a few that are only partially evidenced.

- Public materials clearly affirm religious/metaphysical foundations (Christ-centered education, Nicene Creed alignment, covenantal community, discipleship, piety) and a goal oriented toward formational aims (virtue, wisdom, love of God, neighbor) rather than mere credentialing. This anchors Element 1 and Element 2 strongly in classical territory.   
- Curriculum signals include liberal-arts framing, Latin study, and classical identity, but explicit mention of Great Books/Great Conversation and of grammar/logic/rhetoric as the core curriculum is not clearly documented in public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 3.  
- Pedagogy is described at a program level (two days on campus, three days at home, tutoring support) but without explicit, recurring references to Socratic seminars, memory work, or scholé-style contemplative practice in the site text, which filters Element 4 to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Faculty presence is strong (teacher profiles, living-curriculum orientation) and aligns well with classical expectations, supporting Element 5 as CLASSICAL. 
- Parent/community partnerships are explicit (co-teaching ethos, faith-based formation, open engagement with families), supporting Element 6 as CLASSICAL. 

Overall, the aggregate assessment places Acacia Classical Academy in the CLASSICAL category, with notable strengths in religious and purpose-driven aims, liberal-arts framing, and community partnerships, and with some gaps in explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and certain pedagogy specifics.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Make Great Books/Great Conversation explicit: Public materials should clearly reference a Great Books program or canon discussions and show how students engage with classic texts beyond Latin study.
- Explicitly name core classical pedagogy methods: Document Socratic seminars, memorized or recited texts, and guided dialogue as routine upper-grade practices; include examples of student speeches or rhetorical exercises.
- Strengthen the grammar/logic/rhetoric articulation: Publicly confirm a structured Trivium approach (grammar, logic, rhetoric) at the core of the curriculum and show representative examples of student work in each stage.
- Expand on “Scholé” and contemplative practice: Include language and examples of contemplative learning days, memory work, and reverent study to align with scholé criteria.
- Provide more explicit Great Books integration across history, philosophy, and literature: Show how Western civilization texts and primary sources contribute to the curriculum alongside Latin.
