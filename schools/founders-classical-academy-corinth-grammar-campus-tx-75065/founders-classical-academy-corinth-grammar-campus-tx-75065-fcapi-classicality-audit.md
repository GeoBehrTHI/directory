## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy – Corinth (K–5) Grammar Campus TX 75065 presents a clearly classical orientation in its public materials, with strong emphasis on virtuous character formation, liberal arts, and a partnership model with parents as co-educators. The campus script frames education as formation of the whole person—cultivating truth, goodness, and beauty through a liberal arts curriculum rooted in Western tradition, and through practices such as parent partnership and community engagement. This aligns with many core elements of classical education, notably the emphasis on formation, virtue, and a liberal-arts curriculum. However, while the school articulates several hallmark classical features (liberal arts, Trivium-related language, virtuous formation, parent partnership), certain explicit components that often accompany a fuller Classical framework (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation engagement, liturgical/theological integration, and clearly delineated contemplative pedagogy like scholé) are not consistently foregrounded in the publicly accessible material. Overall, the school demonstrates strong classical signals but with some gaps that prevent a clean CLASSICAL rating across all six categories.

- Overall aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Key strength highlights:
  - Explicit language of truth, beauty, goodness, virtue, formation, and the liberal arts in public materials (Cate. 1 & 2).  
  - Clear articulation of a liberal arts, Western-tradition curriculum (Latin, logic, philosophy, classic literature) and a culture of moral character and intellectual discipline (Cate. 3 & 5). 
  - Strong parent/community partnership orientation (co-educators) and public-facing focus on forming virtuous scholars (Cate. 6). 

- Important opportunities to move toward full CLASSICAL status (where indicated as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL): explicit theology/ liturgical practices, formal inclusion of Great Books/Great Conversation in daily praxis, and more explicit scholé/restful pedagogy signals.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize truth, beauty, and goodness as foundational ideals: “tuition-free, K–5 classical education rooted in truth, beauty, and goodness” and “formed the whole person” with virtuous citizens. 
- Language around virtue and character is prominent (“wise and virtuous citizens”; formation of the whole person). 
- Clearly Western-tradition liberal arts orientation is stated (“Rigorous liberal arts curriculum grounded in Western tradition”). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present (per the Six Elements lexicon): truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, formation, Western tradition, moral formation, virtue ethics, wisdom. 
- Negative keywords: not clearly present (no explicit signaling of relativism, secularism, or values-neutral approaches in the visible public materials). 

Why this rating
- The school foregrounds transcendental ideals (truth, goodness, beauty) and virtue formation, which are central to the religious/metaphysical axis of classical education. However, explicit grounding in theology, liturgical practices, or sacramental/communal worship signals are not clearly foregrounded in the public-facing materials reviewed here. The absence of explicit liturgical/theological integration places the category in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL, despite strong signals of moral realism and formation. 

What would move the needle
- Add explicit theology-grounding language or liturgical/community practices (e.g., daily chapel, liturgical calendar, sacred music) to establish a firmer theological/metaphysical grounding.
- Include explicit references to the transcendental aims (logos, teleology, natural law) in public materials to strengthen the transcendental/metaphysical frame.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The mission language foregrounds formation and virtue: “Forming Scholars. Cultivating Virtue. Leading with Purpose.” and “education should elevate the mind and shape the soul,” with classical commitments.  
- Public statements tie education to virtue, wisdom, and the cultivation of human excellence rather than mere workforce preparation: “a tradition that pursues knowledge, promotes virtue, and prepares students for lives of integrity, service, and liberty.” 
- Recurrent phrase: “rooted in truth, beauty, and goodness” as a core educational aim. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, telos (implicit in formation toward virtue and integrity), truth/beauty/goodness, liberal education. 
- Negative keywords: not present in the public materials (no explicit emphasis on college/career readiness or skills-first framing). 

Why this rating
- The school's stated aim centers on formation, virtue, and the cultivation of human excellence, with explicit classical framing (liberal arts, Western tradition). This aligns closely with the classical ideal of education as formation toward the true, good, and beautiful, rather than merely job preparation. The rhetoric strongly supports CLASSICAL. 

What would move the needle
- Strengthen explicit articulation of telos through curriculum goals (e.g., clearly state aims like “formation of wisdom for human flourishing” and connect them to daily practice).
- Tie Great Books/Great Conversation more explicitly to the core mission to reinforce the fullness of classical purposes.

The visible material does not always show direct Great Books engagement in the public snippets. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum strongly signals classical content (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin; philosophy; classic literature) and liberal-arts integration, but the explicit, consistent public articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation and formal history-as-a-curricular throughlines is not as overt in the accessible public material as in a full Great-Books program. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation rather than CLASSICAL, pending fuller public confirmation of Great Books engagement. 

What would move the needle
- Publicly foreground Great Books as a consistent core with “the Great Conversation” framing across grades, and specify historical breadth through primary-source engagement.
- More explicit inclusion of grammar, logic, and rhetoric as visible, stage-based curriculum milestones (with examples of student work and assessment).

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Technology-free classrooms are highlighted as part of the Founders Difference, suggesting a contemplative and focused environment. 
- The site publishes a Trivium-focused framework (via “Trivium Explained” link), indicating attention to the classical pedagogy sequence (grammar/logic/rhetoric). 
- The presence of lower- and upper-school structures implies a progression toward Socratic and rhetorical training in higher grades (as per classical pedagogy norms), though explicit statements of Socratic seminars or formal dialectic discussions are not heavily foregrounded in the public text. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: scholé/restful learning signals (technology-free environment), Socratic dialogue (implied by Trivium focus), rhetoric and oratory (via Trivium), memorization/recitation (via classical pedagogy lineage) though not always explicitly labeled in public text. 
- Negative keywords: explicit emphasis on project-based/experiential-only pedagogy is not evident in the public materials; the presence of a technology-free, contemplative approach suggests some non-rigorous/narrow pedagogy signals, but without strong contradiction. The public text does not strongly emphasize pure modern constructivist/project-based pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The public materials show classical pedagogy signals (Trivium framing, contemplative environment via tech-free classrooms) but lack explicit, consistent documentation of full Socratic seminars or a comprehensive program of mindfulness-based scholé days in a public-facing, grade-by-grade manner. This makes the pedagogy grade SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Publicly articulate a fuller, explicit pedagogy map: confirm and describe regular Socratic seminars or dialectic discussions in upper grades; describe formal memory work, recitation, and chanted/pedagogical repetition in early grades; provide sample lesson structures that embody scholé days and contemplative cycles.
