## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of Atlanta (CAATL) presents as a clearly Catholic, classical high school rooted in a robust theology-driven mission and a public-facing classical curriculum. Across public materials, the school foregrounds Catholic faith, liturgical life, and virtue formation, while articulating a traditional liberal-arts orientation (including Latin, philosophy, theology, and the humanities) and a pedagogy that emphasizes a three-pillared framework: Intellect, Character, and Spirituality. Several elements align strongly with classical education norms—theological formation, integration of liberal arts, and a high value placed on virtue and formation. Some pedagogical specifics (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars, or detailed Great Books/Great Conversation commitments) are less explicit in public content, which introduces a modest degree of interpretive uncertainty about the exact pedagogy. Overall, CAATL lands as CLASSICAL, with room to further crystalize certain pedagogical practices to reach full general alignment.

Key drivers of classification:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Clear, ongoing Catholic formation (Mass daily, theology, Catechism), oath-related fidelity to Magisterium, liturgical life, sacral art, and chaplain presence. This strongly supports a Classical, religiously grounded foundation. Evidence: daily Mass, theology instruction, Catechism, Magisterium fidelity, liturgical life.  
- School Purpose and Goal: Mission foregrounds formation, virtue, and a classical/catholic telos aimed at leadership, saints, and competent, holistic human excellence, not solely job preparation. Evidence: “joyful leaders and saints,” classical tradition, truths of Catholic faith, formation toward college and life. 
- Curriculum: Strong liberal-arts orientation is evident (classics of Western thought, Latin requirement, integrated humanities emphasis). Evidence: “classics of Western thought,” Latin requirement, reference to curriculum overview, and the three-pillars framing of intellect.  
- Pedagogy: Public materials emphasize a contemplative/remembered-seed approach (e.g., singing/memory in lower grades; high-school emphasis on rhetoric and public speaking appears in arts/culture framing) but explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy is not consistently stated in public pages. This yields a “Somewhat Classical” signal on pedagogy, pending more explicit explicit Seminars/Socratic structures in public materials. Evidence: lower-school singing and memory, emphasis on rhetoric and speeches in arts program; absence of explicit, consistent Socratic/seminar language in public pages. 
- Faculty: Public materials describe qualified, devout faculty and leadership, with a visible governance/board structure, and the Headmaster’s credentials align with classical education lineage. This supports a classical faculty profile. Evidence: Headmaster credentials, board presence, faith-forming commitments.  
- Parents & Community: The school emphasizes parent engagement and transparency (open houses, shadow days, parent-facing sessions, accessible visits), indicating a classical partnership with families. Evidence: Admissions information, open houses, shadow days, parent sessions.

- Quick Facts framing the mission as Catholic and the school as a classical, Catholic institution; emphasis on the magisterium and sacred formation. 
- Our History describes a Catholic identity anchored in St. Catherine of Siena parish and canonical alignment within the Archdiocese.

Why this rating
- The school publicly foregrounds a sacramental, doctrinal, liturgical, and ecclesial axis in daily life and academics, which is the strongest possible signal of a Classical Catholic foundation.

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- The program aims to prepare for college, professional life, and service to family, country, and Christ, indicating formation over narrow job-readiness emphasis. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating
- CAATL foregrounds formation, virtue, and knowledge rooted in truth and goodness, aligning with classical teleology rather than mere information or workforce preparation.

Why this rating
- The materials consistently present a liberal-arts curriculum with classical elements (grammar/logics/rhetoric framing, Latin, Western thought, integrated humanities, and a focus on formation rather than mere information), supporting CLASSICAL status.

### Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The Three Pillars framework signals a holistic approach to intellect, character, and spirituality, with emphasis on a cohesive, content-rich education. This aligns with classical aims but public materials are less explicit about traditional pedagogical markers (Socratic seminars, recitation, etc.). 
- Lower-school pedagogy evidence includes singing, memory, and a focus on wonder and restrained, contemplative elements; upper-school mentions of rhetoric and oratory are present in arts and public-speaking emphasis, but explicit Socratic seminars are not consistently documented in public materials. 
- The presence of a house system and classical cultural practices (Oxford/Cambridge reference) hints at aristocratic, tradition-based pedagogy rather than fully modern, constructivist approaches. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: contemplative/scholé, wonder, memory/recitation, rhetoric, oratory, debate, speeches, classical pedagogy, living curriculum (conceptually implied), sacred/liturgical integration in instruction.
- Negative keywords present: Explicit project-based learning, purely experiential learning, or secular/constructivist pedagogy are not documented in public materials; absence is notable rather than presence.

Why this rating
- CAATL demonstrates a strong classical orientation through formation-centric aims and a broad classical curriculum, but explicit articulation and consistent public-facing evidence of classical pedagogical forms (e.g., Socratic seminars across grades) are not always explicit. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating, with room to explicitly codify and publish the full pedagogical playbook (e.g., explicit Socratic and recitation practices and detailed Scholé days) to achieve full CLASSICAL status.

Aggregate assessment
- Category classifications: CLASSICAL (Cat 1), CLASSICAL (Cat 2), CLASSICAL (Cat 3), SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (Cat 4), CLASSICAL (Cat 5), CLASSICAL (Cat 6).

What Would Move the Needle

- Pedagogy (Category 4): Publicly codify and publish explicit Socratic/seminal pedagogy practices (e.g., a clear framework for Socratic seminars, the use of classical dialectic in upper grades, and structured memory work and recitation) to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy (Category 4): Increase explicit mention of Great Books/Great Conversation and direct articulation of interdisciplinary integration across subjects in public-facing materials, including concrete examples (syllabi, sample “Great Conversation” readings).
- Curriculum (Category 3): Publish a more explicit list of canonical works and primary sources (Great Books) across grades, including the integration of primary texts in history, philosophy, theology, and literature, to reinforce the Great Books/Great Conversation identity.
- Public Materials (Cross-cutting): Expand language about “Scholé” days, contemplative rest, and rest/recitation-based pedagogy in the public-facing narrative to provide clearer alignment with classical pedagogy norms.
