## Executive Summary

Field School Chicago (ZIP 60644) presents a consciously classical, Christian, and diverse learning community. Public materials frame the school as a diverse, classical, Christ-centered institution with a curriculum rooted in timeless truths, moral formation, and a habit-driven approach to education. The school openly embraces Charlotte Mason influences, Great Books, and a humanities-rich program (Bible, literature, art, music; history through timeline-based study; Book of Centuries). There is clear emphasis on worship, daily theology, character formation, and family involvement, including a Family Tree Organization and volunteer opportunities. Taken together, these elements align strongly with a Classical framework, particularly around formation of character, cultivation of virtue, engagement with timeless content, and a community-centered pedagogy that integrates faith with reason.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Key drivers of this classification include: explicit Christ-centered and worship practices; emphasis on virtue, formation, and moral imagination; a humanities-forward curriculum with Charlotte Mason influences; use of Great Books and primary texts; memory-friendly, habit-forming pedagogy; and robust family/community involvement. The materials also show a continuing commitment to diversity and service within a classical framework, which reinforces the holistic formation emphasized by classic models. While some components (e.g., explicit mentions of grammar/logic/rhetoric pedagogy) are less overt, the combination of Great Books, habit formation, virtuous inquiry, and a historically rooted curriculum strongly supports a Classical orientation.

What would move the needle for a fuller CLASSICAL alignment (if pursued further): more explicit articulation and documentation of a complete Trivium progression (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across the K–8 spectrum; clearer demonstration of Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades; and more explicit textual citations to primary Great Books within the core curriculum to strengthen the Great Conversations aspect. Additionally, richer integration of liturgical/prayer practices as formal curricular components (beyond worship and faith-life activities) could further shore up Element 1.
