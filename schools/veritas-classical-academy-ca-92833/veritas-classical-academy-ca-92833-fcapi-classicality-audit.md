## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical Academy (Fullerton, CA, 92833) demonstrates a robust classical-Christian orientation across six rubric categories, with strong alignment to Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The Pedagogy category shows partial alignment and would benefit from greater explicit integration of Socratic/dialectic practices and contemplative pedagogy to reach a fully Classical profile. Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL, with one category (Pedagogy) standing as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The school’s public materials emphasize Christ-centered formation, the liberal arts (Trivium and Quadrivium), Great Books/Great Conversation sensibilities, and active parental partnership.

Key insights:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strongly present: Christ-centered mission, God’s Word, discipleship, and the paideia framework are repeatedly invoked.
- Purpose and Goal emphasize formation, human flourishing, wisdom, and virtue rather than mere workforce preparation.
- Curriculum centers on liberal arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) and a classical trajectory in the grammar, logic, and rhetoric sequence, with explicit language about formation through the liberal arts.
- Pedagogy shows concrete lower-school memory/recitation and early singing, with upper-school rhetoric and discussion-oriented elements; however, explicit, sustained Socratic seminars/Scholé are not consistently evidenced in all public materials, yielding a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for this category.
- Faculty portrayal aligns with classical ideals (model teachers, living curriculum, mentorship, and a “portrait of teacher” ethic).
- Parents & Community are clearly engaged as partners in formation, with explicit calls for parental involvement, book studies, and open campus interactions.

Aggregate Assessment

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Rationale: The school strongly satisfies Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community through christ-centered formation, liberal-arts-driven curriculum, and a community-centered model. Pedagogy shows significant classical elements (memory/recitation in early grades; logic/rhetoric progression; rhetoric-focused programs) but lacks uniformly explicit long-running Socratic seminars and fully sustained Scholé across all grades in public materials, yielding SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Pedagogy. With five of six categories CLASSICAL and one category SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, the general characterization of Veritas Classical Academy as CLASSICAL is warranted.

- What would move the needle (if aiming to solidify CLASSICAL across all six categories)
    - Pedagogy: Explicit, school-wide implementation of regular Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars across multiple grade bands; integrate Scholé/restful days into a formal cadence; expand memorization/recitation and chanting as structured, co-curricular ritual within a stable schedule.
    - Curriculum: Publicly articulate and publish a cross-curricular Great Books/Great Conversation program across K–12; deepen explicit references to the liberal arts progression in syllabi; ensure historical engagement remains a central through-line rather than a secondary emphasis.
    - Community: Expand formal parent-education programs (book studies, lectures) beyond initial sessions; provide more visible parent-teacher partnership milestones.

- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY # ABSTRACTION
    - Veritas Classical Academy’s public materials align strongly with Classical education principles: Christian formation, paideia, Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books posture, and parent partnership. The Pedagogy category shows partial alignment (lower-grade singing/memory; upper-grade logic/rhetoric), but lacks consistently explicit Socratic/dialectic sequencing across the spectrum, which is the primary area for needle-moving improvement. The school’s aggregate profile is CLASSICAL with one category at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, indicating a strong classical orientation with targeted growth opportunities in pedagogy.
