## ABSTRACT

Tyndale Classical Institute in Laurel, Mississippi presents a CLASSICAL-leaning program within a Christian-education framework, anchored by a curriculum that emphasizes Latin, Greek, literature, logic, and music, and by explicit statements about truth and beauty as the aims of education. The school expresses a theology-infused view of learning (Gospel of Christ, Word of Truth) and a Great-Books sensibility via references to “great minds” and “great authors of Western civilization.” However, its public materials do not provide explicit, describable pedagogical practices (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholé, or restorative memory work), nor do they publicly establish a living-faculty model or formal parent/community engagement programs. As a result, three categories align clearly with classical aims, while three categories lack explicit classical-practice signals, yielding an overall assessment of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong Christian/theological framing with explicit references to truth, beauty, and liturgical-like sentiments (Word of Truth, Gospel, catechesis). CLASSICAL.
- School Purpose and Goal: Formation toward virtue and wisdom, with Great-Books-oriented framing; formation over mere information. CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum: Clear classical structure (grammar-like/Latin, logic, rhetoric implied through program, great authors, Western civilization). CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy: Lacks explicit statements about classical-pedagogical techniques (Socratic, scholé, etc.). NOT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty: Public content does not present a living-curriculum or explicit faculty culture; NOT CLASSICAL.
- Parents & Community: No explicit parent/community partnership statements in public copy; NOT CLASSICAL.

Overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
