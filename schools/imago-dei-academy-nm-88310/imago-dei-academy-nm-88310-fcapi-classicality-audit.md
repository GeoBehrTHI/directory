## Executive Summary

Imago Dei Academy NM 88310 presents a clearly classical Christian educational posture. Across multiple public materials, the school foregrounds Christ-centered formation, the idea that “All education is discipleship,” and a classical framework anchored in grammar, logic, and rhetoric. The school emphasizes formation over information, partnership with families, and a faculty culture described as mentors who embody and model virtue. While it strongly evidences religious/metaphysical foundations, formation goals, and family/community partnerships, its curriculum and pedagogy show robust classical structure (grammar–logic–rhetoric) but with limited explicit references to Great Books or a fully articulated Great Conversation. The overall assessment is CLASSICAL, with two categories showing only SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to certain missing elements or less explicit emphasis (notably in explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references and certain pedagogy specifics). In aggregate, the school earns CLASSICAL.

- Evidence highlights include: a theology-centered philosophy of education (All truth is God’s truth; Scripture integrated into all subjects; Statement of Faith with historic creeds) and a mission/purpose statement that centers formation, virtue, and wisdom through a Gospel-centered classical model.   
- The public materials show explicit references to classical elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric; memory work; rhetoric training) and a structure that positions the teacher as a living curriculum within a community-centered, family-partnered framework.  
- There is strong evidence of religious foundation, formation goals, and parental/community partnership, with explicit statements about partnering with families and a statement of faith.

  - Quantitative note: Positive indicators dominate; 100% of the clearest religious/metaphysical signals are positive in tone.
- Why this rating
  - The school’s core materials explicitly ground education in biblical/theological commitments, discipleship, and confessional statements, aligning strongly with Transcendental moral realism and liturgical/theological orientation. This category is robustly CLASSICAL.

  - There is explicit mention of upper-school rhetoric and the goal of training students in eloquence and public speaking (rhetoric and oratory). 
- Positive/ Negative keyword analysis

The explicit “experiential learning” or overly constructivist approaches are not foregrounded. 
  - Quantitative note: Mixed signals; clear classical pedagogy elements exist, but explicit, wide-scale Socratic/seminar practice and scholé restfulness are not plainly documented across all materials.
- Why this rating
  - The school demonstrates classical pedagogy elements (grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, memory work, rhetoric training) but lacks comprehensive explicit documentation of certain elements (e.g., explicit, sustained Socratic seminars across grades; explicit scholé days). This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Aggregate Assessment

- Overall Classification: CLASSICAL
- Rationale: A strong classical Christian core across Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, Faculty, and Parents/Community, with robust curricular and pedagogical elements rooted in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and formation-focused aims. The public materials show less explicit emphasis on Great Books and a formal, universal Great Conversation, and while pedagogy includes core classical practices, explicit, widespread Socratic/seminar structures are not comprehensively documented. Taken together, the school aligns with CLASSICAL, with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL elements primarily in Curriculum and Pedagogy due to this explicit gap.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly incorporate and publish a formal Great Books reading program and Great Conversation elements (names of authors/works, reading sequences, and assessment tied to the classics list). Consider a published cross-curricular Great Books slate (Homer, Plato, Augustine, Shakespeare, etc.) and a clear rationale for how these texts integrate with theology and virtue education.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Document and expand explicit use of Socratic discussion/Seminars across grades, with sample seminar structures, teacher training in Paideia-style pedagogy, and a publicly stated commitment to scholé/restful learning days. Include examples of student-led seminars and formal debate opportunities to demonstrate rhetoric training in practice.
