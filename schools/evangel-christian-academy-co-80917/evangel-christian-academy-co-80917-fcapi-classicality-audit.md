## Executive Summary

Evangel Christian Academy (ECA) in Colorado Springs presents a clearly classical Christian orientation across its publicly accessible materials. The school consistently frames its program as Christ-centered, rooted in a Christian worldview, and organized around classical pedagogical phases (Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric). Curriculum and pedagogy emphasize the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integration of faith with learning, and formation of character and virtue, aligned with foundational classical criteria. The school also foregrounds community life, partnerships with families, and a “Portrait of a Graduate” that envisions graduates who live out a biblical worldview in service to church, family, and broader culture. Based on the public materials, the six classical school categories are overall CLASSICAL, with strong evidence across Religion & Metaphysics, Purpose, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parent/Community engagement.

Key supporting signals include: explicit Christ-centered and biblical worldview framing across curricula; articulation of virtue, wisdom, and moral formation; explicit reference to the Trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and the Logic/Rhetoric stages; recognized classical curriculum resources and ACCS alignment; liturgical and communal practices (chapel, discipleship, prayer); visible emphasis on partnerships with families and church, and a shared mission to form students for service in Christ. Citations to the school’s own pages and materials substantiate these observations, including Our Core Convictions, Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric structure, and the Portrait of a Graduate materials. See evidence from Evangel’s site materials (mission/vision/philosophy, grammar/logic/rhetoric pages, chapel and Bible-infused practices, and family/community sections) and from the Six Essential Elements framework used for rubric alignment.

Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Upper grades emphasize Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training; the public materials reference the Rhetoric School as the final phase of the Trivium, signaling a Socratic/seminar-based approach in higher grades. 
- Lower school appears to emphasize memory work, singing/chanting, and substantial memorization as a means of deepening memory and delight (Scholé-like aspects). The Grammar School materials describe foundational memorization and recall-based routines, including Bible verse memory and structured recitation. 
- The school emphasizes integrated faith-and-learning and a formation-focused pedagogy (formation through imitation and practice; engaged with literature, history, and philosophy in ways consistent with classical pedagogy). This is visible in core statements about a Christ-centered, liberal-arts-informed approach and the use of classical pedagogy to cultivate truth and eloquence. 
- The Arts, music programs, and performance opportunities (band, choir, visual arts) function as integrated elements of a holistic pedagogy, reinforcing memory, rhetoric, and aesthetic education in a classical framework. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords drawn from the Six Elements for Pedagogy: “Socratic dialogue,” “Scholé (restful learning),” “Memorization and Recitation,” “Formation through imitation and practice,” “Socratic discussion/seminars/debates,” and “rhetoric training.” ECA materials explicitly show: (a) Rhetoric School and Socratic/seminar-based upper-level pedagogy; (b) lower-level memorization and recitation; (c) memory-driven practice and chapel/Bible memory as part of formation.   
- Negative keywords: None prominent; no strong evidence of anti-Socratic methods or purely utilitarian, non- contemplative pedagogy. Some sources indicate rest and contemplation as part of the learning pace (aligned with Scholé), which is a positive signal rather than a negative one in classical rubric terms. 

Why this rating
- Pedagogy at ECA aligns with classical expectations: a cradle-to-high-school progression from grammar/memory-based foundational learning to Socratic dialogue and rhetorical mastery; incorporation of contemplative/restful elements; and an emphasis on formation through imitation and practice. CLASSICAL.
