## ABSTRACT

Logos Academy York presents a CLASSICAL profile across all six rubric categories. The school’s public materials consistently emphasize Christ-centered formation, virtue and wisdom, a liberal-arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; seven liberal arts; great books/great conversation), and a pedagogy that includes Socratic discussion, narration, recitation, and contemplative practices. Faculty and parent-community integration align with the classical ideal of the living curriculum and collaborative partnership with families. A notable tension lies in the explicit inclusion of college/career readiness as an outcome within Educational Philosophy, which introduces a modest deviation from strict formation-over-information primacy. Despite this, the evidence strongly supports a CLASSICAL classification, with each category showing robust alignment to classical education criteria and the Positive Keyword Indicators.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes Christ-centered framing, biblical worldview, liturgical practices, and formation language. Positive keyword indicators dominate with little to no negative signals. 

- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes formation and virtue as primary aims, with intelligence plus character. Some college/workforce-readiness framing appears but formation remains central. 

- Curriculum: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes liberal arts sequence and seven liberal arts, Great Conversation framing, and canonical content. 

- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes Socratic/dialogue pedagogy and memory-based methods, with contemplative/restful elements. 

- Faculty: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes living curriculum and mentorship language; faculty development and collegial culture. 

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes collaborative family partnerships and open-campus involvement. 

Overall: CLASSICAL
