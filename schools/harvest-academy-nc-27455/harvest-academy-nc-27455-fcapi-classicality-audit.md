## Executive Summary

Harvest Academy NC 27455 presents a clearly classical-leaning program within a Christian, University-Model framework. The school foregrounds a Christian worldview, a traditional Trivium-aligned curriculum, a parental-partnership model, and a commitment to formation of character and virtue alongside intellectual development. Public materials emphasize a three-stage Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/Western canon through select middle-school literature, and a heavy role for families as co-educators. This places Harvest Academy solidly in the Classical camp for Curriculum and Pedagogy, with strong Religious/Metaphysical foundations and a robust Parents & Community orientation. However, the site is comparatively thin in making explicit the full range of Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy and certain liturgical practices that some classical schools highlight. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (with strong CLASSICAL tendencies in core areas and explicit Christian formation), driven by a demonstrable commitment to formation, the Trivium, Great Books, parent partnership, and a University-Model integration of home and school.

Key strengths
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Explicit Christian worldview; Statement of Faith and biblical worldview framing all disciplines; head-of-school articulation of faith as central to schooling. 
- Curriculum: Clear Trivium framing (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and a strong emphasis on Great Books/history through specific middle-school reading lists; explicit articulation of a liberal-arts core within a Christian framework. 
- Pedagogy within a Classical frame: University-Model structure blends on-site and home-based learning in service of a formation-oriented approach; emphasis on memory work and classic texts in certain grade bands; a language of “formation” and “virtue.” 
- Parents & Community: Strong partnership with families; explicit parent training and co-teaching expectations; opportunities for parent information nights and church/community integration. 

Key growth areas (needle-moving opportunities)
- Pedagogy specificity: The public materials could more explicitly document Socratic seminars, disciplined dialogue, and regular memory-work or chant (Scholé) practices at multiple grade levels, as used by some classical schools. The current materials emphasize the University-Model and a classical framework but are less explicit about ongoing Socratic/recitation traditions beyond the Trivium outline. 
- Liturgical/communal practices: While there are explicit faith-based elements, more explicit references to liturgy, communal worship rhythms, and sacred practices would align even more closely with the Six Essential Elements’ liturgical-practices indicators.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Harvest Academy explicitly describes a University-Model structure that blends on-site instruction with home-based learning, emphasizing parental involvement and aTeacher-parent partnership. - Pedagogical emphasis on a three-stage Trivium is described, and upper grades are described as including rhetoric and persuasive communication; lower grades emphasize memory and foundational knowledge.
