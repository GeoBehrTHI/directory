## Executive Summary

American Classical Academy Rutherford (ACAR) demonstrates strong to robust alignment with classical education criteria across all six rubric categories. The public materials reveal a mission centered on character formation, virtue, and truth-seeking; a goal that foregrounds formation over mere information; a curriculum explicitly anchored in the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the study of Western civilization and Great Books; pedagogy that blends contemplative elements with Socratic discourse and rhetoric; a faculty ethos described as knowledgeable, virtuous, and teacher-led; and a deep partnership with families and the broader community. Core phrases such as “the True, the Good, and the Beautiful,” “content-rich Classical Education,” “Socratic Dialogue,” “teacher-led classrooms,” “core virtues,” and explicit family partnerships recur throughout ACAR’s materials, aligning strongly with classical lexicon and criteria. These signals appear consistently across mission statements, pledges, curriculum descriptions, and governance/family materials, and are corroborated by the knowledge-criteria lexicon used to define classical indicators. Overall, ACAR Rutherford merits a CLASSICAL classification across all six categories.

- Evidence highlights include: ACAR’s mission and pledge emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty; formation of virtue and character; a curriculum centered on the liberal arts and Great Books; explicit reference to Socratic dialogue and teacher-led instruction; and a charter-community emphasis that positions families as co-educators. These align with the core positive indicators in the classical rubric and lexicon. See mission/policy statements and curricula descriptions cited below.     

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - ACAR’s materials emphasize enduring truths and virtue in a way that echoes transcendental moral realism. The mission statement describes “training the minds and developing character” through a content-rich Classical Education rooted in moral practices and civic virtue, signaling that education aims at truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable ideals. 
  - The School Pledge explicitly ties learning to the True, the Good, and the Beautiful, signaling a metaphysical frame around education. 
  - ACAR speaks of core virtues (courage, responsibility, friendship, wisdom) as targets of formation, reinforcing the transcendent moral aims of the school. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; moral practices; civic virtue; transcendent ideals (as extracted from the pledge and mission).  
  - Negative keywords: none clearly evident in public materials.
- Why this rating
  - The materials foreground objective moral ideals and the formation of virtue in a context that treats truth, goodness, and beauty as foundational realities, with explicit moral commitments and virtuous aims. This aligns strongly with the “Transcendental moral realism” and “Grounded in Theology” (where theology is not explicitly stated but moral-theological framing appears) signals in the classical lexicon. The absence of explicit liturgical or theological ritual language does not undermine the clear moral/metaphysical framing, which is the core of this category in ACAR’s materials. CLASSICAL.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The mission centers on character formation and virtue through a liberal, classical education, rather than mere job preparation. The materials emphasize formation over information and the pursuit of human excellence rooted in classical aims. 
  - The School pledge and honor/virtue language reflect a telos oriented toward truth, goodness, and beauty, i.e., the pursuit of higher ends beyond vocational outcomes. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: formation; virtue; wisdom; liberal education; telos; pursuit of the true, good, beautiful. 
  - Negative keywords: not evident in public materials (no emphasis on workforce readiness or 21st-century skills in the sense of career preparation).
- Why this rating
  - ACAR’s articulated goals center on formation and human excellence, with explicit aim to cultivate virtue and wisdom via a classical mission and liberal education frame. This maps cleanly onto the classical category’s positive indicators, with no strong counter-indicators present. CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The curriculum is described as “content-rich” with emphasis on formal liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and “Socratic Dialogue” is explicitly referenced as a pedagogy. Great Books/Western Civilization emphasis and primary resources are highlighted, including Latin and other core norms of classical education.  
  - Hillsdale K-12 initiative and Western tradition emphasis indicate a curriculum designed around classic texts, Great Books, and a long historical arc. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric); Great Books; primary sources; Latin; history/Western civilization; Socratic dialogue; wonder and integration of subjects (integration/wonder signals).  
  - Negative keywords: absence of project-based learning dominance; no social-studies terminology substituting history (public materials emphasize history within a classical frame). 
- Why this rating
  - ACAR’s public-facing materials explicitly articulate the core curriculum elements of classical education (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Western civilization; Latin; content-rich and wonder-driven). The presence of Great Books and primary texts, in particular, aligns with the Positive Keywords for Curriculum. CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Pedagogical notes explicitly include Socratic dialogue and upper-school seminars/debates with rhetoric training; lower-school experiences include memory-oriented practices and chanting or singing to reinforce learning, aligning with scholé/restful as well as active, disciplined study. The site also markets “teacher-led classrooms” and “traditional teaching methods.” 
  - The presence of “Socratic Dialogue” in the curriculum and explicit references to upper-school rhetoric and public speaking strengthens the case for a classical pedagogy. 
  - The mission emphasizes a disciplined, character-formation approach with decorum and virtuous inquiry, consistent with a contemplative and rigorous pedagogy. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Socratic seminar/dialogue; narration/recitation; memory work; singing/chanting for memorization; contemplative/restful learning (scholé); rhetoric; debate; upper-school rhetoric training. 
  - Negative keywords: very little evidence of purely constructivist/experiential-only approaches; no overwhelming emphasis on project-based learning as the dominant pedagogy. 
- Why this rating
  - ACAR’s materials consistently describe pedagogical practices (Socratic dialogue, memory work, chanting, rhetoric exercises, upper-level debates) that align with classical pedagogy. The explicit listing of teacher-led instruction and the integration of memory and recitation reinforce a scholé-inflected approach. CLASSICAL.

### Element 5: School Faculty — CLASSICAL

### Element 6: School Parents and Community — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - ACAR positions families as central to formation and as co-teachers with the school; explicit partnerships with parents, family formation opportunities (book studies, seminars, gatherings), and welcome visits are described. The charter framework emphasizes family involvement and partnership in education.

  - Positive keywords present: partnership with parents; parents as co-teachers; parent formation; reading groups; seminars/book clubs; open campus; hospitality; parent covenant.

- Why this rating
  - ACAR strongly emphasizes parental partnership and community engagement, which aligns with the classical model of an engaged, formation-focused community. CLASSICAL.
