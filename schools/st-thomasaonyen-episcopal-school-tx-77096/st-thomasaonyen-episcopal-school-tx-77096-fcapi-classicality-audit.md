## Executive Summary

Saint Thomas' Episcopal School (STE) presents a robust classical Christian profile that strongly aligns with core classical education principles. Public materials from STE emphasize a Christ-centered, Christian classical curriculum, daily chapel, a liberal-arts orientation, and a mission of forming honorable men and women through a classical education. These signals support a CLASSICAL rating across most dimensions, with especially clear strength in religious/metaphysical foundations, school purpose and goal, and community/family engagement. Some aspects of pedagogy appear less explicit in public materials (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars or scholé-focused routines), which yields a category of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Pedagogy, while Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community show strong classical signals. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key observable patterns
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: daily chapel, Christ-centered worldview, Episcopal identity, doctrinal grounding, worship as part of daily life. Evidence: daily chapel and Christ-centered worldview language appears across STE materials ; Episcopal identity and theology are foregrounded in About content .
- Purpose and goal center on formation, virtue, and wisdom rather than mere job-preparation. Evidence: mission statements emphasize forming honorable men and women via classical education rooted in a Christian worldview ; Headmaster notes on education for life, not just college prep .
- Curriculum is described as liberal-arts oriented with emphasis on classical instruction; grammar, logic, and rhetoric are present in the public materials (e.g., lower school grammar, upper-school logic) and a strong liberal-arts framing. Evidence: “private, PK-12 school that upholds Christian values and educates through classical instruction” and explicit mention of a liberal-arts oriented Upper School program with logic as an elective ; lower-school grammar referenced in Kindergarten–Grade 1–5 overview (grammar in the early grades) ; Upper School electives include logic, etc. .
- Pedagogy signals are classical-adjacent but not always explicit in terms of traditional Socratic/seminar formats in the public pages; there is strong emphasis on rigorous classical standards and memory/recitation patterns, but explicit “Socratic” pedagogy is not consistently named in the public content. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal for Pedagogy with a path toward CLASSICAL if Socratic/seminar elements are made explicit. Evidence includes the overall classical framing, and explicit mention of a “liberal arts curriculum” with opportunities for debate and public speaking in Upper School, but without repeatedly explicit Socratic/dialogic pedagogy in the public-facing text .
- Faculty portrayal is positive and central to the living curriculum; teachers are described as caring, called to teaching, and integral to the school’s mission. Evidence: “talented, caring faculty who see teaching as a calling” plus “forming honorable men and women through a classical education” language .
- Parents & Community emphasis on partnership and openness to school community; STE highlights parent involvement, campus visits, and family engagement as part of the classical ethos. Evidence: “close-knit community of families who value educational excellence and share a commitment” ; admissions and campus-visit openness; parent resources and community features visible in multiple pages .

  - Quantitative estimation: Positive hits ~2–4 explicit classical-pedagogy terms in the public content; Negative hits ~0–1; Overall, modest explicit alignment with traditional classical pedagogy language, but not consistently named in the public pages. CLASSICAL signals exist, but explicit Socratic/scholé language is not consistently present.
- Why this rating
