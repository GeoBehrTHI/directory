## Executive Summary

Northeast Ohio Classical Academy (NEOCA) presents a primarily classical orientation with clear commitments to traditional liberal arts, virtue formation, and Western-civilizational study, anchored by a Hillsdale College K-12 curriculum. The school foregrounds moral character, civic virtue, and a content-rich liberal-arts core, while presenting opportunities for student memory work, classical language study, and geometry of a traditional curriculum. Public materials emphasize truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and wonder, and consistently frame education as formation and character shaping, not merely information delivery.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): If the school wishes to move toward a fully CLASSICAL standing in this category, it could:
  - Add explicit theological grounding or liturgical/community worship language to public materials (optional within a public-charter context, but frequently present in many classical schools’ “liturgical” or “communal” elements). A clear articulation of theological underpinning or liturgical practices would strengthen the Liturgical/Communal Practices and Grounded in Theology signals.
  - Integrate explicit liturgical or theological framing in the curriculum narrative (e.g., a section detailing liturgical rhythms or catechesis alignment) to meet full classical criteria for liturgical community and theology.

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): To elevate Pedagogy toward CLASSICAL:
  - Include explicit evidence of Socratic pedagogy and seminars in upper grades on public pages or in formal program documentation (e.g., documented Socratic seminars, Harkness methods, or rhetoric/debate programs with public-facing descriptions).
  - Increase emphasis on Scholé/restful learning and memory working/recitation on public materials to reflect a deeper adherence to the classical pedagogy tradition.

- Element 2, 3, 5, 6 currently show CLASSICAL alignment. Minor enhancements in public-facing content (e.g., more explicit Great-Books/Great-Conversation language, and explicit Socratic or memory-work references) could further reinforce the classical standing.

- Concrete moves
  - Publish a public-facing “Pedagogy Snapshot” detailing Socratic seminars, Harkness-style discussions, and upper-grade rhetoric projects with examples.
  - Add a public-facing page or document describing liturgical or communal practices (where applicable) or theological grounding that complements the Hillsdale-based curriculum.
  - Include explicit Great-Books/Great-Conversation language in curriculum pages, with sample course lists and annotated reading lists.
