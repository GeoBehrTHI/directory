## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy Lewisville, TX (75067) shows a robust classical orientation across most of the Six Elements rubric. Public materials on the school’s site consistently foreground a knowledge-rich liberal-arts curriculum, explicit emphasis on Great Books and Western civilization, and a pedagogy that blends dialectic/rhetoric in the upper grades with knowledge-building foundations in the early years. There is clear alignment with formation of character, virtue, and wisdom, along with explicit attention to grammar, logic, and rhetoric as core tools of learning. Public materials also highlight a partnership model with families and a culture of parent involvement.

Two areas temper the overall classical alignment. First, while the school uses a classical framework and emphasizes virtue, truth, beauty, and moral formation, explicit references to liturgical practices or theology as grounding metaphysical foundations are not plainly evident in the public website content. Second, some elements of pedagogy (e.g., explicit singing/chanting in lower grades) are not described on public pages; the material emphasizes dialectic, rhetoric, and memory-based work in different stages rather than a blanket scholé/restful-learning language across all grades. Taken together, these factors place the school on a strong classical footing, with some room for deeper alignment in liturgical/theological framing and lower-grade contemplative practices.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

- Evidence of CLASSICAL alignment is strongest in Curriculum and Pedagogy, where Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Latin/Greek roots, and Western civilization are foregrounded, along with a clearly formation-focused rationale for education. See curriculum/k-12 language and Great Books emphasis, including Latin/logic in the middle and high school, and the emphasis on original sources and Great Books. 
- The school also explicitly describes formation through virtue and moral education, which aligns with the classical aim of formation rather than information. 
- There is evidence of parental partnership and community involvement in the school’s materials, consistent with the classical model’s emphasis on parents as co-teachers and engaged communities.

- If the school sought to move toward a stronger CLASSICAL standing in Pedagogy, it could explicitly document lower-grade contemplative practices (Scholé) and memorization/recitation routines (chanting, memory-work) in public materials to align with the liturgical, memory-rich pedagogy signals in the Six Elements lexicon.
