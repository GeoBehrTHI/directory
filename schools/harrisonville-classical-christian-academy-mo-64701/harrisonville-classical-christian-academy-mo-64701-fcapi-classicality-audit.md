## Executive Summary

Harrisonville Classical Christian Academy (HCCA) demonstrates clear classical and Christian orientation, anchored by a biblical worldview, Memoria Press curriculum, and engagement with the Western classical tradition (great books, Latin, liberal arts). Public materials emphasize formation of character, wisdom, and virtue, and they position parents as partners in a classical education journey. The school also signals a robust community and parental involvement, with explicit references to a Statement of Faith and a mission to train students in leadership and service within a Christian framework. However, explicit, in-depth articulations of core classical pedagogy (e.g., sustained Socratic seminars in upper grades, Scholé-style restfulness, and a fully explicit articulation of the Trivium/Quadrivium in public-facing materials) are less visible in the readily accessible site content, suggesting mixed strength across the full six-element rubric.

Overall, the school falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. It exhibits strongest alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Parents & Community, and strong alignment in Curriculum, with more modest but present alignment in School Purpose and Goal and Pedagogy, and some growth potential in clearly detailing all six classical pedagogical modes (especially explicit Socratic/seminar methods and a fully explicit Trivium/Quadrivium articulation). The aggregate assessment reflects a balance of classical strengths with areas where explicit public articulation could be sharpened.

Key evidentiary signals include: the biblical Christian framing and Statement of Faith; the Memoria Press-based classical curriculum and emphasis on Great Books and Latin; the mission to form character and wisdom; and explicit calls for parental partnership and involvement. Citations drawn from the school site content include statements about a historic biblical worldview, non-denominational Christian identity, and the classical curriculum rooted in Memoria Press (and great books) along with references to a parental partnership ethos.
