## Executive Summary

Faith Lutheran School Derby KS 67037 presents a clearly confessional, Christian, classical-inclined approach within a K–6 setting. The school emphasizes Jesus Christ, a Biblical worldview, and liturgical or family-centered practices, and it explicitly notes a transition to a classical model of education. The curriculum incorporates classical resources (Latin, Memoria Press, Veritas Press, Progeny Press, etc.) and a structure that resembles classical education (grammar-type subjects in the early grades, memorable Bible-based recitations, and a phonics/classical literature emphasis). Pedagogy includes singing, memory work, and devotional life in lower grades, with evidence of Socratic-style elements not clearly documented in the public materials for upper grades. The school foregrounds parental partnership and family-faith formation as core elements of the program.

Overall aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- This evaluation leans toward a classical spine (theology-infused formation, classical curriculum materials, explicit classical modeling), but the public materials do not consistently demonstrate full classical criteria (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation exposure, explicit Socratic seminars, and explicit articulation of transformation through formation rather than information across all grades). See category-by-category details below.

- What would move the needle: more explicit articulation and visibility of Great Books/Great Conversation, explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, and a clearly defined, public-facing “formation through virtue and wisdom” statement that ties direct evidence of traditional liberal-arts pedagogy (grammar-logic-rhetoric) across all grade bands; plus a public commitment to liturgical rhythms as a patterned, ongoing practice in the upper grades (beyond chapel and family worship).

What Would Move the Needle (for a move to CLASSICAL)

- Make Great Books/Great Conversation explicit in public materials: publish a formal statement that the curriculum includes Great Books or canonical Western literary works appropriate to each grade band, plus explicit connections to the Great Conversation across grades, with sample book lists and learning objectives.
- Publicly document a clear logic-rhetoric trajectory: provide a visible map showing how grammar, logic, and rhetoric are sequenced across K–6 (or K–8) with specific courses or modules named and described (e.g., introductory logic in late elementary; formal rhetoric and public speaking in upper elementary).
- Expand explicit Socratic pedagogy for upper grades: publish classroom models or videos showing Socratic seminars, debates, and seminar-style learning in practice, with teacher guides and student-learning objectives.
- Strengthen the “formation” teleology: publish a concise, public-facing framework that ties formation of virtue, wisdom, and character to specific classroom practices, assessments, and chapel- and family-faith routines.
- Enhance liturgical & contemplative practices as a formal strand: lay out a calendar and program of liturgical formation (not only family worship and chapel, but school-wide liturgical seasons, devotional rhythms, and sacred music curricula) with explicit alignment to classical pedagogy.
- Continue to articulate parental partnership as a core pillar: publish a formal “Parents as Co-Teachers” program with specific trainings, book studies, and home-learning resources aligned to classical education principles.
