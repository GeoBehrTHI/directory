## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Padre Pio (Sarasota, FL) presents a robust classical-Catholic model, consistently aligning with the six elements of classical education. Public materials emphasize a Catholic cultural core (Mass, prayer, liturgy, and daily worship) alongside a liberal-arts curriculum shaped by the Great Books tradition, Socratic dialogue, and memory-based pedagogy. The school frames its mission as formation of virtue and wisdom rather than mere information or workforce preparation, with explicit reference to Formation, Virtue, and the Cultivation of the True, Good, and Beautiful. The faculty is depicted as experienced and aligned with a living-curriculum approach, and the community actively engages parents through informational sessions, open houses, and family involvement. Public materials also highlight the school’s internal culture—House system, daily Mass, and a pilgrim-facing formation that ties academic study to Catholic faith and Western civilizational heritage.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong CLASSICAL signals (Catholic daily life, Incarnation-centered pedagogy, liturgical practices, Mass, prayer, sacred music, liturgy, and theology support). Evidence: Catholic culture as the heartbeat of daily life; Incarnation-centered education; Mass at school; daily ritual and sacred elements.   
- Purpose and Goal: Strong CLASSICAL signals (formation of virtue and wisdom, contemplation, and a telos aligned with truth, goodness, and beauty). Evidence: formation-focused language, Culture Vitae, humane formation of leaders/saints.  
- Curriculum: Strong CLASSICAL signals (liberal arts framework; grammar/logic/rhetoric emphasis; Great Books/Western canon; history-integrated approach; emphasis on wonder). Evidence: humanities program braided with Socratic method; classical literature including Homer, Dante, Shakespeare; Latin/Greek offerings; integration of history, philosophy, theology.  
- Pedagogy: Strong CLASSICAL signals (Socratic dialogue, memorization/recitation, contemplative/restful elements, and rhetoric in upper levels). Evidence: Socratic seminars; “Learning Through Dialogue”; memory and chant/memorization/repetition; discussion-based pedagogy.

Evidence: Headmaster with long record; biographies illustrating virtuous leadership and professional formation; faculty involvement in school culture.
