## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy – Schertz (Schertz, TX) presents itself publicly as a classical school that emphasizes virtue, truth, beauty, and the liberal arts. The campus materials repeatedly articulate a classical orientation (classical education, liberal arts curriculum, virtue-focused goals) and describe partnerships with families. However, the public-facing materials do not consistently foreground explicit theology, liturgical practice, or the full spectrum of classical pedagogical practices (e.g., Socratic seminars, memorization/chants, and “scholé” rhythms) that the classical rubric often expects at all grade levels, especially in the upper grades. As such, the school demonstrates meaningful classical intent and content, but with notable gaps relative to a fully developed classical program across all six categories.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key observations across the six categories:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty, and virtue; lacking explicit theology or liturgical practice in public materials. Evidence of classical orientation is clear, but theological grounding is not openly stated on the site. 
- Purpose and Goals: Clear formation of character and virtue as the primary goal; formation rather than information is implied through virtue-focused language. The school emphasizes forming virtuous citizens, aligning with classical aims. 
- Curriculum: Public statements reference a “traditional liberal arts and sciences curriculum.” However, explicit Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric sequencing, and the Great Conversation are not clearly stated in the site copy. This yields partial alignment with the curriculum criterion. 
- Pedagogy: The public materials emphasize a traditional/classical approach but do not consistently document the full spectrum of classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars in upper school, chant/memory routines, Scholé practices). This is a mixed signal. 
- Faculty: Public-facing pages show named faculty and staff, signaling an engaged faculty body; however, the materials do not explicitly describe the faculty as “living curriculum” mentors or portraits of teacher ideals in the way classical rubrics sometimes require. 
- Parents & Community: The school emphasizes partnership with families and has parent-focused enrollment actions; explicit program for parent formation is not strongly detailed on the site, though there are signals of collaboration with families. 

Note: The knowledge-base sources (Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and Positive/Negative Keyword Lexicon) provide the canonical criteria and vocabulary for a fully classical implementation (e.g., Great Books, Scholé, Socratic pedagogy, liturgy, and a “living curriculum”). These lexicon resources show that true classical fidelity includes explicit theology, liturgical practice, and a full spectrum of classical pedagogy beyond what the FCA Schertz site currently emphasizes. ## What Would Move the Needle

- Strengthen explicit theology and liturgical/community practices in public communications to satisfy the Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices criteria (Element 1). Include statements about theology integration, chapel presence, sacred music, or liturgical calendar alignment.
- Elevate explicit Great Books and Great Conversation framing in the Curriculum section (Element 3). Add language around a core Great Books list, classical authors, and a clear progression through grammar/logic/rhetoric, with history woven throughout the curriculum.
- Document specific pedagogical practices that align with classical pedagogy for upper grades (Element 4). Include mention of Socratic seminars, Paideia-style discussions, rhetoric training, and a formal Scholé rhythm (rest, contemplation, and memory work).
- Make the Faculty a more explicit part of the “living curriculum” by publishing a teacher-formation statement, portrait of ideal teacher-student relationships, and professional development in classical pedagogy (Element 5).
- Expand explicit, structured Parent Formation and Community-Engagement programs (Element 6). Publish details about parent book clubs, family catechesis, home-school partnerships, and regular campus visits as a standard practice.
