## Executive Summary

Trinity Classical Academy CA 91355 presents a clearly classical orientation rooted in a Christ-centered, classical curriculum and pedagogy. Public-facing materials repeatedly affirm a Christian worldview, a focus on virtue and wisdom, the Great Books tradition, and the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as the backbone of its curriculum. The school emphasizes formation over mere information, and consistently frames education as a gateway to character, wisdom, and life-long formation. There is strong evidence of Socratic and rhetoric-rich instruction, with explicit references to Socratic dialogue in class and to upper-school seminars and oratory, alongside a robust commitment to a liturgical and communal life (chapel, worship, and dedicated school-wide rituals).

Overall, applying the Six Essential Elements rubric in combination with Trinity’s public materials yields a CLASSICAL classification across most categories, with particularly strong alignment for Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy. Element 5 (Faculty) shows alignment, but the explicit articulation of the faculty as the “living curriculum” and related portraits is less explicit in public materials, which places that category toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Element 6 (Parents & Community) demonstrates genuine partnership with families and visible community engagement, supporting CLASSICAL status as well.

- Aggregate classification: CLASSICAL
- Principal strengths: explicit Christ-centered classical framework, formation-centered goals, Trivium-based curriculum with Great Books, Socratic pedagogy in upper levels, liturgical/community life, and clear parental/community partnership.
- Areas that could strengthen to reach full alignment: explicit articulation of faculty as the living curriculum; consistent, explicit language about parents as co-teachers beyond partnership language.

Evidence for this assessment is drawn from Trinity’s public materials (Head of School Welcome, About pages, Classical Education pages, Missions/Foundations statements, and program descriptions) and cross-referenced with the classical rubric and keyword lexicon from the knowledge base documents.
