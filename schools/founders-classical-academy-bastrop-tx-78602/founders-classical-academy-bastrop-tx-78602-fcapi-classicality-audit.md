## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy Bastrop (TX 78602) presents a clearly classical-oriented mission and program, centering on virtue, truth, beauty, and a liberal-arts orientation. Public-facing materials emphasize a classical approach, a focus on formation (virtue, character, wisdom), and a partnership orientation with families. However, the visible pedagogy and some curricular specifics do not uniformly align with the fullest classical model (notably, the site lacks explicit references to Great Books, Socratic seminars in the upper grades, and a “living curriculum” / teacher-as-mentor ethos in the public materials). On balance, the school sits between Classical and Somewhat Classical, with several core elements well-supported but with notable gaps in pedagogy, faculty modeling, and explicit curricular attributes standard to a fully classical program.

Overall rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key insights:

- Positive anchors: explicit liberal-arts framing; mission to form virtuous citizens; emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty; clear partnership language with families; commitments to character formation and Wisdom.
 - Evidence: “We utilize a classical approach to education characterized by a traditional liberal arts and sciences curriculum and whole-group teaching methodology with an orientation towards truth, beauty, and goodness” and “the end (goal) of public education in America is to form responsible citizens and virtuous people” ; ;.
- Gaps to reach full classic status: absence of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis; limited public articulation of Socratic pedagogy or scholé; pedagogy described as “whole-group” rather than explicitly Socratic or dialectical in the public materials; limited public statements about a teacher-as-living-curriculum or strong faculty formation culture.
 - Evidence of gap: no explicit Great Books or Great Conversation claims in the public content; pedagogy described as “whole-group teaching methodology” rather than Socratic seminars in the available materials.

What Would Move the Needle

- If the school aimed to reach CLASSICAL in all six categories, concrete steps could include:
 - Pedagogy (Element 4): Adopt and publicly articulate Socratic seminars and disciplined memory-work (recitation, chanting) in appropriate grade bands; incorporate Scholé-style contemplative days; specify how upper-grade seminars and rhetorical training will be delivered.
 - Curriculum (Element 3): Explicitly adopt and publicize Great Books/Great Conversation components, including core primary sources and canonical literature; specify the Seven Liberal Arts and Quadrivium in curriculum structure where feasible; articulate cross-curricular integration with a clear plan for history across subjects and primary-source emphasis.
 - Faculty (Element 5): Develop a formal “living curriculum” model where teachers embody and model classical virtues and intellectual habits; publish a faculty portrait/character profile; implement ongoing teacher formation around classical pedagogy and mentorship.
 - Religious/Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1): If desired, deepen explicit theological or liturgical dimensions (where aligned with school mission and state policy) and describe liturgical or communal practices (even if nonsectarian) that nurture reverence, wonder, and moral formation without compromising compliance.
 - Parents & Community (Element 6): Expand explicit parent formation offerings (book studies, lectures) and ensure transparent, regular opportunities for parents to observe and participate as co-educators; maintain open-campus days and formal parent-led groups with clear mission statements.
