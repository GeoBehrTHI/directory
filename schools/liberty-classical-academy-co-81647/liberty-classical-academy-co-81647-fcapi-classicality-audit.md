## Executive Summary

Liberty Classical Academy (New Castle, CO, 81647) presents a robust classical Christian framework across most rubric domains. The school explicitly anchors its mission in Christian faith, integrates theology with classical disciplines, and foregrounds the liberal arts (grammar–logic–rhetoric) along with Great Books / Great Conversation, and a University-Model approach that emphasizes formation through a partnership with families. Public materials consistently reference truth, goodness, and beauty as organizing ideals, and emphasize formation of character and virtue, not merely information or career preparation. Collectively, the six classical-school categories skew toward CLASSICAL, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) showing modest tension toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to explicit lower-school singing/memory work and some ambiguity about strictly Socratic seminar use in upper grades. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key insights at a glance:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strong and explicit (Statement of Faith, Biblical worldview, virtue/theology grounding).
- Purpose and Goal emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and a teleological orientation toward truth/beauty/goodness.
- Curriculum centers on the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Great Books, and a Great Conversation, with coherent history-driven unit studies.
- Pedagogy shows classical structure (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and emphasis on memory and integrative learning; explicit Socratic/seminal methods are less overt in the public material, placing Pedagogy at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Faculty portrayal stresses professional, mentor-like teachers, living-curriculum orientation, and staff development aligned with the school’s Christian/classical aims.
- Parents & Community are deeply engaged as co-educators, with ongoing programs (Practical Parenting Classes, meetings, campus visits) and open parent involvement policies. 

# ABSTRACT

Liberty Classical Academy (New Castle, CO, 81647) aligns strongly with core classical education premisses (virtue/formation, Great Books, liberal arts, theology-integrated curriculum, heavy parental partnership). The aggregate classification is CLASSICAL, with a single category (Pedagogy) ranking SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to the blended, non-uniform emphasis on some non-Socratic, non-memorization pedagogy. Overall, the school demonstrates a coherent classical framework with explicit religious foundations.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
 - Lower/grammar stage pedagogy includes memory-based elements (singing, chanting, memory-review) in line with scholé-era pedagogy, as seen in preschool and grammar-focused materials.
 - The school emphasizes a grammar/logic/rhetoric progression and memory work, consistent with classical pedagogy in form.
 - Upper grades mention Socratic-like discussion or rhetoric training in public materials? The public site emphasizes rhetoric and public speaking in the rhetoric stage, but explicit Socratic seminars in practice aren’t consistently documented in public text; some materials cite “Great Conversation” and original texts as core, which often implies discussion-based engagement but not explicitly labeled as Socratic seminars.
 - Public-facing materials emphasize integration of learning (unit studies, Western history) and experiential/arts components (theatre, field days), pointing to a blended pedagogy rather than a single rigid method.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
 - Positive keywords present: memorization/recitation, chanting, narration, restfulness/scholé, Socratic dialogue (implicit in “Great Conversation” and rhetoric training), seminars/debates (language appears in the rubric but explicit examples are less consistent in public text).
 - Negative keywords present: experiential learning and project-based emphasis appear in some classical-education lexicons as potential divergences; the school’s public materials show more integrated unit-study and experiential components, which can be seen as a partial divergence from strict “recitation/narration only” pedagogy. The lexicon lists such as “experiential learning” as a potential negative signal, but the public content frequently pairs it with classical aims (unit studies, history/narrative). This yields a mixed signal.
 - Quantitative note: positive keywords identified include memory-work, narration, recitation, chanting (present in public text and related philosophy); negative cues appear less explicit in text but are present in the broader classical lexicon; overall, positive signals exist but with some ambiguity in the explicit pedagogy phrasing.

- Why this rating
 - The public materials demonstrate a fundamentally classical pedagogy (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, memory-work, narration). However, the explicit depiction of Socratic seminars and strict scholé/restful pace in high school is not consistently documented in the public-facing site content, and there are blended/experiential elements. This justifies a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation rather than full CLASSICAL.

Citations: Preschool/grammar memory language (singing/recitation) ; Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric structure and Great Conversation ; Upper Humanities and public text referencing cross-disciplinary units (Tapestry of Grace, etc.) ; Public theatre/experiential activities (e.g., theatre productions) as part of curriculum integration

## What Would Move the Needle

- Not applicable here since the aggregate rating is CLASSICAL. The singular SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal in Pedagogy could be addressed by a public articulation of explicit Socratic/seminar-based practices and a more consistent articulation of schola-based restfulness and chant/memorization traditions in upper grades. If the school wanted to move toward a fully CLASSICAL Pedagogy designation, it could:
 - Publish concrete, K–12 evidence of Socratic seminars or seminars-like discussions in upper grades with exemplars and rubrics.
 - Increase explicit description of memory-work, recitation, and chanting as a core, repeated practice in the upper grades (and tie it to assessment).
 - Highlight how Scholé (restful, contemplative pace) is maintained across grade bands with specific schedule patterns.

- Otherwise, the core religious, curricular, and community commitments appear well aligned with classical education and would remain CLASSICAL with minor, targeted enhancements in Pedagogy documentation.
