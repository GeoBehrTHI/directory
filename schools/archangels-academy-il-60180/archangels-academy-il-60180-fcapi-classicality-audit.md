# Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Bishop Ryan Catholic School (https://bishopryan.com/) presents a strong Catholic identity with abundant liturgical life (daily prayer, daily Mass, liturgical-year celebrations) and an explicit emphasis on virtue in its public messaging. However, its academic program, as represented on the crawled pages, does not present hallmark features of a classical curriculum or pedagogy (e.g., the Trivium, Great Books, Socratic seminar, Latin as core), and it highlights modern program elements such as AP opportunities and customized learning plans. Net: Formation and worship are strong; classical curricular/pedagogical distinctives are not evident on the site materials reviewed.   

Method: This audit uses the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators drawn from Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and the companion lexicon to quantify signals across six categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose & Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community). These lexica define classical-positive terms such as truth, goodness, beauty; Trivium/Quadrivium; Great Books; Socratic; scholé; chanting; and cautionary/negative signals such as AP-heavy, personalized learning, project-based/experiential emphasis, and social studies replacing history.   

School website used for this audit: https://bishopryan.com/

# What would move the needle
If Bishop Ryan wishes to be recognized clearly as a classical school based on public materials:
- Publish curricular distinctives:
  - Explicitly name the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and, where applicable, Quadrivium; list Latin (and/or Greek) as required/core; publish grade‑level Great Books/primary source lists; use “history” rather than “social studies” as a course label.  
- Describe pedagogy:
  - Highlight Socratic seminars (upper grades), chanting/recitation and memory work (lower grades), and an ethos of scholé/restful learning; showcase rhetoric deliverables (speeches, orations, theses). 
- Clarify assessment/advanced work:
  - If AP remains, frame it as subordinate to classical goals (depth over speed), or publish classical capstones as the academic pinnacle (e.g., senior thesis/oration). 
- Faculty profile:
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” emphasizing mentors/tutors as the living curriculum, with ongoing classical formation for faculty. 
- Parent formation:
  - Offer and publicize classical‑education seminars/book clubs for parents; articulate the parent‑school partnership in classical terms.
