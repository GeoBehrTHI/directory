# Classicality Audit Report

Executive Summary
- School: Barry County Christian School (BCCS)
- URL audited: https://bccsk12.com/
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Why: BCCS publicly affirms core hallmarks of classical Christian education—virtue formation; theology as “queen of the sciences”; the Trivium; Great Books; and the use of original sources. Evidence for pedagogy is strong at the grammar and rhetoric ends (memorization/singing; persuasive expression), but explicit signals for Socratic seminar/scholé and faculty formation are not yet visible on the site. Parent partnership is affirmed in principle, though fewer concrete parent-formation practices are documented online. These gaps keep the overall rating at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL despite strong positives in the first three categories. Evidence examples: “Theology is the queen of the sciences” and an integrated “Biblical framework” for all subjects; Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric); engaging Great Books and “ad fontes” original sources; early grades “memorizing, singing, and rhymes” and upper school “present ideas persuasively.”   

Method note: Keyword analysis and scoring below use the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators and definitions from Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and the classicality lexicon, applied across six categories (Religious/Metaphysical, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents/Community).

- Category ratings: C1 CLASSICAL; C2 CLASSICAL; C3 CLASSICAL; C4 SOMEWHAT; C5 NOT; C6 SOMEWHAT

Rationale: BCCS shows strong classical identity in its foundations, purpose, and curriculum—explicitly employing the Trivium, Great Books, ad fontes, and theological integration (theology as “queen of the sciences”), and grounding education in virtue and the transcendentals. Public materials currently provide fewer concrete details about Socratic/seminar practice, restful learning/scholé, faculty formation/portraits, and structured parent formation, keeping the aggregate just below a full CLASSICAL designation.   


What Would Move the Needle (to CLASSICAL)
- Pedagogy: Add explicit references to “Socratic seminar,” “Harkness,” “disputation,” and “scholé/restful learning,” and showcase upper-school orations/senior theses.  
- Faculty: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher,” calling teachers mentors/models of virtue; describe ongoing classical faculty formation (e.g., Great Books seminars, classical pedagogy training). 
- Parents & Community: Highlight parent-formation practices (book clubs, classical education evenings), family liturgical rhythms, and open-visit policies. 
- Religious life: If applicable, describe chapel, hymnody, prayers, or liturgical calendar events integrated into school life.
