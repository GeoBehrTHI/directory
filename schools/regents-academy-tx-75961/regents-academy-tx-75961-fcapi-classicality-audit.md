## Executive Summary

Regents Academy TX 75961 presents a clearly classical and Christian orientation across most dimensions of the classical rubric. Public materials describe the school as “distinctively classical and decidedly Christian,” with a governance and educational philosophy rooted in Scripture and Christian catechesis. The curriculum centers on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), literacy through literature and great books, Latin study, and a robust integration of theology, history, and the sciences. Pedagogy emphasizes a mix of chant/memory in younger grades and Socratic/seminar-style inquiry in upper grades, aligning with the classical model. There is explicit emphasis on formation, virtue, and character, and the school foregrounds the partnership with families and parent-involvement through events, volunteering, and formation activities. The school’s public materials also frame the Great Books/Genealogy of Western civilization as central to its mission, including explicit references to Latin, literature, theology, and classical languages as core content. There is strong evidence for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Parents & Community. The Faculty category has comparatively less overt public articulation of the “living curriculum” or teacher-as-model-per-se language, which makes that category slightly weaker than the others.

- Overall, the aggregate impression is CLASSICAL, with a notable but patchy area of improvement around a more explicit articulation of faculty as living exemplars within a classical framework (see What Would Move the Needle). Where some categories show full alignment (Categories 1–4, Element 6), Element 5 shows only partial alignment, yielding a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal there. The Executive Summary below provides category-by-category detail and evidence, followed by an ABSTRACT that synthesizes the six-category assessment.

- Key supporting materials from Regents Academy include explicit statements of Christian/theological grounding, Trivium-based curriculum, and Great Books–oriented reading, Latin study, and historical depth, alongside documented parent involvement and volunteer partnerships. For instance, the school identifies itself as a Christian classical school and references a curriculum built around theology, logic, rhetoric, Latin, literature, and history, with a clear mission to form virtue and mature character in students. ### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Regents Academy positions itself as a distinctly classical Christian school with explicit statements of biblical authority, doctrinal framing, and Christ-centered aims. For example, public materials emphasize that “every area of faith and life” is governed by Scripture and that “All truth is God’s truth,” grounding all knowledge in a theistic framework. The school’s educational philosophy explicitly ties education to biblical authority and the Triune God, and its governance indicates Scriptural oversight.  
- Foundational religious language is pervasive: references to “the lordship of Jesus Christ,” Coram Deo, and a Christian worldview explicit in mission statements and vision for graduates. The materials also describe the Bible as the authoritative Word of God and present a Statement of Faith.  

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: truth, God, Scripture, theology, Word of God, Coram Deo, Christian worldview, liturgy/worship (concepts tied to sacred life), virtue and holiness (virtue-related language appears in mission/vision). Evidence of “All truth is God’s truth” and “unified Christian worldview” are explicit. 
- Negative keywords: None observed in public materials (e.g., relativism, secular humanism, value-neutral approaches are not endorsed in the public materials). The materials consistently affirm transcendental realism and theological grounding. 

Why this rating
- The materials consistently foreground transcendental/moral realism and theological grounding, with explicit Scriptural authority guiding decisions, curriculum, and community life. The presence of a Statement of Faith, a theology-grounded educational philosophy, and references to Coram Deo align strongly with the Positive Keywords for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and counter any relativistic or secular framing. The absence of explicit negative signals about secularism in the public materials supports a CLASSICAL rating for this category. 

Notes: The presence of liturgical/community practices and explicit theology is anchored in school materials (e.g., “theology,” “Scripture,” “Bible is the Word of God”). The Great Books/Great Conversation framing is connected with Christian worldview formation rather than a purely secular metaphysical program, reinforcing CLASSICAL in this category. Citations reflect foundational beliefs and governance indicated in the school’s materials.

Why this rating

The Great Conversation, formation through classical education, and Christian worldview align with the Positive Keywords associated with the Purpose and Goal category. The absence of a vocational emphasis in the public materials supports CLASSICAL rather than a workforce-centric or information-formation only framing. Citations reflect mission and vision statements and the formation-centric framing.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogical approach includes contemplative/restful elements (Scholé) and memory-friendly practices in early grades, as indicated by chanting, singing, and memory work in lower grades. The school also emphasizes Socratic-style discussion, seminars, or debate in upper grades and training in rhetoric (addressing Element 4’s upper-school pedagogy). The presence of both modes aligns with the classical pedagogy spectrum.  
- Specific methods such as chanting, recitation, and memory-building (lower school) are explicitly present in the site’s Elementary content (e.g., “Enchanting Chanting”) and are consistent with classical practices. 
- Upper-school pedagogy features Socratic discussion, seminars, rhetorical training, and opportunities to deliver speeches, consistent with the Trivium’s rhetoric stage and classical seminar formats. The curriculum content highlights logic/rhetoric and rhetoric-based courses, with senior-level rhetoric and capstone-style components described. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic seminar, recitation, chanting, memory work, scholé, seminar, dialectic, rhetoric, public speaking, debate, memory-based learning, lively discussion, logical reasoning, guided discussion. The Regents materials mention Lang/Latin teaching and Omnibus pedagogy, as well as the “classical model” of education using grammar/logic/rhetoric.  
- Negative keywords: The lexicon lists negative indicators such as “experiential learning focus,” “project-based only,” and “constructivist.” Regents materials show project-based elements in some contexts (e.g., dual-credit class options) but also emphasize memory, recitation, and rhetoric as core pedagogical modes, which mitigates against a full negative classification. The explicit presence of chanting and Socratic/seminar pedagogy supports CLASSICAL rather than NOT CLASSICAL; no overt negative signals are dominant in public content. 

Why this rating
- The presence of contemplative/restful elements and memory-based lower-school pedagogy, along with Socratic/seminar and rhetoric-focused upper-school pedagogy, is a strong alignment with classical teaching methods. The public materials emphasize both Scholé-like restfulness and rhetoric-based, dialogic learning, yielding a CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy. Citations show the lower- and upper-school pedagogical cues.  

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The public materials emphasize the school’s classical Christian identity and the faculty as exemplars in practice, but explicit statements that describe faculty as “living curriculum,” “model teachers,” or “portrait of a teacher” are less directly articulated in the public-facing site text. The framework documents describe such faculty characteristics as a core element of classical schools, but the Regents site does not consistently foreground these teacher-centric portraits in accessible public content. 
- There are indirect signals of strong teacher leadership and virtue-based expectations in mission/vision statements, and of a governance structure (board-based decisions guided by Scripture) that suggests a culture of serious formation. However, explicit characterizations of faculty as “living curriculum” or “portrait of teacher” are not always overt in public pages. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: “teacher/mentors” language is not strongly foregrounded in the public pages; the lexicon’s positive indicators would include terms like “living curriculum,” “portrait of a teacher,” and “faculty as core,” which are not clearly and consistently used on the site. Some mission/vision language implies teachers as bearers of virtue and knowledge, but explicit phrases are sparse. 
- Negative keywords: None clearly present in public content that would run counter to classical faculty ideals; the absence of explicit “facilitator on the side” or “certification-driven jargon without classical context” language is noted—but not explicit evidence of actual practice. The absence of explicit “faculty living curriculum” language is the core reason for a weaker rating in this category. 

Why this rating
- While the school’s mission and academic plan imply a high regard for faculty leadership and moral exemplar-hood, the public-facing content does not consistently include the explicit, formalized language that the classical rubric uses to describe “Living Curriculum” and “Portrait of the Teacher.” This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Faculty, with room for stronger articulation of teacher leadership and exemplar roles in the public materials. Citations reflect the lack of explicit vocabulary in public pages and the presence of mission-based virtue language.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were to drift toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL or not fully CLASSICAL, the most needle-moving improvements would involve:
  - Element 5 (Faculty): Add explicit, public-facing statements and materials that describe the faculty as the living curriculum, with portraits of teacher ideals, professional development in classical pedagogy, and published faculty biographies that illustrate exemplary classical practice. This would transform Element 5 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL by making the faculty dimension explicit and central.
  - Element 5 could be further strengthened by documenting formal faculty collaboration, a classroom-embedded classical pedagogy model, and teacher-led exemplars in annual reports or on the website. This would align the public narrative with the Six Essential Elements’ expectations for faculty as mentors and living curriculum. 
  - Element 6 already shows strong parent engagement, but strengthening “Parent Formation” offerings (book groups, regular parent seminars, liturgical/yearly family events) and publicly sharing parent testimonials or case studies would further solidify Element 6 as CLASSICAL.

- Concrete steps:
  - Publish a Faculty Profile page or section detailing teacher qualifications, classical training, and examples of living-curriculum demonstrations in classrooms.
  - Create a public-facing “Faculty as Living Curriculum” feature demonstrating how teachers model virtue, pedagogy, and mastery of classical content in practice.
  - Expand parent-formation programming with recurring book studies, lectures, and home-education partnerships, and publish a faculty-led calendar of these events.

- These needle-mover steps are optional since five of the six categories already land at CLASSICAL, and only Element 5 requires strengthening to reach uniform CLASSICAL consistency. No major reconfiguration is required beyond explicit articulation of faculty practices and living-curriculum language.
