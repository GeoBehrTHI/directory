## Executive Summary

Virginia Classical Academy (VCA) in Virginia Beach (ZIP 23454) presents a robust classical Christian program anchored in explicit theological foundations and a clearly articulated classical pedagogy. Public materials show a strong commitment to biblical authority, the centrality of Christ, and a covenantal community, alongside a multi-year K4–12 program organized around the grammar–logic–rhetoric (Trivium) progression. The school emphasizes formation and wisdom within a liberal-arts framework, and it integrates parental partnership as a central feature of its model. Concrete signs of classical practice include graded stages (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), a cross-grade emphasis on memory, recitation, and chant in the lower grades, a Socratic/slam-dunk ethos at the Logic/Rhetoric stages, and a stated aim to cultivate lifelong learners through a curriculum built on foundational liberal arts. The school also actively involves parents (chief educators in its model), offers family resources, and provides a hybrid half-day model that aligns with covenantal community principles.

Category-by-category snapshot (as analyzed against the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric) indicates a consistent CLASSICAL orientation across all six domains, with strong religious grounding, formation-focused aims, a clearly described Trivium-centered curriculum, a pedagogy that blends contemplative/restful memory work with Socratic and rhetorical practice, a faculty culture implied as mentors and living curriculum, and explicit parental involvement as co-educators and partners in formation.

Key site evidence and alignment with the classical rubric include:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: explicit Christian formation, Gospel-centered foundation, and a Statement of Faith anchored in biblical authority (We Believe the Bible to be the inerrant Word of God) and covenantal community framing.
- School Purpose and Goal: mission framed as formation and lifelong learning for God’s glory, with a liberal-arts vision and a Christian telos for truth and knowledge.
- Curriculum: organized around the Grammar–Logic–Rhetoric sequence; explicit lower/upper school divisions; use of classical authors, primary sources, and liberal-arts orientation; references to seven liberal arts (via the classical education framing).
- Pedagogy: a blend of memory-based, chant/recitation in the Grammar Stage, and more dialogic, rhetorical/argument-focused work in the Logic and Rhetoric Stages; explicit mention of upper-school rhetoric and oratory; contemplative/restful elements present in the pedagogy.
- Faculty: evidence of staff presence, professional recruitment (Staff section), and a culture aligned with living curriculum and classical formation (implicit).
- Parents & Community: explicit statement that parents are chief educators in a hybrid model; partnerships with families, and resources for parent formation (Family Resources, books like The Lost Tools of Learning, etc.).

Overall assessment: CLASSICAL

Note: The school’s public materials show a consistent classical Christian orientation—explicit theology, a clear Trivium-based curriculum, a pedagogy that blends memory work with Socratic dialogue, and a strong emphasis on parental partnership. While some categories show more explicit wording than others (e.g., explicit “Great Books” language appears in classical education literature, and is present in the school’s own dialogue about classical education), the public materials strongly align with the Six Elements framework.
