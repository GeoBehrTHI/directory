## Executive Summary

Lexington Latin School (LLS) presents a robust classical Christian program centered on formation, virtue, and the classical liberal arts. Public materials consistently frame education as formation in truth, goodness, and beauty, rooted in a Christian worldview and the Great Tradition. The school foregrounds Latin, Great Books/Great Conversation through a curriculum that emphasizes the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Sacred Scripture, and a life of virtue. Public materials also highlight a strong parental partnership, a House-based upper-school culture, and a disciplined traditional classroom ethos.

Overall, the school earns a CLASSICAL rating across most criteria, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) showing only partial alignment where explicit Socratic/seminar-based practices are not as plainly documented as other elements. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Evidence emphasizes a Christian, Gospel-guided education, liturgical/worship implications, and the goal of forming Christian character and wisdom. 
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Emphasis on formation, virtue, and wisdom; education as a human formation aimed at truth/beauty rather than job training. 
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Explicit focus on Latin, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books/Great Conversation, and a liberal-arts orientation. 
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Recitation/memory and contemplative/restful elements are documented for lower grades; upper grades show rhetoric and classical study, but explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy is not consistently evidenced across the high school years. 
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. Public materials describe a traditional, teacher-led model with mentors and a “portrait” of teachers and a culture of virtue; named administrators and faculty indicate a strong living-lesson approach. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong emphasis on partnership with parents, parental formation resources, and open access to visits/gatherings.

Aggregate Assessment

What Would Move the Needle
- Element 4 (Pedagogy) could move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL with targeted enhancements:
  - Implement explicit, documented Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy at the upper-school level, with publicly available exemplars of Socratic discussions and sample Great-Books seminar prompts.
  - Expand publicly accessible descriptions of scholé days, contemplative pauses, and memory-work integration across all grades, with concrete examples beyond lower-school recitation.
  - Publish a formal “Pedagogy Playbook” showing how Great Books, Trivium-based content, and rhetoric are integrated in classroom practice, including teacher training materials.
  - Provide explicit, public articulation of a systematic, high-school-level Socratic program (e.g., Paideia-style seminars) and how it connects with the Great Conversation in texts like Shakespeare, Greek tragedy, and canonical works.

- No other category requires major needle-moving changes given the strong alignment across religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose/goal, curriculum, faculty, and parent/community.
