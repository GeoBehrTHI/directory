# ABSTRACT

Ignite Classical School (Shreveport, LA, 71108) presents a CLASSICAL orientation with strong religious/theological framing (Christ-centered, Word of God) and a robust faculty/community model (small classes, long-term teacher relationships, living-curriculum emphasis) as well as explicit family partnership (Parent Covenant). The overall public materials convey a classical posture, with substantial evidence for formation and character emphasis, though the explicit Great Books/Great Conversation framework and some classic pedagogical practices (Socratic seminars, Scholé, memorization culture) are not consistently documented in the accessible public materials. Consequently, the aggregate assessment trends toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear CLASSICAL strengths in Faculty and Parents/Community and solid but incomplete alignment in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy.

Key textual anchors from the work

- Public materials identify Ignite as Christ-centered and Christian (Word of God) with a non-denominational Christian identity and theology-grounded framing.
- The public-facing faculty narrative highlights 3-Year Teacher Relationships and a living-curriculum approach (faculty as core) as central to the school’s identity.
- Parent involvement is foregrounded via a Parent Covenant and family-partnering language.
- Curriculum is described as classical in nature, with Elemental Classical Science and other classical resources, but lacks consistently explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language in the public copy.
- Pedagogy is framed as classical in nature and multigrade, with emphasis on relationships and formation, though explicit Socratic discussion and scholé practices are not consistently documented.

Course-corrective notes for a MORE distinctly CLASSICAL profile

- Explicitly publish the Great Books/Great Conversation plan, including recommended titles and discussion formats by grade level.
- Clarify and publish a formal statement of the transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty) and how these are cultivated across the curriculum (formation of affections, moral imagination, and contemplation).
- Document specific pedagogical practices (Socratic seminars, memory/recitation routines, rhetoric training) with examples from K–12 and a timetable for rollout.
- Expand liturgical/community practice language (chapel, worship rhythm, service) to connect daily classroom life with sacred traditions, if appropriate to the school’s identity.
