## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of the Most Holy Eucharist (CAMHE), located in Yuba City, CA, presents a robust classic-Catholic program that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric. Public materials on CAMHE’s site foreground a Catholic, classical formation anchored in the liberal arts, Great Books, Socratic pedagogy, and the moral formation of students through prayer, liturgy, and communal life. The school repeatedly emphasizes intellectual formation in tandem with spiritual and moral formation, while situating its curriculum within the Chesterton Schools Network and Catholic tradition. This combination signals a CLASSICAL orientation across the rubric’s six categories. Additionally, the school’s explicit framing around a triad of Pillars (Intellect, Character, Spirituality), a strong emphasis on Great Books and the Socratic Method, and visible parent/community engagement through the Chesterton Schools Network further anchors CAMHE in the classical tradition.

Key sources from CAMHE’s public materials repeatedly reference Catholic identity, Great Books / Socratic method, formation of character, and partnership with a parent-driven network. For example, CAMHE positions itself as “a joyfully Catholic High School in the classical tradition,” highlights a Humanities program braided across history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages with a Socratic method, and catalogs its network’s emphasis on parent-led Catholic education. These signals strongly reflect transcendental moral realism, formation, and an integrative liberal-arts curriculum. The public materials also emphasize ritual and communal practices (Mass, prayer life, liturgical cadence) and a culture of communal formation, which correspond closely to the positive indicators in the classical-school lexicon and rubric.

Citations and evidence drawn from CAMHE’s site and communications are included throughout to ground the analysis.

- Evidence of Catholic identity and classical framing: the site describes CAMHE as a “joyfully Catholic High School in the classical tradition” in Yuba City, CA. It also notes mass attendance and a distinctly Catholic culture, and references GK Chesterton as patron, along with a Chesterton Schools Network connection.
- Evidence of Great Books, Socratic method, and integrated humanities curriculum: the Humanities Program braids history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages using a Socratic Method, with a clear Great Books/Great Conversation orientation.
- Evidence of formation-centric aims (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) and the emphasis on virtue and Catholic formation: the “Three Pillars” framework and statements about forming joyful leaders within a Catholic context.
- Evidence of community/parent-network engagement: the Chesterton Schools Network aims to inspire and encourage parent-led Catholic schools nationwide, highlighting a model of parent partnership rather than a purely student-directed approach.

Overall, CAMHE’s public materials demonstrate a consistent CLASSICAL orientation across the rubric’s six categories, anchored in Catholic faith, Great Books / Socratic pedagogy, formation, and community partnership.
