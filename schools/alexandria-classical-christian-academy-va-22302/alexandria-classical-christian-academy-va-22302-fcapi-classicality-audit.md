## Executive Summary

Alexandria Classical Christian Academy (ACCA) VA 22302 presents a robust classical Christian orientation across all six rubric categories. Public-facing materials consistently frame ACCA as a Christ-centered, formation-first school that embraces the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; etc.), a Great-Books/Humanities approach to history and literature, Socratic (and occasionally dialectic) pedagogy in upper grades, and a strong faculty-student/parent-partnership culture. Multiple pages articulate liturgical or liturgy-adjacent practices, explicit theology, and a stated aim to form virtuous, thoughtful citizens rather than merely prepare students for college or career. Evidence from ACCA’s site includes the school’s explicit formation-centric mission, a clear emphasis on Great Books and the Trivium, and declared commitments to parent partnership and a living, relational faculty culture. These signals align closely with the classical education rubric and support a final assessment of CLASSICAL across all six categories.

## Executive Summary — Category-by-Category Assessment

### Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- ACCA presents a Christ-centered, non-denominational Christian orientation with explicit reference to Scripture and faith formation. The site emphasizes that education is “anchored in Christ” and rooted in historic Christian faith, with teachers who revere Scripture and a student culture formed around Christian worship and formation. 
- Public materials note liturgical practices and reverence in worship, including references to worship in the church context and a formal Statement of Faith, signaling liturgical and communal dimensions to education. 
- The mission/values framing includes the belief that Holy Scripture is central and that education is enacted within a Christian worldview, reinforcing theological grounding and a sacramental/metaphysical frame of truth, goodness, and beauty. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: truth, goodness, beauty (themes echoed in formation and Great-Books framing), theology, liturgy, worship, reverence, wonder, sacred music, chant, creeds, church-year worship. Evidence of liturgical year, reverence in worship, and a stated theological foundation appears across ACCA materials.   
- Negative keywords present: None clearly evidenced in ACCA materials (no explicit emphasis on relativism, secularism, or value-neutral ethics in the public-facing content).

Why this rating
- ACCA’s public materials consistently foreground a Christian theological frame, liturgical practices, and a communal worship ethos, aligning with the Positive Keyword indicators for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations. The absence of secular or relativist signals reinforces a CLASSICAL rating in this category.

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- The Great-Conversation, transcendent aims, and telos vocabulary appear in their classical framing (i.e., aiming at truth, goodness, beauty; formation of character; pursuit of wisdom). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating
- The school explicitly defines its goal as formation and the cultivation of virtue and wisdom, with a teleological orientation toward Truth, Goodness, and Beauty. This strongly matches the Positive keyword indicators for School Purpose and Goal, supporting CLASSICAL.

### Category 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- ACCA’s academics emphasize the seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; with reference to the Quadrivium), and the use of Latin and logic in middle and high school (i.e., grammar in elementary; logic in middle school; rhetoric upper levels). This is a direct alignment with Classical education’s core curriculum structure.  
- The curriculum includes Great Books / primary sources, living history readings, and a contrastive approach to history (history taught as living story rather than social studies). This supports the Great-Books/Great-Conversation aspect of classical education. 
- The materials explicitly describe the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as central to the pedagogy, and indicate a developmentally staged approach to content. 
- A preference for memorization, chant, and liturgical elements in the grammar stage (lower school) demonstrates early engagement with memory and wonder in curriculum delivery. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), Great Books, primary sources, Latin, history as living story, study of literature, classical languages, dialectic, memorization/chanting, interpolation of sacred/written texts, and integration of arts and sciences.
- Negative keywords present: The explicit phrase “We teach history in lieu of social studies” appears, which the rubric treats as a contextual positive (refuting social-studies framing) but is listed in the lexicon as a potential negative signal if it signals a substitution that undermines historical study in a modern sense. In ACCA’s context, this aligns with classic history-focused pedagogy, not a negative trend per se. Overall, the content supports classical curricular structure.

Why this rating
- ACCA’s curriculum statements consistently align with the classical curriculum lexicon: liberal arts focus, Great Books, Latin/Logic/Rhetoric, history-as-living-narrative, and a developmentally staged Trivium/Quadrivium. This supports CLASSICAL.

### Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower-school pedagogy includes singing/chanting and memory work as a central feature, aiming to delight and deepen memory in a restful, formative way. This matches scholé and contemplative/remembering pedagogy for early years. 
- Upper-school pedagogy includes Socratic discussion, seminars, or debates to develop logic and rhetorical skills, as well as opportunities to train in rhetoric and public speaking. 
- The Seam between lower and upper grades shows an intentional transition from memory-based, reverent, and contemplative practices to dialectic, rhetorical, and seminar-based methods, consistent with classical pedagogy. 
- The school’s public materials describe the trivium and quadrivium as organizing pedagogical principles, including a developmental stage approach to learning (grammar in elementary, logic in middle, rhetoric in upper grades). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic seminar, dialectic, disputation, narration, recitation, chanting, memory work, progymnasmata, rest/Scholé, seminar, Paideia, rhetoric exercises, speech, declamation, oratory, debate, liturgical integration, and upper-school rhetorical exercises.
- Negative keywords present: The lexicon identifies experiential learning-focused, project-based, and constructivist approaches as negative; ACCA’s materials emphasize memory/chanting and Socratic/seminal discourse rather than purely project-based or student-directed constructs, aligning with classical pedagogy rather than constructivist extremes. No explicit project-based-only language is foregrounded.

Why this rating
- ACCA’s teaching methods show the core classical pedagogy: contemplative, memory-based activities in the grammar stage, paired with Socratic seminars and rhetorical training in the logic/rhetoric stages. This aligns with CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For a CLASSICAL rating, no major needle-moving changes are required. If desired, minor enhancements could further strengthen the Category 3 and Category 4 signals by:
  - Providing explicit, public-facing, grade-wide Great-Books reading lists and a formal, ongoing “Great Conversation” program across lower and middle grades, to more visibly map Great-Books to grade-level outcomes.
  - Increasing explicit, documented cross-subject integration demonstrations (e.g., sample interdisciplinary units showing how history, literature, and science converge across a term) to reinforce the unity of truth across disciplines.

- These are optional enhancements and not prerequisites for maintaining CLASSICAL status.
