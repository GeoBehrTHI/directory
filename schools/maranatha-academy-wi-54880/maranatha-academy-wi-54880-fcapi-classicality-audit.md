## Executive Summary

Maranatha Academy Wisconsin 54880 presents a strongly classical orientation anchored in Christian, specifically Christ-centered, formation. The public-facing materials emphasize a classical framework (The Trivium: Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and a discipleship-oriented pedagogy within a private Christian school context. Public content frequently foregrounds formation of character, virtue, and a Christian worldview, with stated aims to prepare students for life in home, church, and community rather than merely for college or the job market. Taken together, the school rates CLASSICAL across five of the six classical criteria, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) appearing SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL as the pedagogy is clearly traditional and dialogic in some aspects but does not consistently demonstrate explicit Socratic/seminar-style practice or Scholé/restful learning in the publicly available texts. The aggregate profile for Maranatha Academy is CLASSICAL.

Key evidence driving this assessment includes explicit references to Christ-centered education, a discipleship approach to learning, and a classical Trivium-centered curriculum (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) in publicly available pages. The Dorothy Sayers quotation used by the school further signals alignment with the transcendental aims of classical education (truth, goodness, beauty) and a formation emphasis beyond information transfer. The site also highlights a low student-to-teacher ratio, dedicated faculty, and parental partnership, which support the six-element rubric. The most needle-moving opportunity to push toward a fuller CLASSICAL classification would be to articulate and expand explicit Great Books engagement and a more explicit, text-based Socratic/Scholé pedagogy within the upper grades, as well as liturgical/community practices in a way that aligns with the category-specific indicators.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 4 (Pedagogy), explicitly publish and highlight classroom practices that align with classic pedagogy signals: Socratic dialogue/seminars, Scholé/restful days, memory work/recitation, and opportunities for students to practice or deliver speeches (rhetoric). Publicly describe a structured plan for how these classical methods are implemented across grade levels (K–12) and include sample units or lesson sketches.
- For Element 3 (Curriculum), provide explicit public-facing Great Books and Great Conversation components, including representative authors and works across key cycles, to strengthen the Great Books signal and connect curriculum to the broader classical canon.

- For Element 2 (Purpose and Goal), maintain and further publicize formation goals with explicit, measurable statements about moral and intellectual formation, including a clear articulation of telos (the true, the good, the beautiful) in the school’s mission language.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community), deepen and publicize parent-formation programs (book studies, lectures, gatherings) with published schedules and outcomes to further reinforce parents as co-teachers in the classical model.
