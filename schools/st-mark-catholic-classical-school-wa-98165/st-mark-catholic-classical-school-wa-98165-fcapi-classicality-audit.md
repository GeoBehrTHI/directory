## Executive Summary

St. Mark Catholic Classical School (Shoreline, WA, 98165) presents a robust Catholic classical articulation in its public materials. The school foregrounds a Catholic identity, a Trivium-based curriculum, a history-grounded and integrated approach, liturgical and communal practices, and a strong emphasis on character formation and virtue. Public materials consistently connect education with formation of the whole person—spiritual, intellectual, emotional, and physical—while promising parental partnership as co-educators and a living-teacher model of virtue. Taken together, these signals align strongly with classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements rubric. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL across the six categories, with particularly strong alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. Pedagogy shows clear classical scaffolding (Trivium orientation) but includes Montessori elements in the early years, which while not strictly classical, do not derail the overall classical orientation.

Key notes drawn from the public materials:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are well represented through explicit Catholic identity, liturgical practice (Mass, morning prayer, chaplets), and theological framing (Spirit of Truth, Theology of the Body, Virtues in Practice). Evidence: Catholic curriculum and liturgical life are described in multiple pages, including mission/philosophy and religion curriculum. 
- Purpose and Goal centers on formation, virtue, wisdom, and a view of education as formation rather than purely information. 
- Curriculum explicitly identifies the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integrated/interdisciplinary study, and historical/theological scaffolding as backbone elements. Great Books as a formal program isn’t spelled out with that exact label on every page, but the emphasis on “great thinkers” and a Great Conversation-oriented ethos appears in public materials. 
- Pedagogy shows classical scaffolding through grammar/logic/rhetoric and a mix of contemplative/restful elements (Scholé language appears in foundational texts the school and associated classical education literature emphasize). The site also evidences lower- and upper-school activities (e.g., singing, discussions in later years as part of a classical approach). Public materials show Montessori in preschool, which is not strictly classical but does not negate a classical core. 
- Faculty is presented as a living curriculum, with teacher modeling of virtue and a team of educators who guide and mentor students. 
- Parents & Community are foregrounded as co-educators and partners in formation; materials emphasize active parental involvement, teacher-parent collaboration, and opportunities for families to engage through the website and in-person visits.
