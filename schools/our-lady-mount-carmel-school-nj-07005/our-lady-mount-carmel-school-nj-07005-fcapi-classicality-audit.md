## Executive Summary

Our Lady of Mount Carmel School (NJ 07005 Boonton) presents a strong Catholic, virtue-centered educational identity with a clearly articulated sacramental life and Catholic formation. Public-facing materials emphasize faith as foundational, a sacramental/ liturgical rhythm, service to others, and a commitment to forming virtuous citizens. At the same time, the school blends Montessori elements with a classical-inflected emphasis on literature, the liberal arts, and classic culture (e.g., Shakespeare and Great Books-like engagement). The result is a hybrid that leans toward classical orientation in spirit and aims, but remains anchored in Catholic formation and Montessori practice in structure. Based on the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with particularly strong CLASSICAL signals in religious formation, virtue-focused purpose, and community/faculty alignment, but with notable Montessori influences in curriculum and pedagogy that temper a fully classical profile.

Key strengths
- Strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: explicit Catholic identity, sacramental life, liturgical practices, and Catholic worldview integrated across grades.
- Purpose and Goal aligned to formation and virtue: emphasis on forming character and serving others, not solely workforce readiness.
- Faculty and Parent/Community engagement: visible living-curriculum teachers, parish/campus involvement, and family/community partnerships.

Key growth opportunities (the needle movers)
- Curriculum clarity toward the classical Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as an explicit, cross-grade framework, with explicit Great Books exposure at all levels beyond Shakespeare.
- Pedagogy that foregrounds deliberate classical methods (Socratic dialogue, seminars) in upper grades, with clearly stated expectations across K–12.
- Greater articulation of the liberal arts backbone (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Latin/Greek options) to strengthen the “classical” spine and reduce ambiguity with Montessori elements.

The following analysis provides category-by-category evidence, keyword analysis, and rationale, followed by concrete steps to move toward a fuller CLASSICAL designation.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Explicit statements emphasize virtue, human excellence, and moral purpose; there is a clear orientation away from mere job-preparation toward human excellence and moral imagination.

Positive keyword analysis

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school identifies itself as a Montessori Preschool & Classical Kindergarten–8th Grade Academy, signaling a blend of Montessori structure with classical aims (e.g., emphasis on the liberal arts sensibility and Great Books through Shakespeare, and engagement with “the best of our rich intellectual and artistic heritage”). 
- Curriculum description highlights five Montessori areas (Practical Life, Language, Math, Art, Sensorial) with integration of science and history, plus direct engagement with Shakespeare and the broader classical heritage in some materials. This shows a hybrid approach rather than a pure classical curriculum. 
- Public materials emphasize “the greatest literary, scientific, mathematical, and artistic ideas” and direct reading (e.g., Shakespeare) rather than a purely secondary or summarized treatment, which aligns with classical philosophy in spirit. 
- The presence of “Montessori curriculum” alongside “classical” framing in the site headings and materials indicates a two-track curriculum approach rather than a singular classical pathway. 

Positive keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: liberal arts framework (explicitly “classical” in branding), Shakespeare, Great Books/Great Conversation spirit (explicit Shakespeare), reading literature directly (not just about it), “classical” branding in the curriculum (OLMC Academy).
- Negative indicators: Montessori pedagogy and structure (Practical Life, Sensorial, Montessori methods) provide a non-classical curricular backbone that tempers the classical alignment. The explicit Trivium/Quadrivium language (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and explicit “Great Books” sequences beyond Shakespeare are not consistently foregrounded across all grade levels in the public materials.

Why this rating
- The curriculum is clearly hybrid: Montessori methods and a classical literacy/literary engagement exist side-by-side. While Shakespeare and classical literature are highlighted, and “great books” ideas appear, the explicit, uniform classical curriculum framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric across grades with a defined Great Conversation trajectory) is not consistently stated across the materials. This justifies a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating in this category.

Citations: Montessori-classical branding; five-area Montessori curriculum; Shakespeare emphasis; direct literature engagement. 

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The presence of “restful learning” and “scholé days,” contemplation, and wonder as part of pedagogy aligns with classical pedagogy’s emphasis on contemplative, memory-enhancing practices. These features show a clear classical influence in teaching style. 
- Younger students are described as singing, chanting, memory-focused recitation (lower school), which matches the classical pedagogy emphasis on memory work and ritualized learning often used in early education. 
- Upper grades reference Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training; however, explicit evidence of ongoing Socratic pedagogy across all high-school grades is less clearly demonstrated in the public materials, and the content often highlights a broader “group inquiry” approach. This yields a partial classical pedagogy signal. 
- There are explicit references to rhetoric and public speaking “upper school rhetoric theses, capstone orations,” indicating classic rhetorical training at least in upper grades. 

Positive keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: scholé, contemplation, wonder, memory work, narration/recitation, chanting, seminars, dialectic (Socratic) in some form (upper school projects), rhetoric, public speaking, and capstone orations.
- Negative indicators: The public materials do not consistently emphasize pure experiential or project-based pedagogy as the dominant mode; however, Montessori elements and a student-centered approach are present, which can dilute the pure classical pedagogy signal.

Why this rating
- Pedagogy shows a mix of classical-style practices (memory work, chanting, recitation, seminars, rhetoric in upper grades) with Montessori-influenced, student-centered, and experiential approaches. The evidence supports substantial classical pedagogical signals but not a uniformly explicit, all-grades-Socratic/seminar-centric approach. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is appropriate for Pedagogy.

Citations: Scholé and contemplative language; lower-school chanting/memory; upper-school seminars & rhetoric; overall student-centered language.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum clarity and transparency as a fully classical spine
 - Explicitly articulate the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as a cross-grade framework and connect it to daily activities, assessments, and reading lists.
 - Build a visible, year-by-year Great Books sequence beyond Shakespeare to demonstrate a continuous Great Conversation across grades (e.g., Homer, Plato, Augustine, Shakespeare, etc.), with corresponding discussion formats.
 - Ground the curriculum with clear declarations of classical subjects (Latin or classical languages) and a defined history/Western civilization arc, separate from Montessori pedagogy where feasible.

- Pedagogy enhancement toward classical methods
 - Expand explicit Socratic/seminar-based activities across middle and upper grades with documented rubrics, expectations, and outcomes to deepen the classical dialogue model.
 - Increase opportunities for public speaking and rhetorical exercises (continuous across grades, not only in upper grades) to reinforce rhetoric training as a core habit.

- Identity strengthening of the classical spine
 - Integrate language heritage (Latin/Greek options) and formal grammar/logic/rhetoric sequences as a unified scaffold rather than dispersed, ad-hoc references.
 - Provide explicit alignment between Great Books, Classical Liberal Arts, and classroom activities (memory work, narration, discussions) with tangible outcomes and standards.

- Documentation of classical signals in public materials
 - Ensure public pages reliably reflect the Trivium framework, Great Books trajectory, and explicit classical pedagogy (in addition to Montessori elements) to reduce ambiguity and strengthen the CLASSICAL signal in Curriculum and Pedagogy.

- Strengthen liturgical and metaphysical coherence
 - Maintain and deepen liturgical practices while highlighting how these connect to literary, historical, and moral formation across grades. Provide explicit mappings between liturgical life and curricular themes (truth, goodness, beauty) to reinforce transcendental aims.

Citations for needle-moving items
- Classroom Great Books evidence and Shakespeare emphasis; explicit textual references to classical approach; Montessori blend noted in curriculum. 
- Socratic/seminar potential and rhetoric emphasis in upper grades. 
- Parent/Community engagement practices.
