## Executive Summary

St Benedict Classical Academy (Ma 01760) presents a robust Catholic, classical education model with clear formation aims, a liberal-arts curriculum, and visible integration of traditional pedagogical practices. Public-facing materials emphasize a Catholic worldview, moral formation, and the cultivation of virtue alongside intellectual excellence. The school demonstrates multiple hallmarks of classical education—notably a Catholic theological foundation, a curriculum oriented to liberal arts with Latin language study, and a pedagogy that includes structured seminar-style work in at least the upper grades and traditional memory/recitation practices in some settings. The overall pattern across six categories most strongly supports CLASSICAL, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) showing some evidence of non-Socratic or mixed-method pedagogy that warrants the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation for that category. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key, text-supported insights:

- The school explicitly names Catholic identity and religious formation as core, including references to religious education, the Magisterium, and a culture rooted in Catholic tradition. This aligns with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category.
- Virtue formation, formation of character, and the aim of forming wise and virtuous students are central in mission-oriented language, supporting the School Purpose & Goal category.
- The curriculum features liberal-arts elements (Latin starting in Grade 3; a classical curriculum; National Latin Exam for middle schoolers) and a curricular emphasis described as “classical, Catholic education.” This provides strong evidence for Curriculum.
- Pedagogical practices include a named Middle School Seminar, indicative of seminar-style work, with possible upper-school rhetorics or debates implied by classic-pedagogical norms (though explicit Socratic dialogue in upper grades is not exhaustively documented on the site). This yields SOMEWHAT vs CLASSICAL nuance for Pedagogy.
- Faculty data (41 faculty members, 40% with advanced degrees; low student/faculty ratio) supports a model of model teachers and mentors, aligning with the Faculty category.
- Parental and community orientation is evident through materials describing partnership with families, parental formation content, and open campus visits, consistent with the Parents & Community category.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The Curriculum Overview page (Curriculum Overview – St. Benedict Classical Academy) discusses an interwoven, integrated curriculum across levels, with references to ancient civilizations through geography and virtue education. While Latin is explicit, there is less explicit, public mention of Great Books, the Great Conversation, or a comprehensive Trivium/Quadrivium structure in the public pages.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: Latin, classical education, liberal arts, grammar, logic, rhetoric (implied by “classical education”), National Latin Exam, interdisciplinary integration
- Negative keywords present: lack of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation, lack of explicit reference to Trivium/Quadrivium on public pages (beyond the general classical framing)

Quantitative estimate

- Positive keywords observed: 4

- The curriculum shows strong classical features (Latin, liberal-arts framing, classical education language). However, explicit mention of Great Books/Great Conversation and a complete Seven Liberal Arts articulation (Trivium/Quadrivium) is not clearly demonstrated across public pages, which places it in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Citations: Curriculum overview and Latin emphasis.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public materials show a Middle School Seminar, which signals seminar-style instruction and a move toward more dialogic, textual inquiry in at least some grades. This aligns with classical pedagogy in higher grades.
- There are references to rigorous intellectual engagement framed through a Catholic/liberal-arts lens, with emphasis on virtue and formation, which can support contemplative or Socratic elements in upper grades. The Headmaster’s message emphasizes engagement with the Great Works of art, music, and literature of the West, coupled with religious education and character formation.
- Lower-level practices such as singing and memory work (scholé-like memory and chant traditions) are implied through the classical lexicon and broader classical education lexicon; the site does not consistently publish explicit lower-school chant/memory routines, but there are signals of traditional memory-choral aspects in classical pedagogy.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: Socratic seminar, seminar, discourse, rhetoric, memory work, recitation, chanting, discipline, contemplation, scholé (conceptually present in classical framing)
- Negative keywords present: experiential learning focus, project-based learning emphasis, constructivist-only approaches (not evidenced on SBCA site; the presence of a seminar suggests more diálogo-based pedagogy rather than purely project-driven)

Quantitative estimate

- Positive keywords observed: 2–3 concrete items (e.g., Middle School Seminar; classical/Scholé-oriented framing)
- Negative keywords observed: 0 concrete signals in public materials (no explicit “constructionism” or “project-based only” statements found)

Why this rating

- Public-facing materials demonstrate some classical pedagogy (Seminar in middle school; classical framing). However, explicit mentions of a full Socratic-method, rhetoric training in upper grades, and widespread chanting/memory-recalling routines across all grades are not consistently documented. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is warranted.

Citations: Middle School Seminar and classical pedagogy framing.

What would move the needle

- Publish explicit, school-wide statements about Socratic seminars and oratorical training in upper grades; provide examples of upper-school seminars/debates, visible Great Conversation initiatives, and a formal plan for Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy on the site.
- Publicly document and showcase lower-school chanting/memory work routines as a hallmark of scholé and a restorative, contemplative aspect of daily routines.
- Include a formal articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation in curriculum pages with representative titles and units.

Citations: General pedagogy references in headmaster narrative and curriculum overview.
