## Executive Summary

Second Baptist School (SBS) demonstrates a clear Christian, discipleship-centered educational identity with strong religious foundations, a formation-oriented purpose, and a faculty culture that aligns with classical ideals in several respects. The school exhibits robust religious and communal practices (chapel, Bible instruction, prayer, biblical worldview, and Christ-centered mission) that strongly align with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category. It shows a formation-oriented purpose aligned with virtue-dynamic goals, though its explicit classical vocabulary (e.g., Great Books, Great Conversation, liberal arts structure, and explicit Trivium/Quadrivium curricula) is not evident in the public materials; this yields a Somewhat Classical assessment for the School Purpose and Goal category. The Curriculum category largely does not meet classical milestones (no explicit Great Books, no public evidence of Trivium/Quadrivium, etc.), which places SBS in the Not Classical category for that dimension. Pedagogy displays some classical-method elements (memory work, contemplative/restful practice, and liturgical/chapel rhythms) but lacks explicit widespread Socratic seminars or high-school-level rhetoric training across the board, resulting in Somewhat Classical for Pedagogy. The Faculty category shows teachers modeled as living exemplars and as the core of the school (consistent with classical patterns), supporting a Classical rating. Parents & Community reflect strong partnership with families, openness, and community formation, which is supportive of classical aims but does not consistently present a formal, “parents as co-teachers” framework at scale across departments, yielding a Somewhat Classical assessment.

Overall, SBS lands in the realm of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The dominant religious formation and community aspects pull certain categories toward CLASSICAL, but gaps in explicit Great Books-centric curriculum, Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy, and a universal “parents as co-teachers” framework keep the aggregate from reaching FULL CLASSICAL. The following category-by-category breakdown documents this nuanced assessment and provides concrete steps that would raise SBS toward full CLASSICAL status.

ABSTRACT
Second Baptist School presents a hybrid profile: a robust Christian formation program with strong religious and communal practices, coupled with a mission that emphasizes formation, virtue, and leadership. While SBS excels in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Faculty, and shows Somewhat Classical momentum in Pedagogy and Parents/Community, its public-facing curriculum does not yet demonstrate the full Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium structure required for a CLASSICAL label. The aggregate assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear opportunities to enhance Great Books engagement, Trivium-based pedagogy, and deeper parent-education alignment to reach CLASSICAL.

- Visual Arts and other disciplines frame God-centered aims (“God as Creator,” knowledge of God and self) and a biblical worldview across divisions. 
- Public mentions of “living biblically,” “capturing students to think critically, live biblically and lead courageously,” and a school-wide emphasis on faith-based formation appear across SBS pages.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly adopt and publicize a Great Books/Great Conversation component within core curriculum, including a school-wide or grade-structured reading list and discussions that align with classical canon and Western philosophical/historic intellectual tradition.
- Introduce and publicly document a formal Trivium-based pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across more grade levels, with explicit opportunities for students to practice logical argumentation, structured writing, and public speaking in both lower and upper grades.
- Elevate the explicit role of parents as co-teachers by providing structured, ongoing programs (parent seminars/book clubs, home-learning guides aligned with classical aims) and formal visitation/participation models in classrooms and school life.
- Ensure public materials consistently articulate the six-element classical framework (including Great Books, scholé/restful learning, and dialogic pedagogy) to strengthen the curriculum and pedagogy categories toward CLASSICAL.
