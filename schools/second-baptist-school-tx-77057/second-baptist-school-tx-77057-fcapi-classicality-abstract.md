## ABSTRACT

Second Baptist School presents a robust Christian formation program with a strong religious and communal life that anchors its identity. The Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category is CLASSICAL due to explicit Christ-centered mission, Bible instruction, weekly chapel, and a biblical worldview orientation. The School Purpose and Goal category is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, anchored by formation and virtue-oriented aims but lacking explicit classical vocabulary or teacher-facing language around Great Books/paideia. The Curriculum category is NOT CLASSICAL, as SBS public materials do not demonstrate Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or the traditional liberal-arts canon. Pedagogy shows SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL traits (memory work, contemplative rhythms, liturgical elements) but lacks widespread explicit Socratic seminars and full rhetoric-based pedagogy across the entire program. Faculty are CLASSICAL, with a living-curriculum approach and model-teacher leadership. Parents & Community are SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong partnership emphasis and community formation, but without a fully articulated “parents as co-teachers” framework across the school.

Overall assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Note: This evaluation synthesizes SBS public-facing materials with the classical rubric in the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School, incorporating explicit keyword matches from the classical lexicon. The conclusions align SBS’s religious formation and community strengths with classical aims while identifying specific curriculum and pedagogical gaps needing attention to reach full CLASSICAL status.
