## Executive Summary

Ascent Classical Academy of Grand Junction (ACAGJ) demonstrates strong classical alignment across most rubric categories, with notable strengths in mission/purpose, curriculum emphasis, faculty quality, and parent/community partnership. Public materials repeatedly emphasize a classical, content-rich liberal arts approach, including a focus on virtue formation, classical virtues, and engagement with Great Books and Western Civilization through a Hillsdale-supported framework. Pedagogical practices show a mix of Socratic/seminar-style inquiry in upper grades alongside traditional teacher-led instruction and memory-oriented elements in lower grades. Overall, the school presents as CLASSICAL, with two categories showing only SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL elements due to remaining gaps (notably explicit liturgical/theological grounding and fully rested/Scholé-informed pedagogy in all grades). The aggregated assessment across the six categories supports CLASSICAL with strong evidence in Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community, and more tempered signals in Religious Foundations and Pedagogy.

Key takeaways:
- Foundational aims center on virtue, moral character, and civic virtue within a content-rich Classical framework, reinforced by Hillsdale-supported resources and Great Books orientation. This strongly supports the CLASSICAL designation in most categories. Evidence: Mission/Vision statements, core virtues, Hillsdale-supported curricular philosophy, and Great Conversation references.   
- Curriculum evidence includes liberal-arts emphasis, Latin, European and American history, classical literature, and senior-thesis capstone components, indicating robust classical curricula.  
- Pedagogy shows a balance: upper-level Socratic/seminar/rhetoric emphasis; lower-level memory and drill elements; explicit statement of teacher-led instruction as a norm in some materials, which moderates to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in pedagogy.  
- Parents & Community are clearly positioned as partners and co-educators, with explicit calls for family involvement and accessibility to visits. This aligns strongly with classical-community expectations. 

 aggregates to a CLASSICAL overall classification.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Core Virtues include Courage, Moderation, Justice, Responsibility, Prudence, Friendship, and Wonder, signaling a framework centered on virtue formation and reverence for beauty and moral ideals. 
- The Hillsdale College aspiration and the Great Conversation framing suggest engagement with transcendent ideals (truth, goodness, beauty) through a classical canon, even if explicit theological language is not foregrounded in public materials. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present (from the school’s public materials): virtue, moral character, civic virtue, wonder, Great Conversation (and related Great Books ecosystem).  
- Negative keywords present: none identified in the public materials regarding this category (no explicit liturgical practices or theology references). 

Why this rating
- The school clearly emphasizes virtue and moral formation as central aims, with an explicit focus on truth/goodness/beauty via Great Books-based framing, but it does not foreground explicit theology, liturgical practices, or a formal theological grounding in its public-facing materials. This combination supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL categorization for this category rather than full CLASSICAL, given the absence of overt theological grounding or liturgical/community worship language.  

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- The stated purpose centers on forming virtue and wisdom and asserting the reality of truth, goodness, and beauty (as part of the classical aim). 
- The Great Conversation/Great Books orientation (via Hillsdale-supported program) signals engagement with canonical texts and enduring human questions, aligning with classical aims beyond mere information transfer. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: virtue, formation, wisdom, truth, goodness, beauty, Great Conversation, moral character, civic virtue.  
- Negative keywords present: none explicitly indicating a secular or vocational-only aim; no hints at a reductionist, information-only goal. 

Why this rating
- The school's stated objective centers on formation of character and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty through a classical framework, not simply workforce preparation. This aligns strongly with Classical and supports CLASSICAL in this category. 

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating
- The school publicly commits to a classical curriculum framework with grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books canonical study, Western Civ emphasis, and a Senior Thesis capstone. This aligns squarely with CLASSICAL in Curriculum.  

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Upper School indicates Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and use of logic; students are trained in rhetoric with opportunities to give speeches. This is a hallmark of classical pedagogy in higher grades. 
- Lower School reportedly engages in singing and chanting with regular review to deepen memory and delight, and a pedagogical emphasis on wonder (memory-based recall and delight in learning) is noted. This aligns with some Scholé-like, contemplative elements in early years but coexists with rigorous content. 
- Materials also emphasize teacher-led instruction and a traditional classroom approach, with a Hillsdale-supported framework that includes professional development and coaching for teachers.  

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic, seminars, debates, rhetoric, Senior Thesis (capstone), teacher-led instruction, classical pedagogy, memory/review, contemplation (implicit in “Scholé”-style discussion in higher grades).  
- Negative keywords present: some public materials emphasize a mix of traditional and non-Schélique elements (e.g., explicit mention of teacher-led instruction and lower-year singing) without a uniform rest-focused Scholé approach in all grades; no explicit heavy emphasis on full contemplative rest in all grades. This yields a partial presence of contemplative rest and slow pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The presence of Socratic/seminar methods in upper grades and rhetoric emphasis, coupled with strong teacher-led instruction and a public Hillsdale-supported framework, signals a classical pedagogy in spirit but with mixed lower-year practices (singing/memory). This is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, acknowledging strong classical elements in higher grades while noting less uniform implementation across all grades.
