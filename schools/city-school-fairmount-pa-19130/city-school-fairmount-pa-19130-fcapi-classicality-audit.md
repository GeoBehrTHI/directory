## Executive Summary

The public-facing materials repeatedly frame the school as a Christian institution and highlight religious life components (e.g., Bible instruction, prayer-oriented content) alongside a broad curriculum (including STEAM, arts, languages, and athletics). However, there is minimal explicit articulation of classical education hallmarks such as the Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy, scholé (restful contemplation), or a formal “living curriculum” approach. On balance, the public materials demonstrate a recognizable religious orientation and solid program offerings, but they do not consistently express the core six-element classical framework needed for a CLASSICAL rating in most categories.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school aimed to move toward a CLASSICAL orientation, concrete steps could include:
    - Introduce and foreground a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across grades, with explicit integration of primary classical texts and Great Books as foundational reading, coupled with a clear justification of how history, philosophy, and literature intersect.
    - Adopt formal scholé practices (restful learning days, contemplation, nature-based wonder) and clearly articulate contemplative pedagogy as a foundational element in lower and upper grades.
    - Implement Socratic seminars and structured seminars (Paideia-style) in upper grades, including public student oration, debates, and masterful rhetoric training aligned with a classical rhetoric sequence.
    - Elevate the faculty role as living curriculum with professional development focused on classical pedagogy, and present a clear portrait of the teacher as exemplar of virtue and intellectual leadership.
    - Develop explicit parent formation programs: classical book clubs, study groups, family liturgy guidelines, and open campus visits as a formal co-teaching model with the school, with regular community liturgical events and family participation in classical study.
    - Catalogue and emphasize a formal “Great Conversation” approach with a curated Great Books program (starting with selected classics and integrated discussions across disciplines).
    - Ensure public materials explicitly reference classical terms (truth, goodness, beauty; scholé; telos; virtue; liturgy; the Great Conversation) and demonstrate how the school's mission aligns with the classical framework.

Note: Because the aggregate classification is NOT CLASSICAL, the above “needle-moving” recommendations are provided as potential future enhancements rather than current requirements.
