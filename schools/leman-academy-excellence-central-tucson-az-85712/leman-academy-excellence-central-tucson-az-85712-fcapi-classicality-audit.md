## Executive Summary

Leman Academy Central Tucson presents as a clearly classical-leaning school with strong emphasis on virtue, character formation, and a public-charter, tuition-free model. Public materials describe a classical framework, close partnerships with families, and an intentionally small, community-oriented climate. The site explicitly foregrounds virtue, wisdom, and character, along with a foundation of timeless values, while also offering enrichment in the arts and languages (evident Latin instruction). However, explicit, text-level evidence of several core classical pedagogy elements—such as a stated triad of liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), regular Great Books / Great Conversation experiences, and liturgical or theological foundations—are not plainly visible in the public-facing materials. On balance, the school demonstrates a coherent classical orientation, but it does not consistently articulate all six elements at the depth expected for a full CLASSICAL rating. Overall, this evaluation places Leman Academy Central Tucson in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category.

Aggregate Ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale for aggregate: Across categories, Leman Academy Central Tucson demonstrates a coherent classical orientation (emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and character; Latin language presence; small-community feel; strong parent partnership). However, explicit articulation of several canonical classical pedagogy and curriculum elements—such as the explicit triad of liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), regular engagement with Great Books/Great Conversation, and liturgical/theological foundations—are not clearly documented in public materials. This combination yields a balanced, CLASSICAL-leaning but not fully CLASSICAL rating overall, i.e., SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly publish the full liberal-arts framing and Great Books components.
    - Add a public-facing statement confirming the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and, where appropriate, Quadrivium elements.
    - Include explicit references to Great Books / Great Conversation and a planned reading list across grades.
    - Clarify how the curriculum fosters wonder and integrated discovery with cross-curricular coherence.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Document and highlight explicit classical pedagogy practices in public materials.
    - Describe the use of Socratic dialogue, seminars, and debates in upper grades with examples or sample rubrics.
    - Outline how memory work, chant, or scholé (quiet study) appears in lower grades, with evidence of how these practices support deep memory and delight in learning.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Strengthen the “living curriculum” framing in public materials.
    - Publish a faculty-profile standard that enumerates teacher virtues, professional development expectations, and how teachers model virtue and wisdom.
    - Include testimonials or case studies that illustrate teachers acting as mentors and living exemplars of the curriculum.
- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): If appropriate and aligned with the school’s mission, clarify theological or liturgical dimensions.
    - If the school intends to integrate theology, clearly describe these aspects in public materials (e.g., liturgical rhythm, communal practices, or theological grounding) to align with the Positive Keyword framework.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and expand parent-education initiatives.
    - Add regular parent book studies, workshops, and opportunities for parents to participate in the classical formation process as co-teachers, with schedules and topics published.
