## Executive Summary

The Classical Christian Academy Bethel WV 25403 (TCCA), as represented in the school’s public materials, presents a classical Christian model that emphasizes formation, virtue, and a faith-integrated curriculum anchored by the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium alongside Great Books and a biblical worldview. The school explicitly frames its mission around formation of character, truth, goodness, and beauty, with Christ-centered instruction, biblical literacy, and a farm/experiential component that ties learning to creation and stewardship. These signals align strongly with classical education principles in several categories, particularly in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and School Purpose & Goal. Evidence shows a clear emphasis on virtue, truth, and beauty, a robust Christian theological framing, and a curriculum designed around liberal arts and Great Books rather than secular or vocational preoccupations. Some aspects—especially the Pedagogy and Faculty categories—are less explicit in the public materials, which yields a more nuanced assessment in those areas.

Aggregate classification based on the six-category rubric: CLASSICAL, with some categories strongly CLASSICAL (e.g., Religion/Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and School Purpose & Goal) and a few categories showing partial but substantial classical alignment (Pedagogy and Faculty). This assessment uses keyword signals from the classical rubric and public content from the school’s materials.

- Evidence highlights include explicit theological grounding and biblical literacy, a Trivium/Quadrivium framework, Great Books emphasis, and formation-focused language. See statements of faith, biblical literacy, and Trivium/Quadrivium references, as well as the “What Sets Us Apart” and “Curriculum” sections. 
- Positive classical indicators (truth, goodness, beauty; formation; Great Books; Trivium/Quadrivium; liturgical/communal elements) are present in multiple public materials. 
- A few areas (notably explicit pedagogy terms like Socratic seminars in the public pages) are less visible in the crawl, contributing to a cautious but favorable overall reading. Where explicit pedagogical terminology is absent, the school’s classical approach is still strongly implied by the presence of Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, and formation language.

What Would Move the Needle

- Pedagogy: If the school more explicitly documents and standardizes classical pedagogy practices (e.g., regular Socratic seminars, scholé days, memory-work routines, and chanting in appropriate lower/upper school contexts) in public materials, the Pedagogy category would reach CLASSICAL. Concrete examples: publish a pedagogy guide detailing how Trivium/Quadrivium are implemented daily, include sample freshmen/seminar formats, and outline memory-work sequences and chant-based practices. 
- Faculty: Develop and publish a formal “living curriculum” portrait of the teacher-student role, with faculty profiles and a documented professional-growth plan aligned with classical formation. This would strengthen Element 5 to CLASSICAL. 
- Curriculum: Maintain and strengthen the explicit Great Books program and ensure a publicly available scope-and-sequence showing the Great Conversation across grades, with regular shared reading and discussion protocols. This would reinforce Element 3 as CLASSICAL. 
- Community: Expand parent-formation programming (e.g., classical book clubs, parent-study series, and family liturgy resources) with public calendars and summaries to further anchor Element 6 as CLASSICAL.
