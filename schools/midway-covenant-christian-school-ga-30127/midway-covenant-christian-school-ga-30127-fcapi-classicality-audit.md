## Executive Summary

Midway Covenant Christian School (Powder Springs, GA 30127) demonstrates a strong alignment with classical Christian education as defined by the classical education lexicon and Six Essential Elements framework. Across the six categories, the school publicly emphasizes a Christian, covenantal, and classical approach, foregrounding the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, Latin, and a curriculum anchored in biblical truth and Western civilization. Evidence of liturgical and communal practices, a robust theology-centered mission, and a family-centric parental partnership further reinforce its classical orientation. All six categories trend toward CLASSICAL, with explicit references to virtue formation, true/good/beautiful philosophy, and a living faculty-student partnership. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Executive decisions are grounded in explicit textual evidence from the school’s public materials (home pages, mission statements, faith statements, curricula pages, and program descriptions) and aligned with the Positive/Negative Keyword indicators from the Six Essentials Elements lexicon.

- Evidence sources include: the school’s public materials describing classical Christian aims, covenantal education, and a Trivium-inspired curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), along with references to Great Books, Western history, Latin, and the Bible as foundational to all subjects. ## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public statements frame the school as a classical Christian institution rooted in God’s Word and biblical standards. The home page and “A Letter From Our Head of School” describe an environment in which “Christ, the giver of all wisdom and knowledge, is pre-eminent,” and where the curriculum, instruction in virtue, and communication are rooted in biblical standards.  
- The school’s faith documentation (Our Faith, Westminster Confession of Faith) explicitly anchors truth claims in a Reformed, confessional framework (Sovereignty of God, Inerrancy of Scripture, The Triune God). 
- The vision emphasizes Holy Scripture as foundational for all learning and character formation; prayers and theological reflection permeate daily life, while biblical truths guide discipline and curriculum. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords observed: truth/true, virtue, theology/scripture, creed, liturgy/prayer (public prayer), holy, God’s word, providence, imago Dei (conceptual alignment via biblical anthropology), worship/chapel (liturgical concepts).
- Negative keywords: none clearly present in the public materials (no explicit relativism, secular humanism, or values-clarification signals in the school’s own mission statements).

Why this rating
- The materials demonstrate transcendental moral realism via a theologically grounded curriculum, explicit theology and creedal frameworks, liturgical prayer and communal worship practices, and a covenantal, biblically anchored formation of character. This aligns strongly with CLASSICAL in religious/metaphysical foundations as defined by the Six Essentials.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum is clearly described as liberal arts-oriented with grammar, logic, and rhetoric as core elements (the Trivium). The school highlights Great Books and primary sources, Latin, and the study of Western civilization, alongside history instructed in a continuous, integrated fashion.

Bible is foundational to all subjects, with dedicated Bible study in elementary and middle school.  
- The elementary/middle-school articulation includes explicit emphasis on Latin, classical literature, and Western civilization as core components. The school references the Great Books and a connected, interwoven curriculum (history, literature, art) with a strong biblical integration. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords observed: liberal arts, trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, primary sources, Shakespeare, Iliad, Odyssey, Beowulf, Western civilization, Latin, history/chronology, Bible as foundational to all subjects, catechesis, and recitation/narration in line with classical pedagogy.
- Negative keywords: the lexicon notes potential negative signals such as “social studies terminology replacing history” or “project-based learning dominance.” The MCCS materials emphasize history as history and Western civ, not replacing it with social studies phrasing, and there is no dominant project-based framing seen in the excerpts.

Why this rating
- The explicit inclusion of the Trivium, Great Books (and Great Conversation), Latin, and Western civilization history, along with a biblically integrated curriculum, strongly aligns with CLASSICAL curricular expectations.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogical aims reflect the classical progression and the literature indicates a tri-level approach: grammar stages (reading, phonics, memory work, narration), logic stage (critical thinking, analysis, reasoning), and rhetoric stage (oral expression, argument, and writing in later years). The middle-school description emphasizes logic and inter-relatedness of subjects, suggesting a maturation of reasoning and argumentation. 
- Explicit examples of pedagogy include Great Books exposure during the middle school years, and a focus on the inter-relatedness of subjects through logic and structured reading. The grammar stage emphasizes memorization and recitation, with Bible memorization and catechesis integrated. 
- Bible and classical education are described as foundational to all subjects, with the Bible taught in its own course and integrated across disciplines, signaling a thoughtful, restive, contemplative approach in line with scholé principles. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords observed: Socratic seminar (mentioned in the lexicon as an ideal pedagogy, though explicit mention here is more indirect), narration, recitation, chanting, memory work, liturgical integration, seminars, and oratory/debate in upper levels, restfulness/scholé, contemplation, wonder.
- Negative keywords: explicit “experiential learning focus” and “project-based learning” are listed as negatives in the lexicon; MCCS materials do not emphasize a dominant project-based or experiential-only pedagogy, instead highlighting traditional classical methods.

Why this rating
- The materials describe the classical progression (grammar/logic/rhetoric), use of Great Books and primary sources, and memory-based pedagogy in lower grades, along with logic-focused inquiry in middle/high school. While explicit “Socratic seminars” are not repeatedly titled, the overall pedagogical approach remains classical, with careful notes on memory work, recitation, and careful reasoning. This supports a CLASSICAL rating.
