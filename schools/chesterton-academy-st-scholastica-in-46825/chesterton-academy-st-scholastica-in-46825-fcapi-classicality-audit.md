## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Scholastica (Fort Wayne, IN, 46825) presents a pronounced classical-Catholic model. The public materials emphasize a Catholic identity, liturgical life (Daily Mass, sacred rhythm), and a deliberately integrated liberal-arts curriculum that foregrounds the Trivium and Quadrivium, Great Books, and Socratic pedagogy. The school also highlights formation in virtue, a culture of life, and robust parental involvement as core elements of its mission. Taken together, these signals align strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and place the school firmly in the CLASSICAL category across all six rubric domains.

Key takeaways:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit and integral (Catholic tradition, Incarnation-focused curriculum, liturgical life, theology-infused framing) and supported by daily worship and Catholic identity.
- The school’s purpose centers on formation, virtue, wisdom, and a transcendent sense of truth/goodness/beauty, not merely college/career preparation.
- The curriculum is declared liberal-arts–rich (grammar/logic/rhetoric, history as a throughline, Great Books references, and integration with philosophy/theology).
- Pedagogy combines contemplative rest with Socratic inquiry and memory-work, with evidence of upper-school seminars and oratorical opportunities; some traditional elements appear alongside a modern, technology-minimized approach.
- Faculty and leadership roles are clearly defined (Head of School, Executive Director, Board), with a living-curriculum flavor implied by faculty leadership and stewardship signals.
- Parent partnership and community involvement are foregrounded (shadow days, admissions events, open-campus orientation), with explicit mentions of family partnership and shared formation.
