## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Therese (Campbell Hall, NY 10916) presents a clear and active classical-education profile anchored in Catholic faith, liberal-arts breadth, and a pedagogy that centers on formation of character and intellect. Public-facing materials emphasize a Catholic inculturation, the cultivation of virtue, and a three-pillar model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) that threads together curriculum, pedagogy, and community life. The school describes itself as offering a classical, integrated curriculum with a liberal-arts orientation, a strong focus on truth, goodness, and beauty, and a commitment to Socratic discussion and rhetoric in higher grades. Evidence from the site also highlights a governance and staffing model aligned with classical education norms (e.g., a Headmistress and instructors in Latin, literature, logic, rhetoric). Community signals include parent-partnership through networks and opportunities to engage families around classical-education topics and school events. Overall, the Chesterton Academy of St. Therese maps closely to a CLASSICAL classification across all six rubric categories.

Key evidentiary signals include: the inculturated Catholic identity (mass participation, Catholic voices, and the three-pillar model), the explicit framing of a liberal-arts, integrated curriculum with emphasis on Western thought and the Great Conversation, the presence of Socratic seminars and rhetoric training, and the stated aim to form virtuous, thinking students rather than merely prepare for college or career. The school also provides visible faculty roles (e.g., Headmistress and subject-area instructors) and community-facing elements (open houses, shadow days, mass presence), which align with the classical tradition’s living-lesson and partnership with families.
