# Classicality Audit: Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School (Waco, TX)

Website used for audit: https://bishopreicher.com

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School (BLR) presents a distinctly Catholic identity with robust liturgical life (weekly Mass, Eucharist, regular prayer) and explicit virtue formation grounded in St. Thomas Aquinas. Curriculum pages indicate classical elements such as “classical literature,” Latin, integrated humanities, and a four-year theology sequence. However, the site does not explicitly commit to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), nor does it advertise classical pedagogies like Socratic seminar, chanting/recitation, or rhetoric orations. Several pages foreground contemporary college-prep outcomes, AP coursework, and hands-on STEM/technology, which dilute a fully classical orientation. In sum, BLR blends Catholic college-prep with some classical features.

Key strengths
- Liturgical life and theological integration (Mass, Eucharist, prayer; catechesis) are strong and prominent, aligning closely with classical Catholic foundations   .
- Virtue formation is explicit and Aquinas-based; “saints and scholars” purpose appears repeatedly  .
- Curriculum shows “classical literature,” Latin, great authors (Homer, Sophocles, Shakespeare), and a four-year theology sequence, with an “interconnected humanities” emphasis   .

Primary gaps
- No public articulation of the Trivium/Quadrivium, nor visible Socratic/seminar/recitation/chant practices.
- Strong emphasis on robotics, Google Classroom, “21st century” tech skills, PSAT/AP framing—language that maps to negative indicators in the classical lexicon when it eclipses formation and wonder   .

Six Elements benchmark used
- Positive/negative keyword indicators and category definitions taken from the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” keyword framework and the supporting lexicon (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty; Socratic, scholé, chanting; vs. 21st‑century skills, project‑based dominance, college-and-career emphasis)    .

## Aggregate Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Strong marks in Category 1 (Religious/Metaphysical) and solid classical signals in Category 3 (Curriculum) are offset by the absence of explicit classical pedagogy (Category 4), a modest/implicit faculty formation posture (Category 5), and mixed goal‑framing with modern prep language (Category 2). Parent partnership is present but could be further developed (Category 6). Overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear potential to grow into CLASSICAL by clarifying pedagogy and the Trivium and foregrounding formation over outcomes in public materials.

---

## What would move the needle
- Declare the Trivium/Quadrivium publicly: add pages that name grammar, logic, and rhetoric as the “tools of learning,” and show where they live in the curriculum K‑12 (logic course in grades 7–9; rhetoric with speeches and orations in high school)  .
- Publish pedagogy: state use of Socratic seminar, disputation, narration/recitation, chanting/memory (especially K–6), and capstone rhetoric orations in grades 11–12  .
- Make “restful learning” and wonder explicit schoolwide (not only preschool): reference scholé, contemplation, depth over breadth (multum non multa) in academics pages and schedules .
- Reframe tech/STEM pages: cast robotics/technology as handmaids to liberal learning and rhetoric (e.g., demonstrations that “delight and instruct”) rather than primarily 21st‑century skill acquisition; reduce project‑based jargon .
- Faculty formation: post a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Faculty of Friends” note; mention regular faculty colloquia/seminars in Great Books/classical pedagogy .
- Parent formation: add parent book studies or “What is Classical Education?” sessions; share reading lists and recorded talks on TGB, Trivium, scholé, and virtue cultivation .
