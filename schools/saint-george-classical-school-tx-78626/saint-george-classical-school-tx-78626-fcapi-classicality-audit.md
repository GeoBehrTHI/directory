## Executive Summary

Saint George Classical School (TX 78626) presents a distinctly Classical Christian program framed around the liberal arts, Great Books, and a catechetical, covenantal approach to family involvement. Public-facing materials emphasize a triple emphasis on theology/scripture-guided learning, formation of virtue and wisdom, and an integrated curriculum anchored in the seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) and the Great Conversation. The school also frames its pedagogy around a collaborative model that blends on-campus instruction with home-based study under parental co-teachers, while maintaining a strong emphasis on liturgy, worship, and communal practices. Taken together, the publicly visible materials indicate a robust Classical orientation with a clear Christian foundation and a family-centered, covenantal partnership with parents.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Key drivers supporting CLASSICAL status

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: guiding by Scripture, Christian worldview, and liturgical-communal practices (chapels, daily prayer, singing) embedded in the program.
- Purpose and goals center on formation, virtue, wisdom, and the Great Conversation, not merely job preparation. The mission foregrounds virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful, with an explicit telos beyond information transfer.
- Curriculum is explicitly liberal-arts-based (Trivium/Quadrivium), with Great Books and primary sources, and a paideia-informed rationale; public statements name Memoria Press/Veritas Press and Rod and Staff as curriculum providers, reinforcing classical content.
- Pedagogy includes contemplation, memory work, and communal worship, with upper-level (Socratic-style) elements implied through Great Books/Great Conversation, and a shift to on-campus + home-co-teaching for collaborative education.
- Faculty references indicate on-campus classical teachers and a focus on character formation; the public materials emphasize trained teachers and a “living curriculum” orientation through classical pedagogy.
- Parents & Community are intentionally integrated as co-educators in a covenantal model; there is explicit emphasis on parental partnership, training, and open campus touchpoints.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Collaborative model: campus on 2–3 days/week with at-home learning by parents; this hybrid approach blends traditional classroom learning and homeschooling. This is explicitly described and signals a classical pedagogy adapted for family involvement.
- Lower-school pedagogy includes singing/chanting/memorization aspects noted in classical pedagogy (memory work, recitation). The school emphasizes memory-based practices and classical formation.
- Public materials emphasize a “Three Pillars” pedagogy and a paideia-informed approach (classical formation of loves, knowledge, and values). While not always explicit about every category (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars in upper school), the materials do emphasize dialogue-driven classical learning and memory-based methods as part of foundational practice.
- There is less explicit public articulation of Socratic seminars or formal upper-school seminars/debates; the publicly available content leans toward collaborative homeschool-style pedagogy and Great Books orientation rather than a plainly stated Socratic-oral-seminar framework.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive indicators found: collaborative pedagogy; memory work/recitation; worship-based and contemplative elements; paideia emphasis; Great Books oriented discussions implied.
- Negative indicators found: explicit, public articulation of Socratic seminars/semina not clearly stated; explicit restfulness/scholé language is not uniformly present in the public materials, though some elements exist (memory work, contemplation implied by classical framing).
- Frequency estimate: mixture of positive pedagogy signals with some missing explicit elements (Socratic seminars, formal seminars) in the public materials.

Why this rating

- The pedagogy aligns with classical aims in many respects (memory, recitation, Great Books, paideia, and a liturgical/communal rhythm). However, explicit, public-facing statements about upper-school Socratic seminars, debates, and the full spectrum of classical discussion formats are not consistently documented in the available materials. This yields a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical for pedagogy.

Cited evidence: Collaborative model and memory/chanting references.
