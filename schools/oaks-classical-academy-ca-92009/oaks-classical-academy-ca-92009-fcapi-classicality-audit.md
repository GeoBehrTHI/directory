## Executive Summary

Oaks Classical Academy CA 92009 presents a clearly Christian orientation coupled with a stated aim of delivering a classical liberal arts education. Public materials on the site emphasize Christ-centered education and aims to cultivate discernment of truth, goodness, and beauty, which aligns with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal elements of classical education. However, explicit, public evidence of core classical curriculum details (e.g., grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Latin/Greek; a full Trivium/Quadrivium structure) and robust Socratic pedagogy is not clearly documented in the school’s public materials. Taken together, the school demonstrates foundational classical aspirations and a Christian frame but falls short on several explicit classical markers in its public-facing curriculum and pedagogy disclosures. As a result, the overall classification is NOT CLASSICAL, with notable pockets of CLASSICAL alignment and a few areas that would be required to move toward a full Classical model.

Key signals from public materials:
- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: Strong Christian framing (Christ-centered, rooted in Christ; truth, goodness, beauty), which aligns with transcendental aims when paired with theology-oriented schooling. See Our Mission and Deep Hope statements.  
- School Purpose & Goal: Emphasis on formation, discernment of truth/goodness/beauty, and virtue-oriented aims in a Christian context. 
- Curriculum: Public materials consistently describe a “classical liberal arts education,” but do not publicly enumerate the Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, or explicit language instruction in grammar/logic/rhetoric on the site excerpts provided. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL orientation rather than a full Classical curriculum claim. 
- Pedagogy: No explicit reference in the public materials to Socratic seminar, scholé, chanting, or other classical pedagogy elements; the absence points toward NOT CLASSICAL pedagogy in public-facing content as presented. 
- Faculty: Public materials show standard “faculty & staff” listings but do not publicly describe a living-curriculum model or mentor-style pedagogy on the site excerpts provided. This is insufficient for CLASSICAL faculty signals in public content. 
- Parents & Community: There is no explicit public articulation of parents as co-teachers or extensive parent-formation programs on the site excerpts; the public-facing materials do not demonstrate a strong partner-parents model. (Public materials excerpted do not provide explicit evidence for this category.)

Aggregate Assessment

- Overall classification: NOT CLASSICAL
- Rationale: The school demonstrates a Christian classical ethos and a stated aim of a classical liberal arts education, which aligns with two of the six categories (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; School Purpose & Goal). However, public-facing curricular detail (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium; grammar/logic/rhetoric), explicit classical pedagogy (Socratic/Scholé/memory-based work), and explicit faculty and parent-community commitments as described by classical standards are not clearly evidenced in the site excerpts provided. The combination of strong religious framing with partial classical curriculum and pedagogy signals yields a NOT CLASSICAL overall rating.

Citations: Evidence of Christ-centered mission and virtue/true/beauty framing.  

What Would Move the Needle

- If Oaks Classical Academy aims to achieve a CLASSICAL rating, publicly document and implement the following:
  - Curriculum: publicly articulate a Trivium/Quadrivium sequence, include explicit Great Books or canonical texts, and identify explicit emphasis on grammar, logic, and rhetoric across grade bands. Display a clear map showing linear progression from grammar/recitation to rhetoric and logic, with integration of Great Books where appropriate. Include explicit history across the curriculum and attention to Western civilization as part of the classical canon. 
  - Pedagogy: publish explicit references to Socratic seminars or other classical discussion formats (e.g., scholé), recitation/memory work, and daily or weekly contemplative elements aligned with scholé. 
  - Faculty: articulate the living-curriculum concept, with teacher mentors modeling classical virtues and pedagogy; publish a “portrait of a teacher” and a “core-competencies/character” profile for faculty.
  - Parents & Community: formalize a partnership with families as co-educators, offer parent-formation programs, book studies, and opportunities for parent involvement in classroom life; welcome parent visits and cultivate a family-centric classical culture.
  - Theology integration: continue and more explicitly connect theology with classical method, ensuring ongoing integration across disciplines (as suggested by Christian classical education resources).
- These changes would move the school toward a FULL CLASSICAL rating across all six categories.
