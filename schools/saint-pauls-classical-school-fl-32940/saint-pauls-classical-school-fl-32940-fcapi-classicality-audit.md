## Executive Summary

St Pauls Classical School Fl 32940 (Saint Paul’s Classical School, Melbourne, FL) demonstrates a robust classical-education orientation anchored in religious formation, virtue formation, and a clearly articulated structure for liberal-arts pedagogy. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered, liturgical, catechetical community with an explicit aim of forming wisdom and virtue in students, not merely preparing for work. Evidence from the school’s site highlights theological grounding (Catechesis, Prayer, Worship), formation of character and virtue, and dialectic/rhetoric-centric pedagogy in line with the Trivium tradition (grammar, logic, rhetoric). While the program clearly exhibits classical features (grammar/logic/rhetoric in curriculum, religious formation, and Socratic-style engagement in upper grades), explicit references to Great Books, the Quadrivium, or a formal, universal Great Conversation are less visible in the public materials. Taken together, the school is best characterized as CLASSICAL overall, with especially strong alignment in religious-metaphysical foundations, purpose and goal, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement; curriculum shows strong classical signposts but could strengthen explicit Great-Books/Great-Conversation articulation to reach a higher level of “fully Classical” in the strict lexicon of traditional classical education.

- Evidence from campus pages and program descriptions highlights worship and liturgical rhythm as parts of daily life, reinforcing liturgical and communal practice. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The presence of liturgical/prayer practices and catechesis signals a strong religious/metaphysical foundation. This justifies CLASSICAL for Element 1.

- Public materials emphasize formation and virtue with a holistic educational approach, and the school identifies as classical in orientation, suggesting an integrated, cross-disciplinary approach in line with classical pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Trivium, Dialectic, Rhetoric, Great Books/Great Conversation (contextualized in the classical canon by the Six Essentials doc, though explicit Great-Books language is not consistently visible in every school-material page), formation, integration, wonder (conceptualized in classical pedagogy). The public materials show grammar/logical-rhetorical development via the Dialectic/Rhetoric language and course sequences. 
- Negative keywords present: not clearly evidenced in public materials; no explicit emphasis on modernized, purely skills-based or job-focused curricula. However, explicit mentions of “Great Books” are not consistently visible in the site text, which is a key hallmark of classical curricula in the Six Essentials framework. This creates a partial gap relative to a fully “CLASSICAL” curriculum signature. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum shows strong classical scaffolding (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and a vocationally neutral, formation-centered orientation; however, the public-facing materials do not consistently showcase explicit Great-Books engagement or a fully articulated Great Conversation, which are core markers in the strict sense of the Six Essential Elements. Therefore, Curriculum is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

The school’s public materials and course sequences reflect the Trivium progression (grammar/logic/rhetoric) that is central to classical pedagogy. 
- Text references to “Socratic discussion” and the role of memory, song, and recitation in early grades are consistent with traditional scholé (restful, contemplative learning) alongside more rigorous, dialectic practices in the upper grades. The Six Essentials framework highlights these teaching-method features as hallmarks of classical pedagogy. 
- The presence of liturgical/prayer integration suggests pedagogy that weaves formation and contemplation into daily practice, consistent with scholé rhythms. The site explicitly notes daily prayer and worship integration. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue/discussion, dialectic, great conversation, rhetoric, scholé, contemplative learning, memorization/recitation, formation through imitation, and singing/memory-based routines in early schooling (as suggested by the classical pedagogy vocabulary and the public course structure). 
- Negative keywords present: none clearly evident in the public-facing materials (no overt emphasis on project-based, entirely student-led, non-Socratic pedagogy; rather, evidence points to a structured progression from contemplative to dialectic/rhetoric). The presence of rigid, canon-based pedagogy aligns with classical norms rather than anti-classical approaches. 

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates clear classical pedagogy indicators: a progression from grammar to logic to rhetoric (and associated dialectic activities), with the upper grades engaging in Socratic-style inquiry and rhetorical training, alongside contemplative elements (Scholé) at appropriate stages. This supports CLASSICAL.

This would tighten alignment with the Six Essentials’ explicit “Great Books/Great Conversation” criterion and the Quadrivium as a curricular anchor. (Evidence basis: The Six Essentials document highlights Great Books and the Great Conversation as core components; current site materials show strong Trivium signals but fewer explicit Great Books statements.)

The presence of Dialectic/ Logic/Rhetoric and the public emphasis on formation and virtue are already strong; an explicit Great-Books articulation would confirm the signposted Great Conversation dimension.
