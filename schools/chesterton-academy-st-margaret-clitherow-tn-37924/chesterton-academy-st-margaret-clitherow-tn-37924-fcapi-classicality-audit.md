## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Margaret Clitherow (Knoxville, TN) presents a structured, explicitly classical, Catholic high-school program within the Chesterton Schools Network. Public-facing materials emphasize a culture of life, reverent liturgy, and a liberal-arts–driven curriculum built around Great Books, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and Socratic pedagogy. The school foregrounds formation of virtue and wisdom, not merely information or job-ready skills, and integrates theology, worship, and sacramental life into daily life and academics. Evidence from the school’s site highlights daily Mass, liturgical practice, catechesis, and a mission to form joyful leaders and saints through a deeply Catholic, classical framework. The curriculum blends history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages with a strong emphasis on classical texts, Socratic inquiry, and exposure to great books, all supported by a model of mentorship and parish/school partnerships. These characteristics align strongly with classical education criteria across all six categories, indicating a full CLASSICAL classification.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Catholic identity, liturgical life, theology integrated with academics, and a “Culture of Life” formation emphasis. Evidence includes daily Mass, Catholic formation, and incarnational pedagogy.   
- Purpose and Goal: Education aimed at virtue, wisdom, and human flourishing within a classical frame; formation over mere information; explicit reference to truth, goodness, and beauty as real.  
- Curriculum: Liberal-arts foundations (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, history integrated across disciplines, and a Great Conversation approach with Socratic pedagogy.   
- Pedagogy: Socratic seminars, memory work within a contemplative/reverent framework, and upper-level rhetoric training; integration of a restorative, contemplative pace with rigorous inquiry. 
- Faculty: Descriptions of the headmaster and faculty as mentors who form the living curriculum; visible faculty–student interactions and parish oversight.  
- Parents & Community: Active parent engagement through events (e.g., Odyssey Day), tours, and a culture that partners with families; open-door visitation and community involvement.
