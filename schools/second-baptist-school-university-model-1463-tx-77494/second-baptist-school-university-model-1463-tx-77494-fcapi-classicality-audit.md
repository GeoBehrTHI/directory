## Executive Summary

Second Baptist School University-Model (SBSUM) at the 1463 campus in Katy, TX presents a robust Christian classical framework consistent with the core signals of classical education. Public materials describe a Christ-centered, biblical worldview with Gothic-level emphasis on truth, beauty, and goodness, anchored by a Trivium-inspired curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a University-Model structure that blends on-campus and satellite (home-based) learning. The school highlights partnerships with families, a chapel-centered culture, and a faculty built around mentorship and living examples of virtue. While SBSUM explicitly markets itself as classical and university-model, some elements of the public materials emphasize mission, discipleship, and community-building rather than a plainly articulated “great books / great conversation” focus or a fully explicit, book-centered liberal-arts canon. Taken together, SBSUM 1463 aligns strongly with Classical education, with Element 2 representing a nuanced zone where formation and virtue-language are foregrounded but certain canonical classical indicators (e.g., explicit Great Books / Great Conversation emphasis) are less overt in public pages.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- Key drivers of CLASSICAL classification: explicit articulation of classical pedagogy (grammar/logic/rhetoric), a liberal-arts-inspired curriculum with Latin/Greek exposure, a strong emphasis on truth/beauty/goodness and a biblical worldview, chapel and liturgical-like community rhythms, and a clear faculty-centered living-curriculum/mentorship ethos. Public materials also stress the parent-school partnership in alignment with classical education norms. Evidence includes explicit references to classical education, the trivium, Christ-centered formation, and the university-model approach, along with a structured on-campus/satellite model that preserves classical pedagogy within a family- and church-centered ecosystem. See representative citations: “The curriculum is fashioned after classical education, which is a methodology involving three stages (grammar, logic, and rhetoric)” and “Second Baptist School University-Model is founded on our core values of Christ-centered education … Word of God” and “Our mission is to equip students to think critically, live biblically and lead courageously …”

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 – School Purpose and Goal: Introduce explicit statements—in public-facing materials and on-page sections—about classical telos (true, good, beautiful) and Great Books/Great Conversation as a formal curricular element. This would strengthen the alignment with the more rigorous “formation” language in the lexicon and move this category toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 3 – Curriculum: While strong in grammar/logic/rhetoric and languages, add explicit, published, public-facing references to Great Books as part of the curriculum or Great Conversation as a formal practice. This would bolster the canonical Great Books signal without diminishing the current strengths in the Trivium and liberal-arts curriculum.
- Element 4 – Pedagogy: Enhance explicit articulation of restorative contemplative “scholé” moments and details about structured Socratic seminars across grades, including public examples of on-campus Socratic discussions and rubrics. While present, more explicit public language would strengthen the category’s alignment.
- Element 5 – Faculty: Continue to emphasize “living curriculum” language and expand public narratives that showcase faculty mentoring, portraits of ideal teachers/students, and community mentorship programs to further embed the “living curriculum” identity.
- Element 6 – Parents & Community: Maintain and broaden parent-formation programming (book clubs, reading groups, and structured training) and continue to promote open campus events. Consider documenting measurable outcomes of parental involvement (e.g., student outcomes and community engagement metrics) to reinforce and expand the partnership narrative.
