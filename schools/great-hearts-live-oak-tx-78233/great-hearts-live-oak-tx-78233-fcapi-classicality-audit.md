## Executive Summary

Great Hearts Live Oak (GHLO) presents a robust classical-education profile across all six rubric categories. Public materials consistently foreground Truth, Goodness, and Beauty as the core aims, with a heavy emphasis on a Socratic, liberal-arts-based curriculum, a Great Books orientation, and strong partnerships with families. The school explicitly frames its pedagogy around the Socratic Method, classical texts, and a formation-focused purpose, while describing faculty as models of virtue and partners in the learning process. Parent and community involvement is integrated through formal structures (e.g., school-parent compact) and ongoing opportunities for engagement.

Overall, GHLO aligns most strongly with classical criteria in Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community, with clear evidence in the public materials of liberal arts content, Great Books, Socratic teaching, teacher as exemplar, and active parent partnership. Religious and metaphysical foundations are present through the commitment to truth, goodness, and beauty and a transcendent view of learning as formation, though explicit theological liturgy is not foregrounded in the available public materials. The six-category assessment yields a consistent CLASSICAL rating across all categories.

Key takeaways:
- Core classical markers are explicit: Truth-Goodness-Beauty, liberal arts core, grammar-logic-rhetoric, Great Books, Socratic method, and formation of character.
- Public materials depict strong faculty development and partnerships with families as central to the school’s mission.
- The absence of overt liturgical or formal theological rituals in the publicly-available materials limits Category 1 from a pure liturgical/theological framing, but does not undermine the overall classical orientation.

Cited evidence and keyword anchors drawn from GHLO materials include phrases such as pursuing Truth, Goodness, and Beauty; the Socratic Method as the primary instructional tool; a foundation in grammar, logic, rhetoric, history, natural science, and philosophy; Great Books and the Great Conversation; and explicit statements about partnerships with parents and communities.
