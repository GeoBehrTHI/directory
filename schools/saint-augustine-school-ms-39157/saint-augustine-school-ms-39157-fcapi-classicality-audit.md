# Classicality Audit: Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School

URL audited: https://bishopreicher.com/

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School (PreK–12) shows strong classical alignment in its religious and metaphysical foundations and in its stated purposes (formation in virtue; pursuit of what is true, good, and beautiful). The curriculum reflects elements of the Catholic liberal arts tradition with theology, sacred scripture, Latin, and classical literature in the program. However, there is limited public evidence of distinctively classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, formal rhetoric training, chanting/recitation, scholé) and little articulation of a classical faculty culture. The site also emphasizes modern/STEM and “21st‑century skills” initiatives, which dilute classical signals. In aggregate, the school reads as classical in foundation and intention but mixed in execution and articulation—thus SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key strengths
- Robust Catholic formation with weekly Mass, Eucharistic devotion, catechesis, and explicit virtue formation (prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance), with repeated appeals to truth, goodness, and beauty    .
- Curriculum includes theology, classical literature, Latin, and classic authors (e.g., Homer, Sophocles, Shakespeare) consistent with a Great Books orientation  .

Key growth areas
- Publicly document classical pedagogy (Socratic discussion, dialectic, rhetoric capstones, chanting/recitation in the lower grades, scholé/restful learning), and articulate a classical faculty culture and formation plan.
- Balance technology/STEM messaging with clear, primary commitments to liberal arts, Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, and the Great Conversation per the keyword framework in Six Essential Elements and lexicon  .

## What would move the needle (to CLASSICAL)
- Publish a clear pedagogy page:
  - Name Socratic seminar/dialectic in upper school; debate, disputation, and formal rhetoric capstones/orations; describe progymnasmata and imitation as method—aligning to the Pedagogy keywords .
  - For lower school, describe memory work, chanting/recitation, and singing as daily practices; reference scholé/restful learning and formation in wonder .
- Strengthen curriculum articulation:
  - Name the Trivium/Quadrivium; explicitly label Great Books and the Great Conversation; clarify “history” (not “social studies”), primary sources, and timeline work per the Curriculum keywords .
- Faculty formation:
  - Add a “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and note ongoing faculty formation in classical pedagogy; highlight teachers as mentors/models, a “faculty of friends,” and collegial inquiry .
- Parent formation:
  - Offer (and publicize) parent seminars/book clubs on classical education; provide a parent covenant or similar articulation of shared formation .
- Re‑balance messaging:
  - Keep STEM/tech language in its proper place; foreground liberal arts, virtue formation, and Great Books to ensure classical telos is primary, per negative/positive keyword guidance in the Six Elements .
