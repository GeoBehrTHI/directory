## Executive Summary

Liberty Classical Schools Georgia (GA 30082) presents a robust classical-education model grounded in liberal arts, Great Books, and historically oriented pedagogy. Public-facing materials emphasize virtues, character formation, a story-rich history curriculum, and a time-tested liberal arts approach, all of which align strongly with core classical education tenets such as truth, virtue, and beauty, as well as the Great Conversation. The school clearly showcases liberal-arts content, Socratic inquiry, and a deliberate focus on forming virtuous citizens. Evidence of parental partnership and community-building further reinforces classical aims, though explicit liturgical/theological practices are less visible in the public materials.

Overall, the school demonstrates strong classical alignment across most rubric categories, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) and Element 5 (Faculty) showing more modest alignment than Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations), Element 2 (Purpose and Goal), Element 3 (Curriculum), and Element 6 (Parents & Community), which are more fully classical. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- There is explicit reference to Socratic questioning as a central mechanism for deepening understanding and for encouraging students to articulate their reasoning (“why” and “so what”). This is a hallmark of classical pedagogy in the upper grades. 
- The curriculum describes upper-school seminars, debates, and rhetoric training as components of pedagogy, signaling incorporation of Socratic and rhetorical methods. 
- There is a general emphasis on a combination of rigorous inquiry with opportunities for memory, recitation, and contemplation in classical pedagogy, though lower-level material on singing/chanting is not explicitly documented in public materials. The general approach is to blend rigorous inquiry with reflective practice but with less public detail on lower-school singing or contemplative/restful pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic, seminars, debates, rhetoric, Great Conversation, memory/recitation (implied in grammar-logic-rhetoric progression). 
- Negative keywords: not evident in public postings; no explicit statements about value-neutral pedagogy or exclusively project-based/experimental teaching in the public content. The absence of certain “restful/contemplative” phrasing is noted but not overtly negative.

Why this rating
- The presence of Socratic discussion and rhetoric-focused training demonstrates classical pedagogy in key elements, but explicit statements about lower-school contemplative/restful pedagogy and full articulation of all six pedagogical modes (restful, memory-based, Socratic, seminars, etc.) are not as explicit as the strongest classical exemplars. Hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were to strengthen toward a fully classical model, focusing on explicit articulation of the “living curriculum” role of faculty (Element 5) would move the needle. Publicly stating how faculty embody virtues, mentor students, and model classical dispositions could elevate Element 5 to CLASSICAL.
- Increasing explicit articulation of liturgical/theological integration (Element 1) where appropriate, or clearly delineating why theology is or isn’t part of the curriculum, would further clarify Foundation alignment for readers who expect theological grounding in some classical models.

Note: No major needle-moving changes are required given the overall CLASSICAL rating, but explicit statements in Element 5 and enhanced articulation of liturgical/theological alignment would push all six categories toward stronger CLASSICAL consistency.
