## Executive Summary

Donum Dei Classical Academy (San Francisco, ZIP 94110) presents a robust Classical Christian orientation across public materials visible on its site. The school foregrounds formation, virtue, and the liberal arts within a Christian-theological frame, with explicit references to biblical truth, worship, and a paideia-informed approach. The curriculum is framed around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with an integrated, historically informed, and Wonder-filled pedagogy. There is clear attention to community and parental partnership as central to education. Based on a six-category rubric derived from the classical-school criteria, Donum Dei aligns strongly with CLASSICAL standards, with some elements more explicitly developed than others (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language is present but not always overtly labeled). Overall, the school earns a CLASSICAL rating.

Key insights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are overtly present (biblical truth, worship, God, and paideia language) and central to the mission. Evidence includes explicit statements of a biblical worldview and the aim to educate “the whole human child” within a Christian framework.   
- Purpose/goals emphasize formation and virtue in a classical frame, rather than purely workforce preparation. This is reflected in mission statements and “pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty.”  
- Curriculum centers the liberal-arts Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and history integration, with examples of grammar/logic/rhetoric and historical study embedded in public materials. Great-Books language is discussed in the broader classical literature lexicon, though explicit Great Books labeling is not always shown in the site’s pages.  
- Pedagogy shows a blend of contemplative/restful methods (Scholé) and active, Socratic-like discussion in upper grades, with lower grades using chant/singing/memory to deepen learning. Evidence includes lower-grade singing/memory approaches and upper-grade rhetoric/seminar-like activity.  
- Faculty are described as living exemplars and mentors; materials describe a “faculty and staff” focus with an emphasis on formation and partnership. Evidence includes explicit references to faculty as living curriculum and to the faculty’s role in formation. 
- Parents & Community are positioned as partners/co-teachers in the educational project, with open invitation to visits and ongoing parent formation opportunities.
