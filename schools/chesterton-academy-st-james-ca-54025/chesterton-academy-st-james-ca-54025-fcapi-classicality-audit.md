## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. James (Menlo Park, CA) presents a robust classical-Catholic educational model with clear alignment to core elements of classical education. Public materials emphasize a joyfully Catholic, classical formation that foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty, integration across disciplines, and a liturgical, communal life. The school explicitly locates its mission in formation and virtue within a Catholic framework, introduces students to great thinkers, and embeds a liturgical life including choral vespers and parish-connected events. These features point to strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, a formation-oriented School Purpose and Goal, a curriculum anchored in classical canon and Great Books-like references, and a family-centered Parents & Community orientation. Evidence also shows a clear emphasis on a community-centric, living-faculty model (e.g., a Faculty Gallery and “Our Model of Education”) and a House system that mirrors enduring Christian educational traditions. Collectively, these elements situate Chesterton Academy of St. James as CLASSICAL across most categories, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) presenting some ambiguity due to less explicit public-documented emphasis on Socratic seminars or explicit rhetoric training.

Key insights:
- Religious foundation and liturgical life are explicit and central (Catholic identity, liturgical activities, chapels, and church-linked culture) , .
- Curriculum communicates a classical arc via reference to great thinkers and canonical figures (Socrates, Aquinas, Dante; Homer, Euclid, Newton) and a unified, integrated approach that aims at Truth, Goodness, and Beauty , .
- The school highlights formation over information, with explicit language about forming students as joyful Catholic minds and practitioners of virtue and wisdom , .
- Family partnership is a visible cornerstone (parents as co-teachers, volunteer culture, and tuition-affordability through community involvement) , .
- Pedagogical specifics such as explicit Socratic seminars or formal Great Books syllabi are not uniformly documented in public pages, yielding a partial gap in Element 4 (Pedagogy) that suggests SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL without additional public articulation , .
