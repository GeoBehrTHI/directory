## Executive Summary

Liberty Common Schools (Fort Collins, CO) presents a robust, knowledge-centered, classical-leaning program across its K–12 system, with explicit emphasis on core knowledge, Great Books, liberal arts, and a strong parent partnership. Public-facing materials repeatedly foreground timeless aims—virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful—and describe a curriculum designed to build a contextual foundation of knowledge, cultivate character, and prepare students for thoughtful citizenship. The school openly rejects anti-intellectual trends and promotes a structured, traditional approach to education, including memory work, recitation, and Socratic-style discussion at the high-school level. Taken together, the six-category evaluation yields an overall CLASSICAL classification for Liberty Common Schools, with two areas showing only partial alignment (notably religious/metaphysical depth and explicit faculty portrait/formation language). The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL.

Executive Summary — Category-by-Category Snapshot

- Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Emphasis on virtue and the true/good/beautiful, plus a stated rejection of anti-intellectualism; occasional references to transcendent ideals in classical tradition, but little explicit theology or liturgical practice on public pages. See statements about virtue, truth, goodness, beauty, and anti-anti-intellectualism in Our Philosophy and Classical Education materials.  
  - Positive keyword analysis: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; civic virtue. Estimated positives outpace negatives.  
  - Why this rating: The school foregrounds transcendent aims and virtuous formation but lacks explicit, stated theological or liturgical foundations in public pages. This yields a partial alignment with the Religious/Metaphysical Foundations criteria. 

- Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Formation-focused aims; emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and a liberal-arts orientation; explicit grounding in the real, knowable true/good/beautiful; attention to formation over mere information; Great Books and classical ideals.  
  - Positive keyword analysis: formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education, true/good/beautiful, telos, Great Conversation. 
  - Why this rating: The public materials consistently articulate formation toward virtue and human excellence, aligning closely with classical aims of education beyond job preparation. 

- Category 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Core Knowledge Sequence as foundational curriculum for K–8; strong emphasis on Great Books/Great-Books lists; liberal-arts integration; Latin; Mos Maiorum; Singapore Math; history as a throughline across disciplines; emphasis on a coherent, cumulative knowledge base.  
  - Positive keyword analysis: Core Knowledge Sequence, Great Books, Great Conversation, liberal arts, Latin, Mos Maiorum, history, coherence, knowledge base.  
  - Why this rating: The curriculum materials align with the classical emphasis on grammar/logic/rhetoric through explicit content—though Core Knowledge provides a knowledge-base approach rather than a purely ritual Great-Books core, it is paired with extensive classical content (Great Books, Latin, history across the curriculum). This yields strong CLASSICAL alignment. 

- Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Public materials describe a classical pedagogy featuring memorization, recitation, narration, and Socratic-style discussion at the upper levels; explicit reference to classical education as a time-tested method with a focus on virtue and the liberal arts. 
  - Positive keyword analysis: Socratic, narration, recitation, chanting, memory work, rhetoric, seminars, discussion, scholé/restful learning. 
  - Why this rating: The presence of Socratic-style engagement, memory-based pedagogy, and emphasis on rhetoric for upper grades demonstrates a strong classical pedagogy beyond rote, matching the Six Elements expectations. The explicit “Scholé” approach is reinforced by Classical-Education materials. 

- Category 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Public pages foreground teacher leadership, the importance of teachers in delivering a knowledge-based program, and the notion of “the thinking framework” and moral formation, but explicit statements about the faculty as “living curriculum,” “portrait of a teacher,” or “faculty as a community of mentors” are less evident on the core site pages. 
  - Positive keyword analysis: living curriculum, portrait of a teacher, faculty as mentors (where mentioned in classical-literature discourse, not consistently on Liberty pages). The site references the leadership of teachers and their role in the classroom but with less explicit language about a formal faculty-portrait or professional formation in classical pedagogy. 
  - Negative keyword analysis: limited explicit statements about a teacher-as-living-curriculum or a formal “portrait of a teacher”; absence of detailed faculty development language beyond general statements. 
  - Why this rating: The program shows clear teacher leadership and pedagogy consistent with classical aims, but the public-facing materials lack explicit, robust language about a formal “living curriculum” or teacher-portrait as a defined, codified element. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL categorization. 

- Category 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Strong parent partnership ethos; explicit language that parents are co-teachers; volunteer expectations; open campus culture; parent formation through site content and suggested readings; information nights and family involvement.  
  - Positive keyword analysis: partnership with families, co-teachers, parental engagement, home-school alignment, parent formation, open campus, volunteering, family culture. 
  - Why this rating: The school clearly frames parents as integral to the educational project and actively engages families as co-educators, aligning with classical expectations for robust parent-community partnership. 

Aggregate Assessment

- CLASSICAL: 4 categories (2, 3, 4, 6) align CLASSICAL; 2 categories (1, 5) align SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The preponderance of the six categories supports a CLASSICAL overall classification for Liberty Common Schools.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Category 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Increase explicit articulation of theology or liturgical/community practices within public-facing materials, while preserving the secular, classical core. This would strengthen alignment with the “Grounded in Theology” and “Liturgical and Communal Practices” criteria without compromising the school’s established orientation.

- For Category 5 (Faculty): Expand explicit public articulation of the faculty as the living curriculum, including a formal portrait of teacher roles, ongoing classical pedagogy professional development, and a clearly described teacher-formation path. This would move Category 5 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
