## Executive Summary

Wellmont Academy (St. Petersburg, FL 33710) presents a christ-centered, classical-leaning program that blends Socratic dialogue, project-based learning, and faith-formational practices. Public materials consistently frame education as formation of character in a biblical framework, with explicit emphasis on Christ-centered instruction, chapel and biblical discussions, and service-oriented community life. Pedagogical methods include discussion-based learning and Socratic dialogue in the upper levels, complemented by field experiences, dual enrollment, and a heartily communal culture supported by parent involvement and campus community activities.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- The school clearly communicates religious/metaphysical foundations (Christ-centered, biblical worldview, chapel, Bible-centered chapels, service and discipleship) and a strong emphasis on formation and virtue in its stated mission and practices.
  - Evidence includes explicit Christ-centered framing and biblical worldview integration across curricular and co-curricular activities. 
  - Public materials describe faith-formative practices (Chapel, service projects, discussions tied to Scripture). 
- The pedagogy and curriculum support a classical orientation (Socratic dialogue in upper school; project-based academics; emphasis on virtue and formation; teacher as mentor; living curriculum).
  - Socratic dialogue and project-based learning are described as central practices in the upper levels. 
  - The faculty framing emphasizes mentors modeling virtue and the living curriculum. 
- Areas that signal a somewhat continued development toward full classical rigor exist (e.g., explicit Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium language is not consistently explicit in public materials; explicit “Great Books” and formal Trivium/Quadrivium references are not readily evident in the site copy). These gaps place Element 3 and 2 in a more nuanced zone (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) alongside categories that are clearly CLASSICAL.

- Abstract synthesis: Wellmont Academy evidences a robust classical framework anchored in Christian formation, with explicit Socratic pedagogy, virtue formation, and strong parental/community involvement. The strongest momentum rests in Religious Foundations, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents/Community. The Curriculum and Purpose areas show classical tendencies but lack some explicit canonical classical markers (Great Books, explicit Trivium/Quadrivium emphasis) in readily visible public content. This yields an overall CLASSICAL status, with some category-level notes for improvement to reach full canonical status.

- What would move the needle: For a shift toward FULLY CLASSICAL, the school could foreground explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, clearly articulate Trivium/Quadrivium progression, and further codify a language of “formation” as the telos of education within mission statements (including a clearly stated aim beyond college/career readiness). Additionally, stronger public-facing articulation of partnership with families as “co-teachers” could strengthen Element 6 and its alignment with classical-community norms.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials frame Wellmont Academy as Christ-centered and biblically grounded, with a philosophy of learning that weaves faith into daily academics and community life. 
- The school emphasizes biblical worldview integration across subjects and chapel-based worship/formation. 
- Official materials emphasize God’s Word as a guiding principle and a soul-safe, faith-centered community. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Christ-centered, biblical worldview, chapel, Bible, God’s Word, faith in action, service projects. (Representative citations: Christ-centered framing in mission; biblical worldview; chapel; Bible studies; God’s Word emphasis; faith-informed service.)   
- Negative keywords present: none detected in public materials (no explicit relativism/neutral ethics signals in the accessible public copy). 

Why this rating
- The presence of explicit transcendental/moral-realism framing and a theology-integrated approach (Christ-centered, biblical worldview, chapel, Scripture-infused learning) strongly aligns with the Positive side of the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations rubric. The cadence of liturgical/community practices further confirms liturgical and communal dimensions. This category is CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Add explicit language that signals a sustained transcendent metaphysical realism (e.g., explicit statements about the objective reality of truth, goodness, beauty as real and knowable, beyond theology alone) and provide a formal liturgical calendar or regular liturgical practices linked to the curriculum beyond chapel.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Mission statements emphasize Christ-centered formation and the cultivation of human identity, virtue, and leadership within a biblical framework. 
- Materials describe formation-oriented aims, with emphasis on holistic development (formation of character) and balanced academic growth; dual enrollment and career pathways are present, signaling a blend of formation and preparation for further education/work. 
- Statements foreground faith, formation, and purpose in daily schooling; “God-written identity” language appears in mission-related materials. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Christ-centered, soul-safe, balanced academic excellence, God-written identity, formation (implicit), character development (implied), leadership (implied). Representative lines show formation and identity framing within a biblical context. 
- Negative keywords present: Not obviously present (no explicit “workforce-first” or career-readiness displacing formation signals visible in the public copy). However, the presence of career pathways and dual enrollment introduces a practical workforce element, which tightens the boundary toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than a pure formation emphasis.

Why this rating
- The school clearly centers formation and virtue in its purpose statements, but public content also foregrounds college and career pathways and practical preparation. The result is a strong formation orientation, yet not exclusively character/virtue formation to the exclusion of work-readiness pathways. This places Element 2 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL territory.

What would move the needle
- Strengthen explicit articulation that education is formation-first with virtue and wisdom as telos, minimizing or clearly subordinating work/career readiness as the primary aim. Add explicit text like: “The goal of education is formation of the person (virtue, wisdom) before preparation for work.”

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The site emphasizes a classical tone through a Christ-centered, discussion-driven approach and the integration of history, literature, and worldview across the curriculum (e.g., “Socratic dialogue” in upper school; “Project-Based Academics” and Bible-centered integration). 
- There is explicit use of “Socratic dialogue” and “project-based” modes in middle/high school curricula. 
- History is integrated across subjects and not merely labeled as “social studies” in the public copy; upper school content references history/government and cross-subject integration. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Great Books/Great Conversation is not consistently explicit, but there are mentions of “great conversation” language in classical-lens materials (public copy shows Socratic dialogue and cross-disciplinary dialogue). Project-based learning is a recurring theme, as is history integrated across disciplines. Representative: “Socratic dialogue”; “Project-Based Academics”; “biblical worldview” integration; “history across the curriculum.” 
- Negative keywords present: Explicit references to Great Books or Seven Liberal Arts (grammar/logic/rhetoric) are not consistently explicit in public content. The classical markers such as grammars, logic, and rhetoric are not consistently foregrounded in the accessible site copy.

Why this rating
- The curriculum shows classical-adjacent features (Socratic dialogue, integrated history, project-based learning, biblical worldview), but it lacks explicit, consistently stated mentions of core classical markers (grammar/logic/rhetoric as explicit curricular pillars and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments). This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Add explicit language and measurable commitments to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as foundational curricular pillars, plus explicit Great Books/Great Conversation components within the curriculum map.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize Socratic dialogue in upper grades and an emphasis on discussion-based, collaborative inquiry (Socratic dialogue, debates, seminars). 
- Project-based academics and faith-in-action elements illustrate a balance of rigorous inquiry with applied, real-life learning. 
- Some lower-level practices (e.g., devotional moments, chapel, and music) suggest a rhythm of contemplative/harmonized learning as part of pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue, project-based, seminars/debate, rhetoric, devotional/chapel, faith-in-action, living curriculum. Representative: “Discussion-Based Learning,” “Socratic dialogue,” “Project-Based Academics,” “Chapel,” “Bible-centered chapels.” 
- Negative keywords present: Not prominently indicated in the public copy (no explicit critique of Socratic methods or absence of contemplation). The site shows a mix of rigorous discussion with contemplative elements, consistent with classical pedagogy rather than a strongly secular, purely project-based approach.

Why this rating
- The pedagogy strongly aligns with classical methods (Socratic dialogue in upper grades, fairness of discussion, rhetoric and public speaking opportunities, and structured project-based work). The presence of a contemplative/Scholé flavor is less explicit but is embedded through devotional/moral formation practices rather than a purely high-pressure, test-driven approach. This category is CLASSICAL.

### Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates a clearly classical emphasis on family partnership and parent formation, aligning with traditional “parents as co-teachers” motifs. This category is CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Strengthen explicit, public articulation of ongoing parent education programs (e.g., classical education book studies, seminars) and a transparent pathway for parent involvement in curriculum and school governance to further elevate the public-facing classical articulation.

Aggregate Assessment

- Overall, Wellmont Academy demonstrates a robust Classical orientation in Religious Foundations, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents/Community. Element 2 (Purpose/Goal) and Element 3 (Curriculum) show strong classical influence but with some modern emphasis on college/career pathways and project-based approaches that make them SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully canonical across all six categories. The aggregate classification is therefore CLASSICAL.

- What would move the needle for categories rated SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (Element 2 and Element 3):
  - For Element 2: Emphasize telos as formation-fit explicitly over workforce-readiness in primary mission statements; reduce potential emphasis on “career-first” signals in core mission language.
  - For Element 3: Add explicit classical curricular markers (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Trivium/Quadrivium) and clear Great Books/Great Conversation commitments in the curriculum map and public materials.
  - For Element 4: Continue to deepen and publicly codify the balance of contemplative Scholé with Socratic/argumentative pedagogy to solidify a fully classical pedagogy narrative.
