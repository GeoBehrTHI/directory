## Executive Summary

Imago Dei Academy (IDA) in Ladysmith, Wisconsin presents a mainly classical, Christian program that aligns strongly with the core six elements of classical education. The school identifies itself explicitly as classical and Christian, with ongoing affiliation to ACCS and CLSA, and anchors its curriculum in Memoria Press and Highlands Latin School frameworks. Public materials emphasize the Liberal Arts, Great Books, Latin/Greek study, and a liturgical, chapel-centered culture, including explicit Christian worship and Bible-centered pedagogy. Evidence shows a coherent integration of theology, foundational classical aims (virtue, wisdom, truth), history-centered curriculum, and a pedagogy that includes memory work, recitation, and liturgical practices. Parents are positioned as partners in formation, and the faculty emphasize living curriculum and mentorship. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL across all six categories, with ample textual grounding in the school’s materials.

Key insights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit (Christian worldview, chapel, liturgy, Imago Dei).
- Purpose/goals are oriented toward virtue, wisdom, and formation (not just workforce prep).
- Curriculum emphasizes liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, Latin/Greek, and Western history.
- Pedagogy leans toward memory, recitation, and a liturgical/educational rhythm; some evidence of Socratic elements is indirect but the rhetoric/logic emphasis is clear.
- Faculty materials present teachers as living exemplars and mentors; “teacher as living curriculum” is reinforced in materials.
- Parent/community partnership is foregrounded (open houses, tours, parent involvement, connection through newsletters, and family engagement).

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogy includes contemplative/restful elements (scholé-like cadence) as part of classical pedagogy; lower grades feature singing/chanting/chanting to deepen memory and delight; and upper grades emphasize logic, rhetoric, and opportunities for oral expression (speeches, debates, and rhetoric training). The public materials explicitly describe singing, memory, and liturgical/prayer-centered components in the lower grades and a higher emphasis on rhetoric in the upper grades. 
- The upper school curriculum includes logic and rhetoric as part of English credits, indicating a movement toward formal rhetorical training. 
- There are clearly liturgical and memory-based elements (chant/sacred music; prayer; catechesis) in daily practice. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: scholé (implied by contemplative rhythm), memory work, recitation/narration, chanting, liturgy, seminar/colloquy (through emphasis on rhetoric and structured discussion); memory-based and liturgical calendar integration (from the lexicon and site). Privately available notes show emphasis on singing and prayer in lower grades; rhetoric and logic in higher grades. 
- Negative keywords present: minimal explicit mention of experiential-only or project-based learning as the sole method; the site does not emphasize student-directed, constructivist approaches as the sole method. However, explicit Socratic seminar language is not strongly present in public postings, which reduces the strength of a full Socratic pedagogy signal. 

Why this rating
- The school clearly embeds classical pedagogical patterns (memory, recitation, chant; liturgical/communal practices; logic/rhetoric emphasis) but the explicit, heavy emphasis on Socratic seminars or continuous deliberative dialogue (a hallmark of high-school classical pedagogy) is present in less direct form. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL classification: grounded in classical methods with strong memory/chant/liturgical elements and strong logic/rhetoric, but less explicit Socratic/seminar emphasis in public-facing content.
