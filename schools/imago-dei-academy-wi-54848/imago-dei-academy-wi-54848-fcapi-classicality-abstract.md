## ABSTRACT

Imago Dei Academy (WI 54848) demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL alignment across six key elements of classical education. Evidence shows a Christian, liturgical, and Virtue/Wisdom-centered foundation (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations), with a clear formation-centric purpose (School Purpose and Goal). The Curriculum centers on Liberal Arts, Great Books, Latin/Greek, and Western history, with a clearly articulated classical lineage (Factor: CLASSICAL). Pedagogy includes memory-based, chanting, and liturgical elements, with explicit focus on logic and rhetoric in high school; some explicit Socratic-style pedagogy is not strongly documented in public-facing materials (Factor: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL). Faculty are portrayed as mentors and living curriculum, and the Parents & Community framework emphasizes active parental partnership and formation (Factors: CLASSICAL for both Faculty and Parents/Community). The aggregate assessment places IDA firmly in the CLASSICAL category, with room to strengthen explicit Socratic pedagogy to move to a fully CLASSICAL designation in Pedagogy.

Key takeaways by category:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL — explicit Christian worldview, chapel, liturgy, and Imago Dei language.
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL — formation toward wisdom and virtue, Christian worldview, Great Books.
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL — liberal arts; grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin/Greek; Great Books; Western history.
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — strong memory/chant/ritual and liturgical elements; explicit Socratic seminar language is less visible publicly; potential to strengthen explicit seminar structures.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL — teachers as living curriculum and mentors; living portraits and staff engagement.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL — active partnership with parents and community involvement.

Citations reflect the school’s own materials corroborating these findings (e.g., the public-facing statements on classical Christian identity, chapel and liturgy, curriculum emphasis on Latin/Greek/Great Books, and parent/community engagement).
