## Executive Summary

Holy Family Classical Academy (HFCA) presents a robust Classical-Education profile anchored in Catholic faith, formation of character, and a curriculum steeped in Great Books, the Western Canon, and the liberal-arts scaffolding of grammar, logic, and rhetoric. Public materials foreground daily prayer, sacramental life, and a motto centered on truth, goodness, and beauty. The school consistently emphasizes formation of the whole person, virtue, and contemplation, while also highlighting a love of learning that transcends mere test performance. These factors collectively support a CLASSICAL classification, with only a few gaps that keep certain elements from asserting full legitimacy as a fully realized Classical school across all six categories.

Key strengths across the six categories include:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: clear Catholic identity, daily prayer and liturgy, sacramental life, and a motto centered on truth, goodness, and beauty.
- School Purpose and Goal: explicit formation of the whole person, formation over information, and a contemplative life in service to God.
- Curriculum: explicit attention to the Western Canon, Great Books, and grammar-stage scaffolding, Latin, and classical works.
- Faculty: parent-authenticated claims that teachers model virtue and truth, goodness, and beauty; teachers described as qualified and caring.
- Pedagogy: evidence of joyful learning environments, memory-based learning in early grades, and a growing emphasis on classical content; explicit Socratic pedagogy is not clearly stated in public materials.
- Parents & Community: active family engagement through testimonials, newsletters, and donation/volunteer culture, though explicit “parents as co-teachers” language is not prominent in the site copy.

Overall, HFCA is classified as CLASSICAL with some room for growth in pedagogical specificity (especially explicit mention of Socratic/Seminars in the upper grades) and in a stronger articulation of parent-as-co-educators. See abstracts by category for detailed keyword analyses and justifications, followed by concrete steps to move toward full CLASSICAL status.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Pedagogy (Element 4): Introduce explicit, public-facing statements about Socratic discussion, seminars, or debates in the upper grades, and document how logic and rhetoric are taught through formal seminar formats. Create a published blueprint of the upper-school pedagogy that shows how Socratic methods, seminars, and debates are implemented, including sample rubrics and student outcomes. This would move Element 4 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
- Parents & Community (Element 6): Add explicit language and programs that position parents as co-teachers or formal partners in the classical curriculum (e.g., parent workshops on Great Books, classical pedagogy, and reading lists; regular parent-teacher-student seminar nights; and published parent-education resources). While testimonials and involvement exist, codifying parent-as-co-educators would push Element 6 toward CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum (Element 3): Increase explicit public emphasis on the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and the Quadrivium elements in the upper grades) and provide concrete examples of how each stage integrates with Great Books and primary texts. Publish a concise, public-facing curriculum map that ties the grammar-stage to logic and rhetoric journeys, including canonical texts and sequences. This would strengthen Element 3 toward full CLASSICAL.
