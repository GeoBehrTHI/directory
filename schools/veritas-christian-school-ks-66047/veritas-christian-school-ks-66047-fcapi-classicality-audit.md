## Executive Summary

Veritas Christian School (KS 66047) presents a distinctly Classical Christian program that is deeply oriented toward formation of character within a biblical framework. The public materials consistently foreground a Christ-centered worldview, Biblical formation, and the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as the core architecture of curriculum and pedagogy. Evidence indicates strong alignment with Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and with School Purpose/Goal, as well as with Faculty and Parents/Community aspects. Some elements of the full classical rubric—most notably explicit Great Books engagement and a fully described, integrated Great Conversation across history and literature—are less clearly articulated in the publicly available site content, which places Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) as Somewhat Classical rather than Fully Classical. The aggregate assessment, however, lands in CLASSICAL, given the weight of institutional emphasis on virtue, faith-based formation, Trivium-based curriculum, and a living-teacher/model approach.

Key insights:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are actively embraced: Christ-centered, Biblical worldview, Word of God, and liturgical-like language appear in core messaging and values. This supports a CLASSICAL rating for Element 1.  
- Purpose and Goal emphasize formation and virtue within a Biblical frame (formation, wisdom, virtue, human flourishing, and Christ-centered leadership). This supports CLASSICAL for Element 2.  
- Curriculum centers the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and treats history through a classical lens, but explicit Great Books / Great Conversation and Quadrivium elements are not clearly demonstrated in the site content, placing Element 3 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.  
- Pedagogy shows a mix: clear Trivium framing and opportunities for Socratic-style inquiry could be inferred, but explicit, scalable Socratic/seminar pedagogy across grades is not uniformly documented in public pages, supporting a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 4. 
- Faculty profiles and campus communications convey a culture of virtue, mentoring, and a “living curriculum” through teachers, aligning Element 5 with CLASSICAL expectations. 
- Parents & Community messaging emphasizes partnership with families and church communities, parent formation, and open-campus involvement, supporting CATEGORY 6 as CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The Academics section presents the Trivium explicitly: Grammar (K-6), Logic (7-8), Rhetoric (9-12). This is a hallmark of classical curriculum structure. 
  - The core phrasing on values emphasizes grammar, rhetoric, and logic as models of education, aligning with Trivium-based curriculum. 
  - There is clear emphasis on Biblical framing and classical educational aims, but explicit Great Books / Great Conversation or Quadrivium elements are not clearly documented in public site content. The site does not publicly list Great Books or primary-sources canon in the same way as some classical programs; the Great Books/Grote conversation language is not explicit in the visible materials. 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, history (in the sense of Western civilization framing), classical education framing, liberal arts orientation in the Trivium model. (Academics page; wording around Trivium) 
  - Negative keywords present: lack of explicit Great Books and Great Conversation language; absence of explicit Quadrivium, Latin/Greek, and primary-source engagement in the public-facing materials. Positive Great Books signals are not clearly documented on the site. This triggers a shortfall relative to a full Classical curriculum in the strict sense. 

- Why this rating
  - The presence of Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric and a Trivium framework demonstrates solid classical content. However, the absence of publicly documented Great Books/Great Conversation and Quadrivium-level elements (and explicit historical/philosophical cross-curriculum integration) places this category as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than Fully Classical in the rubric.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Pedagogical cues align with a classical approach: lower grades emphasize memory-oriented activities (e.g., recitation, chant, memory work) and a calmer, contemplative approach; upper grades emphasize Socratic discussion, seminars, or rhetoric-focused speaking opportunities (as implied by the Trivium framework and classical pedagogy language in the site’s descriptions). The Academics page references the grammar/logic/rhetoric progression and a structured approach to learning. 
  - The site emphasizes a biblical worldview and Christ-centered instruction, with teachers who “tailor instruction to each individual” within a classical-Christian framework, which suggests a living, mentor-based pedagogy rather than a purely lecture-based approach. 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Socratic discussion/seminars (implied by the Trivium pedagogy), narration/recitation (classical pedagogy), memory work, liturgical/calendar integration, rhetoric training, public speaking, and structured memory-based recall. (Classical pedagogy lexicon cues; site mentions of Trivium-based pedagogy and memory-oriented activities) 
  - Negative keywords present: explicit signals of non-classical, constructivist, or project-based-only approaches are not evident in the public content; however, the site does not fully document a campus-wide Socratic program in every grade, and some elements (e.g., project-based learning emphasis, experiential learning) are not clearly enumerated as core practice. This yields a Mixed signal rather than a full Classic pedagogy in explicit public documentation. 

- Why this rating
  - The pedagogy is clearly informed by classical methods (grammar/recitation, Socratic-like inquiry, rhetoric). Yet, explicit, scalable documentation of Socratic seminars across all high school grades and a full articulation of ritualized scholé/restful pedagogy across the entire K-12 program are not uniformly documented on public pages, leading to a Somewhat Classical assessment.

Aggregate assessment (Element-by-Element): CLASSICAL

- The school exhibits CLASSICAL strength in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, Faculty, and Parents/Community, with solid CLASSICAL alignment. Curriculum and Pedagogy show robust Classical elements but with some gaps (notably explicit Great Books/Quadrivium integration and uniform, documented Socratic pedagogy across all grades). These gaps move Categories 3 and 4 toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, while Categories 1, 2, 5, and 6 clearly reach CLASSICAL.

- Overall, Veritas Christian School KS 66047 is BEST DESCRIBED AS CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Add explicit Great Books and primary-source engagements as a core, cross-disciplinary element (e.g., a canonical Great Books list, regular primary-text readings across subjects, and documented cross-curricular connections that reflect the “Great Conversation”). Explicitly name Great Books and Quadrivium elements (e.g., emphasis on Western civilization through primary sources, Latin/Greek where feasible) to move to FULL CLASSICAL. 
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Document and scale explicit Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars across more grades, with a public articulation of the memory-work and contemplative pedagogy (scholé) as a district-wide practice. Demonstrate a consistent upper-school rhetoric pipeline and opportunities for public speaking and declamation across all grades. 
- Element 3/4 Synergy: Publish a holistic, publicly accessible articulation of how Great Books, Great Conversation, grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, and memory-based pedagogy are interwoven across grades (K–12), to shift Element 3 to CLASSICAL and Element 4 to CLASSICAL.

- Summary needle-moving recommendations (concise):
  - Publish and publicize a canonical Great Books list with integration across disciplines.
  - Expand and document rigorous Socratic pedagogy across upper grades with structured seminars.
  - Explicitly connect history and literature to the Great Conversation and to the Great Books canon.
  - Continue to foreground liturgical and communal practices as formal components of pedagogy (to reinforce Element 1’s liturgical alignment).
