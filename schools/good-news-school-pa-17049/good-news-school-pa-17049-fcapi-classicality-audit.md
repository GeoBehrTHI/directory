## Executive Summary

Good News School (McAlisterville, PA, 17049) presents a distinctively religious, church-centered educational context. Public-facing materials emphasize Biblical faith, church life, and Christian formation of both students and families. While the site evidences a robust program of faith-based formation and parental partnership, it lacks clear, explicit indicators of classical liberal-arts education as defined by the Six Essential Elements (e.g., Great Books, the Trivium/Trios, Great Conversation, Socratic pedagogy, or scholé-based contemplative memory work) in its public materials. On the classical rubric, this leads to a mixed positioning: strong religious formation and family-partnered learning align with some classical aims, but the absence of core classical curricular and pedagogical features yields a NOT CLASSICAL posture in several categories. Taken together, the aggregate picture lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL at best, edging toward NOT CLASSICAL given the emphasis on theology, church life, and Christian formation over explicit classical curriculum and pedagogy.

Key takeaway: The school’s strengths lie in religious/metaphysical grounding and explicit parent/community partnership within a Christian framework. The major gaps are explicit classical curriculum (Great Books, liberal arts emphasis) and classical teaching methods (Socratic inquiry, memorization/recitation, Scholé pedagogy). Addressing these gaps would move the school toward a fuller CLASSICAL profile.

Therefore, NOT CLASSICAL on the rubric’s terms, with clear church-theology emphasis.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The Good News materials emphasize formation and the integration of faith, which align with formation-oriented goals.
- Negative indicators (e.g., explicit “career/workforce readiness” emphasis or a solely information-based aim) are not present in the visible materials.

Why this rating

However, without explicit classical aims like “liberal arts for formation” or articulation of true/beautiful as universal teloi in the public materials, the alignment sits between formation-focused and explicitly classical. Hence, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.
