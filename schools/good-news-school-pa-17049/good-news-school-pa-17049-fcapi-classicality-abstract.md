## ABSTRACT

- Overall Classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Why: The Good News site exhibits strong religious/metaphysical grounding (Element 1) and explicit parental partnership (Element 6) which align with core classical aims of virtue-formation and formation through community. Element 2 shows formation-oriented goals, though without explicit classical telos beyond religious formation. However, the Curriculum (Element 3), Pedagogy (Element 4), and Faculty (Element 5) sections lack explicit classical features such as Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy, and the “living curriculum” faculty model. Consequently, the aggregate classification settles at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

- Summary of 6 Categories
    - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
    - School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL
    - Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL
    - Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL
    - Parents & Community: CLASSICAL

- Key implications: The school’s religious formation and family-partnered approach are meaningful strengths. To achieve a full CLASSICAL ranking, the school would need to articulate and implement a classical liberal-arts curriculum (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium) and a pedagogy anchored in Socratic inquiry and Scholé.

- Notable evidence sources
    - Bible/theology grounding and worship emphasis in public materials.
    - Parental partnership in Christian education and home formation.
    - Christian Education Committee language about equipping parents/children with the Word and home-based formation.
    - Classical education benchmarks and definitions (Six Essential Elements / ICE materials) used to frame the analysis.
