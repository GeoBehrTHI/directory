## Executive Summary

Great Hearts Christian Academies – Gilbert (GHCA Gilbert) presents a distinctly classical Christian education profile. Across all six rubric categories, GHCA Gilbert aligns strongly with classical aims: it grounds its mission in truth, goodness, and beauty; it emphasizes formation over mere information; it implements a robust classical curriculum with Scripture integration; it employs a pedagogy oriented toward Socratic inquiry and contemplative (scholé) practice; it presents faculty as mentors and a living curriculum; and it partners with families and the broader faith community to form a shared educational culture. Public materials consistently reference Christ-centered formation, the Great Hearts classical model, Great Books/reading lists, morning prayer, and mission statements that center on virtue and character formation. These patterns are well-supported by GHCA Gilbert’s own pages on mission, curriculum, pedagogy foundations, academy life, admissions, and culture, which collectively document a cohesive classical-Christian program. Evidence drawn from the school’s site and academy materials shows explicit commitments to truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and a liberal-arts–driven formation pursued through dialogic pedagogy, living text, and prayerful, liturgical rhythms.

### Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL

The K-8 curriculum includes English/Literature, History, Latin, Reading Lists (K-5 and Middle School), and a focus on classical authors and Great Books through assigned lists. This demonstrates explicit liberal-arts underpinnings and canonical texts.  
- The curriculum documentation highlights the Seven Liberal Arts spirit (grammar, logic, rhetoric) via explicit elements such as grammar in English, Latin, reading lists, and rhetoric-focused elements (e.g., Latin and composition in upper grades). The presence of Great Books and a “Great Conversation” orientation (reading lists; classic texts) aligns with classic curriculum architecture. 
- The “Curriculum” page explicitly states “Great Hearts classical curriculum with key refinements and additions” including morning prayer and a focus on sacred texts.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

This is a hallmark of classical pedagogy (Socratic discussion, dialectic, memory- and recitation-based approaches) and is reinforced by mentions of living books, formation through memory, and a “scholé” (contemplative) dimension. 
- The school emphasizes a contemplative/restful dimension (scholé) and memory practices (chanting, memory deepening) in alignment with classical pedagogy; lower/early grades emphasize wonder, singing, and memory devices as educational drivers. These elements align with the recommended classical pedagogical approaches. 
- There are explicit references to upper-grade Socratic discussion and rhetoric training within the classical framework, with a progression from foundational memory and contemplation to dialogic inquiry and public speech in higher grades. (Curriculum and Academy Life materials.) 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic instruction, seminars, discourse, rhetoric, memory work, chanting, wonder, scholé, living books, classical pedagogy, great conversation, dialectic, lecture/recitation (where applicable), public speaking, debates. These terms appear across foundation, curriculum, and academy-life materials.  
- Negative keywords: The presence of experiential/project-based language is minimal; the lexicon’s negative signals for pedagogy (e.g., over-reliance on project-based or constructivist approaches) do not characterize GHCA Gilbert’s public materials. This absence supports CLASSICAL. 

Why this rating
- GHCA Gilbert’s pedagogy is anchored in Socratic questioning, classical memory and recitation, and a progression toward rhetoric and public speaking, with a contemplative/scholé dimension. This matches the classical pedagogy framework (even in programs that extend through middle school) and supports CLASSICAL.

### Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Why this rating

Aggregate assessment
