## Executive Summary

Southeast Ohio Classical Academy (SOCA) presents a robust classical-leaning program centered on moral formation, character, and a content-rich liberal arts-oriented curriculum. Public-facing materials emphasize virtue, truth, and beauty as real, knowable ideals; a goal of forming virtuous citizens rather than merely preparing students for the workforce; and a curriculum that foregrounds reading classics, a liberal arts orientation, and teacher-led, disciplined pedagogy. This alignment signals a classical direction, but with notable gaps: explicit theological or liturgical foundations, and explicit high-school–level Socratic pedagogy are not clearly foregrounded in the available public materials. Taken together across all six categories, SOCA earns an overall rating of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- Religious/metaphysical foundations are present in terms of virtue, moral formation, and transcendental ideals (truth, goodness, beauty), but explicit theology or liturgical worship is not evident in the public content. This yields a partial classical alignment in Element 1.
- Purpose and goals center on virtue and wisdom, formation, and the reality of truth and beauty, which aligns strongly with classical aims (Element 2: CLASSICAL).
- Curriculum includes liberal-arts–macing elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric appear as a frame in classical education theory; SOCA emphasizes classics, reading the classics, Latin, grammar and composition, and a Hillsdale-based curriculum). However, explicit seven liberal arts articulation, and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language are less directly stated in SOCA’s public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 3.
- Pedagogy shows a mix: contemplative/repose and morning assemblies (scholé-like rhythms) plus teacher-led instruction; high-school Socratic seminars are not clearly evidenced in public materials (Element 4: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL).
- Faculty and community engagement are solid: visible emphasis on virtuous teaching, “living curriculum,” and partnership with families; the school positions teachers as mentors and the community as co-educators (Categories 5 and 6: CLASSICAL).
- The aggregate classification, balancing strong virtue formation and community engagement against some gaps in explicit Classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic high-school pedagogy, explicit Great Books language), falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

 - While publicly present, further codify the living-curriculum concept with explicit examples of faculty modeling virtues and professional development anchored in classical pedagogy (Element 5).

# ABSTRACT

Southeast Ohio Classical Academy (SOCA) demonstrates a robust classical orientation with clear emphasis on virtue, formation, and a content-rich curriculum. Across the six categories, SOCA shows strong alignment in mission/purpose (formation of virtue and truth-seeking), curriculum (classical reading and liberal-arts orientation), faculty (living curriculum and mentor-teacher model), and parent/community engagement (co-teacher partnerships and openness to visitors). However, explicit theological/metaphysical framing and certain high-school classical pedagogy elements (Socratic seminars, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language, Seven Liberal Arts articulation) are not consistently evident in publicly accessible materials. This leads to an overall SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. SOCA foregrounds virtue, truth, and beauty, and includes communal/even liturgical rhythms like Morning Assembly, but lacks explicit theology or liturgical worship in public materials. Positive alignment with transcendental moral realism is present, but explicit theological grounding remains underrepresented in public communications. (Evidence: mission, vision, and communal practices; classical-education framing.) 
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. SOCA emphasizes formation, virtue, and the reality of truth/beauty as guiding ideals, aligning with classical aims beyond mere workforce preparation. 
- School Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The curriculum includes a strong classic-reading emphasis, Latin, grammar/composition, and cross-disciplinary aims, with Hillsdale/K-12 alignment, but the public materials do not consistently articulate a full Seven Liberal Arts framework or a formal Great Books/Great Conversation program.
- School Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Elements of contemplative rhythm and teacher-led instruction exist, but explicit high-school Socratic seminars or debate programs are not clearly evidenced in public materials.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. The living-curriculum approach and mentor-teacher model are clearly present, with explicit faculty portraits and emphasis on virtuous teaching.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong parent-partnering culture, parent-education opportunities, and welcoming access to school events are evident.

Overall, SOCA presents a coherent classical frame with consequential strengths in virtue formation, curriculum emphasis on classics and liberal arts, faculty emphasis, and strong parent/community engagement. The gaps—explicit theological framing, fully articulated Great Books/Great Conversation pedagogy, and explicit Seven Liberal Arts articulation—explain the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL aggregate. The identified needle-moving opportunities point toward amplifying classical depth across theology, Great Books, and Socratic pedagogy to reach a full CLASSICAL status.
