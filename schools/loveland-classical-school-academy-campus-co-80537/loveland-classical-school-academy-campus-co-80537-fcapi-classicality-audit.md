## Executive Summary

Loveland Classical School Academy Campus Co 80537 presents itself as a public charter K–12 institution with a classical branding and a mission centered on character formation, virtuous conduct, and partnership with parents. Public-facing materials emphasize virtue, critical thinking, academic effort, and the cultivation of core virtues as guiding aims. The site does not prominently advertise explicit theology, liturgical practices, or a Great Books-based curriculum. It does, however, foreground character formation, virtues, and parental partnership as central to its mission.

Overall, based on the six-category classical-rubric, the school shows clear alignment with certain classical aims (notably in School Purpose and Goal and Parents & Community) but limited explicit evidence across Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty, and only partial alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations. The aggregate assessment is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete strengths in virtuous formation and family partnership, and notable missing anchors such as explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium coverage, and the full range of Scholé-style pedagogy in public-facing materials.

Key takeaways:
- Strengths: Emphasis on virtuous character, moral formation, and family partnership; public articulation of core virtues and a mission centered on excellence and service; visible commitment to parental involvement.
- Gaps: Absence of explicit Great Books/Trivium-based curriculum language, explicit theology or liturgical practice, and explicit pedagogy markers (Socratic dialogue, Scholé rhythms, memorization/recitation) in the public materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school aims to advance toward a full CLASSICAL designation, the following needle-moving steps are recommended:
  - Curriculum shift: Introduce explicit classical-curriculum language and components (Seven Liberal Arts, Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books) into formal communications and planning, or clearly map Core Knowledge into a classical framework where appropriate, and articulate how Great Books or canonical texts are integrated.
  - Pedagogy adoption: Incorporate visible classical pedagogy markers (Socratic dialogue, Scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, and imitation of masterworks) into the school's approach and provide public examples of these methodologies in practice.
  - Theology/Liturgy clarity (where appropriate): If the school intends to maintain a non-religious or secular charter identity, clarify the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category’s stance (e.g., move toward a more explicit explanation of transcendental aims or, conversely, justify secular humanist alignment with classical formation). If the school intends to incorporate religious elements, articulate them clearly in policy and practice, including liturgical and communal practices, while staying within charter commitments.
  - Faculty development: Highlight a faculty profile that positions teachers as living exemplars of virtue and mentors for students, with explicit statements about teacher formation and pedagogy aligned to classical aims.
  - Curriculum transparency: Publish a publicly accessible curriculum map that demonstrates the alignment with classical aims (liberal arts, Great Conversation, Great Books, primary sources) and how students engage with canonical texts or foundational thinkers.
  - Deepen Great Books/Great Conversation engagement: If staying with a classical aim, explicitly incorporate Great Conversation through canonical texts and structured discussions, including opportunities for student-led inquiry, Socratic seminars, and rhetoric training for upper grades.

- Rationale: The needle-moving changes focus on making explicit the classical elements already present in the school’s mission (virtue formation, parent partnership) and addressing the current gaps in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty language to reach a full CLASSICAL designation.
