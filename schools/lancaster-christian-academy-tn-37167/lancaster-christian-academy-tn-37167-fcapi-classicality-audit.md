## Executive Summary

Lancaster Christian Academy TN 37167 (as reflected in the associated school-crawl materials from Spring Hill Christian Academy on the SHC site) presents a robust Classical Christian education profile. Public-facing materials consistently frame education as formation and character formation through a Christian, liturgical, and traditional liberal-arts pathway. Core elements appear across the public materials: the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books and the Great Conversation, memory-based pedagogy (chanting, recitation, and musically reinforced learning in the grammar stage), Socratic discussion in upper grades, and explicit integration of theology, Scripture, and worship into daily life and curriculum. Family partnerships with parents as co-educators are repeatedly prioritized, and the school emphasizes virtue, moral formation, and a God-centered worldview. While not state-accredited, SHC is certified by Christian accrediting bodies (TACS and AACS) and stresses a liturgical, communal, and reverent approach to schooling.

Applying the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education rubric (via the knowledge-base criteria and keyword lexicon) yields CLASSICAL across all six categories. The school’s public materials exhibit foundational religious-metaphysical aims (truth/beauty/goodness, Scripture, liturgy, weekly chapel), a telos of virtue and wisdom (formation over information), a Trivium-centered curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books; Great Conversation), a pedagogy combining contemplative/restful and Socratic/seminar approaches, a faculty vision aligned with living-mentor teachers and a culture of virtue, and a pronounced partnership with parents and the broader Christian community. The lexicon-based keyword analysis (positive/negative keyword indicators) supports a strong Classical orientation, with explicit textual anchors such as “Truth, goodness, beauty,” theology/scripture, liturgy/chapel, Great Books, Great Conversation, Trivium, memory through chant and recitation, Socratic discussion in upper school, and parent partnership. No significant negative-classical signals (e.g., heavy emphasis on project-based or career-prep language as the sole aim) appear in the public materials.
