## ABSTRACT

Founders Classical Academy Mesquite (75149) is assessed as NOT CLASSICAL across the six elements of the classical rubric. The public materials visible in the school’s crawl emphasize conventional early-childhood care, enrichment programming, and open-parent access rather than the core classical pillars: transcendental moral realism; theological grounding; Great Books/Great Conversation with a Trivium-based curriculum; Socratic pedagogy in higher grades; a living-faculty curriculum; and a deeply integrated parent-as-co-educator model. Keyword analysis against the Six Essential Elements framework confirms the absence of explicit classical signals in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty, with only a partial alignment in Parents & Community (open-door policy and parental updates via SproutAbout). The aggregate evaluation is NOT CLASSICAL.

- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: No explicit transcendental moral realism, theology, liturgy, or sacred communal practices evidenced. 
- School Purpose & Goal: No explicit formation-focused or truth/goodness/beauty-oriented mission statements visible. 
- Curriculum: Public materials reveal infant-to-pre-K programming and electives, but no liberal-arts curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric) or Great Books. 
- Pedagogy: No clear classical pedagogy (Socratic, Scholé, memorization through memory work, or rhetoric training) is described. 
- Faculty: Praises of staff exist, but no living-curriculum or model-teacher framework is described. 
- Parents & Community: Open-door policy and parent-communication app usage indicate some partnership with parents, but not the robust co-education/parent-education model characteristic of classical schools. 

Overall, the school presents NOT CLASSICAL through six category lenses, with minor alignment in Parents & Community that is insufficient to raise the aggregate rating. The corpus of classical criteria (as detailed in the Six Essential Elements and supported by ICE materials) would require substantial additions to policy language, curriculum design, pedagogy, and family partnerships to move FCAM Mesquite toward classical education.
