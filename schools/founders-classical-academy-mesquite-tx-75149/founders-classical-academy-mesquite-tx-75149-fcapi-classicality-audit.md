## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy Mesquite (75149) is evaluated as NOT CLASSICAL based on the publicly observable content in the school’s crawl materials and cross-referenced against the classical rubric drawn from theSix Essential Elements of a Classical School (1). The available public materials from the school’s crawl emphasize early-childhood and elementary care, enrichment electives, family-facing technologies, and an open-door parental approach, but they do not demonstrate core classical elements such as Great Books culture, Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), sustained Socratic pedagogy in middle/upper grades, liturgical or theological grounding, or a formalized living curriculum. The knowledge-base rubric defines six domains—Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose and Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community—and assigns a level (Classical / Somewhat Classical / Not Classical) based on keyword matches to Positive/Negative indicators. In this assessment, Category-by-Category keyword analyses yield predominantly Not Classical results, with only partial alignment in Parents & Community due to an open-door policy and parent-facing apps, which is insufficient to elevate the overall rating to Classical or Even Somewhat Classical. The aggregate judgment is NOT CLASSICAL.

- Theoretical rubric anchors (from the Six Essential Elements) emphasize transcendental morals (truth, goodness, beauty), theology, liturgical practices, Great Books and Great Conversation, contemplative pedagogy, a Trivium-based curriculum, and a living faculty/parent partnership as foundational to classical education. These indicators are not evidenced in the school’s public materials, per the crawl results and the knowledge-base rubric. See the Six Essential Elements keyword framework for reference (Positive keywords include truth, virtue, Great Books, scholé, liturgical practices, etc.; Negative cues cover secular/values-neutral or overly job-focused schooling) . The ICE pamphlets and related classical-education literature further illustrate the classical aims (restful learning, memory, the triad of grammar-logic-rhetoric, and the Great Conversation), which are not explicitly mapped in the Founders Classical Academy Mesquite crawl content available here .

## What Would Move the Needle

- For a rigorous move toward CLASSICAL, FCAM Mesquite would need to demonstrate, in public-facing content, explicit alignment with the Six Essential Elements, including:
  - A clear Religious/Metaphysical Foundation: explicit references to transcendent truths, virtues, and beauty; theology or liturgical practices; communal worship or sacred time integrated into daily life (or a stated theology-informed framework).
  - A Purpose and Goal aligned with virtue and wisdom formation, not job-preparation or purely information-based learning, with explicit language about forming humans of character and intellect.
  - A Curriculum built on liberal arts fundamentals (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a throughline of Great Books/Great Conversation, with regular study of history as a continuous through-line (not as “social studies” alone) and explicit engagement with wonder as educational fuel.
  - Pedagogy featuring contemplative/restful elements (Scholé), early-language memory and singing in lower grades, and high-school-level Socratic seminars, debates, and rhetoric instruction in upper grades.
  - Faculty as living curriculum and mentors modeling virtue, forming lasting relationships, and a clearly articulated teacher-student-mentor culture.
  - A robust Parents & Community program featuring active partnership with parents as co-educators, structured parent-education opportunities, and easy, frequent on-site access for visits and involvement.

Citations: The Six Essential Elements framework (Positive Keywords and category indicators) provides concrete targets; classical-education literature (ICE Pamphlet) outlines specific practices such as Scholé, Great Books, and living-teacher models as moves toward classical education ; and Classical Education literature describes the pedagogical and partnership practices that define the “needle” changes (e.g., Scholé, Great Conversation, teacher formation) .

Notes: Given the current observed alignment (largely non-classical in curriculum, pedagogy, and metaphysical foundations), the needle-moving changes would need to be substantial and publicly demonstrated, including a clearly articulated classical curriculum, classical pedagogy, and a theology/formation framework, along with an explicit partnership model with parents and a living-teaching faculty.
