## Executive Summary

Regina Coeli Academy (RCA) Pa 19001 presents a robust Catholic, classical framework centered on the liberal arts, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and a liturgical, faith-informed approach to education. Public materials emphasize a Catholic formation, a time-tested classical curriculum, small class sizes, and an emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and community partnership with families. While RCA strongly signals classical foundations—particularly in its curriculum, theology, and pedagogy cues—the site does not overtly market a Great Books or explicit “Great Conversation” emphasis, which is a hallmark in some classical schools. This places RCA in the CLASSICAL category overall, with two clear areas that align more with a “SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL” characterization (Curriculum and Pedagogy emphasis that could more fully align with certain classical descriptors, notably the explicit Great Books/Great Conversation tradition and some high-level Socratic-seminar pedagogy that typically appears in higher-grade classical programs). See category-by-category notes below for evidence, keyword tallies, and the rationale for each rating.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- Core religious identity and liturgical life are clearly integrated into daily learning (Mass, liturgy, catechesis, Magisterial teaching, Scripture and Tradition) . 
- The curriculum centers on classical liberal arts and the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with explicit Catholic formation woven through the curriculum (Latin/Greek instruction noted; Great Books not explicitly listed)  .
- Pedagogy includes contemplative/restful elements and memory work (e.g., singing, recitation in lower grades; public speaking and rhetoric focus in some upper-level elements) with evidence of seminars and student forums in practice (Student Forum; public speaking expectations)  .
- Faculty are presented as hands-on mentors and the school emphasizes small class sizes and a living-curriculum approach; Parent and community partnership is highlighted (family formation, open campus, parent involvement)   .

Overall assessment: CLASSICAL
- The aggregate signals across six categories indicate a consistent alignment with classical education principles: a faith-centered, Trivium-informed curriculum; a liturgical and communal Catholic life; a living-teacher/mentoring faculty; and an active partnership with families and the broader community. The primary reason the Curriculum and Pedagogy categories are marked SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is the relatively limited explicit emphasis on Great Books and entirely Socratic/Hogarth-style seminar pedagogy in the publicly available materials—elements many other classical schools foreground strongly. The presence of Trivium, liberal arts framing, liturgical integration, small-class culture, and a robust Catholic formation and family-partner model, however, makes RCA Classically aligned overall.

What Would Move the Needle (only if SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)
- Add explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and corresponding content to the Curriculum and reading lists, and highlight a plan for integrating Great Books into the Grades 6–8 sequence to align with a stricter Great Books-based classical model. 
- Expand visible, scalable Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades (even if not a full high-school program) to echo classical seminar culture, with clear rubrics, exemplar prompts, and student-led discourse.
- Strengthen history-as-history across the curriculum with explicit chronological framing and primary-source study that aligns with classical history pedagogy; emphasize Western Civilization as a through-line across grades.
- Deepen explicit formation in Great Books discussions, memory work, and recitation in more grade bands, including structured opportunities for students to present argumentative and rhetorical pieces.
- Heighten explicit parental formation content on the website (e.g., structured book studies and parent seminars) to reinforce the co-teaching partnership with families and to broaden participation in classical formation outside the classroom.
