## Executive Summary

Ascent Classical Academy of Greenville (Greenville, SC 29607) presents a robust Classical model as reflected in its public materials. The school consistently foregrounds core classical aims—virtue formation, a content-rich liberal-arts curriculum, and disciplined pedagogy grounded in tradition—while also signaling a strong family-partner model typical of Classical schools. Across six rubric categories, the school demonstrates substantial Classical alignment, with Element 1 showing partial alignment due to a relatively lighter emphasis on explicit theology or liturgical practice. The aggregate assessment across the six categories is CLASSICAL, with Element 1 marked as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The school’s public-facing materials repeatedly emphasize truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, memory-work, Great Books/primary sources, Socratic elements, and a parental partnership orientation, all of which map to the Six Essential Elements rubric.

Key signaling keywords observed on the school site include: classical education, liberal arts, Core Knowledge, Great Books, primary sources, Socratic discussion, memory work (narration, recitation), virtue and moral character, civic virtue, wonder, and partnership with families. The public materials also describe a mission to train the minds and improve the hearts of students, via a disciplined, orderly environment and a content-rich curriculum in the liberal arts and sciences. Together, these elements align with a deeply classical approach, with the strongest signals in Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community, and a clearly present but more modest articulation in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence

  - The mission statement emphasizes virtue, moral realism, and reverence for the moral order within a liberal-arts framework (virtue, moral character, civic virtue) as part of forming humane citizens .
  - The site repeatedly links to “truth, goodness, and beauty” and to wonder as educational anchors (e.g., statements about training minds and hearts, and engagement with beauty and moral inquiry) (e.g., The Pursuit of Truth at a Time of Unrest and related content) .

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, moral character, civic virtue, wonder, contemplation, moral imagination.
  - Negative keywords observed: none identified in public materials.
  - Frequency signal: multiple explicit mentions of truth/goodness/beauty and virtue across mission-focused content; no explicit theology or liturgical practice cited in public pages.

- Why this rating
  - Strong alignment with transcendental moral aims and virtue formation, but explicit theology, liturgy, or communal liturgical practices are not foregrounded in the Greenville public materials. Thus, Element 1 reaches a solid SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than a full CLASSICAL due to the lighter theological/liturgical emphasis in public materials.

Citations: Truth/virtue/beauty and moral formation cited in mission and vision pages; explicit transcendentals language in Extended Mission; civil virtue and moral character emphasis in program descriptions.

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: formation, liberal education, paideia, classical mission, telos, pursuit of the true/good/beautiful, heightening wisdom and virtue, character formation, moral imagination, great conversation.
  - Negative keywords observed: explicit emphasis on workforce preparation/career-readiness is not present; no direct emphasis on 21st-century skills or job-market metrics.
  - Frequency signal: Strong presence of formation telos and classical aims; minimal to no appearance of workforce-focused language.

- Why this rating
  - The school explicitly presents formation, virtue, liberal education, and a classical mission as the core aims of education, with a strong emphasis on truth/beauty/goodness in the classical framework. This aligns with CLASSICAL scoring in Element 2.

Citations: Formation/virtue/liberal education/telos language in mission statements; Great Conversation and true/good/beautiful framing in curriculum content.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

  - Negative keywords observed: minimal to no reliance on separate “social studies” framing; no explicit emphasis on project-based learning dominating the curriculum (public materials emphasize content knowledge and classical texts rather than a project-first approach).
  - Frequency signal: Multiple explicit classical curriculum anchors; low presence of non-classical “project-based” dominance or social studies substitution; strong Great Books/primary sources/grammar-logic-rhetoric signals.

- Why this rating
  - The site materials present a content-rich, liberal-arts-forward curriculum with explicit emphasis on Trivium/Quadrivium-adjacent ideas, Great Books/primary sources, and a coherent historical-cultural framework. This aligns solidly with CLASSICAL criteria in Curriculum.

Citations: Core Knowledge description; Great Books/primary sources; Trivium elements from curriculum content; Western civilization framing.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If Element 1 remains SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (due to a lighter explicit theology/liturgy signal), the needle could move toward FULL CLASSICAL by:
  - More explicit articulation of theological grounding (or liturgical/communal practices) within public-facing materials, while preserving the non-denominational stance if required. This would address the Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices criteria.
  - Adding explicit statements about liturgical rhythms, communal worship, or sacred tradition in attendance or chapel contexts, where aligned with the school’s philosophy.

- Optional enhancements (not strictly required given current strength, but would fortify CLASSICAL alignment):
  - A clearer articulation of the Great Books component as a formal requirement or explicit Great Conversation framing within the Great Books curriculum map (beyond primary sources and Core Knowledge) to reinforce the Great Conversation criterion.
  - Expanded explicit reference to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as foundational to K–12 pedagogy across curriculum maps, with concrete examples of how these three arts shape instruction in each grade level.

Citations: Theoretical alignment with the Six Elements’ Element 1 subcriteria; suggested enhancements for theology/liturgy signals.
