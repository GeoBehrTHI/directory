## ABSTRACT

Christ's Legacy Academy (CLA) demonstrates a solid classical Christian orientation, anchored by explicit Trivium-based curriculum, a biblical worldview, and strong family partnerships. In the six-category rubric, CLA scores CLASSICAL in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Faculty, and Parents & Community; and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy. This suggests a robust classical identity with some gaps in explicit canonical curricular components (notably Great Books/Great Conversation) and detailed contemplative/pedagogical practices in public materials.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL, driven by explicit theology, Scripture authority, and a discipleship framework. Positive keywords (God, glory to God, biblical worldview, Scripture, Word of God, inerrant/infallible Word) are prevalent; negative signals are absent. 

- School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, as formation and biblical purposes are strong, but explicit expressions of classic telos, virtue, and paideia language are less overt in the public materials. The presence of “classically educate” signals classical alignment, but a fully explicit classical telos is not consistently articulated. 

- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear Trivium framing (grammar, logic, rhetoric) but less explicit Great Books/Great Conversation content within the crawl results. Positive signals include Trivium and classical framing; gaps exist regarding canonical Great Books references. 

- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with Trivium-based emphasis and rhetoric training; however, explicit Socratic seminars, scholé/restful pedagogy, and direct evidence of contemplative pedagogy are not consistently documented in public content. 

- Faculty: CLASSICAL, supported by the “living curriculum” concept and teacher-as-mentors framing, with staff development and Christ-centered expectations. 

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL, with strong partnership language and parental involvement as central to classical formation. 

Overall, CLA presents a credible CLASSICAL orientation with meaningful strengths in religious foundations, faculty, and parent/community engagement, and with room to more explicitly articulate canonical curriculum components (Great Books) and certain pedagogical practices (Socratic seminars, scholé rhythms) in its public materials.

- Sources and key signals: CLA Distinctives (Mission, Vision, Trivium); Authority of Scripture; Great Books/Great Conversation signals are not strongly explicit in crawl text; Classical lexicon signals ground the rubric and keyword framework.
