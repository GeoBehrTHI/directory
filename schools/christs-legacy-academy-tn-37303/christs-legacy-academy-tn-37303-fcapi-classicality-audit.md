## Executive Summary

Christ's Legacy Academy (CLA) in Athens, TN presents a clearly Christian and classical orientation, underpinned by explicit commitments to Trivium-based learning (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a biblically-centered worldview, and robust partnership with families. Public materials from CLA emphasize a biblical foundation, discipleship, and a mission to form character and wisdom in students within a classical framework. The school’s public materials also showcase a strong faculty and community orientation, including staff biographies and a stated mission to partner with families in the formation of students. However, while the institution foregrounds classical language (Trivium) and covenantal, biblical aims, explicit references to several canonical classical components (notably Great Books/Great Conversation and certain contemplative pedagogies that frequently characterize classical schools) are less evident in the public content. On balance, CLA demonstrates a solid classical Christian orientation, with stronger evidence in religious foundations and mission/community, and more modest explicit alignment in some curricular/educational-method facets.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- The strongest evidence lies in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Faculty, and Parents & Community, where explicit references to God, Scripture, discipleship, Trivium, and partnership with families are visible. See the CLA Distinctives (mission, vision, theology, and classical approach) and the articulation of Trivium-based learning as core to the program. 

- The Curriculum and Pedagogy dimensions show clear classical scaffolding (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric), but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and some traditional pedagogical practices (e.g., Socratic seminars, memory work, scholé) are not consistently foregrounded in the public material. This leads to a category reading of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for those areas. 

- The Faculty and Parents & Community categories show strong alignment with the classical model and a robust, forming partnership with families, which supports a CLASSICAL reading for those areas.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Details

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school presents a theologically grounded mission and worldview. Primary statements emphasize partnership with families to educate students to think and live for God’s glory, i.e., a transcendent aim tied to virtue and truth in a biblical frame. 
- Authority of Scripture is explicit: “The foundation for all we teach and do is the inerrant, infallible, authoritative Word of God” and “All Scripture is God-breathed.” These quotations anchor moral realism and theological grounding in the public materials. 
- The school articulates a discipleship model and a preeminent Christology within a classical framework, reinforcing a sacramental/theological orientation and a liturgical-leaning sense of formation within community. 
- The Vision and Mission foreground biblical literacy, Scripture-informed discernment, and a God-centered telos for students. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators found (examples from public materials): God, glory to God, biblical worldview, Scripture, Word of God, inerrant/infallible Word of God, discipleship, Christ, preeminence of Christ, theology. Across the sources, roughly 7–8 distinct positive signals are evident (e.g., “biblical worldview,” “Authority of Scripture,” “Trivium” as a core framework, “Discipleship Model”). 
- Negative indicators: None of the negative signals from the lexicon (e.g., secular humanism, relativism, values clarification) are present in the public materials. The content is consistently theologically anchored rather than secular. The classical lexicon’s negative signals are not evidenced here. 

Why this rating
- The explicit theological framing, scriptural authority, and discipleship orientation align strongly with the RELIGIOUS AND METAPHYSICAL FOUNDATIONS criteria of a classical school. The absence of any secular competing claims or explicit relativistic messaging further supports a CLASSICAL designation for this category. The presence of the Trivium in their classical approach further anchors this category in a traditional classical frame.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize formation and character in a classical, biblical frame rather than mere information transfer. Phrases such as “form a biblical worldview,” “disciple students,” and a mission to develop discernment and godly thinking appear in the school’s stated aims. 
- The Mission emphasizes forming students to think and engage the world for God’s glory in a classical, well-formed manner, with emphasis on virtue-informed thinking and moral formation. The Vision speaks to discerning thinking and articulacy within Scriptural framing. 
- The presence of “classical” phrasing—i.e., “classically educate” and “classical approach”—demonstrates an orientation toward paideia and virtue education rather than solely job-readiness or information delivery. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators observed: formation, virtue (implied via discipleship and godly formation), wisdom (implied via discernment and thoughtful reasoning), liberal education and classical mission (explicit CLA language). The materials repeatedly frame education as formation and worldview-building rather than purely technical job-readiness. The explicit "classically educate" language supports alignment with the classical mission. 
- Negative indicators: The materials do not emphasize work-force readiness, 21st-century skills, or career-preparatory language in a direct sense; however, explicit references to the Great Books/Great Conversation are not strong in the public materials. This reduces the degree to which all standard “purpose-and-goal” markers are affirmed. From the lexicon, the strongest negatives would be workforce-focused rhetoric, which is not present. 

Why this rating
- The school’s stated aims clearly align with formation and virtue-oriented education consistent with classical ideals; however, there is less explicit articulation of the full spectrum of classical purpose markers (e.g., explicit paideia framework, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation phrasing). The result is a strong classical orientation in purpose but not a fully explicit articulation across all the canonical markers, hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The program explicitly highlights a Trivium-based pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) which implies a mix of memorization (grammar stage), reasoning (logic stage), and oratory/writing (rhetoric stage) across grade levels. This aligns with classical pedagogy fundamentals. 
- The public content references upper-level rhetoric training and expected rhetoric output (e.g., “logic and rhetoric in all subjects” and the emphasis on critical thinking and reasoning). The exact phrasing about Socratic seminars or seminars/debates is not consistently explicit, though the pedagogical direction suggests a classical orientation in higher grades. 
- Lower-school pedagogy in public content is less explicit about contemplative or restful approaches and about singing/chanting/memory work (which are common features of scholé in many classical programs). The explicit mentions of singing/reminiscent memory in lower grades are less visible in CLA’s public pages, though the broader “living curriculum” framing exists in the governance material. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: Trivium framing; rhetoric training in upper grades; pivotal classical pedagogy concepts such as memory, recitation, and disciplined reasoning are suggested by the overall classical approach and job postings that emphasize professional development and classical formation. 
- Negative indicators: Direct mentions of contemplative/scholé rest or singing/chanting in lower grades are not clearly evidenced in the public crawl; explicit statements about Socratic seminars or regular Socratic discourse in the upper school are not strongly visible in the crawl content. The presence of a strong classical approach exists, but the explicit pedagogy signals typically associated with scholé and contemplative/restful learning are not clearly documented in the public content. 

Why this rating
- The pedagogy is solidly classical in its Trivium orientation and rhetoric emphasis, but the absence of explicit, consistently documented contemplative/restful pedagogy (scholé), formal Socratic seminars, and explicit lower-to-upper-school contemplative practices in the crawl content keeps this category from a full CLASSICAL rating. The evidence places CLA in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category for Pedagogy.

### Element 5: School Faculty — CLASSICAL

- The school emphasizes the faculty as mentors and the “portrait of a teacher” that frames expectations for teacher conduct and formation, aligning with the classical model of teachers as the living curriculum. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators found: living curriculum, faculty as mentors, portrait of teacher/student ideals, Christ-centered professionalism, collaboration and collegiality (as implied by staff development and teamwork). The CLA materials frequently use language that positions teachers as moral exemplars and central to formation. 
- Negative indicators: No clear negative signals; the materials do not present anti-classical pedagogy or anti-classical staff models. The lexicon’s negative signals (e.g., “facilitators” on the side, purely certification-driven jargon) are not evident in the CLA materials; rather, there is emphasis on character and formation. 

Why this rating
- The faculty portrayal as mentors, exemplars, and “living curriculum” strongly aligns with classical tradition. The combination of mission-driven staff expectations and Christ-centered teacher formation supports a CLASSICAL designation for the Faculty category.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): Introduce explicit, language-forward articulation of virtuous formation (virtue, wisdom, telos) and connect those terms to a clearly stated paideia framework beyond “classical” branding. Publish a succinct statement listing the exact classical aims (e.g., formation, pursuit of truth/beauty/goodness) with explicit references to classical virtues.
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Add explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and commitments to studying canonical texts and primary sources alongside the Trivium structure. Include a public-facing articulation of core canonical texts and a short justification for their role in Western civilization and Christian thought to strengthen the map of the curriculum.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Document and publish explicit use of Socratic/seminar-style pedagogy in upper grades, plus a plan for integrating contemplative/scholé practices (restful learning, memory work, chant) as part of a weekly rhythm. Provide examples of upper-level seminars, debates, and structured rhetorical exercises with documented outcomes.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Expand the “living curriculum” concept with more explicit faculty portraits, professional development plans centered on classical pedagogy, and a visible faculty handbook or charter that describes teacher ideals, virtues, and measurable formation targets.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Create a recurring, public-facing calendar of parent formation opportunities (book studies, lectures, family liturgies or celebrations) and a clear, accessible path for open-campus visits and ongoing parental involvement that aligns with classical community expectations.
