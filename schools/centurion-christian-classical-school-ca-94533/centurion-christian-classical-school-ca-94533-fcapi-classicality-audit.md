## Executive Summary

Centurion Christian Classical School CA 94533 presents a distinctly classical Christian program. Across public materials on its site, the school foregrounds Christian orthodoxy (catechesis, confessional grounding, and Scripture memorization), a classical liberal arts curriculum (Trivium, Quadrivium, Great Books, Great Conversation), and a formation-focused mission (virtue, wisdom, human flourishing) centered on serving Christian families. The pedagogical language emphasizes both reverence for tradition and memory work, with explicit emphasis on theology as foundational to all subjects. Based on the Six Essential Elements framework, the school aligns strongly with CLASSICAL elements in most domains, with a clearly articulated formation focus, confessional theology, classic curriculum, and community partnerships with families and churches. The strongest alignments occur in religious/metaphysical foundations, school purpose/goal, curriculum, faculty orientation, and parent/community collaboration. Pedagogy shows substantial classical signals (catechesis, memory work, trivium-informed structure), though explicit mention of high-school Socratic seminars or extended upper-school rhetoric seminars is less visible in the material reviewed. Overall, Centurion would be classified as CLASSICAL, with a notable strength in theological integration and Great Books/the Great Conversation, and a potential needle-mover opportunity around expanding explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were NOT CLASSICAL or SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, concrete changes would be suggested here. Since Category 4 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, the following needle-moving steps could move the school toward full CLASSICAL alignment:
 - Implement explicit, regular upper-grade Socratic seminars or seminars with formal rhetoric/speech components to showcase the upper-level classical pedagogy in practice (documented in curriculum guides and annual planning). This would strengthen the Pedagogy signal from SOMEWHAT to CLASSICAL.
 - Expand explicit references to organized “Scholé” or contemplative slow-learning days in all grade bands, with published schedules and outcomes to reinforce Restful Learning as a formal component of daily practice.
 - Publish a formal “Teachers as living curriculum” handbook with concrete exemplars of teacher modeling, mentorship roles, and ongoing classical pedagogy professional development to reinforce Faculty signals.
 - Increase public documentation of great books lists and history-integrated units across all grade bands, including detailed scope-and-sequence maps that demonstrate the integration of Trivium/Quadrivium with Great Books across the curriculum.
