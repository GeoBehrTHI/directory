## Executive Summary

Thomas MacLaren School (Colorado Springs, CO 80915) presents a coherent classical-education orientation expressed through a K–12 liberal-arts framing, a heavy emphasis on Great Books and Great Conversation, Socratic pedagogy, and a shared, sequenced curriculum with limited tracking. Public-facing materials consistently foreground the liberal-arts ethos, memory-work and recitation in pedagogy, and a culture of lifelong learning and human formation. While the school exhibits many elements aligned with classical education, there are modern notes (e.g., Core Knowledge references, mixed curricular signals around history-focused vs. humanities-focused framing) that introduce nuance and prevent a unilateral determination of being fully classical in every dimension. Overall, the school leans CLASSICAL, with some notable areas that could be strengthened to achieve a more consistently classical profile across all six categories.

Key signals supporting CLASSICAL orientation

- Explicit emphasis on Socratic seminars, the Great Conversation, and a liberal-arts education across K–12, including a common, sequenced curriculum (no tracking) and a clear focus on memory work, recitation, and the cultivation of virtue and wisdom through humane letters and philosophy-oriented study. These features appear repeatedly in the school’s public materials.
- Strong same-track, curricular coherence and a litany of Great Books/primary-text engagement (Humane Letters, Western canon, Greek/Hebrew/scriptural readings, etc.), with statements about forming characters and developing a “great conversation” across grades.
- Pedagogy that foregrounds citizenship of mind through seminars, memory, recitation, and rhetoric opportunities, alongside a philosophy of learning as a lifelong, contemplative pursuit.

What would move the needle

- If the aggregate assessment were to tilt toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, concrete steps would include:
    - More explicit articulation of the Trivium and Quadrivium as the structural backbone of the curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric and mathematics/geometry/music/astronomy) in public messaging.
    - Clearer alignment of all curricular strands with the Great Books framework (versus the Core Knowledge reference) to minimize potential signaling of non-classical curricular priorities.
    - Strengthened, explicit language around the role of parents as co-educators in the classical formation process.

# ABSTRACT

: The school demonstrates a robust classical orientation through Great Books/Great Conversation, Socratic pedagogy, and a shared liberal-arts curriculum, with ongoing formation of virtue and wisdom as core aims. There are nuanced signals (e.g., Core Knowledge references) that introduce non-classical threads; nevertheless, the dominant elements align with classical education.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school were aiming to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL in all six categories, concrete steps could include:
    - Explicitly codifying and publicly communicating the Trivium/Quadrivium framework as the curricular backbone (grammar/logic/rhetoric and mathematics/music/astronomy as core strands) and showing how all grades cycle through these components.
    - Deepening the “Great Books/Great Conversation” framing in official curriculum documents (beyond humane-letters) to showcase a sustained, canonical-text approach across grade bands.
    - Strengthening theology/metaphysical signaling (where appropriate in their charter context) through explicit references to transcendentals and a theologically informed, contemplative classroom ethos, while maintaining a public, non-denominational profile.
    - Expanding explicit parent-formation and co-teaching programs (parent seminars, reading groups, family liturgy resources) to strengthen the Parents & Community category.
