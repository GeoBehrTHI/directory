## Executive Summary

St Francis de Sales School (Newark, OH 43055) presents a clearly Catholic, classical-oriented program that centers faith, virtue formation, and a deliberately classical curriculum within a parish school context. Public-facing materials emphasize daily Catholic religious formation, liturgical life, and a classical curriculum, with explicit statements about evangelization, discipleship, and a sacred-liturgical learning environment. The school also highlights a strong partnership with families and active community opportunities (e.g., SPICE, EdChoice). While the site foregrounds core classical commitments (classical curriculum, Catholic formation, and communal worship), explicit references to some classical-methodologies (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or Socratic pedagogy) are less overt in the public material. Taken together, the school strongly aligns with classical Catholic education in multiple dimensions, with room to more visibly document certain classical-methodology specifics on its site.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Executive-level takeaway: The school demonstrates robust Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and a strong formation goal, and it clearly commits to a classical framework in its mission and curriculum presentation. However, the public materials could be more explicit about some canonical classical elements (e.g., detailed articulation of the Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, and explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades) to reach a full, fully-documented CLASSICAL status. The presence of SPICE, EdChoice engagement, and a staff-focused mission strengthens the Parents & Community and Faculty dimensions. See category-by-category details below.

Public pages describe daily religion instruction, Mass multiple days per week, Eucharistic Adoration, Rosary, Lectio Divina, and communal prayer. 
- Materials also emphasize evangelization, discipleship, and a sacramental worldview, with statements about formation in faith and a sacramental understanding of reality. 
- Foundational Catholic identity is reiterated in the “Our Mission” and “K-8 Classical Catholic Education” pages, including explicit articulation of a Catholic, ordered cosmos created in Christ and the centrality of religion to the curriculum.

- Negative keywords: none evident in the public materials (no relativism, secular humanism, or secular ethics signals observed in these pages).

Why this rating
- The school’s materials overtly bind the educational project to Catholic theology, liturgy, and communal worship, with explicit statements of evangelization and discipleship. This maps directly to the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category as Classical in orientation, given the strong transcendental and liturgical framework. The absence of secular or relativistic signals in the public materials supports a Classical alignment in this domain.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

The presence of liturgical life, daily prayer, and a mission to form the whole person suggests a pedagogy that values contemplation, disciplined formation, and community discipline. The SPICE program further signals a focus on inclusive formation and a humane approach to teaching. 
- The classical Catholic framing includes a suggestion of a pedagogy that integrates formation with content, including a disciplined religious formation alongside academic content. The site’s “Is Classical Catholic Education Relevant?” and “Characteristics” pages indicate a pedagogy oriented toward a traditional, formation-forward approach. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Scholé/restful contemplation, memory work/recitation, liturgical integration, formation through imitation, reverence/wonder, catechesis, and reverence. The knowledge lexicon lists terms like Socratic dialogue, memorization/recitation, and memory work as positive indicators for pedagogy, while signaling potential negatives like over-emphasis on experiential/constructivist approaches. The public site demonstrates a strong emphasis on prayer, liturgy, and a formation-forward pedagogy, with less explicit emphasis on Socratic seminars or explicit recitation-based memory work in the visible pages. 
- Negative keywords: The site does not foreground an exclusively project-based, experiential-only pedagogy; however, explicit Socratic seminars and dialogues are not consistently advertised across the visible pages (which keeps this category from a full Classical classification). Why this rating
- The pedagogy leans toward a classical formation approach, combining contemplative and liturgical elements with curricular aims. The lack of explicit, consistent documentation of particular classical teaching methods (e.g., explicit, repeated Socratic seminars or rigorous usage of memory work across the upper grades) means the site remains “SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL” rather than fully CLASSICAL in pedagogy.

## What would move the needle

- Make Great Books, the Trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric), and Great Conversation themes explicit on the public site across multiple pages, with concrete examples of how these are woven into daily K-8 learning, to elevate Element 3 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Provide explicit references to Socratic pedagogy, especially in upper grades, with sample seminar structures, topics, and assessment practices, to move Element 4 toward CLASSICAL.
- Strengthen and publicly surface an explicit “parents as co-teachers” framework (beyond SPICE and enrollment materials), including parent formation programs, at-home learning guidance, and regular parent-partnership events to push Element 6 toward CLASSICAL.
- Include a clear articulation of the classical curriculum sequence (grammar/logic/rhetoric progression in K-12, with explicit historical engagement and Great Books plan) on the Core Curriculum pages to reinforce Element 3 as CLASSICAL.
- Publish a concise, catchy synthesis of the school’s classical pedagogy, including references to memory work, recitation, and liturgical integration, to strengthen Element 4 and Element 5 alignment in public materials.
