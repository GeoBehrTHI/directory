## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical School in 47112 (Corydon, IN) presents a notably theologically grounded, classical-leaning program with clear emphasis on biblical worldview, virtue-centered formation, and strong parental partnership. Public materials consistently highlight a biblical foundation (Biblical Truth, Biblical Worldview, Statement of Faith) and a desire to form independent, thoughtful students through classical methods. However, explicit articulation of core classical curriculum elements (Great Books, grammar–logic–rhetoric sequence) and explicit Socratic pedagogy are not clearly demonstrated in the school’s public materials. The overall assessment, based on the Classical rubric and keyword indicators, lands in the realm of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious foundations are strong and explicit (biblical worldview, statements of faith, theology). This strongly supports Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations) as CLASSICAL.
- The school emphasizes classical instruction and formation but lacks explicit public references to the Great Books program, the liberal-arts curriculum sequence (grammar–logic–rhetoric), and a clearly stated, widespread Socratic pedagogy in the public-facing materials. This pushes multiple categories toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.
- Parental partnership is strongly evident (parents as co-educators; collaboration with families). Element 6 (Parents & Community) clearly aligns with CLASSICAL.
- Faculty are described as solid Christian tutors; the public materials do not consistently articulate the full “living curriculum” or a formal teacher-portrait, placing Element 5 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Categories 3 (Curriculum) and 4 (Pedagogy) show limited explicit evidence of core classical curricular and pedagogical practices; these lean toward NOT CLASSICAL in the absence of explicit Great Books/grammar–logic–rhetoric emphasis and Socratic pedagogy.

- Curriculum explicitness: Public articulation of the Liberal Arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Trivium/Quadrivium structure, and explicit reference to Great Books/Great Conversation in the curriculum sequence.
- Pedagogy expansion: Public detailing of Socratic seminars, Paideia-style discussions, memory work, dictation, chanting or singing as regular practices, and reflective scholé days.
- Faculty signaling: Publicly surface the living-curriculum concept, teacher portraits, and professional development focused on classical-pedagogical methods.
- Liturgical/communal integration: More explicit references to liturgical practices, regular communal worship or liturgy, and structured memory of sacred traditions in the school day.
- Family formation: Expanded parent-formation offerings (book studies, parent seminars) and more explicit invitation to open-campus opportunities and parent-led involvement in the school’s classical mission.

These concrete steps would move the school toward full CLASSICAL across all six categories.
