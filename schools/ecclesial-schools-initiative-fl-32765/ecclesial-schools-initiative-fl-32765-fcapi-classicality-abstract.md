## ABSTRACT

Overall Classification: CLASSICAL

Key takeaways:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are solidly CLASSICAL, anchored in Christian theology, liturgical practice, and ecumenical church partnerships. The public materials emphasize Morning Prayer and a common mission grounded in Christian education across parish-based campuses.  
- School Purpose and Goal are CLASSICAL, focusing on formation, virtue, wisdom, and the good through Christian education and the liberal arts rather than merely job preparation. 
- Curriculum presents a strong liberal-arts orientation and a formation-driven ethos, but lacks explicit, public documentation of canonical Great Books or the Great Conversation, placing Category 3 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Pedagogy shows a contemplative, wonder-centered approach with a strong early-childhood emphasis on play and memory, but explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades is not clearly documented in public materials. This places Category 4 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Faculty framing aligns with classical models, with teachers described as the living curriculum and mentored by a culture of virtue and skill. This is CLASSICAL. 
- Parents & Community are CLASSICAL, with explicit partnerships between families and schools, parent-partnership steps in enrollment, and active involvement in scholarship access and funding.  

Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL
- The network demonstrates clear classical-Christian alignment across foundations, formation-oriented aims, and community partnerships, with strong evidence of a liberal-arts curriculum and faculty role as living curriculum. The explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and high-school Socratic pedagogy, while not evidenced in public materials, are areas where the school could further reinforce its CLASSICAL alignment.

What specifically moves the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL categories)
- Category 3: Add explicit Great Books and grammar-logic-rhetoric sequencing to the curriculum, and publish a public map showing Great Books requirements and reading lists by grade.
- Category 4: Document and publish explicit high-school-level Socratic seminars, debates, and rhetoric instruction with rubrics and exemplar lessons; provide case studies or sample units to demonstrate the pedagogical shift toward rigorous classical discourse.
- Category 6: Continue to articulate parental co-teaching roles with structured parent education programs and measurable outcomes, reinforcing the partnership model.
