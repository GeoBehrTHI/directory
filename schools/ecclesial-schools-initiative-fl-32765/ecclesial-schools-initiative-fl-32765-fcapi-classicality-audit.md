## Executive Summary

The Ecclesial Schools Initiative (ESI) campus network serving Oviedo/St. Alban’s and Northland in Florida presents a robust classicalChristian orientation with a clear formation focus, a visible commitment to making private Christian education accessible through parish partnerships and Florida scholarship programs, and a public-facing pedagogy centered on a classical liberal arts framework. The public materials emphasize Christian theology, liturgical practices (Morning Prayer), ecumenical church-based campuses, and a formation-driven mission. While the materials consistently affirm classical virtues, formation, and a liberal-arts emphasis, explicit references to Great Books, Great Conversation, or widespread Socratic pedagogy are less evident in the public facing content. The faculty are framed as living curriculum in the classical sense, and parental partnership is a foundational element of the model.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- The Religious & Metaphysical Foundations are clearly present (ecumenical church-based campuses, morning prayer, Christian identity, theology-informed aims).
- The School Purpose & Goal emphasize formation, virtue, and the real-knowledge ideals (truth, goodness, beauty) as central aims, not merely job preparation.
- The Curriculum presents a liberal-arts emphasis and a focus on wonder and formation, but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references are not overtly stated in the public materials.
- Pedagogy signals a contemplative/wonder-filled approach (awakening wonder through play and poetic knowledge) with some elements of traditional pedagogy; explicit high-school Socratic seminars are not clearly documented in the public-facing content.
- Faculty framing aligns with classical models (faculty as living curriculum, model teachers, mentorship) per known classical-education rubrics.
- Parents & Community are foregrounded through partnerships, co-educator expectations, and explicit family engagement strategies (parent partnership processes, scholarships, and family involvement).

Note: This assessment draws on the school’s own public-facing content (campus pages, mission/faith commitments, enrollment documentation, and program descriptions) as captured in the crawl data, and cross-references the classical-school rubric from the knowledge-base materials. ## What Would Move the Needle (only applies if SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)

- For Curriculum (Element 3): Explicitly incorporate and publicly document a canonical Great Books program and Great Conversation framework, including a clear listing of required Great Books and how historical eras and authors will be engaged across grades. Explicitly name grammar, logic, and rhetoric as a structured, sequenced core in relevant grade bands to push from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- For Pedagogy (Element 4): Publish explicit high-school pedagogy details that include Socratic seminars, structured debates, and formal rhetoric instruction in upper grades (and milestones for when/how students are expected to participate). Provide samples of seminars or rubrics to demonstrate the application of the Socratic method in classes.
- For Curriculum (Element 3) and Pedagogy (Element 4) more broadly: Add explicit history-integration across disciplines (not just “history as a subject” but history woven into science, literature, and theology curricula) and a public articulation of the “Great Conversation” as a guiding through-line across grades.
- For Public Communications: Increase explicit statements about the role of memory, chant/singing in the lower grades as a formal pedagogy component, with examples of chants or songs used in the curriculum.
- For Administration and Culture: Continue and publicize the portrait of teachers and students—the “living curriculum”—with formal, published teacher-portrait standards and annual professional development that aligns with classical aims.
