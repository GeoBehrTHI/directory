## What Would Move the Needle

- Adopt and publicly articulate a clear classical curriculum structure (e.g., Seven Liberal Arts, Trivium/Quadrivium; Great Books; Great Conversation) and publicly reference these elements on the site, with explicit mention of how they shape curriculum and pedagogy (Categories 3 and 4).

- Integrate and publicize explicit theological/themetical foundations (Category 1), including references to transcendental truth, goodness, and beauty; liturgical or liturgical-adjacent practices; and a formal statement of faith or Christian worldview that connects to other disciplines.

- Elevate the faculty narrative (Category 5) by presenting teachers as models of virtue, a living curriculum, and a collaborative faculty culture; share quotes or mini-profiles illustrating virtue, mentorship, and collegiality.

- Strengthen pedagogy (Category 4) by detailing the method mix: Socratic seminars in appropriate grade bands, memory/restful learning (Scholé), and guidance on imitation and practice with exemplars in literature, history, and mathematics.

- Expand and document parent-education initiatives (Category 6) with formal parent learning programs (e.g., classical book studies, catechesis, or home-centered formation) to deepen the partnership with families and to anchor formation in the home as part of the classical project.

- Increase explicit signaling around "great books" and "great conversation" in the curriculum pages, including sample reading lists, canon references, and how students engage with primary sources.

- Provide a transparent mapping of how the school’s mission aligns with the classical framework (truth, goodness, beauty as real; formation over information; integrated curriculum; restfulScholé pace; and the pedagogy of memory and dialogic inquiry).
