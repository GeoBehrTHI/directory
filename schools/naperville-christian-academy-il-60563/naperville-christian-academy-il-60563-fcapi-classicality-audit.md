## Executive Summary

Naperville Christian Academy (NCA) demonstrates strong alignment with classical Christian education principles across all six rubric categories. The public materials consistently frame the school as a Classical Christian institution grounded in a Biblical worldview, with explicit Trivium-based curriculum and pedagogy (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), a focus on formation (virtue, wisdom, and character) rather than mere information, and an emphasis on faculty who model virtue and a partnership with parents and the broader Christian community. Evidence includes mission statements, Biblical Worldview articulation, Trivium-based curriculum descriptions, lower- and upper-school pedagogical approaches (memory work, recitation, Socratic discussion, rhetoric training), and clear statements about faculty as mentors who embody the living curriculum. The school also highlights open engagement with parents and a family-centered culture in its classical framework. Overall, this school is CLASSICAL.

Cited materials drawn from both the knowledge-base (Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and related lexical/criterion guidance) and the school’s crawl content (Naperville Christian Academy public materials) substantiate this classification. Positive indicators such as theology, liturgy/worship, Coram Deo living, virtue formation, the Trivium, great books/ classical canon references, Socratic and rhetoric-based pedagogy, faculty as mentors, and active parental partnership are frequently present. Negative indicators from the provided materials are not apparent. See evidence throughout for explicit mentions of truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, scholé, wonder, and related classical markers. Key sources include the school’s mission and Biblical Worldview statements, the Classical Model description, and evidence of parental/community engagement.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- Grammar School materials reference a chronological study of history, Latin grammar, literature from canonical authors, and poetry—consistent with a liberal-arts curriculum and Great Books orientation. 
- The Classical Model documentation describes a three-stage progression that aligns with a liberal arts curriculum and Great Books emphasis. 
- Specific curricular details include history study across grades, study of ancient civilizations (Greece and Rome), and integration of literature, arts, and music within academic content, consistent with the Great Books/Great Conversation tradition. 
- The school emphasizes Latin grammar, history, poetry, literature, and art/music integration as part of a unified classical curriculum. 
- The school is a member of the Association of Classical and Christian Schools (ACCS), which anchors its curricular philosophy in the classical tradition. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Quantitative note

Why this rating
- The explicit use of Trivium-based structure, integration of Great Books and classical canon references, and the historical-liberal-arts emphasis align with classic curricula expectations. The absence of a dominant shift toward non-classical methods (e.g., a purely project-based or STEM-first emphasis) supports a CLASSICAL rating. 

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Upper-school pedagogy features Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training—explicitly described as the mode of instruction in high school, with opportunities for public speaking and rhetorical composition. 
- The school emphasizes contemplative/restful aspects of education alongside rigorous study, consistent with scholé approaches. 
- The “Pedagogy and Participation” materials describe structured seminars and reading discussions at the upper-school levels. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Negative keywords (signals of non-classical pedagogy): overly experiential/project-based-only approaches, student-centered discovery as the sole method; these are not the dominant signals in the school’s publicly available materials.

Quantitative note
- Positive signals exceed any counter-signals; estimated positive vs. negative keyword signals: ~8:0, supporting CLASSICAL. The presence of Socratic/seminal pedagogy at the upper level and memory-based lower-level practice is a hallmark of classical pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The combination of contemplative, memory-based practices in the lower grades and Socratic/seminar/rhetoric-based instruction in the upper grades fits the classical pedagogy model well. The explicit mention of memory work and chanting in lower grades and rhetoric training in upper grades solidifies CLASSICAL status.
