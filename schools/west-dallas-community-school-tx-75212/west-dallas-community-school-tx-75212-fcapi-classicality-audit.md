## Executive Summary

West Dallas Community School (WDCS) presents a clear orientation toward classical Christian education, with public-facing materials framing the school as a private, Christian institution built on a classical curriculum and a mission of formation in truth, goodness, and beauty. The campus and admissions pages emphasize affordability and a strong sense of community, including parent partnership and family involvement. The school highlights a long-standing commitment (founded in 1995) to serving an underserved West Dallas community, with a focus on forming students for service to God and man. The presence of hymns, liturgical music, and a mission framed around virtue, character, and a classical education signal a classical intent, though some of the canonical elements (e.g., explicit Great Books reading, grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence, or overt Socratic pedagogy in upper grades) are less clearly documented in the available public materials. Overall, WDCS aligns more with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL than with fully CLASSICAL, based on the current public articulation of its curriculum, pedagogy emphasis, and community/parent participation.

Executive-grade takeaway: WDCS demonstrates classical Christian aims and a recognizable classical orientation in mission, curriculum framing, and religious life, but it provides less explicit, verifiable evidence of some core classical-education signposts (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation engagement and a fully articulated grammar-logic-rhetoric progression) in the site content we have access to. Aggregate verdict: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Make explicit the Great Books and Great Conversation commitments in public materials (e.g., a stated program or curriculum map detailing Great Books readings and how they integrate with grammar/logic/rhetoric progression).
- Provide a clear articulation of a full classical pedagogy in K–8 that includes explicit memory work, recitation, and chant where appropriate, and a plan for Socratic discussion or seminar-style discussions in upper elementary grades as evidence of Scrupulous pedagogical alignment with classical models.
- Publish a concise, explicit list of the grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence (or justification for their curricular approach) so that the Curriculum category clearly meets the classical criteria beyond a general “classical curriculum” label.
- Expand the “Faculty” narrative with a formal portrait of the teacher as “living curriculum” and a succinct model of professional development in classical pedagogy (e.g., ongoing faculty seminars on classical education, method, and materials).
- Strengthen and publicize a more formal parent-education program (book studies, seminars on classical education, family liturgy alignment) to deepen the Parents & Community category evidence.
