## Executive Summary

Ambrose Bridge Campus (The Ambrose School) presents a robust, publicly observable alignment with classical Christian education. Across six rubric categories, the school's materials consistently emphasize formation through truth, goodness, and beauty anchored in a Christian worldview, with explicit reference to classical pedagogy (grammar-logic-rhetoric), Great Books, and Socratic inquiry. Public materials repeatedly foreground Christ-centered, Scripture-grounded education, the Great Books/Great Conversation tradition, and memory-based, liturgical, or chant-like elements in lower grades alongside Socratic seminars and thesis-level rhetoric in upper grades. In every observable facet—curriculum, pedagogy, faculty orientation, and parental engagement—the school evidences a classical orientation, with minimal signaling of non-classical or secular aims.

Key evidence includes explicit Christ-centered articulation and biblical authority, the claim that formation supersedes mere information, a curriculum that foregrounds Seven Liberal Arts and Great Books, a pedagogy that includes Socratic discussion and memory work, and a faculty/community culture built around partnership with families in a liturgical, virtuous framework. The school’s public materials also emphasize parent partnership and a shared mission with families to cultivate virtue, character, and wisdom.

Aggregate rating: CLASSICAL.

- What would move the needle: Not applicable, as all six categories fall squarely in the CLASSICAL range based on available public materials. If there were a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category, concrete steps would be listed, but none are required here.

- The site describes a three-phase grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, with grammar-stage memory work (chants, songs), logic-stage dialectic and Socratic discussion, and rhetoric-stage thesis and oratory. This is a canonical classical pedagogy progression. 
- Content references integration of disciplines and the reading of great texts with discussion anchored in Scripture, showing both Socratic and traditional forms of classroom dialogue and memorization.

- Quantitative note: Positive pedagogical signals are numerous and consistent; no explicit negative pedagogy signals appear in public content.

Why this rating
- Observed pedagogy aligns with the classical schema: systematic grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, memory-based lower grades, Socratic upper-grade seminars, and rhetorically grounded upper-level expression.

Citations
