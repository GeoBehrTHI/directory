## Executive Summary

Emet Classical Academy NY 10022 presents a consistently classical profile across its public materials, anchored by explicit emphasis on the liberal arts, classical languages, the Great Books canon, and a Bethlehem-inspired synthesis of Jewish religious formation with Western civilization. The school situates itself as a Jewish classical prep school that aims to form virtuous, intellectually capable students who are grounded in Torah study as well as the study of Western thought and culture. Public materials repeatedly couple "virtue" and "beauty" with a rigorous classical curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), classical languages (Hebrew and Greek/Latin), and the study of Western civilization, including field trips to Jerusalem, Athens, and Rome, as part of a broader emphasis on formation rather than information alone. There is explicit attention to community and parental partnership, with language that parents are partners or co-educators in formation and a commitment to openness (admissions, inquiries, and leadership pages invite parental involvement).

The six-category analysis below adopts the Six Essential Elements framework and keywords drawn from the thematic lexicon provided in the Classical School knowledge base. In this evaluation, Emet is classified as CLASSICAL across all six categories, with strong alignment in religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose/goal, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement. The site’s rhetoric and program details consistently reflect a classical mission, including a seven-pillar educational philosophy that foregrounds grammar/logic/rhetoric, classical languages, Western civilization, and virtuous formation within a Jewish context.

Key evidence highlights:
- Clear articulation of a seven-pillar program centered on classical arts of learning (grammar, rhetoric, logic) and classical languages (Hebrew, Greek/Latin) alongside a deep commitment to Western civilization and Jewish learning. 
- Explicit statements about formation and virtue, and about preparing students for lives of Jewish purpose and civic leadership, framed within a Western-civilization-human-excellence lens. 
- Public references to Great Books, the Great Conversation, and field experiences in Jerusalem, Athens, and Rome, demonstrating a curriculum that integrates history, literature, and culture within a classical framework. 
- Public materials showing Orthodox Jewish practice (calendar observance, Kashrut) and a strong Jewish identity integrated with the curriculum. 
- Leadership and faculty materials emphasizing a traditional, mentor-based model (Head of School; advisory board with distinguished scholars) and a living-curriculum ethos.
