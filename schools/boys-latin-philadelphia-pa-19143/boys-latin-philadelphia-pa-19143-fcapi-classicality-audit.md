## Executive Summary

Boys’ Latin of Philadelphia presents a public charter school that leans toward classical education in its stated foundation (notably a Latin-language emphasis) while integrating robust contemporary practices (1:1 technology, extended learning time, and college-advising programs). Based on a rubric drawn from the classical-school knowledge base, the school exhibits several core classical elements—most clearly in its Latin-centric curriculum and in character formation and aspirational language around virtue and leadership—but it does not consistently manifest all six elements at the level typical of a thoroughly Classical model (e.g., explicit Great Books, explicit Scholé/restful pedagogy, or a fully articulated Great Conversation). The aggregate assessment, therefore, places Boys’ Latin in the SOMEW HAT CLASSICAL range, with pronounced Classical affinities in Curriculum and Pedagogy, plus notable strengths in Family/Community engagement, while Religious and Metaphysical Foundations appear less explicit in public-facing materials.

Key supporting observations:
- Foundational classical orientation is explicit in mission language emphasizing “a classical Latin education” and a culture of critical thinking, personal responsibility, and character development. 
- The public-facing site highlights a number of classically inflected elements (Latin language requirement, daily Latin classes, a “living” character-education frame, advisory/seminar structures, and leadership development) alongside contemporary practices (1:1 devices, extended day, and a broad array of extracurriculars).  
- The school clearly foregrounds partnership with families and a shared mission to prepare students for college and leadership, signaling strong Parents & Community alignment, albeit without a fully spelled-out parent-formation program in the public materials.

What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rankings)
- Expand explicit Great Books/Great Conversation elements (public-facing language and course listings) and document a more explicit Great Books program or canonical-reading sequence.
- Make explicit the pedagogical elements of Scholé and contemplative/restful learning in lower and upper grades, with public-facing language about quiet reflection or “restful curriculum” days.
- Strengthen explicit “portrait of a teacher” and “living curriculum” language in external communications (public pages) to align more tightly with classical-faculty concepts.
- Deepen parental formation offerings via book studies, lectures, and family liturgies, and communicate these publicly as ongoing co-education with families.

What would move the needle (Concrete, school-wide actions)
- Add a formal Great Books/GW sequence to core humanities and history courses, with public-facing syllabi and reading lists.
- Include explicit Scholé language in mission/policy pages (e.g., rest/restful days, contemplative pedagogy, and memory-work traditions) to strengthen Pedagogy alignment.
- Publish a “Portrait of a Graduate” that foregrounds classical virtues in practice (truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, eloquence) and tie it to teacher practice and assessment.
- Expand Parent Formation programming publicly (book clubs, lectures, family liturgy) and publish a year-long schedule of parent-partner activities.
