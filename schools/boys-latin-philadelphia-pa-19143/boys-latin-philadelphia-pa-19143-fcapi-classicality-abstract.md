## Abstract

Overall Classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale: The Boys’ Latin site demonstrates several core features associated with classical education—most notably, a foundation in classical Latin education, character formation, and seminar-like learning structures—while integrating contemporary school practices (extended day, 1:1 technology, college advising). The absence of explicit references to Great Books/Great Conversation and to the full spectrum of liturgical/metaphysical language prevents a full CLASSICAL designation, but the Weight of Classical affinities across Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Family/Community engagement is substantial, yielding a SOM EW HAT CLASSICAL classification.

Element-by-Element Summary (evidence and keyword analysis)

Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — NOT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: Mission statements foreground a classical Latin foundation and communal virtues, but public materials do not foreground theology, liturgy, or explicit metaphysical commitments beyond virtue-based language. Example language: “our foundation a classical Latin education, the positive influence of brotherhood, and rich relationships.” 
- Positive keyword indicators: 0 direct matches to liturgical/theology liturgy/creed language; 0 explicit transcendental-theology terms seen in public copy.
- Negative keyword indicators: 0 explicit signs of religious liturgy, sacral practices in public text.
- Why this rating: The school’s public materials emphasize character and classical language as foundation rather than explicit transcendental/metaphysical commitments or liturgical practices.

Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: Mission language foregrounds formation of the student in a classical frame and stresses critical thinking, personal responsibility, emotional intelligence, and character development. “We prepare boys for success in college and beyond, using as our foundation a classical Latin education, the positive influence of brotherhood, and rich relationships. We are a community that values and cultivates critical thinking, personal responsibility, emotional intelligence, and character development.” 
- Positive keyword analysis: Positive matches include critical thinking, personal responsibility, character development (aligned with formation/virtue) and “foundation” of classical education.
- Negative keyword analysis: No explicit “college and career ready” or “21st-century skills” framing appears as a counterweight in the public copy.
- Why this rating: The language shows formation, virtue, and wisdom dimensions (formation/character development, critical thinking) but remains rooted in college-prep and modern-school practices; thus, not fully Classical, but leaning toward Classical formation.

Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: Explicit Latin language emphasis and a Latin-language requirement; daily Latin classes; a foundation in classical education. “Latin language requirement,” “classical Latin education” appear as foundational material; there is also broad coursework across English, Fine Arts, History, Latin, etc. 
- Positive keyword analysis: Latin, classical languages, and "foundation" (Latin-centric curriculum) align with Classical Curriculum signals; Great Books/Great Conversation signals are not explicit in public text.
- Negative keyword analysis: Not explicitly listing Great Books/Great Conversation or trivium/quadrivium as core syllabus; less emphasis on Great Books canon in public text.
- Why this rating: Strong Latin- and classical-language emphasis plus broad classical subject mix, but no explicit public mention of Great Books or Great Conversation in canonical form; thus, Somewhat Classical.

Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: The BL Experience describes an Intimate Learning Environment, 12:1 student/teacher ratio, and a structure around Advisory and Seminar; leadership/character-education elements, and a senior fieldwork/college-advising track. Public copy explicitly references Advisory and Seminar in the context of character education.  
- Positive keyword analysis: Seminar, gracious pedagogy, memory/recitation-type elements are present (Advisory and Seminar). Virtue-based education and public “Honor Pledge” language are aligned with a classical pedagogy approach.
- Negative keyword analysis: Not explicit on full Socratic-dialogue-heavy upper-school pedagogy in the public copy; explicit “Socratic” is not clearly stated though the Seminar language is present.
- Why this rating: Pedagogical practices show classical-influenced components (Seminar/advisory, memory/character formation) but do not demonstrate full, explicit Socratic-dominant pedagogy across all grades or a full explicit “scholé” restful pedagogy in public text.

Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: The public materials describe “dedicated teachers who challenge them to meet their full potential” and portray teachers as part of a collaborative learning ecosystem. The “living curriculum” is a classical notion, though not spelled out as such in the public copy. The BL Experience includes partnerships and a faculty-student culture with extensive programs, but explicit “portrait of a teacher” in public materials is not strongly explicit.
- Positive keyword analysis: references to dedicated teachers, mentoring, and a living- curriculum feel; “the living curriculum” is a classical construct, though not widely publicized in the BL pages.
- Negative keyword analysis: No explicit “facilitators/ Guides on the side” language; no direct depiction of faculty as a “living curriculum” phrase in the public copy (though it appears in classical lexicon material and could be inferred from the way teachers are described).
- Why this rating: A solid positive direction toward a classical faculty model, but not every element (e.g., explicit portrait of teacher) is publicly visible; thus Somewhat Classical.

Element 6: Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Evidence: Public materials emphasize partnership with families; the Mission & Vision text calls Boys’ Latin a “collaborative community of motivated students, supportive families, and dedicated educators.” Also, there is a substantial Extended Family Network and multiple parent-facing programs (e.g., EFN; family workshops). 
- Positive keyword analysis: Partnership with Families, parent formation, family involvement, open campus, etc., appear in public materials; EFN and family-programs show active family engagement.
- Negative keyword analysis: The materials do not publicly advertise a comprehensive, formal parent-formation curriculum in the classical sense (publicly visible links exist, but the depth and cadence of formation activities are not as explicit as some classical schools’ materials).
- Why this rating: The school demonstrates strong family engagement and a culture of parental partnership, with documented parent programs; however, the public materials do not consistently present a full “parent-formation program” in the classical sense. Thus, Somewhat Classical.

Aggregate assessment
- The six-category picture skews toward a classical orientation, but with gaps (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation, explicit liturgical/metaphysical language, and some of the deeper classical pedagogy language). Final ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Executive Summary — Concrete, category-by-category notes
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: NOT CLASSICAL. Evidence shows public material centering on classical education and virtue in a secular/parochial sense (no liturgy/theology). 
- School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Formation and virtue language exists within a classical frame, with a strong emphasis on critical thinking and character; but the presence of college-prep and leadership tracks temper the pure formation emphasis. 
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Latin emphasis is explicit; Great Books/Great Conversation not publicly highlighted, but classical-language education and formation are clear. 
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Seminar/advisory language appears; some elements suggest a Paideia-like approach, but not a full Socratic-dominant model stated publicly. 
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Public materials emphasize dedicated teachers and a collaborative culture; a fully explicit “portrait of a teacher” or living-curriculum language is less explicit in public copy. 
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Public statements emphasize partnership with families; EFN and family programming exist, expanding parental roles beyond customers toward co-education.

However, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and explicit liturgical/metaphysical programs are not consistently present in public text, which root-category the school in SOM EW HAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.
