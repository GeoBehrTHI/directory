## Executive Summary

Leman Academy of Excellence Sierra Vista (AZ 85635) presents itself publicly as a K–8 charter school offering a classical education focused on building knowledge and character. The website emphasizes a tuition-free, community-oriented environment with small class sizes, personalized learning, and “whole-child enrichment” in the arts, athletics, and academics. While the school clearly adopts a classical orientation (the site repeatedly uses the phrase “classical education”), explicit references to theological grounding, liturgical/communal practices, and heavy engagement with Great Books or Socratic pedagogy are not readily evident in the accessible public materials. Taken together, the school currently appears to be SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: it embodies core classical vocabulary (classical education; character; rigorous curriculum; personalized learning) but lacks explicit articulation of several key elements that the classical rubric treats as foundational (e.g., theology, liturgy, Great Books/Great Conversation, or sustained Socratic pedagogy at the high-school level) in its public-facing materials.

Key observations driving this assessment:
- Core classical framing is present: “Leman Academy of Excellence Sierra Vista is a tuition-free public charter school offering a classical education… read deeply, write clearly, think critically, and lead with character.” 
- Explicit religious/metaphysical foundations are not foregrounded in public content; theology, liturgy, or worship are not cited in the visible materials. This reduces the alignment with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category of a fully classical program. 
- The curriculum and pedagogy sections visible on the site emphasize reading, critical thinking, and character; however, direct references to the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; trivium/quadrivium), Great Books/Great Conversation, or Socratic seminars are not overtly stated in the public pages we examined. This tempers the curricular/pedagogical alignment toward full classical criteria. 
- The Parents & Community category is strongest: the school highlights partnerships with families, a close-knit community, and a collaborative faculty–parent dynamic; public materials emphasize parent involvement and partnerships, which aligns with the classical model’s emphasis on parent participation.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make theological or metaphysical grounding explicit (if aligned with the school’s mission): publish a concise statement clarifying any religious or moral framework, theological influences, or liturgical practices, and how these inform curriculum and culture.
- Publicly articulate Great Books/Great Conversation and core triadic grammar-logic-rhetoric (Trivium) and Quadrivium elements, including how they structure K–12 across the curriculum; publish a public curriculum map showing the Great Books list and representative authors, with a transparent sequence from K–8 (and how memory work or chanting may be used in early grades).
- Document and publicize specific pedagogical practices aligned with classical education (e.g., Socratic seminars in upper grades, narration/recitation in lower grades, memory-work, and periodic “scholé” days) and provide examples of student work to illustrate these practices.
- Expand the "living curriculum" narrative by including explicit statements about the teachers as models of virtue and the portrait of a teacher; share teacher-formation programs and professional development rooted in classical pedagogy.
- Enhance explicit Family/Community formation programming: publish book-club style parent formation resources, schedule regular parent gatherings, and provide clear ways for parents to participate as co-teachers beyond volunteering (e.g., formal parent-study groups, liturgical calendars, and family-learning guides).
