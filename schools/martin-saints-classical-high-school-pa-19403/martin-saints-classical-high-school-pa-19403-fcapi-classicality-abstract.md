## ABSTRACT

Martin Saints Classical High School (MSC) presents a robust Catholic, Chesterton-inspired classical education. The school foregrounds Catholic identity, liturgical life, and a faith-integrated liberal arts curriculum, with a clear emphasis on formation, virtue, and contemplation in its mission. Pedagogy highlights Socratic discussion, memory work, and a focus on rhetoric and public speaking at upper levels, consistent with classical education. The publicly documented curriculum emphasizes an integrated humanities program across history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages, taught through a Socratic method, with historical breadth from ancient to modern periods. Faculty and governance are portrayed as living mentors within a community of parents and students, and parents are explicitly positioned as co-teachers in partnership with the school. Collectively, these elements place MSC firmly in the CLASSICAL category across most dimensions, with Category 3 (Curriculum) showing strong classical features but encountering less explicit, enumerated articulations of all Trivium components in the public materials (which would push it to a fuller CLASSICAL rating for that single category).
