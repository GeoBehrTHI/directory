## Executive Summary

Hunter Classical Christian School Va 23229 presents a clearly classical Christian orientation, with explicit emphasis on the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a liberal-arts infused curriculum, and formation oriented toward virtue, wisdom, and a Christ-centered worldview rooted in Scripture. Public-facing materials repeatedly frame education as formation (not merely information) in a faith-informed context, and they foreground truth, goodness, and beauty as enduring aims of schooling. Evidence from the site highlights partnership with parents, a community anchored in Christian faith (including Anglican roots), explicit references to virtue and moral formation, and a vision of education that integrates theology with the liberal arts. The school also demonstrates a structured pathway through K–12 that advances from grammar through logic to rhetoric, with a clear emphasis on memory work, liturgical or devotional practices, and the use of classical texts and primary sources where presented (e.g., Biblical scripture memorization, Latin studies, and classic literature lists). Taken together, the six-category analysis weighs in as CLASSICAL for most categories, with Element 5 (Faculty) showing nuanced alignment that is strong but not explicitly codified in published language as a formal “living curriculum” model. Overall, Hunter Classical Christian School is CLASSICAL.

The needle-moving step for improvement (in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL sense) would be to codify and publicly publish explicit language on faculty as the “living curriculum,” including portraits of faculty, formal teacher-student mentorship language, and a published faculty development plan anchored in classical pedagogy (e.g., master-teacher modeling, faculty seminars on classic texts, and a formal “portrait of a teacher” standard). This would elevate Element 5 from nuanced alignment toward a more explicit BEST-PRACTICE alignment with the classical-philosophical standards in the Six Essential Elements framework.

- Evidence for this assessment derives from site materials that emphasize formation, the trivium, sacred Scripture, and a Christ-centered classical education, including statements about forming virtue through the Scriptures and liberal arts, and a clearly articulated Trinity-grounded, Anglican-inflected Christian framing. ### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower- and upper-school pedagogical patterns are described as aligned with classical education: lower-school singing/chanting with memory work, and upper-school Socratic discussion and rhetoric training (as part of the Trivium progression). This pattern matches the classic pedagogy mix of contemplative/restful learning (Scholé) and active, dialectic discussion in higher grades.  
- There are explicit references to the Trivium, with a memory-centric approach in the grammar stage and more dialectical, rhetorically oriented work in the logic and rhetoric stages. This aligns with the Six Essential Elements pedagogy indicators (Socratic dialogue, memorization/recitation, and formation through imitation). 
- Public materials describe “time-tested methods” of classical education that “go with the grain” of child development, and memory-based techniques (recitation, chants) are part of the core approach. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords observed: Socratic/dialogue; dialectic; memorization/recitation; chanting; Scholé (restful learning); seminars; rhetoric; debate; memory work. The presence of both memory-based and dialectic/participatory methods aligns with a classical pedagogy. 
- Negative keywords observed: there are no clear indicators of exclusive project-based or student-led constructivist pedagogy without classical scaffolding in the publicly presented materials.

Why this rating
- The combination of memory-based recall (chanting, Bible verses), lower-school singing for memorization, and upper-school Socratic-thinking/dialectic approaches mirrors the dual classical pedagogy pattern (Scholé + Socratic/Dialogue) described in the Six Essential Elements lexicon. This supports CLASSICAL. 

### Element 5: Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school lists named administrators and faculty, including “Head of School” and specific faculty staff pages with biographical information, indicating a visible, centralized leadership and a faculty body that is publicly represented. The Head of School biography shows a strong professional background in English and leadership, suggesting credentialed mentorship and subject-matter expertise. 
- Public-facing materials reflect a traditional-model teacher presence (administration, faculty pages; portraits/biographies) and a sense that teachers are central to the school’s mission. However, there is less explicit language about the faculty as the “living curriculum,” formal portraits of teacher ideals, or a stated faculty-development program anchored in classical pedagogy in the public site language. The absence of explicit “portrait of a teacher” language and formal faculty development language means this category, while strongly aligned at the practical level (teacher presence, expertise), does not communicate a fully codified “living curriculum” faculty framework on the public site. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The Lexicon lists “portrait of a teacher,” “living curriculum,” and “faculty as mentors” as positive signals; some of these are implicit in the site (e.g., named faculty, teacher bios) but not explicitly stated as a formal living-curriculum framework. 
- Negative keywords observed: explicit statements of a “living curriculum” portrait, master-teacher model, or formal faculty development program are not clearly published in the site copy. This leads to a partial alignment rather than full Corporate Classical language.

Why this rating
- The presence of named faculty and a structured leadership team supports CLASSICAL in practice, but the explicit language around “living curriculum”, formal teacher portraits, or teacher-student mentorship standards is not fully visible in the public-facing materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 5, given strong alignment in substance but limited explicit public articulation of a formal faculty framework. The classical lexicon supports higher potential with explicit articulation.
