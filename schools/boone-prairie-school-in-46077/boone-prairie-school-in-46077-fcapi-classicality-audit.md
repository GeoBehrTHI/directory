## Executive Summary

Boone Prairie School in 46077, operating as Advent Lutheran Classical School (ALCS) in Zionsville, Indiana, presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning formation model. Public-facing materials emphasize theological grounding, formation of virtue and wisdom, and a liberal-arts framework, with explicit mission language tying character formation to a Christian catechetical context. The school communicates classical aims (e.g., “classical education” and “liberal arts tradition”) and emphasizes formation, spiritual formation, and parental engagement, while offering small-class instruction and opportunities for family involvement. Taken together, the school demonstrates strong Classical tendencies with Christian distinctives, yielding a Overall assessment of Somewhat Classical. The strongest evidence of classical orientation lies in explicit religious/metaphysical foundations and formation-focused aims; the curriculum and pedagogy are less explicitly detailed in the public materials (e.g., explicit Great Books or the full Trivium/Quadrivium scaffolding is not spelled out on the site), though the Liberal Arts emphasis and classical wording are clear. The aggregate pattern across six categories suggests a robust classical orientation tempered by strong Christian formation, placing the school in the Somewhat Classical category rather than fully Classical.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicit Great Books and Great Conversation: Add clear, public articulation of engagement with Great Books, primary sources, and classic texts (e.g., a stated Great Conversation framework) across grade bands. This would strengthen Element 3 substantially.
- Explicit Seven Liberal Arts scaffolding: Publicly describe Trivium components (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium components (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) in curriculum and pedagogy sections (even if some elements are implicit, naming them publicly helps).
- Scholé and chant/memory work: Include documented use of contemplative/restful learning days (Scholé), memorization/recitation practices, and, where appropriate, chanting or liturgical memory in lower grades, with teacher guidance and assessment.
- Formal “living curriculum” portrait: Publish a clear description of faculty as mentors and the “living curriculum” in practice, including teacher development, model exemplars, and community expectations.
- Expanded parental formation programs: Provide structured parent-education offerings (e.g., book studies, classical education seminars) beyond open houses, and document partnerships with families as co-educators in public materials.
- Greater articulation of history and Great Books within the history/arts integration: Emphasize cross-disciplinary treatment of history, philosophy, literature, and the sciences as a unified, classical curriculum with explicit Great-Books orientation.
