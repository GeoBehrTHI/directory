## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of the Immaculate Heart (IL, 60012) presents a Catholic, community-centered classical-education model that foregrounds religious formation and liturgical life alongside a public-facing curriculum/activities structure. Public site content shows explicit spiritual life offerings, including a mission-driven Catholic identity and practices such as pilgrimages, house-system fostering communal life, and faith-based events. These signals align with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Liturgical/Communal Practices elements in the classical rubric. However, the publicly visible site content provided through the school’s crawl does not consistently disclose the full triad of classical-pillars (explicitly detailed Great Books reading, the Seven Liberal Arts framework, and explicit Socratic pedagogy with Trivium/Quadrivium articulation) in text on the site itself. Taken together, the school demonstrates a strong classical orientation in attachments to formation, Catholic identity, and communal rhythms, but the public materials examined do not unambiguously confirm all six classical criteria across all categories. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

This aligns with the Positive Keywords around theology, liturgy, and sacred practice in the classical lexicon. Evidence from the crawl includes explicit references to spiritual life and liturgical pilgrimage events.
- Purpose and Goal: The site emphasizes mission and formation-oriented language through institutional branding and Catholic identity, but explicit articulation of “virtue and wisdom” as the school’s sole or primary educational telos is not textually explicit in the public materials provided. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal rather than full CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum: The site maps to a “Curriculum” page and a declarative “Our Model of Education,” but textually explicit Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts, and Great Conversation language is not clearly present in the visible crawl content. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal.
- Pedagogy: There is no strong, explicit articulation of Socratic seminars, Scholé, or the full range of classical-pedagogical methods in the crawl text. This yields a NOT CLASSICAL signal in the public materials as shown, though the school’s identity strongly suggests classical aims.
- Faculty: The site shows a Faculty Directory; however, the living-curriculum, mentorship, and “portrait of a teacher” language typical of classical schools is not explicitly described in the accessible crawl text. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal.
