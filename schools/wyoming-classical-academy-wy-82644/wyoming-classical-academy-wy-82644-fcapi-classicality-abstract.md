## Abstract

The public materials place significant emphasis on formation (virtue, character, and civic virtue) and a liberal arts framework, with explicit references to reading, comprehension, and eloquence in speech and writing. ## ABSTRACT

Wyoming Classical Academy demonstrates a classical-leaning profile, with strong emphasis on virtue, moral character, and civic virtue, embedded in a liberal-arts framework. Category-by-category analysis places Categories 2 (Purpose and Goal) and 6 (Parents & Community) as CLASSICAL, while Categories 1, 3, 4, and 5 show substantial classical elements but fall short of full classical articulation in the public-facing materials, yielding a GENERAL ASSESSMENT of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregate result is a school that aligns closely with classical aims in many respects, while leaving room for stronger, explicit articulation of canonical classical pedagogy (Great Books, Socratic seminars, grammar-logic-rhetoric as curricular pillars) and the theological or liturgical dimensions that some classical schools emphasize. The most impactful reforms to move toward full CLASSICAL would be to make Great Books and Socratic pedagogy paradigms explicit, to articulate living-curriculum and faculty-mentorship language, and to more publicly integrate theology or ethical formation in alignment with the school’s mission and community values.
