Public materials from the school emphasize gospel-centered, holistic Christian formation, a robust liberal-arts framework, and a cross-curricular, experiential pedagogy. ABSTRACT

Imago Dei Neighborhood School presents a robust religiously framed, liberal-arts oriented program with a clearly formed mission to educate the whole person within a Christian framework. In examining the six classical-elements rubric, the school demonstrates strong alignment with Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and with School Purpose & Goal, and shows significant strength in Parents & Community engagement. It exhibits substantial alignment with Curriculum and Pedagogy through cross-curricular, experiential learning and memory-focused practices. However, it lacks explicit and consistent articulation of core classical markers such as Great Books/Great Conversation, Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; quadrivium), and a fully explicit Socratic pedagogical practice in public communications. The Faculty category shows presence but not a deeply articulated “living curriculum” by teachers. Taken together, the aggregate classification is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with identifiable needle-movers that, if addressed, could push the school toward CLASSICAL.

- Element 1 — Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: gospel-centered Christianity; Bible-centered instruction; Creator-focused language. Positive keywords dominate; no strong negative signals. 
  - Summary: Strong religious-metaphysical grounding and liturgical/community cues.

  - Summary: Partial alignment; solid classical flavor but not fully explicit on all canonical classical curriculum items.

- Element 4 — Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: narration/recitation/nature study; experiential learning; teacher influence noted (Marva Collins, Charlotte Mason). Lacks explicit high-school Socratic seminars in public materials.

  - Evidence: visible leadership and team structures; but not a strongly articulated faculty-as-living-curriculum narrative in public materials.
