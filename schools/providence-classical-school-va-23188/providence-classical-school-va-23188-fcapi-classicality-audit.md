## Executive Summary

Providence Classical School Virginia (Williamsburg, VA, 23188) presents a thorough classical Christian program with explicit Trivium-based deliberation, liturgical grounding, and a community-centered pedagogy. Public materials from PCS Virginia consistently emphasize a Christ-centered, biblical worldview embedded in classical education, with lower grades engaged in singing, memory work, and devotional routines, and upper grades engaging in Socratic/Harkness-style discourse and rhetoric. The school explicitly positions itself as the only Christian classical JK–12 school in Williamsburg, emphasizes character formation, and partners with families in the educational process. Taken together with the explicit use of memory work, liturgical elements (chapel, devotions), and a three-stage Trivium, the site evidence aligns closely with classical criteria across all six categories.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key takeaways by category (category-by-category snapshot):
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL – explicit Christ-centered, biblical worldview, liturgical/community practices (memory work, devotions, chapel) and a clear theology-infused approach are visible in multiple PCS Virginia pages.
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL – emphasis on formation of virtue, character, and wisdom, with truth and Christian formation as guiding aims beyond job preparation.
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL – Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), integration of history and literature, Latin/Greek beginnings, and engagement with great writers/studies.
- Pedagogy/Teaching Methods: CLASSICAL – lower grades emphasize memory work, singing, and contemplative practice; upper grades emphasize Socratic/Harkness discussions, seminars, and rhetoric.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL – public-facing descriptions emphasize teacher mentorship, a faculty-led living curriculum, and documented leadership profiles.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL – explicit partnership with families, family engagement, opportunities for parent involvement, and open campus/community activities.

Abstracted aggregate: CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle: Not required as a major change since all six categories align with classical criteria. If anything, maintaining explicit Great-Books–level engagement and continuing to document concrete Great Conversation–style activities (e.g., more explicit references to primary texts or Great Books lists) would strengthen the public articulation of classicism over time.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For a SCHOOL that is already CLASSICAL across all six categories, the public-facing documents would benefit from even more explicit articulation of “Great Books” and “Great Conversation” lists, with explicit examples of primary texts and a published, public-facing reading list or curriculum map. While there is evidence of “great writers of history” and Harkness/Socratic methods, a more explicit Great Books catalog would strengthen the categorical alignment and assist prospective families in quickly grasping the program’s canonical commitments.
- Further, a published alignment map that connects each Trivium stage to specific Great Books, primary sources, and core texts could help make the “living curriculum” more transparent to the public and help benchmark ongoing faculty development against classical standards.
