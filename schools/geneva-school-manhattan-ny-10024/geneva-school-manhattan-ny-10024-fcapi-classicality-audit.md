## Executive Summary

The Geneva School of Manhattan presents a coherent, fullyCLASSICAL profile across all six classical-element categories. Public materials consistently emphasize a time-proven classical education nested within a biblical Christian framework, with explicit attention to Trivium pedagogy (grammar-logic-rhetoric), the Great Books/Great Conversation, and Socratic and rhetorical methods in upper grades. The school frames its mission, curriculum, pedagogy, and community around virtue, wisdom, faith, and the cultivation of character in tandem with intellectual rigor. Evidence from the school’s own public materials demonstrates explicit references to a biblical worldview, theology/creed, liturgical-style communal practices, ongoing formation of character, and a commitment to parents as co-educators and partners in a diverse community with broad financial aid. These elements align closely with the Positive Keyword indicators for all six classical categories, while there is little to no presence of Negative Keyword indicators in the school’s materials.

Key signals include:
- Religious/Metaphysical Foundations: explicit biblical worldview, Christian beliefs, theology, statement of faith, and liturgical-style community life (evidence: mission statements, Christian beliefs pages, weekly assemblies)   .
- Purpose and Goal: emphasis on formation, virtue, wisdom, and leadership in scholarship/faith; focus on love of learning and character formation rather than mere job-prep (evidence: mission statements, distinctives, “classical and Christian” framing)  .
- Curriculum: explicit Trivium framing (grammar-logic-rhetoric), Great Books/readings, reading great books and joining the Great Conversation, and history-centric, integrated learning (evidence: Our Approach and Our Curriculum sections)  .
- Pedagogy: Socratic discussion in upper grades, rhetoric and public speaking, and opportunities for debate; lower/upper-school pedagogy aligned with contemplative/restful elements alongside rigorous inquiry (evidence: Socratic method in upper school; public speaking/debate; rhetoric emphasis)  .
- Faculty: oriented toward virtuous living and living curriculum; faculty as mentors and co-learners; portraits of teachers; Christian commitment highlighted (evidence: Leadership/Faculty pages and statements) .
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with parents as co-teachers, broad family/diversity statements, robust financial aid, open-campus culture and admissions engagement (evidence: parent involvement references, core values, diversity, scholarship fund)  .

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

This report provides category-by-category analysis, grounded in keyword matches against the Positive/Negative keyword indicators from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) lexicon, and cross-referenced with direct textual evidence from Geneva School Manhattan’s public materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For a school already ranked CLASSICAL across all six categories, the primary needle-moving opportunities would be incremental rather than structural. Potential enhancements (not required for CLASSICAL classification, but suggestions to deepen classical fidelity) include:
  - Explicitly foregrounding a formal “Great Conversation” program in public materials with concrete Great Books lists and a stated cadence for reading across grades.
  - Adding more explicit language about the Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) in curricular documentation, beyond the Trivium framing, to reinforce the full classical curriculum.
  - Providing more explicit statements about the liturgical/communal rhythm (e.g., liturgy patterns, worship or chapel formats) in public materials to sharpen the liturgical dimension for families unfamiliar with the school’s ecclesial framing.
  - Increasing formal parent education pieces that are clearly labeled as “Classical Education Book Club” or “Public Lectures on Classical Education” to amplify parent formation efforts.

Note: These needle-moving ideas are not necessary for CLASSICAL status but would further reinforce and communicate the school’s classical identity to external stakeholders.
