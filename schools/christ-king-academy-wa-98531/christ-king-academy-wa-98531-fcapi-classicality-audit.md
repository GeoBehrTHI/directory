## Executive Summary

Christ the King Academy (CKA) in Centralia/Chehalis, WA presents a robust classical Christian program that emphasizes discipleship, classical formation, and strong parental partnership. Public-facing materials consistently frame education as discipleship within a Christian worldview, highlight classical elements (Latin, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; memorization; chant/singing), and stress active parental involvement. The school has explicit statements about being “robustly Christian” and “thoroughly classical,” a strong staff culture, and a schedule and pedagogy that foreground formation, virtue, and spiritual aims alongside intellectual aims. Across six classical criteria, CK A demonstrates strong classical alignment in most domains, with Pedagogy showing strongest alignment to traditional classical practice and some room for broader implementation of Socratic/seminar-based methods. Overall, the school is classified as CLASSICAL.

The absence of secular or relativistic signals supports a CLASSICAL rating here.

Why this rating

Why this rating

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower-school pedagogy features singing, chanting, memory work, and recitation (e.g., Psalms, hymns, Bible/Poetry memorization; regular memory-based practices). 
- Upper-school pedagogy mentions Socratic discussion and rhetoric training in classical models as part of classical education, and “great conversation” framing appears in standards and external texts. Public materials reference Socratic dialogue and debates in classical pedagogy, though explicit, school-wide deployment across grades is less clearly documented in the site’s text. 
- The school uses a Four + One model and a schedule that includes in-class instruction plus home-based work, combining contemplative/restful elements with more rigorous, subject-focused work. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Scholé (restful learning), memorization/recitation, chanting, imitation of models, Socratic dialogue (as a concept referenced in pedagogy materials), and rhetoric training. The site shows strong memory-based practices (recitation/chanting) and a classical emphasis on restfulness at times (evidenced by “Four + One” pacing and mention of contemplative learning in public texts). 
- Negative keywords: Some elements of modern, project-based/experiential learning are not prominent in the public-facing materials; however, the site does show modern scheduling and home-learning components aligned with the Four + One model. The materials do not foreground heavy project-based, constructivist, or strictly experiential-only pedagogy. This yields a mixed signal leaning toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Why this rating
- CK A demonstrates core classical pedagogy features (memory work, recitation, model-formation, and a classical curriculum) but the explicit deployment of Socratic seminars and other high-school-specific seminar formats is not as plainly documented as the other elements. The presence of both strong classical practices and mixed pedagogical formats supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy, with a clear needle-mover potential if Socratic/seminar scaffolding is expanded.

What would move the needle

- Pedagogy (Element 4): 
  - Intensify explicit Socratic dialogue and seminar-based pedagogy across upper and lower grades, with documented schedules and teacher training to implement Harkness-style discussions. This would convert SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL into CLASSICAL by strengthening the primary method of uncovering truth through dialectic.
  - Expand Great Books exposure across disciplines and ensure explicit articulation of “Great Conversation” in core courses (history, literature, philosophy, math) to deepen integration of the curriculum with classical texts.
  - Increase explicit use of scholé/restful learning in daily routines, with dedicated periods for contemplative reading and reflection embedded in the weekly schedule, reinforcing the restful-learning dimension.

- Curriculum (Element 3): Maintain and amplify Great Books/read-original-text emphasis, ensuring explicit primary-source engagement in more courses and integrating classical texts across more disciplines to fortify the Great Conversation.

- Faculty (Element 5) and Parents & Community (Element 6): 
  - Continue expanding the “living curriculum” by providing teacher collaboratives that model virtue and professional formation, with formal portraits and standards that articulate teacher virtues and student virtues in public materials.
  - Broaden parent formation through regular book clubs, lectures, and theology-based study groups that are widely publicized and accessible to new families; deepen parent-co-teacher collaborations to solidify the partnership ethos.
