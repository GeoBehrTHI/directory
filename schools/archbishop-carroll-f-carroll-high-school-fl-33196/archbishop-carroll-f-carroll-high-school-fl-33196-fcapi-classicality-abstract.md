## ABSTRACT

Archbishop Coleman F. Carroll High School presents a CLASSICAL orientation overall, anchored by a clearly articulated religious and communal formation in a Catholic, Carmelite tradition, and a strong partner-driven relationship with parents. The school demonstrates Classical alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and in School Purpose & Goal, with explicit Christ-centered formation, liturgical life, and a family-centered mission. It also shows CLASSICAL alignment with Parents & Community through expressed partnerships with families and open parental engagement.

However, the Curriculum and Pedagogy categories reveal gaps relative to a fully classical program. The publicly available materials describe a robust modern college-preparatory curriculum (theology, English, math, science, social studies, world languages, dual enrollment) and do not publicly articulate the classical liberal arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric) or Great Books/Great Conversation elements, nor do they present explicit Socratic or scholé pedagogy. This lowers Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) to NOT CLASSICAL. The Faculty category is supported by evidence of dedicated faculty and staff, but lacks explicit articulation of the classical living-curriculum or model-teacher portraits in the public materials, resulting in a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 5.

Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL, anchored by Christ-centered formation, Carmelite tradition, liturgical life, and campus ministry. Evidence includes explicit Christ-centered language and liturgical life in school materials.  
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL, with a formation-focused orientation and a foundation in truth, goodness, beauty; family partnership highlighted. 
- Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL, as Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium and Great Conversation are not publicly evidenced; the site describes a modern college-prep curriculum and dual enrollment. 
- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL, lacking explicit classical instructional methods in public content (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholé). 
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with explicit emphasis on dedicated faculty; however, a fully developed classical portrait of the living curriculum is not publicly documented. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL, strong parental partnership language and open-campus engagement. 

If you’d like to move any category toward a fuller Classical alignment, concrete steps could include public articulation of a Seven Liberal Arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), explicit mention of Great Books and the Great Conversation, display of a living-curriculum model for faculty, and clearer articulation of scholé/recitation-based practices in lower/upper grades.
