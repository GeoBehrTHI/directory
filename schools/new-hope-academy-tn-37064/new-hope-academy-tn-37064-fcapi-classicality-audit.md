ABSTRACT

New Hope Academy (Franklin, TN 37064) demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL orientation across most elements of the Classical School rubric. Public materials reveal a Christ-centered, biblically grounded identity with clear theological commitments (Sovereignty of God; Sola Scriptura; Sola Christo), and a mission that centers on formation, virtue, and human flourishing within a biblical framework. Curriculum signals include a Classical Model and Charlotte Mason influences, along with an integrated curriculum and biblical worldview woven into instruction. The school emphasizes formation and virtue (Formative Learning, Virtue) as core aims, aligning with classical aims beyond mere information transfer.

Pedagogy shows a blend of contemplative, memory-based practices (Scholé, narration, living-books) and integrated instruction, though explicit high-school Socratic seminars and formal rhetoric training are not consistently described in public materials, yielding a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy. Faculty are presented as mentors and living exemplars, consistent with the Classical ideal of teachers as living curriculum. Parents and community are deeply engaged as co-educators, with active parent resources and engagement programs.

Overall, the aggregate classification is CLASSICAL, with a single category (Pedagogy) showing partial alignment (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL). The school scores highly on religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose and goal, curriculum, faculty, and parent/community, and demonstrates a coherent classical worldview, modestly tempered by the public articulation of pedagogical practices. The school’s public communications and internal materials corroborate a Classical orientation with room to strengthen explicit high-school Socratic and Great-Books pedagogy.
