## Executive Summary

Redeemer Classical Christian School Maryland (MD 21087) demonstrates strong alignment with classical education, particularly in its explicit commitment to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books-based learning, formation of character, and a Christian worldview that integrates faith with reason. Public-facing materials emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty as transcendent aims; formation of virtue and wisdom as primary goals; a curriculum centered on liberal arts and great works; pedagogy that blends memory work, recitation, singing, and formal Socratic-style discussion; a living, engaged faculty; and an active partnership with families and a hospitable, open-doors community. Taken together, these elements position Redeemer as CLASSICAL across the six categories of analysis.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

- Evidence: The school grounds its entire program in God and Christian faith; the curriculum and daily practice are framed by a Christian worldview, Scripture, and theology as an overarching influence on all disciplines. Public materials include explicit statements that God exists, that all learning is rooted in Him, and that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable through a Christian lens. For example, the school states that “All our learning is rooted in the truth that God exists, that we are made in His image, and that He is reconciling the world to Himself through His Son, Jesus Christ,” and that the study of humanities centers on Truth, Beauty, and Goodness within a Biblical framework. It also references Biblical standards of conduct and the belief that Jesus Christ is Lord of all.   
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords present include truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, wisdom, faith, theology, God, sacred, worship/liturgy (implied through communal faith life), and Christian worldview. Negative keywords (e.g., secularism, relativism) are not evident in the public materials. Overall: high density of positive indicators; no clear negative signals in this dimension.
- Why this rating: The school directly anchors its mission and pedagogy in transcendent Christian truths and theological commitments, with explicit emphasis on the ultimate realities of truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable, and with worship/communal-faith life integrated into school life.

Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence: Core statements emphasize formation of virtue and wisdom, not mere information or job prep. The school describes education as transformation of the whole person, the cultivation of wisdom and virtue, and the formation of students into virtuous human beings. The values of Wisdom, Virtue, and Faith explicitly frame the purpose, with education described as formation rather than information.  
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include formation, virtuous life, human flourishing, wisdom, virtue, telos, contemplation, and moral imagination. Negative signals (such as “career-readiness” or “21st-century skills” as primary goals) are not present in the public messaging. Overall: predominantly positive indicators toward a classical telos.
- Why this rating: The school foregrounds formation, virtue, wisdom, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty as the ultimate aims of education, consistent with classical paideia rather than merely preparing students for work.

Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- Evidence: The curriculum is explicitly framed around the liberal arts, with Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric (the Trivium) as central stages. There is a stated emphasis on “great books” and a canon-based Great Conversation, including the study of Latin and a focus on readings across geometry, poetry, biology, and humanities within a Christian framework. The presence of a thesis project in the senior year illustrates a culmination in rhetorical and intellectual mastery.   
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin, history/Western civilization, wonder, and integration of faith with learning. Negative keywords (e.g., a purely project-based, social-studies-centered or STEM-first approach) are not emphasized in the public content. Overall: strong alignment with the classical curriculum model.
- Why this rating: Redeemer centers on the Trivium, the Great Books canon, Latin study, and a historically grounded, integrated curriculum that seeks to cultivate wonder and humanistic understanding.

Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence: Pedagogical practice is described as singing/recitation/memory work in the lower grades, with a shift to Socratic-style discussion, seminar-style inquiry, and rhetoric in the upper grades. The public materials emphasize restfulness and contemplative learning as part of an education that balances memory with dialogue and argumentation. The senior level explicitly emphasizes persuasive communication and rhetorical skill, with opportunities for oration and public speaking.   
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include scholé/restful learning, contemplation, memory work, narration/recitation, singing/chanting, Socratic dialogue/seminars, and rhetoric. The public-facing content shows these modes (particularly singing/recitation and Socratic-like discussion later). Negative signals anticipated by classical lexicon (e.g., exclusive project-based learning, over-emphasis on non-classical methods) are not dominant in the school's messaging; rather, the school emphasizes a classical pedagogy. Overall: CLASSICAL, with clear evidence of both memory-based early education and Socratic/rhetoric-based upper-level teaching.
- Why this rating: The combination of memory/recitation/chanting in lower grades and Socratic/seminar/rhetoric in upper grades aligns with the classical pedagogy framework, including the Scholé-like aspects and the development of argumentative skills and public speaking.
