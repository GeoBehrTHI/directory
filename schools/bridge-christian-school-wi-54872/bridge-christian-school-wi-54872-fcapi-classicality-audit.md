## Executive Summary

Bridge Christian School Wisconsin (the Bridge Christian School, Siren Covenant Church campus, Siren, WI 54872) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning educational orientation focused on forming character and faith in alignment with Christian doctrine. The public-facing materials emphasize a theology-driven mission (“Training God’s Children in God’s Truth”) and a Classical Christian framework in the curriculum. Evidence from the school’s site shows explicit commitments to faith-integrated education, a Statement of Faith and Philosophy of Education, and a curriculum described as “Classical Christian Education” with history, science, math, philosophy, and art integrated around the truth that all knowledge points back to God. Admissions materials, tuition policies, and parent involvement communications further illustrate a strongly faith-centered community that seeks partnership with families rather than a secular, work-force-preparation emphasis.

Overall classification (across the six classical-elements categories): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale at a glance:

- Strong Classical/Christian foundations evident in mission, theology, and curriculum framing (Categories 1–2).
- Curriculum speaks to classical orientation but lacks explicit, on-site confirmation of core classical pedagogy elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric explicitly named, Great Books/Great Conversation, or explicit Socratic pedagogy) (Element 3; Element 4). This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL score for several categories.
- Faculty and explicit teacher formation language are not clearly articulated on the public site (Element 5), contributing to a Not/Classical signal in that category.
- Parent/community partnership is present (category 6) but is not described with the full “parents as co-teachers” vocabulary beyond volunteering and open-campus posture, producing a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 6.

What would move the needle: If the school explicitly names and showcases classical pedagogical methods (grammar–logic–rhetoric, Socratic seminars, memory work, chanting) and makes the “great books / great conversation” component visible in curriculum and classroom practice, the category scores would move toward CLASSICAL. Additional explicit articulation of the faculty profile as a “living curriculum” and a deeper, documented parent-formation program (beyond volunteer opportunities) would further push the aggregate rating toward CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly name and describe core classical elements in the curriculum, including grammar, logic, and rhetoric, and reference Great Books or Great Conversation; provide a clear plan showing how Great Books are integrated and how students participate in a Great Conversation. # ABSTRACT

Bridge Christian School Wisconsin (Siren Covenant Church campus) demonstrates a clearly Christian, Classical-leaning educational orientation, with mission and curriculum framed around Christian theology and a Classical Christian education model. The school explicitly states its goal of training students “in God’s Truth,” integrates faith with learning, and emphasizes formation, virtue, and wisdom as central aims. The public materials provide strong evidence for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1) and Purpose/Goal (Element 2), a partial Classical Curricular orientation (Element 3), and a limited depiction of Pedagogy (Element 4) and Faculty (Element 5). Parent/community engagement is present but not fully developed in classical terms (Element 6). The aggregate assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reflecting strong religious and formation commitments but with gaps in explicit classical pedagogy, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, and fully detailed faculty-curriculum descriptions.
