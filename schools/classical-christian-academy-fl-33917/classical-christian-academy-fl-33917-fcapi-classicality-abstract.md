## ABSTRACT

Abstract
Classical Christian Academy Florida 33917 (CCA FL 33917) demonstrates a robust classical Christian orientation with a clear emphasis on a Biblical worldview, formation of virtue, and a Trivium-based curriculum. The school’s public materials show explicit commitments to Christian formation, partnership with families, and a classical curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin) with a hybrid on/off-campus model. However, explicit, consistent public emphasis on certain hallmark classical practices—especially sustained Great Books/Great Conversation engagement, Socratic pedagogy across 9–12, and liturgical rhythms as core daily practices—is less consistently demonstrated in the accessible content. The evaluation, drawing on both the school-specific crawl material and the classical criteria lexicon, yields an overall CLASSICAL in Faculty, and Parents & Community; SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Religious Foundations; and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Purpose/Goal. The aggregate ranking is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  Evidence centers on Biblical worldview, Christ-centered formation, and covenantal community language. Positive indicators drawn from the lexicon align with theology and sacred tradition signals.

- Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Formation and virtue-oriented aims are present, but a fully explicit transcendental frame (truth/goodness/beauty) is less foregrounded in public messaging.

- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Classical curriculum elements are present (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin) with some mention of Great Books, but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis is not consistently foregrounded.

- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  There are classical pedagogy elements (memory, recitation; staged Trivium), but explicit, systematic Socratic pedagogy across all upper grades is not consistently evidenced.

- Faculty: CLASSICAL
  Long-tenured, credentialed teachers; living curriculum; teacher as model of virtue.

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL
  Explicit parent partnership and formation; family involvement integrated into the school model.

Key sources drawn from the crawl materials include statements about a Biblical worldview, covenantal parent partnership, the classical curriculum structure (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin), and faculty stability, along with references to parent involvement and a hybrid home/onsite model. These signals align with several Positive Keywords in the classical lexicon and the Six Essential Elements framework, particularly in the domains of theology, formation, and partnership with families. Citations include the school’s mission/vision statements and descriptions of the hybrid classical approach, along with faculty and parent partnership statements.
