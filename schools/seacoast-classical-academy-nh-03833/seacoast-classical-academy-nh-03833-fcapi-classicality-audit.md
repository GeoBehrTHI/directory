## Executive Summary

Seacoast Classical Academy (Newton, NH) demonstrates a strong core alignment with classical-education principles, anchored by a Hillsdale College K-12 curriculum, a Great Books-oriented orientation, and a deliberate emphasis on virtue, truth, and beauty. The school foregrounds formation of character, rigorous content, and a family-centered governance model that places parents as primary partners in education. Pedagogy includes Socratic discussion, memory work, and deliberate language-learning (Latin roots, Greek/Latin study), alongside a memory-rich lower-school culture featuring singing and recitation. The faculty pages portray experienced, virtue-minded leadership and teachers who view themselves as part of a “living curriculum.” The school also emphasizes family partnership and open-parent interactions (parent tours, observations, and parent-led engagement). Taken together, the six-category assessment yields a broadly classical profile with particular strength in Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community; the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations sentiment is present but not anchored in explicit theology or liturgical practice. Overall: CLASSICAL, with Element 1 showing some readiness but lacking explicit liturgical/theological framing.

Key evidentiary highlights:
- Curriculum: Hillsdale K-12 Curriculum; Singapore Math; Core Knowledge; Great Books and primary sources; study of Latin roots; emphasis on Western tradition and primary historical sources. Evidence appears across the Academics and Curriculum pages.  
- Pedagogy: Explicit mention of Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, rhetoric training, and upper-school oratory; lower-school singing and memory-building. 
- Faculty: Faculty bios emphasize virtue, mentorship, and “experienced leadership,” with the Principal and Curriculum Director foregrounded. 
- Parents & Community: Mission emphasizes family rights and parental primacy; explicit statements about parent involvement and observations; open-parent interactions and governance engagement. 
- Religious/Metaphysical Foundations: Emphasis on Western/traditional virtues and moral formation; limited explicit theology or liturgical practices in public materials.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school describes a virtue-centered, Western-tradition orientation and moral formation, including explicit statements about virtue and moral order, and the Logos in the context of classical education. The Mission references “traditions of moral and civic virtue with a central and recurring emphasis on Western and foundational American principles.” 
- The public materials emphasize truth, goodness, beauty as real ideals (via virtue and Western tradition framing) but stop short of explicit theological anchoring or liturgical practices. The presence of virtue-based language is clear, yet theology and liturgical communal practices are not foregrounded.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: virtue, truth, moral order, Western tradition, moral formation, Logos, understanding of virtue. Examples appear in the mission and virtue sections. 
- Negative keywords present: not explicitly present; terms such as “secular ethics,” “values-neutral,” or “liturgical” are not evident in the public-facing materials. The absence is notable rather than presence of explicit negatives.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates classical sensibilities in virtue, moral order, and truth-centered aims, but does not foreground theology, liturgical practices, or explicit sacred rituals in public communications. Thus, it sits between classical and non-theological, landing in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for this category.

- The school explicitly frames education as formation within a liberal-arts, classical framework, including Great Books and a foundational emphasis on human flourishing. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Negative keywords present: none detected (no explicit emphasis on “college/career readiness” or work-skills focus in public statements).

Why this rating

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- The curriculum also foregrounds Core Knowledge components and a primary emphasis on primary sources/history to complement a content-rich classical program. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, primary sources/original texts, Western civilization, history & civics, Core Knowledge, Latin/Latin roots, integration of arts and sciences, literature, and inquiry-based components. 
- Negative keywords present: not observed; no explicit shift away from history to social studies or project-based emphasis; no major deviation from classic-canon curriculum signals in public materials.

Why this rating
- The curriculum is explicitly built on canonical classical elements (Liberal Arts, Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Western canon) and is reinforced by a Hillsdale licensure, supporting a CLASSICAL categorization for Curriculum.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Upper grades: Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training; students are prepared to give speeches and use rhetoric. This is a hallmark of classical pedagogy, especially in upper grades. 
- Lower grades: Singing, chanting, and memory-work culture; regular review to deepen memory and delight; a contemplative and restful aspect in lines with scholé-like practices. 
- Across grades, there is evidence of a “living curriculum” feel through faculty and leadership emphasis on virtue and classical pedagogy; the curriculum is content-rich and designed to train students in knowledge, understanding, and critical thinking. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic, seminars, debates, rhetoric, speeches, memory-work/recitation, chanting, scholé (educational restfulness/contemplation is implied in lower-school pedagogy), integration of contemplation with rigorous content, Great Books orientation.
- Negative keywords present: not evident; no explicit emphasis on purely project-based or constructivist pedagogy dominating (the public materials emphasize direct instruction of content in a classical framework).

Why this rating
- Pedagogy integrates Socratic inquiry and rhetoric with a strong memory-based lower-school culture, consistent with CLASSICAL pedagogy. The presence of upper-grade Socratic/seminar work and lower-grade chant/memory aligns with the classical model, yielding a CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.

- While not required to achieve CLASSICAL across all six categories, explicit emphasis on liturgical practices, sacred traditions, or direct theology (in public materials) would push Element 1 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
