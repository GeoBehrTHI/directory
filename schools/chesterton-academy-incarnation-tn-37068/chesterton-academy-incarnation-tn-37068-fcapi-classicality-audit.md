## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of the Incarnation (CAI) in Franklin, Tennessee presents a robust classical-Catholic educational model. Public materials consistently foreground Catholic identity, daily liturgical life, and a theology-infused, liberal-arts curriculum shaped by Great Books and the Great Conversation. The pedagogy includes Socratic discourse, memory work, and formation through imitation, with a faculty that is presented as living exemplars of virtue and scholarship. There is explicit attention to parent and community involvement through open houses, information nights, and community events. On balance, CAI aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education, earning an overall CLASSICAL ranking across the six categories.

Overall categorization by rubric
- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): CLASSICAL
- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal): CLASSICAL
- Element 3 (Curriculum): CLASSICAL
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): CLASSICAL
- Element 5 (Faculty): CLASSICAL
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): CLASSICAL

Aggregate ranking: CLASSICAL

Key takeaways
- CAI foregrounds transcendental aims and Catholic formation (truth, goodness, beauty) through liturgy, catechesis, and daily Mass, which maps to the Positive Keywords around religious/metaphysical foundations.
- The school’s stated mission emphasizes formation, virtue, and wisdom, with Great Books and a liberal-arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a historically Western-Catholic curriculum.
- Pedagogy features Socratic dialogue, memory work, and a conscious emphasis on contemplative elements (Scholé) alongside rigorous discussion and rhetoric.
- The faculty is presented as a central, virtuous element of the curriculum (the living curriculum), with explicit references to Catholic formation and fidelity to Magisterium.
- Parent and community engagement is evident via open houses, info nights, and fundraising/community activities, signaling a partnership orientation consistent with classical catechesis and culture.

What would move the needle
- No major needle-moving changes are required given the CLASSICAL classification. If desired, focus could be on explicitly documenting every curricular connection to the Great Books and the Quadrivium (where not already explicit in public materials) and on providing more explicit, public statements about parent formation and co-teaching partnerships beyond event-based engagement.

## What Would Move the Needle

- While CAI is CLASSICAL across all six categories, explicit public articulations of a few items could strengthen the alignment further:
  - Publicly enumerate the Great Books list and clearly map each course to the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium components, including explicit references to Latin and classical languages in more places on the site.
  - More explicit, public statements about parent formation roles (co-teaching, home-school partnerships, or family catechesis) beyond event-based engagement, framed within the “Partnership with Families” vocabulary from the Six Essential Elements.
  - Provide a public-facing justification of the Great Conversation framework, detailing how Western canonical texts frame the junior-senior curriculum and how non-canonical texts are integrated (if applicable) without diluting the liberal-arts core.
