## Executive Summary

Coram Deo Classical School (Mount Vernon, IL, 62864) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning program grounded in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a mission to form virtuous, contemplative students within a Christ-centered framework. Public materials emphasize virtue, character formation, and a theologically infused approach to learning, along with a family- and community-oriented ethos. The school prominently identifies as a private classical Christian school serving Jr. Kindergarten through 9th grade, with mission statements and core values centered on Christ, character, and scholarly excellence. The pedagogy and curriculum documents point to classical methods (grammar/logic/rhetoric stages) and a historically-informed approach to education, while faculty are framed as living exemplars and mentors. There is also a visible emphasis on parent involvement and community activities (Paideia Day, events, family applications), suggesting a collaborative model with families.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- A strong religious/metaphysical foundation is evident (Christ-centered mission, theology-integrated values, liturgical-leaning cues within materials). 
- The school’s stated purpose and goals align with formation, virtue, and wisdom rather than mere job-readiness. 
- The curriculum demonstrates core classical components (grammar/logic/rhetoric, history, Bible) and a language of “classical education,” with explicit reference to the trivium and staged learning. 
- Pedagogy appears to reflect classical methods (grammar/logic/rhetoric progression) with an emphasis on memory work and a framework that supports a high school trajectory into rhetoric-based practice, though explicit modernized elements like explicit Great-Books phrasing aren’t consistently visible in public materials. 
- Faculty materials emphasize mentorship and living curricula, with explicit statements about faculty being central to the school’s formation and ongoing professional development. 
- Parent/community engagement is present (family applications, events, Paideia Day), signaling a partnership approach with families and opportunities for parental formation and involvement.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The Classical Methods of Education piece outlines the triad-driven progression (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and discusses the idea of education as formation, with a plumb-line use of Scripture. The structure implies a mix of teacher-led instruction within a classic framework along with opportunities for student engagement through the stages. 
- The presence of a historically-informed pedagogy (trivium progression and a focus on formation and contemplation) suggests an alignment with classical pedagogy, including memory work and potential opportunities for rhetoric and debate as students advance (as indicated by the upper-level capstone/rhetorical emphasis in classical models). 
- However, explicit mention of Socratic seminars, seminars, or structured debates in daily public-facing materials is not consistently visible; the public materials emphasize the grammar/logic/rhetoric framework rather than a comprehensive Socratic method across all grades. This reduces the classification from fully classical to somewhat classical in pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: memory work, recitation, chanting, grammar, logic, rhetoric, scholé, contemplation, seminar, Paideia style, rhetoric exercises, oratory, debate, and living curriculum concepts. The presence of a triad-based pedagogy and “formation” framing aligns with classical pedagogy. 
- Negative keywords: explicit avoidance of constructivist, purely experiential, or project-based-only approaches is not strongly signaled in the available public materials, but the lack of explicit Socratic/seminar implementation in the public-facing description reduces certainty of full Socratic pedagogy. This yields a mixed signal on the Pedagogy category. 

Why this rating
- The program shows a strong classical pedagogy via the grammar/logic/rhetoric framework and formation-focused aims, but explicit, large-scale evidence of Socratic seminars and extensive debate-based pedagogy is not consistently documented in the public materials. This justifies SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Pedagogy.

Consider a dedicated page or section detailing Great Books selections, primary sources, and a cross-curricular Great Conversation framework.

This would strengthen the Pedagogy category toward CLASSICAL by making Socratic/Scholé practices explicit.

Public statements about parent co-teaching roles would reinforce this.
