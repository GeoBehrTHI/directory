# Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Bishop Ryan Catholic School demonstrates a strong religious and metaphysical foundation and explicitly frames its mission around “education in virtue,” but it does not yet present the classical curricular and pedagogical markers (Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Latin as a core language, Socratic seminar, schole/restful learning, chanting/recitation in lower grades, rhetoric training) on its public materials. The site repeatedly stresses formation in truth, goodness, and beauty, daily Mass, the liturgical year, and partnership with parents—all strong classical signals in Categories 1, 2, and 6. However, Categories 3 and 4 lack the classical keywords and practices. Net: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall.

Website audited: https://bishopryan.com/ (see homepage title and mission/formation pages  )

Method: This audit applies the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators from “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” across six categories and quantifies hits before rating each category (framework and keyword lexicon   ).

Key strengths:
- Robust theological grounding, daily prayer and Mass, and explicit liturgical life (Category 1) .
- Mission-level commitment to formation in virtue, truth, goodness, and beauty (Category 2)  .
- Clear language of partnering with parents and community practices (Category 6) .

Primary gaps:
- No public evidence of classical curriculum (Trivium, Quadrivium, Latin, Great Books) or pedagogy (Socratic, scholé, recitation/chanting, rhetoric) (Categories 3–4).

# Aggregate Rating
- Overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Strong evidence in Categories 1 and 2; moderate in 6; limited in 5; lacking in 3 and 4.
  - Justification: The school clearly embraces virtue formation grounded in Catholic theology and active liturgical life—core classical foundations—but does not publicly signal classical curriculum and pedagogy.

  

# What would move the needle (actionable next steps)
If Bishop Ryan intends to be publicly identifiable as a classical school, consider:
- Curriculum
  - Publish an academics page that explicitly names the Liberal Arts (Trivium: grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium: arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), names “Great Books”/primary sources, and highlights chronological “history” (rather than “social studies”), with an invitation to the “Great Conversation.” Include Latin as a core language where feasible.
- Pedagogy
  - Describe K–8 memory work, recitation, and chanting in the lower grades; name Socratic discussion/seminar in upper grades; point to rhetoric training and student orations/theses. Include language about scholé/restful, contemplative learning to balance “rigorous” with “restful.”
- Faculty formation
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher”/“Faculty of Friends” that emphasizes teachers as mentors and models of virtue, and mention ongoing faculty formation in classical pedagogy.
- Parent formation
  - Add parent reading groups or talks (e.g., Lewis’s Abolition of Man, Sayers’s “Lost Tools,” or other classical education primers) and note these publicly as “parent formation.”

These steps would markedly increase Category 3–5 scores and likely elevate the overall rating to CLASSICAL.
