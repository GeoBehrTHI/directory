## Executive Summary

Reformation Classical Academy (Dayton, OH 45403) presents a robust classical Christian program anchored in Reformed/theological foundations, with explicit aims to form virtue and wisdom through the classical method and a biblically integrated curriculum. Evidence from RCA’s public materials demonstrates a coherent alignment with core classical criteria: explicit theological/doctrinal commitments, a stated formation目的 (as opposed to mere information), a curriculum that centers grammar-logic-rhetoric while engaging biblical texts, and pedagogy that blends recitation, memory-work, and rhetorically-focused upper-school work. Faculty are framed as living exemplars of virtue within a “Christ-centered learning” culture, and parent partnership is foregrounded as essential. The school’s materials also show concrete classical elements such as the classical tools of learning (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a principled emphasis on Scripture and confessional standards, and a curricular attention to historical/theological foundations.

Category-by-category, RCA is predominantly CLASSICAL, with two notable areas showing only partial alignment to classical norms (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy rather than fully CLASSICAL in those categories). The aggregate assessment across all six categories is CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- The school is explicitly committed to biblical/theological foundations, confessional standards, and the central authority of Scripture (evidence: Mission/Vision statements; Doctrinal Foundations; Westminster Confession).
- The curriculum and pedagogy are distinctly classical (grammar, logic, rhetoric; memory/recitation; upper-school rhetoric/seminars; Senior Thesis). However, explicit modern “Great Books” framing and some explicit Socratic seminar language are less overt in public RCA materials, which is why Curriculum and Pedagogy are characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.
- Parental partnership and community life are central (open-admissions style, family interviews, parental formation opportunities, and a culture of parent involvement).
