# Classicality Audit: Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School (Waco, TX)

Website used: https://bishopreicher.com

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Why: Public materials clearly and repeatedly foreground Catholic formation (four-year theology sequence; sacred scripture; sacramental life; regular participation in sacred liturgy and music; catechesis; Education in Virtue), which strongly fulfills the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations of a classical school. However, stated aims and program highlights lean toward modern college-prep and STEM/experiential emphases (PSAT/National Merit positioning; AP Calculus; “hands-on learning,” robotics, coding, competitions), with little explicit evidence of trivium/quadrivium framing, Socratic seminar, scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, or parent formation. On balance, this yields strong theological/liturgical grounding with partial classical curriculum signals (classic literature; classical languages) but modern pedagogical posture and purpose statements.  

Framework and lexicon: Analysis follows the Six Essential Elements keyword framework (positive/negative indicators) and the companion lexicon for classical signals (truth, goodness, beauty; theology; liturgy; trivium/quadrivium; Great Books; Socratic; scholé; wonder; chanting; project-based; experiential learning; college and career ready; STEM, etc.).

However, aims and methods—framed around college-prep metrics and experiential/STEM programming—lack the distinctive classical pedagogy (Socratic, scholé, memory/recitation) and comprehensive liberal-arts articulation (trivium/quadrivium, wonder) expected by the Six Elements framework.    

---

## What would move the needle
- Name the telos explicitly: Feature the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty as transcendent, real, and knowable; foreground wisdom/virtue as the goal over college outcomes. 
- Publish the classical architecture: Articulate trivium/quadrivium across K–12; rename “Social Studies” to “History”; add a visible Great Books/primary-sources list by grade and “Great Conversation” language.  
- Pedagogy shift in public: Highlight Socratic seminars (upper school), progymnasmata/rhetoric performances, chanting/recitation and songs (lower school), and scholé/restful learning practices; document showcases (debates, orations, theses).  
- Faculty formation: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and faculty formation in classical pedagogy; present teachers as mentors/models of virtue—the “living curriculum.” 
- Parent partnership: Introduce parent formation (book clubs, seminars on classical education), and communicate parent-school partnership as co-educators.
