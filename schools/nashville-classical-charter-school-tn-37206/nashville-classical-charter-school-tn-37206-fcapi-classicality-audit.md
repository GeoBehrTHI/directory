## Executive Summary

Nashville Classical Charter School (TN 37206) presents a publicly visible program that aligns closely with classical, Christian education traditions. The public materials emphasize a classical, faith-informed approach, with explicit references to Christian theology, the Apostles’ Creed, traditional morality, and the Holy Scriptures, alongside a strongly stated classical curriculum featuring Latin, Logic, Rhetoric, Great Books, and a heavy emphasis on reading canonical texts (Homer, Plato, Cicero, Shakespeare, Dante, Tolstoy, etc.). The pedagogy described in the accessible materials includes elements of memorization and recitation (e.g., Horatius at the Bridge and other classical memory work), a strong emphasis on discussion and Socratic-style inquiry in upper grades, and a combination of rigorous study with contemplative, memory-building practices. The school fosters family involvement through book clubs and parent resources, and it presents a clear institutional model of teachers as mentors within a living-curriculum framework.

Overall, the school is CLASSICAL, with several strong indicators of a classical, Christian formation mindset (religious/metaphysical foundations and curriculum rooted in great books and the trivium). Some categories show room for more explicit articulation of the full classical pedagogy (e.g., outwardly explicit references to scholé/restful learning and a more comprehensive articulation of the faculty living-curriculum philosophy). The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL, with opportunities to strengthen explicit statements around formation-focused aims and broader faculty culture.

Key insights:
- Explicit religious/theological foundations are present (creed, scripture, Christian morality) and paired with a classical curriculum anchored in Latin, classical studies, logic, and rhetoric. This supports Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) as CLASSICAL and Element 3 (Curriculum) as CLASSICAL.
- The curriculum emphasizes Great Books, Western canon, and memorized/recited classical material (Horatius, Latin, poetry), supporting Element 3 and Element 4 (Pedagogy) as CLASSICAL, with strong memory and recitation practices.
- Parental partnership and community engagement exist (e.g., book clubs, open campus), supporting Element 6 (Parents & Community) as CLASSICAL, and indicating a healthy parent-school partnership.
- Some elements of the explicit articulation of formation (virtue, telos, formation of affections) are present but less overt in the public materials; Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) and Element 5 (Faculty) show SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals rather than fully explicit CLASSICAL articulation in every respect.

Abstract: The school is classified as CLASSICAL with notable strengths in religious/metaphysical foundations, curriculum grounded in liberal arts and the Great Books, and a pedagogy that includes memorization, recitation, and Socratic-style discussion in upper grades. Parent partnership and a living-curriculum approach are present but could be more explicitly articulated.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 2 (Purpose and Goal): Provide explicit, public-facing articulation of telos, formation over information, and a clearly stated objective to cultivate virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful as foundational aims. A concise, published statement tying the curriculum and pedagogy to formation, rather than primarily to college/work-readiness, would strengthen the claim to CLASSICAL in this category.
- For Element 5 (Faculty): Expand explicit language about the faculty as living-curriculum models, mentors, and community of practice with a formal portrait of teacher qualifications and a declared faculty development plan reflecting the classical tradition. Publish a public-facing statement on mentorship, faculty collaboration, and shared intellectual life among teachers to strengthen the faculty category.
- For Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy): Consider a more explicit articulation of Scholé (restful learning) and direct connections between memory work (chanting/recitation) and the deepening of understanding across grades. A concise description of how memory, chant, and memorization activities map to deep learning outcomes would reinforce the pedagogical fidelity to classical aims.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand explicit parent-education offerings (beyond book clubs) that articulate classical pedagogy and provide structured opportunities for parental formation aligned with the Great Books and Trivium approach. Publicly publish a clear plan for parent education and community-building to further strengthen the partnership with families.
