## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy - East Lewisville (FCA East Lewisville) presents itself publicly as a classical-education campus. However, based on the six-category classical rubric and explicit keyword indicators drawn from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School, the public materials on FCA East Lewisville show limited alignment with core classical practices beyond branding and a few programmatic breadcrumbs. The public materials reference a “classical approach to teaching” and offer typical school-community features (tours, events, fundraising, etc.) but do not clearly demonstrate engagement with Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, formal Socratic pedagogy, Great Conversations, or the liturgical/theological dimensions highlighted in the classical-education rubric. Consequently, FCA East Lewisville is best described as NOT CLASSICAL in aggregate, with some SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL inflections in pedagogy/community-facing elements.

Key points driving the overall rating

- Evidence of classical branding, but little explicit textual evidence of the six-element canon beyond “classical approach.” This limits a full classical alignment across categories. For example, grades served (K–6) and several events/pages show a standard K–8/co-curricular flavor rather than a Grto-Grade-12 Great Books trajectory or Trivium/Quadrivium emphasis. See public materials noting “Grades Served: K-6” and the site’s frequent reference to a “classical approach” without detailing Great Books or traditional classical curricula.
- Pedagogical signals appear branded as classical (e.g., “School Tours,” “classical approach to teaching”), but concrete, category-defining practices (Socratic seminars, scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, imitation/mastery traditions, or the Great Books/Great Conversation elements) are not clearly documented on the public-facing pages. This yields a SOMEWHAT-leaning signal in Pedagogy but not a robust Classic signal in Curriculum or Pedagogy. See School Tours language emphasizing a “classical approach to teaching.”
- Parent/community signals exist (parent tours, information nights, fundraiser days), but the materials do not provide explicit evidence of parents as co-teachers or a formal, sustained “formation of affections” or “cultural transmission” program beyond general school-community events. This yields a partial alignment in Parents & Community but not a full Classical signal. See Parent Information Night and tours content.

What would move the needle

- If FCA East Lewisville wanted to move toward CLASSICAL, the following concrete steps would most strongly shift the rubric:
    - Curriculum: Document and implement a liberal-arts spine that includes the Trivium and Quadrivium, Great Books/primary sources, Latin (or classical languages), and integration of history with literature and philosophy. Publicly publish a detailed curriculum map referencing Great Books and a canonical Western canon alongside historical depth.
    - Pedagogy: Publish explicit, classroom-level descriptions of pedagogical practices aligned with classical education, including Socratic seminars, scholé-inspired restful learning blocks, memorization/recitation (chanting/poetry), and imitation/mastery models across grade bands.
    - Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: If the school intends a religious dimension, clearly articulate the theological/theistic framework and its integration with the curriculum (while respecting non-sectarian vs. sectarian goals, as applicable). Publicly reference liturgy, ceremony, or communal worship practices if part of the model.
    - Faculty: Develop and publish a “living curriculum” portrait of teacher-mentors, with professional development in classical pedagogy, and explicitly describe mentorship and collegiality structures among faculty.
    - Parents & Community: Expand formal parent-partnership programs (co-teaching opportunities, family catechesis or shared classical readings, parent book clubs, and guided home-learning rhythms) and clearly articulate how parents participate as co-educators.
    - Public communications: Regularly publish progress on classical-curriculum goals (e.g., annual classical framework updates, Great Conversation topics, classical texts read, and student performances in public channels).
