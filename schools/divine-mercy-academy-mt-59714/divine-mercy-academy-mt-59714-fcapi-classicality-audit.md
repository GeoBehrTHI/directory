## Executive Summary

Divine Mercy Academy (Mt 59714) presents a clearly Catholic, classical-model program with a strong emphasis on forming the whole person through faith, virtue, and a traditional liberal-arts framework. The school foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty as transcendent aims, anchors instruction in Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and embeds liturgical and catechetical practices (CGS, Eucharistic life, parish connections). Public materials repeatedly emphasize the Catholic mission, formation of character, and parental engagement via a family-centered House Model. While the school articulates many classical markers, explicit references to Great Books and some traditional pedagogy elements (e.g., heavy memorization/recitation, extended Socratic seminars across all grades) are less visible in the public materials. Taken together, the school is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with stronger signals for Religious/Theological Foundations, Purposes/Goals, and Community/Parent engagement, and more tentative signals for the full spectrum of Curriculum and Pedagogy expectations typical of a fully classical program.

Overall aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Taken together, the aggregate profile leans toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle (for a MORE CLASSICAL standing)

- Expand explicit, school-wide Socratic or seminar-based pedagogy across grades (with teacher training and measurable outcomes) to strengthen the Pedagogy signal.
- Increase explicit articulation of the “living curriculum” concept for Faculty, including a stated teacher-portrait and a visible, shared model of exemplary practice, aligned with “model teachers and mentors” in the public materials.
- Document and publicize a more formal memorization/recitation program (e.g., choral recitations, Latin or classical memory work) that aligns with the Six Elements’ emphasis on memorization and rhythm in learning.
- Deepen the “Great Books” framework as a cross-disciplinary backbone (not just episodic mentions) to ensure the curriculum meets the full spectrum of the “Seven Liberal Arts” and “Great Books/Great Conversation” expectations.
