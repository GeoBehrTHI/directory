## Executive Summary

Mesquite Christian Academy NV 89027 (MCA) presents a clearly classical, Christian orientation in its public materials. The school positions itself as a private Christian school delivering a classical Christian education, with explicit emphasis on God-centered formation, virtue, and a curricular emphasis that includes classic liberal-arts elements (including Latin, logic, and rhetoric). The school also foregrounds core values such as Godliness, Obedience, and Leadership, and emphasizes a partnership with parents and a cooperative community that involves families in the educational project. These signals align MCA with a classical framework that foregrounds transcendental truths, character formation, and a humanities-rich curriculum. At the same time, MCA’s public materials do not consistently articulate certain elements that some classical schools emphasize (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation programming and detailed upper-school-classical pedagogy like formal Socratic seminars). Taken as a whole, MCA most strongly demonstrates CLASSICAL orientations in several domains, while certain elements remain partially developed. Overall, MCA is BEST characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Core signals of CLASSICAL orientation appear in religious/theological foundations, formation aims, faculty narrative, and parent/community partnerships. Evidence includes explicit claims of a Classical Christian Education, God-centered formation, and a core-values framework (Godliness, Obedience, Leadership) alongside curricular claims that include Latin, Logic, and Rhetoric, and a history-inclusive, broad liberal-arts focus. See MCA’s explicit framing as a Classical Christian school and quotes about integrating God’s truth into the curriculum, mission statements, and core values.   
- The school’s curriculum section reveals 7 liberal-arts-oriented elements (grammar/English, Latin, Logic, Rhetoric) and historical coverage across subjects, which maps to the Trivium/Quadrivium framework in the classical lexicon. This identifies MCA with the curricular side of classical education, albeit without explicit statements about Great Books or the full Great Conversation in the public materials. 
- The pedagogy signals in MCA materials lean toward classical forms (memory/recitation, formation through exemplary models, religious/theological framing) but do not consistently document all hallmark outputs (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars in grades K–8). This yields a mixed signal on pedagogy. 
- The parents/community axis is strong: MCA presents open parental involvement, volunteer opportunities, and a partnership model with parents as co-teachers in a Christian formation framework. This aligns with classical expectations around family formation and community life, though not every classic-linguistic signal (e.g., formal liturgical calendar on campus) is explicit. 

Abstract classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- The school explicitly invokes Christian formation and Scripture-focused aims (e.g., “evaluate their entire range of experience in the light of the Scriptures,” “distinguish Truth from Falsehood”). These phrases anchor truth-claims and moral formation within a Christian framework. 
- The core-values section includes GODLINESS, OBEDIENCE, and LEADERSHIP, reinforcing a liturgical-communal and character-formation orientation within a Christian frame. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords found: truth, God, Scriptures, theology (implied through “God’s truth” and “Scriptures”), virtue/character (via Godliness and obedience), church/community imagery (River Valley Bible Church appears in events; liturgical rhythms are suggested by the emphasis on worship-like communal life). 
- Negative keywords: none clearly evident in public materials (no strong signals of relativism, secularism, or value-neutral ethics in MCA’s presented materials). The absence of negative signals supports a Classical orientation in this category.

Why this rating
- The public materials strongly situate MCA within a religious-metaphysical frame, with explicit claims of a Classical Christian approach and theology-integrated pedagogy, which aligns with the Positive/Negative keyword framework’s criteria for Transcendental Moral Realism, Theological grounding, and liturgical/communal practices. The presence of terms like “God-centered,” “Scriptures,” and “distinguish Truth from Falsehood” indicates robust alignment with Element 1’s Classical expectations.

This would reinforce liturgical and communal practices in a more explicit way.
