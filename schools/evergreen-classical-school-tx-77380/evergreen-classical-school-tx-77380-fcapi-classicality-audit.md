## Executive Summary

Evergreen Classical School TX 77380 presents a clearly classical, Christian, and collaborative model. Public materials from the site consistently foreground a Christian, Biblical, and Trivium-based framework, with explicit emphasis on Truth, Goodness, and Beauty, and a commitment to formation of character and virtue. The school openly describes its aim as Biblically anchored, classical, and community-centered, with a strong parental partnership component and a blended (collaborative) pedagogy. Evidence throughout the materials shows explicit reference to the Triune God, Scripture, and worship-oriented language, alongside classical liberal arts content (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and a “Great Conversation” with Great Books, and a call to virtue and wisdom.

Overall classification across the six classical criteria leans CLASSICAL, with a few category-specific nuances that temper full certainty in Pedagogy (category 4). The strongest alignments are in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The Pedagogy category shows substantial classical elements (including contemplative/restful aspects and memory-based practice) but is slightly weaker on explicit Socratic/seminar-based high-school pedagogy in public materials, which places it in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for that single category. The aggregate assessment remains predominantly CLASSICAL.

- Strong religious/metaphysical grounding: Christian/Biblical framing; Triune God; Scripture and worship language.
- Clear teleology toward virtue, wisdom, and formation (not merely information).
- Curriculum built around Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis.
- Pedagogy emphasizes both contemplative/restful aspects (Scholé) and memory-based recitation, with higher-grade opportunities for dialogue/debate; explicit Socratic/seminar language is less prominent in the public-facing materials, yielding a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating in Pedagogy.
- Faculty are presented as living curriculum and partner with families; visible faculty profiles and a Board of Directors indicate a strong community framework.
- Parents as co-teachers and partners are front-and-center; clear emphasis on family partnership and collaboration with church communities.

What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases): provide more explicit, public-facing articulation of Socratic seminar practices in upper grades, broaden explicit “Great Books” integration across core subjects, and foreground Scholé as a daily, stated pedagogy across all grades (not only in lower-grade indications).

- Abstracted overall classification: CLASSICAL.

- Language centers on Christ, God, and worship-like framing (e.g., “Our desire is to create an environment in which the Holy Spirit invites our students to experience the fullness of Christ,” and “worshiping the Trinity as image bearers”). This is reinforced in the firecrawl materials. 
- The Great Conversation / Great Books and the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) are presented as core to the religiously anchored Classical model. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: Christian, Biblical, Triune God, Christ, Scripture, worship, God, Holy Spirit, Trinity, Great Conversation, Great Books, Trivium.
- Negative indicators: None of the explicit secular/anti-religious indicators appear; no conflict with transcendental realism is stated negatively in public materials.

Why this rating
- The public-facing content strongly situates the school within a theologically grounded classical frame and uses liturgical/community language (worship, Trinity, Scripture). This aligns with the Transcendental moral realism and Grounded in Theology criteria from the classical rubric. The absence of secular or non-theological framing supports CLASSICAL here.

### Category 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- Lower grades emphasize contemplative, restful learning and memory-building through singing/chanting (Scholé-like practices) and memorization. This is visible in lower-grade descriptions and organizational materials describing Primer instruction and collaborative activities. 
- Upper grades are described as engaging in Socratic discussion, seminars, or debates and using rhetoric in instruction; the classical model anticipates these discussions; however, explicit public-facing statements about full Socratic-method implementation across all upper grades are not consistently visible in the provided public content. Still, the pathway to debate and rhetoric is indicated in the model. 
- The materials emphasize a blend of contemplative and rigorous elements, consistent with a classical pedagogy that uses both memory-based practice and higher-order discussion. The “Scholé” and memory-oriented approach is explicitly stated in public materials; upper-grade Socratic dialogs/seminars appear as a component of the pedagogy, though not always described in every page. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: Scholé (restful learning), singing/chanting/memorization, formation through imitation and practice, Socratic dialogue/dialectic (as a potential practice in high school), guided inquiry.
- Negative indicators: Explicit, public-facing, universal implementation of Socratic seminars across all grades is not consistently shown in the available public content. The presence of a strong contemplative/memory-based pedagogy without consistent explicit public statements of full Socratic method reduces the absolutism of “Socratic” pedagogy in all grades.

Why this rating
- The school exhibits substantial classical pedagogy elements (restful learning, memorization/chanting, formation through imitation) and indicates high-level Socratic/seminar practices for upper levels, but the public materials do not always present a uniform, fully explicit, grade-wide Socratic approach. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than full CLASSICAL in Pedagogy.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Make Socratic/seminal pedagogy explicit for all grades: Publish a clear, public framework showing how Socratic dialogue and seminars are implemented across middle and upper grades, with sample rubrics and outcomes, to strengthen the Pedagogy category to CLASSICAL.
- Publicly foreground Scholé as a daily, across-the-board practice: While there are hints of contemplative/memory work, a formal, universal statement across all grades about restful learning (Scholé) would strengthen Category 4.
- Expand Great Books integration across core subjects: Emphasize the Great Books as a recurring, grade-spanning requirement beyond the upper grades to strengthen Curriculum and its articulation in the Great Conversation across content areas.
- Increase transparent articulation of “living curriculum” in faculty communications: Publish more explicit language about how faculty embody and enact the living curriculum in day-to-day teaching across grade levels, increasing perceived alignment with the Faculty rubric.
- Document measurable virtue/character formation outcomes: Add public metrics or narrative evidence of character formation and virtue development (to align with the Formation of Affections and Cultivation of Wisdom/ Virtue indicators) and connect to assessment practices.
