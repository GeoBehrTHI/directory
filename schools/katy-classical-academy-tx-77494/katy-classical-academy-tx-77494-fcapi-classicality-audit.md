## Executive Summary

Katy Classical Academy (KCA) demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL orientation across all six rubric categories. Public materials from the school's website emphasize a Biblical worldview, formation of character and virtue, a clearly defined liberal-arts curriculum (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) organized around a four-year history arc, and pedagogy that blends memory work, chanting, discussion, and Socratic-style inquiry as students advance into upper grades. The faculty is framed as partners in a living curriculum, and parents are positioned as co-teachers in a formal partnership, with explicit references to In Loco Parentis and ongoing parental involvement. Taken together, the evidence indicates a cohesive Classical Christian approach, with strong signals in every category that align with the Six Essential Elements framework for classical education. The aggregate ranking is CLASSICAL.

Key evidentiary signals include: a stated Biblical worldview and Christ-centered orientation across shifts in perspective, mission, and program materials; explicit description of the Trivium (Grammar; Dialectic/Logic; Rhetoric) and a four-year historical curriculum cycle; memory-work, chanting, and Socratic/dialogic practices in upper grades; a staff described as a “passionate team” and a living curriculum; and a clear emphasis on partnership with parents, including In Loco Parentis language and co-teacher modeling.

The absence of explicit negative signals further supports CLASSICAL rather than SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.

Citations: Biblical worldview and Christian framing are explicit in multiple pages, including Shift In Perspective and Who We Are, with direct language about Biblical worldview and Christ-centered worldview.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum is explicitly organized around the Trivium: Grammar (K–6), Dialectic (6–9), and Rhetoric (9–12). This is spelled out on the curriculum/levels page. 
- A four-year world-history cycle governs coursework (Year 1–4: Ancient World to present), with literature, geography, fine arts, and history integrated around the historical periods. All students study the same time period at their level, reinforcing a cohesive classical approach. 
- The program emphasizes Latin and other classical languages, along with “classical curriculum” per the site. The program’s own description notes Latin as part of the curriculum and the “classical disciplines of art, history, logic, rhetoric, and apologetics.” 
- The Great Books/Great Conversation orientation is referenced through statements about studying literature and the historical context, and the same four-year cycle reinforces reading across grades. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, dialectic/logic, rhetoric, great books (and the Great Conversation), history, Latin, classical curriculum, literature, geography, logic, apologetics.
- Negative keywords present: None detected in public materials for this category (e.g., social studies-as-history substitution; project-based emphasis that would signal a move away from classical pedagogy).

Why this rating
- The explicit articulation of the Trivium, the four-year history cycle, and classical-language components strongly align with Element 3’s criteria and the Six Elements positive indicators, yielding a CLASSICAL rating.

Citations: Grammar/Dialectic/Rhetoric levels are described on the Curriculum/Learning Levels page; World History cycle and cross-grade unity are described in the program materials.   

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogical practices include a blend of memory work, chanting, recitation, and question-and-answer formats, with a focus on memory and liturgical-like repetition in lower grades; this is documented in detailed public descriptions of classroom practice. 
- In upper grades, there is an emphasis on Socratic discussion, dialogue, critical thinking, and rhetoric, with explicit mention of dialectic and debate-like activities as students progress. The dialectic phase (middle/early high school) emphasizes analysis and argument. 
- The material notes that education should involve wonder and contemplative moments (scholé-like learning) and that some aspects of education should be restful, not overly rigorous at all times. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: memory work, chanting, recitation, narration, dialectic/seminar, rhetoric, discussion, contemplative/scholé, wonder, inquiry, debates.
- Negative keywords present: None detected in the public materials for this category (e.g., “project-based learning” as a sole driver or fully constructivist approaches).

Why this rating
- The pedagogy described aligns well with classical methods (memory work and chanting in lower grades; Socratic/dialectic seminars and rhetoric in upper grades; contemplative/restful moments) and thus supports a CLASSICAL rating.

Citations: Pedagogical practices described in the lower-school chant/memory passages; dialectic/upper-level discussion and rhetoric described in the curriculum-level pages.

## Aggregate Ranking

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Rationale: Each of the six categories shows clear alignment with the Six Essential Elements’ Positive Keyword indicators (truth, virtue, knowledge of the true/good/beautiful; Trinity of grammar/dialectic/rhetoric; Great Books/Great Conversation; Socratic pedagogy with memory work; living faculty; partnered parents). The school’s public materials repeatedly emphasize Biblical-Christian grounding, formation of character, a Trivium-based curriculum, a combination of memory/chanting and Socratic/lectured pedagogy, and a structured parent partnership, with minimal to no signaling of negative indicators in the public materials provided. The knowledge-lexicon-based scoring supports this assessment as well, given the presence of terms like truth, beauty, virtue, theology, liturgy, worship, scholé, wonder, and great books, and the absence of clear negative signals in the school’s materials.
