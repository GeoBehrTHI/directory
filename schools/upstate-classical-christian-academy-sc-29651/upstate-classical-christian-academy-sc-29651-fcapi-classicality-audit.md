## Executive Summary

Upstate Classical Christian Academy (UCCA) in Greer, SC presents as a classical Christian school with clear theological framing and a formation-oriented mission. Public materials emphasize Christ-centered, biblical worldview education, liberal arts formation, and a paideia-inspired approach anchored in truth, goodness, and beauty. While the school demonstrates strong alignment with Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose and Goal, some elements of a full, explicit classical curriculum, pedagogy, and community practices are less directly evidenced in the public materials. On balance, the school is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong religious formation and a robust formation/virtue orientation, and partial alignment in curriculum, pedagogy, faculty portrayal, and parent/community engagement.

Key signals from public materials:
- Religious and metaphysical framing is explicit: Christ-centered education, biblical worldview, and theology as interpretive principle are stated in multiple pieces of content. 
- Purpose/goal emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and truth-guided education rather than mere job/work preparation. 
- Curriculum is described in liberal-arts terms (liberal arts education) and classical framing, with reference to Trivium in ACCS context; direct menu items like Great Books are less visible in the site content, though Great-Conversation language is part of the broader classical lexicon. 
- Pedagogy shows elements of memory work, catechesis, and contemplative/restful learning in public materials; explicit Socratic/seminar-based methods are not clearly demonstrated on the site, though they are highlighted in classical pedagogy lore. 
- Faculty portrayal is present (e.g., a headmaster; founder-led framing; living-curriculum ideas appear in classical discussions) but not deeply elaborated as a formal “faculty portrait” in the public materials examined here. 
- Parent/community engagement is evident in a waitlist/visit-friendly stance and a community-oriented ethos, but explicit programs for parent formation or formal partnership structures are less documented in the pages reviewed.
