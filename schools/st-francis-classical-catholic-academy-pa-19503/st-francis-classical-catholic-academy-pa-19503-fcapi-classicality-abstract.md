## ABSTRACT

Saint Francis Classical Catholic Academy (SFCCA) presents a comprehensive CLASSICAL profile across all six categories of the classical education rubric. Public materials emphasize a Catholic, liturgical, and theologically grounded foundation; a purpose oriented toward formation of virtue, wisdom, and the good—embracing truth, goodness, and beauty; a curriculum built around the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium, with explicit Great Books/western civilization engagement; pedagogy that combines contemplative/restful elements (Scholé), memorization/recitation, and Socratic/seminar-style discourse; faculty positioned as mentors and living exemplars; and a robust partnership with families and community engagement. The school clearly demonstrates transcendental realism and sacred formation, aligning with the core expectations of classical education.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Catholic identity, liturgical practice, theology, and sacred tradition are central, signaling CLASSICAL alignment. Evidence includes Christ-centered pedagogy and liturgical life.  

- Purpose and Goal: Formation-focused aims and a clear emphasis on the true/good/beautiful; Great Books and contemplative aims reflect the classical telos. 

- Curriculum: Explicit structure around the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, and integrated study; history and Western civilization are threaded through the curriculum. 

- Pedagogy: A balance of contemplative Scholé, memorization/recitation, and high-level discussions (Socratic/seminar elements) in upper grades; rhetoric development evident in the Rhetoric School.  

- Faculty: Faculty showcased as skilled, virtuous, and mentorship-oriented; living curricular approach indicated by humanities emphasis and teacher profiles. 

- Parents & Community: Strong partnership with families, parental formation opportunities, and open-campus philosophy; admissions processes reflect family involvement. 

Overall, SFCCA embodies CLASSICAL education as defined by the Six Essential Elements, with explicit Catholic identity, classical curriculum structure, and evidence of ancient-to-modern classical pedagogy, all anchored in a community-focused model that actively engages parents.
