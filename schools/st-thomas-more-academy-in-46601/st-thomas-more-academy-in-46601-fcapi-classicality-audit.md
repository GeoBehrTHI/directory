## Executive Summary

St Thomas More Academy (STMA) presents a clearly Catholic, classical-leaning program with emphasis on virtue formation, a liberal-arts oriented curriculum, and a liturgical, community-centered ethos. Public site materials repeatedly foreground faith, Eucharistic worship, and a curriculum anchored in classical literature, languages, and the liberal arts. Evidence includes a stated Catholic identity with daily Mass, a description of classical education as developing wisdom and virtue, and explicit curricular elements such as primary/classical texts and Latin/Greek study. However, while the school demonstrates strong classical contours in religion, curriculum, and exemplar language, some classical pedagogy markers (e.g., explicit, school-wide Socratic seminars in upper grades, a publicly stated “living curriculum,” or explicit parent-as-co-teacher partnerships) are less directly documented on the site. Taken together, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal) showing stronger, more explicit Classical alignment, and Categories 3–6 displaying solid classical commitments but with room to more explicitly articulate certain classical-pedagogical and community-ownership dimensions.

Key evidentiary threads from the public materials include:

- Catholic identity and liturgical life: “Saint Thomas More Academy is a K-12 Catholic classical school” and daily Eucharistic celebration, with explicit references to Mass as central to community life.
- Classical, liberal-arts curriculum orientation: explicit mention of “classical liberal arts education,” “Careful Reading of Primary and Classical Texts,” and multilingual/arts components (Latin/Greek, music, drama, visual arts).
- Formation-focused goals: language about forming virtue and character, “formation in virtue and character development,” and “freedom for excellence” oriented toward serving others and glorifying God.
- Public articulation of religious-liturgical orientation as a core aspect of education: Eucharist as “source and summit,” and a stated aim to educate in virtue, truth, goodness, and beauty.
