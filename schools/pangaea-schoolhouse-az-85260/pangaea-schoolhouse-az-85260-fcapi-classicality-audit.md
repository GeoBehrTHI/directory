## Executive Summary

Pangaea Schoolhouse appears to be a modern, faith-integrated microschool in Fountain Hills, AZ that explicitly aligns with classical education in its stated framework and practices. The public materials consistently reference the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), the aim to form mind and character, and a pedagogy that blends Socratic dialogue with integrated, project-oriented learning. The school foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable, emphasizes virtue and wisdom as primary goals, and positions parents as partners in formation. Several elements—classical foundations, Socratic discussion, and a strong emphasis on virtue and faith—are clearly present. However, explicit mentions of Great Books/Great Conversation and some traditional memory-based practices (e.g., recitation, chanting, scholé-day memory work) are not as prominently documented in the accessible materials, which places some aspects of the classic program as aspirational rather than fully explicit on the site.

Aggregate ranking: CLASSICAL

Key insights:
- The school clearly centers truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and faith within its mission and daily practice (e.g., daily prayer, Christ-centered community, pursuit of wisdom and virtue) which aligns strongly with the classical end of formation. This is well-supported in the materials. 
- The Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) is explicitly named and integrated with modern, inquiry-based learning, indicating a robust classical scaffolding in curriculum and pedagogy. 
- The public materials emphasize partnership with parents and an engaged community, with multiple pathways (in-person, online, homeschool alliance), which supports a classical ideal of a virtuous community formed in partnership with families.

Provide a public statement or page outlining reading lists and a stated commitment to Great Books as part of the liberal arts core. Also consider clarifying how history is threaded through the curriculum (as a continuous through-line rather than isolated “subjects”) to align with classical history study. This would strengthen the category from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL. See the Six Elements framework for Great Books/Great Conversation cues and the Trivium emphasis. 

- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Reinforce and publicly document traditional memory-work and recitation elements (e.g., prose, poetry, dictation, progymnasmata-style practice) alongside Socratic seminars and project-based work. Making memory-work and recitation part of a formal pedagogy section would push this category toward CLASSICAL. The presence of Socratic dialogue is strong; adding explicit memory-oriented pedagogy would align more fully with the classical model described in the Six Elements lexicon. 

- For Element 5 (Faculty): Consider codifying and publicizing a formal “portrait of a teacher” and a clearer articulation of “living curriculum” in staff profiles and mission statements. While the current materials imply these aspects, a formal, public articulation would strengthen the category’s CLASSICAL alignment. 

- For Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) and Element 1 (Religious Foundations) no major needle-moving changes are required, given the strong presence of formation, virtue, and faith integrated into daily practice and mission.
