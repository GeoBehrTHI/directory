## Executive Summary

St Pius X Catholic School in El Paso, TX (79905) presents a credible classical orientation anchored in Catholic formation. The school public materials emphasize a classical curriculum that includes Latin and engages virtues and classic literature, signaling alignment with the classical mission. The school also explicitly positions itself as a member of the Classical Latin School Association, underscoring its identified identity within the classical education ecosystem. At the same time, explicit details about traditional classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, Great Books reading lists, or a formal Great Conversation program) are less clearly articulated on the site, suggesting a pathway toward classical pedagogy rather than a fully realized classical program across all grade bands. Overall, the school falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong religious/catholic formation and a clear classical curricular orientation, but with room to more fully codify and publicize the full spectrum of classical pedagogy and Great Books components.

Key drivers for the classification:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are clearly foregrounded (Christ-centered environment, sacramental preparation, faith formation, patron saint) and align with classical schools that integrate theology and liturgical life into education. Evidence includes phrases such as “Christ centered,” “sacramental preparation,” and “faith formation is an integral part of our school’s culture” alongside mentions of virtues and Catholic liturgical life. 
- Curriculum shows explicit classical orientation (classical curriculum, Latin, and classic literature) and a stated intention to participate in classical networks, which supports a classical posture.  
- Pedagogy evidence is more aspirational than explicit on the site. There are clear signals of classical aims (Latin, classical literature) but fewer public, detailed statements about Socratic seminars, Great Books, or the Great Conversation. Additional pedagogy signals appear in knowledge-base materials that discuss scholé, singing/chanting, memory, and contemplative elements, which can align with classical pedagogy but are not always stated in SPX site text.  
- Faculty signals emphasize the role of teachers as mentors and the seriousness of staff, with language about highly qualified teachers and a partnership with parents, but without a fully elaborated “living curriculum” portrait. 
- Parents & Community signals strongly emphasize partnership with parents, parental formation, and open campus visits, aligning well with classical education’s emphasis on parent involvement and formation.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Make the classical pedagogy explicit: Publish a dedicated section detailing the full pedagogy (grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, Socratic seminars for upper grades, and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation components). This would elevate Element 4 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Publicly affirm Great Books/Great Conversation: List a current Great Books reading list or annual selection across grade bands, and provide evidence of how Great Books integrate with history, literature, and theology. The addition would strengthen Element 3 and Element 4.
- Expand teacher portraits and faculty development: Add a formal “Living Curriculum” portrait of the teacher, and describe ongoing classical pedagogy training for faculty to meet the classical standard in Element 5.
- Formalize historical integration across disciplines: Demonstrate how history is woven into multiple disciplines beyond social studies, emphasizing Western civilization, primary sources, and timeline-based learning across grades (to reinforce Element 3).
- Increase explicit articulation of the “true/good/beautiful” telos in communications: Include direct statements about truth, goodness, and beauty in mission statements and curriculum descriptions (to strengthen Element 2).
- Deepen parent formation offerings: Expand book studies, seminar series, and home-based liturgy alignment with more frequent opportunities for parents to be co-teachers (to reinforce Element 6 and the general Classical formation approach).
