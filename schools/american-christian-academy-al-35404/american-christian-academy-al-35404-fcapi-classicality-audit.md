## Executive Summary

American Christian Academy (ACA) in Tuscaloosa, AL (35404) presents a public profile that leans strongly toward a religious, formation-centered approach to education, with explicit biblical-theological framing and families as co-partners in education. The publicly visible materials emphasize a Biblical worldview, moral absolutes, prayer, scripture, and a life-oriented aim beyond mere credentialing. However, the school’s curricular and pedagogical language is less explicit about classical liberal arts foundations (e.g., Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Socratic method) and more focused on college-preparatory pathways, life-readiness, and functional alignment with a Christian worldview. Taken together across the six classical-elements categories, ACA demonstrates clear CLASSICAL tendencies in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, and Faculty; strong CLASSICAL signals in Parents & Community; and notable gaps in History/Great-Conversation style curricular commitments and traditional Socratic pedagogy in their public-facing materials. The overall assessment is CLASSICAL, with a few notable gaps that place some domains as NOT CLASSICAL or SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully classical.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

- The strongest affirmative signals are in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, and Parents & Community, anchored by explicit biblical worldview, communal prayer practices, and a partnership model with families. Evidence: ACA’s Biblical Worldview materials, mission statements about partnership with parents, and explicit Christian formation emphasis.

- The Curriculum category shows a robust faith-integrated approach and college-preparatory framing but lacks explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or strongly articulated Great Conversation/History-centered liberal-arts commitments in the public materials, reducing it from fully classical to NOT CLASSICAL in the strict sense. Evidence: Emphasis on Bible, worldview, college-prep, and AP/dual enrollment rather than classical humanities canon.

- Pedagogy signals are mixed: some contemplative/restful learning and spiritual formation are evident, but the public-facing materials do not clearly document ongoing Socratic seminars, classical-memory practices, or rigorous rhetoric instruction in the upper grades. This yields NOT CLASSICAL in Pedagogy. Evidence: Lower-/middle-grade emphasis on Bible classes, memory verses, and a general emphasis on spiritual formation; limited explicit Socratic/seminar language in public materials.

- Faculty signals are strong: teachers described as called to serve, the staff as living examples of faith and character, and a glowing sense of mentorship and partnership with students. This supports CLASSICAL in Faculty. Evidence: Statements about teachers serving God, continuity of care, and staff invested in student success.

- The Parents & Community category is notably strong, with explicit emphasis on partnership with parents, parental formation, and open access to school for families. This yields CLASSICAL in Parents & Community. Evidence: A Partnership with Parents, mission statements about partnership with families, and open-campus-like engagement language.

- Abstract takeaway: ACA aligns with classical aims in several dimensions (theological grounding, formation-oriented purpose, faculty role, and active parent partnership). However, it falls short of a fully classical articulation in Curriculum and Pedagogy (notably lack of explicit Great Books/Trivium-centric curricula and explicit Socratic pedagogy in public documentation). The aggregate suggests CLASSICAL, with room to deepen classical curriculum and instructional methods to reach a stronger, unambiguous classical cadence.

- What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL schools only): N/A here, as the aggregate is CLASSICAL; no category is “Somewhat Classical.” If needed, consider explicit incorporation of Great Books/Trivium pedagogical structures and Socratic seminar practices in upper grades, plus a publicly stated commitment to the Great Conversation and a documented classical curriculum map.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- ACA’s A Biblical Worldview page states that the school teaches a Biblical worldview to students, memorizes scripture, and grounds daily life in biblical truth and the Word of God. This includes explicit statements that instruction from the Bible occurs from preschool through graduation.
- Mission statements emphasize partnership with parents to instill Christian character and academic excellence, underscoring a character-formation foundation aligned with virtue ethics and theological grounding.
- The campus culture includes daily prayer, scripture readings, and chapel-like, liturgical rhythms (prayer, scripture, and communal faith practices) described in the Biblical Worldview materials.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: biblical worldview, Word of God, scripture, Bible, prayer, theology, worship-like practices, moral absolutes (via explicit Christian formation). These map to positive indicators such as theology, sacred scripture, liturgy, reverence, and wonder.
- Negative keywords: not evident in public materials (no signaling of relativism, secular-humanist lean, or values-clarification language). The absence of such negatives reinforces a CLASSICAL alignment.

Why this rating

- The school publicly roots education in biblical-theological foundations, explicitly links daily life to biblical truth, and frames character formation within a theologically grounded worldview. These are core Positive Indicators for Element 1, with minimal to no visible Negative Indicators in the public materials. This strongly supports a CLASSICAL rating for Element 1.

Categories’ score rationale anchors: The lexicon of Positive Keywords (truth, virtue, theology, liturgy, wonder, imago Dei, Logos) is reflected in ACA’s public materials via Biblical Worldview, prayer, and Scripture-centric instruction. Negative signals (relativism, secular humanism) are not evidenced in ACA’s public materials, aligning with CLASSICAL signals. See the classical lexicon and indicators.

- Negative keywords present: minimal to none. The site does not emphasize “workforce readiness” as a primary objective; rather, it emphasizes life preparation aligned with a Biblical worldview.

Why this rating

- ACA communicates formation-focused aims, virtue/character formation, and a telos-oriented vision (truth, goodness, beauty in the classical sense) rather than a narrow vocation-first approach. This supports CLASSICAL in Element 2.

Notes: The public materials emphasize college prep alongside formation, which is common in faith-based non-sectarian settings. ### Element 3: School Curriculum — NOT CLASSICAL

There is substantial evidence of a college-preparatory curriculum and general humanities/arts, but explicit Classical Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, and Great Conversation language is not present in ACA’s public materials.
- The materials highlight Biblical integration and standard Subject-area sequences (Math, Science, History, English) within a faith framework, but do not foreground the Great Books list, Latin/Greek language study, or a seven liberal arts structure as foundational curriculum.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

In ACA’s public materials, these exact classical curricular cues are largely not present; instead, the site emphasizes Biblical integration, college-prep, and standard subjects.
- Negative keywords present: The absence of explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or Socratic pedagogy and a traditional liberal-arts cadence is a negative indicator for Element 3 in the classical rubric.

Why this rating

- The curriculum described publicly is strong in religious formation and college-preparatory aims but does not demonstrate the canonical classical curriculum elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; history-as-Western-civilizational study; memory-based pedagogy). The absence of explicit classical curricular elements places Element 3 as NOT CLASSICAL.

- Evidence shows emphasis on Biblical Worldview, college prep, AP/dual enrollment, and broad subject offerings with faith integration rather than the Seven Liberal Arts framework and Great Books canon. This aligns with NOT CLASSICAL in Element 3.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- ACA’s public materials emphasize Bible classes, integrated biblical truths, and memory verses for Bible lessons; there is a focus on prayer and spiritual formation, which signals a contemplative and formation-oriented pedagogy in the lower grades. There is less explicit documentation of Socratic seminars, rigorous rhetoric training, or high-intensity dialectical teaching in the upper grades within public-facing materials.
- The curriculum pages show standard classroom practices, AP/dual enrollment options, and a broad, career- and college-prep orientation, with limited explicit reference to classical pedagogy (Socratic lectures, memory work, recitation, or scholé). The absence of these elements in the public materials suggests NOT CLASSICAL pedagogy.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: "memory verse," "Bible lessons," "Bible class," "the Word of God" (indicative of liturgical/recitation-style pedagogy, where memory work and recitation are part of tradition). These map to a contemplative/restful pedagogy in the Classical Lexicon.
- Negative keywords present: Absence of explicit Socratic seminars, dialectic methods, or rhetoric-focused instruction in upper grades; absence of clearly stated scholé/restful learning days in the public-facing materials. These are clear signals for NOT CLASSICAL pedagogy.

Why this rating

- While ACA demonstrates some contemplative, faith-centered practices, the public materials do not present a robust classical pedagogy (Socratic method, memory-based recitation beyond biblical memory, or rhetoric-driven speech). The lack of explicit classical pedagogical structures results in a NOT CLASSICAL rating for Element 4.

- The presence of memory-based Bible activities suggests some traditional-cultural practices, but not the full canonical pedagogy associated with classical education. See evidence on prayer/scripture-based routines and memory verses.

## What Would Move the Needle

- As this evaluation lands predominantly in CLASSICAL, there are no category-level “Somewhat Classical” ratings to push toward full classical status. If one sought to further strengthen the CLASSICAL alignment, concrete needle-moving steps could include:
    - Publish a clear Classical Curriculum Map: explicitly articulate Trivium/Quadrivium elements, Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, and a historical-humanities emphasis within the curriculum. This would convert NOT CLASSICAL signals in Element 3 into CLASSICAL signals.
    - Document Pedagogical Practices: publicly outline Socratic seminars, dialectic-based discussions, and rhetoric training in upper grades, plus memory-work and recitation practices aligned with scholé (restful learning) principles.
    - Increase Great-Books Orientation: identify canonical Western literature and philosophical texts used in the curriculum, along with Latin/Greek or classical language study as foundational elements, to strengthen Element 3’s CLASSICAL alignment.

- These noteable steps are not currently required to achieve CLASSICAL given the present aggregate assessment, but they would reinforce a more comprehensive classical identity.
