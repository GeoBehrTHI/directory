## Executive Summary

Westside Christian Academy (WCA) presents a clearly classical orientation anchored in Christian formation. Public materials emphasize a Biblically integrated, classical framework (grammar–logic–rhetoric), a formation-focused purpose, and visible attention to parental partnership and community involvement. The site repeatedly foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty as real, knowable ideals; it describes the Trivium as a core organizing principle; and it highlights a mission of discipleship and Christ-centered education. Taken together, these signals place WCA as CLASSICAL across most of the classical rubric, with Curriculum (Element 3) showing strong classical structure but without explicit, public evidence of full Great Books/Great Conversation commitments in the available public content—this slightly tempers a full CLASSICAL label for that category to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are strongly present via Biblically integrated, Christ-centered language and formation-focused aims. Evidence: mission statements and program descriptions (formation, Trivium, gospel-centered language).
- The school’s stated purpose and goals emphasize formation, virtue, and the real goods of truth, goodness, and beauty, rather than workforce-readiness in public messaging.
- Curriculum shows the Trivium explicitly and positions classical education, but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments are not openly cited in the accessible public pages (category 3 nuance).
- Pedagogy aligns with classical methods (sectioned by the Trivium; occasional Socratic/seminar-like activity appears in content about the classical method and related discussions).
- Faculty structure is presented via explicit “Staff & Faculty” and governance pages, signaling a living curriculum and mentor model.
- Parents and community are foregrounded (Classical Parent University, parent partnerships, and ongoing community fundraising), consistent with classical pedagogy that treats families as co-educators.
