## Executive Summary

St. Stephen’s Classical Christian Academy (SSCCA) in Eldersburg, Maryland embodies a robust Classical Christian program, with explicit integration of the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), a strong emphasis on Great Books and the Great Conversation, and a clearly articulated Christian worldview woven through curriculum, pedagogy, and community life. Public materials consistently frame education as formation of character and mind, centered in truth, goodness, and beauty, anchored by Scripture and prayer. The school presents itself as a parish-based classical school with a mission to develop disciples of Christ, training minds and forming hearts, and it foregrounds virtue, curriculum alignment to the liberal arts, and a partnership with families. On balance, SSCCA aligns with a CLASSICAL classification across all six categories: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose and Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; and Parents & Community.

Key evidence includes: (1) explicit Mission statements emphasizing forming hearts and training minds in a Christian frame, grounded in Biblical truth; (2) a rigorous Classical model centered on the Trivium, Great Books, and historical study across the curriculum; (3) explicit description of lower- and upper-school pedagogy that blends singing/chanting and memory work in early grades with Socratic inquiry, debate, and rhetoric in upper grades; (4) a faculty culture described as mentors within a living curriculum; (5) a strong, ongoing emphasis on parent partnership within a parish-community setting; and (6) clear alignment with a Christian worldview throughout governance and community life. See cited materials from SSCCA’s site, including Mission statements, Classical Model, Biblical Basis, Curriculum descriptions, and Academy Distinctions.

## What Would Move the Needle

No major needle-moving changes are required. SSCCA already demonstrates a cohesive CLASSICAL alignment across all six categories, including explicit Christian formation, Trivium-based curriculum, Great Books orientation, Socratic and rhetoric-focused pedagogy, faculty as mentors, and strong parental/community engagement. To move beyond CLASSICAL to a more holistic, ultra-explicit “absolutely classical” standard in future iterations, the school could consider:
- More explicit mapping of Great Books selections and discussion prompts across all grades to demonstrate a continuous Great Conversation chain.
- A formal, published “living curriculum” document linking teachable moments in each subject to the Trivium stages and to virtue-based outcomes, ensuring uniformity across grade cohorts.
- A more explicit articulation of “scholé” or contemplative practices in daily routines to foreground contemplative rest as a unique classical practice, in addition to active Socratic inquiry.

Note: The above would be additive refinements; current materials already reflect CLASSICAL alignment.
