## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy Queen Martyrs (Sun Prairie, WI) demonstrates a strong classical orientation rooted in Catholic faith, with explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty, virtue formation, and a liberal-arts curriculum. Public materials emphasize a Catholic, classical identity, daily Mass and regular sacraments, and a Three Pillars model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) that frames both curriculum and community life. There is clear alignment with several core classical criteria (Religious foundations, formation-focused purpose, liberal-arts curriculum, and a parental/community partnership), while some traditionally explicit pedagogical and faculty practices are less directly articulated in the public site content (e.g., explicit Socratic pedagogy or a formal “living curriculum” narrative). Overall, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong CLASSICAL elements in Categories 1, 2, and 6, and notable but less explicit alignment in Categories 3–5.

ABSTRACT

This Chesterton Academy Queen Martyrs evaluation applies a six-category classical-education rubric derived from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School, using both the public-school-site content and the classical lexicon to determine orientation. The school presents a pronounced religious-metaphysical foundation (Element 1) through explicit Catholic identity, daily Mass, sacramental life, and a theology-informed pedagogy. The School Purpose & Goal (Element 2) strongly emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful teleology, aligning with classical aims. Curriculum (Element 3) and Pedagogy (Element 4) show liberal-arts/classical alignment with a Western-tradition emphasis and reflective, communal practices—but with less explicit, consistent statements about certain canonical classical-pedagogical methods (e.g., explicit Great Books, Socratic seminars, or scholé days) in the site copy. Faculty (Element 5) is represented through leadership and a formation-oriented environment, yet the public narrative lacks a fully articulated “living curriculum” portrayal of teachers. Parents & Community (Element 6) is strongly developed via open houses, shadow days, and an engaged Catholic community; parental involvement is clearly encouraged and facilitated.
