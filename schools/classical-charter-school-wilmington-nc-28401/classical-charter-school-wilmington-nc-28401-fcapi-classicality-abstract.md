## ABSTRACT

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- This school shows partial alignment with classical education through curriculum signals (Latin, Classical Literature, Chronological Western History) and a faculty emphasis on virtue and high-quality teaching. However, pedagogy centers on Direct Instruction rather than the more canonical classical methods (Socratic dialogue, Scholé, memory work). The religious/metaphysical foundations show a bend toward transcendental aims (truth and virtue) but lack explicit theological and liturgical community practices in public materials. Parent/community engagement exists but is not consistently framed as a robust co-educator partnership in the public materials.

Category-by-category snapshot:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: truth and virtue are foregrounded; theology and liturgical practices are not explicitly presented.
- School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: formation-oriented aims appear in mission-focused language, but explicit formation-over-information framing is not strongly stated.
- Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: Latin, Classical Literature, and Western History signals; absence of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation framing and Trivium language.
- Pedagogy — NOT CLASSICAL: Direct Instruction dominates the public messaging; lack of explicit Socratic/seminar-based practices or Scholé-based pacing in the accessible content.
- Faculty — CLASSICAL: emphasis on high-quality, virtuous teachers and mentorship aligns with the classical teacher archetype.
- Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: there is open enrollment and community activity, but explicit public framing of parents as co-educators is not strongly present.

Overall aggregate: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What would move the needle: Implement explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, adopt and publicize a Trivium-based pedagogy with memory work and Scholé-inspired pacing, incorporate liturgical/community practices, articulate a formal family-partnering model, and foreground canonical texts and Western civilization content in public communications.
