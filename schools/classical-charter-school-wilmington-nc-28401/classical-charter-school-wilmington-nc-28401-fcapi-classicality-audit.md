## Executive Summary

Classical Charter Schools of Wilmington (CCS-Wilmington) demonstrates a hybrid profile that leans toward classical aims in select areas but relies heavily on non-classical pedagogy and messaging in others. Public-facing materials emphasize virtues such as truth and virtue, and they situate the school within a tradition of classical charter networks, with curriculum elements like Latin and Classical Literature appearing in admissions materials. However, the primary instructional approach highlighted in the site copy centers on Direct Instruction and structured environments rather than the full spectrum of classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic dialogue, glorious memory work, or Scholé-based contemplative practice). Taken together, CCS-Wilmington falls into the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category overall, with clear room to move toward a fully Classical profile by expanding explicit Great Books engagement, classical trivium-based pedagogy, liturgical/community practices, and deeper partnership framing with families.

Key drivers for this assessment:
- Evidence of classical curricular elements (Latin, Classical Literature, Western History) alongside strong use of Direct Instruction and high-structure delivery.
- Public materials emphasize virtue, truth, and moral formation, but offer limited or no explicit theology, liturgical practice, or definitional appeals to the full spectrum of classical catechesis.
- Faculty messaging emphasizes quality and virtue in teaching; parental involvement exists but is not framed as a core co-educator partnership in the public materials.
- Overall, the six-category rubric yields CLASSICAL in only a minority of categories; NOT CLASSICAL in one category (Pedagogy) and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in the rest, with an aggregate leaning toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The public overview highlights Direct Instruction as a central instructional approach. Direct Instruction is typically associated with structured, teacher-led, script-driven delivery rather than the inquiry-based, Socratic, or memory-based pedagogy that characterizes classical education (which often features Socratic discussion, Scholé, memorization, and chant-based recall).
  - There is no explicit public-facing documentation of Socratic seminars, Harkness-style discussions, or memory work (recitation, chanting, or liturgical memory practices) on the CCS-Wilmington site copy.
  - Some CCS materials reference “classical curriculum” but the primary instructional description emphasizes Direct Instruction and high structure in the classroom. This is a divergence from core classical pedagogy signals (Socratic dialogue, memorization, rhetoric training, memory-based repetition). 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords signaling classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic dialogue, Scholé, memorization/recitation, memory work, rhetoric) are not present in the public copy. The presence of Direct Instruction is a negative signal relative to classical pedagogy.
  - Negative keywords present: Direct Instruction as the stated method; absence of explicit Socratic or memory-based methods.
  - Quick tally: Positive signals for classical pedagogy: 0; Negative signals present: Direct Instruction referenced; 1 dominant negative signal.
- Why this rating
  - The pedagogy signals are dominated by non-classical methods (Direct Instruction) and lack explicit documentation of core classical pedagogical practices (Socratic seminars, Scholé, memory work). Therefore, Element 4 is NOT CLASSICAL.

Citations: Overview page listing Direct Instruction as a foundational approach.

## What would move the needle

- Explicitly foreground Great Books and the Great Conversation in public messaging, including targeted curriculum statements about reading primary sources and canonical texts.
- Integrate and publicly describe Trivium-based pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and confirm its presence in lower/middle grades, with clear articulation of memory work (recitation, chanting) and Scholé-inspired pacing.
- Elevate a liturgical or communal dimension (even secular, reflective liturgy or ritual elements) that aligns with liturgical and communal practices, signaling a more robust metaphysical grounding.
- Codify and communicate a formal partnership with families as co-educators, with parent formation offerings (book studies, classical pedagogy workshops) and open avenues for parental involvement as core to the school’s mission.
- Strengthen the “Great Books” and Western canon signals (e.g., explicit curricula referencing Homer, Plato, Augustine, Shakespeare, etc.), and articulate a coherent classical formation path through grades K–12.
- Ensure public materials explicitly reference Theology or sacred tradition where appropriate, and present liturgical or ceremonial elements that underscore a sacred or reverent dimension to education, as part of a comprehensive classical framework.
