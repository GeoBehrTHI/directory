## Executive Summary

Trinity School Meadow View (Falls Church, VA) presents a distinctly classical educational posture rooted in liberal-arts formation, engagement with Great Books and primary sources, and a catechetical/historic-Christian framework. Across five of the six classical elements, the school demonstrates a robust alignment with classical education principles: a/theological grounding with transcendent aims; formation of virtue and wisdom as primary goals; a curriculum centered on the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Great Books; pedagogy that blends Socratic inquiry with disciplined practice; and a living, communal faculty model. The sixth dimension—Parents & Community—shows less explicit evidence of structured parental partnerships or formal co-teaching roles on public-facing materials, which keeps this category from fully matching the Classical ideal, though the school’s culture is clearly community-oriented. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL, with room to strengthen explicit parent-partnership practices to achieve a fully holistic Classical profile.

Key takeaways driving the assessment:
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are explicit and integral, with robust Christian/theological framing and scriptural study embedded in curriculum. Evidence includes references to theology, scripture, Old/New Testament study, and a deeply Christian ethos in classroom culture. (Cited materials show Christus-centered language and theology-informed formation)  
- Purpose and goals foreground truth, goodness, beauty as real and knowable, with formation and contemplation emphasized over mere information. The mission and goals articulate discovery of truth, moral formation, and a love of beauty, aligned with classic paideia aims. (Key statements about truth, beauty, goodness, formation, and the moral imagination) 
- Curriculum emphasizes the liberal-arts core (grammar/logic/rhetoric), extensive study of Great Books/original texts, Latin study, and historical breadth. The materials describe ordered knowledge, basic skills, and a habit of greatness, with explicit Great-Books/primary-source emphasis and Latin language study. 
- Pedagogy includes contemplative/restful elements and Socratic-style inquiry, with seminars, disciplined dialogue, memory work, and opportunities for rhetorical development. There are explicit references to seminars, coaching, discussion, and the disciplined exchange of ideas, as well as preparation for classical oratory and debates. 
- Faculty are positioned as living exemplars and “co-teachers” within a collegial, Christian-learning culture; explicit statements describe the faculty as mentors who model virtue and foster a living-curriculum. 
- Parents & Community (Public-facing materials): Evidence of partnership with families and parental formation is weaker in the public-facing materials; the school emphasizes a community of learners but does not clearly publish a formal, shared model of parent co-teaching or routine parent-facing programming. This is the category most visibly weaker in published content relative to classical ideals.

Aggregate assessment across all six categories
