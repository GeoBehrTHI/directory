## Executive Summary

St Ann Classical Academy (Raritan, NJ) presents a robust Catholic, classical education anchored in the Trivium and Quadrivium, a commitment to wonder, virtue, and the integration of faith with reason. Public materials from the school emphasize liturgical life, daily prayer, Catholic identity, and a curriculum designed to cultivate wisdom, virtue, and human flourishing through the liberal arts. Evidence from the school’s own site highlights the seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), the integration of sacred liturgy into school life, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative elements with Socratic/rigorous inquiry in appropriate stages. Based on a keyword analysis against the classical school rubric, the overall assessment is CLASSICAL, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) representing a somewhat less explicit alignment relative to the strongest signals in the other areas. The school’s signs of classical orientation are strong in religious/moral formation, curriculum, faculty, and family/community engagement, consistent with a classical-Catholic profile.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Strong Catholic identity, daily prayer, liturgy and sacraments, and formation in faith together with reason; references to God’s order and divine providence in learning; emphasis on Christian discipleship and sacred traditions.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive signals include Catholic theology, liturgy, sacraments, reverence, wonder, logos, and sacred tradition; little to no negative signals observed in public materials.
    - Why this rating: The school explicitly grounds education in a transcendent moral order and Catholic theology, aligning with transcendental moral realism and liturgical/community practices in the classical framework.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Emphasis on formation of character, virtue, wisdom, and the unity of truth, goodness, and beauty; aim to form the whole person, not merely prepare for work.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, scholé, contemplation, and classical heritage; essentially no evidence of a work- or job-prep orientation in public materials.
    - Why this rating: The materials frame education as paideia—formation of the person toward human excellence and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty.

- Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Explicit reference to the liberal arts (Trivium and Quadrivium), seven liberal arts, and integration of history, literature, and philosophy; attention to Great Books / primary sources in the canon; emphasis on wonder and integrated learning across disciplines.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include liberal arts, Trivium, Quadrivium, grammar, logic, rhetoric, great books, primary sources, Western civilization, history, geography, and sacred arts; negative keywords (e.g., education replaced by social studies terminology or STEM-first framing) are not evident in the site materials.
    - Why this rating: The curricular signals align strongly with classical expectations (grammar/logic/rhetoric; history as a throughline; Great Books; integration and wonder).

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: The site signals a mix of contemplative/restful approaches and memory-based practices (e.g., singing, recitation, and liturgical integration); there are indications of upper-school discourse and rhetoric training, and a liturgical/musical dimension (Schola Cantorum). Public materials also describe integration of faith and reason across subjects.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive signals include Socratic-style discussion, narration/recitation, chanting, memory-work, and upper-school rhetoric; there are clear signals of contemplative/restful elements (scholé, wonder). Negative signals are limited but the explicit articulation of full, routine Socratic seminars or formal high-school seminars/debates is less consistently documented in the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The pedagogy blends classical methods (narration, recitation, memory, liturgical integration) with some modern practices, but explicit, consistent articulation of full Socratic seminars across grades and a fully developed upper-level seminar/debate program is not uniformly visible in the public content. This yields a CLASSICAL orientation with partial “Socratic” signaling, hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Faculty pages and testimonials describe teachers as mentors and living examples of virtuous learning; there is a sense of a "living curriculum" and a community of knowledgeable, virtuous educators; a clear emphasis on character formation and moral exemplars.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include master/teacher as mentor, living curriculum, portrait of a teacher, collegial culture, and moral exemplars; no strong negative signals observed in public materials.
    - Why this rating: The public materials portray faculty as role models and the core of the school’s mission, consistent with CLASSICAL expectations.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: The school emphasizes a mission-driven Catholic community with family involvement and liturgical life; events, feasts, and parish collaboration are highlighted (e.g., Eucharistic Holy Hours, liturgical life, schola, community celebrations). There is active student-family participation and a culture of shared formation.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include partnership with families, parent formation, family liturgy, open campus, and liturgical year events; the lexicon signals such family partnership signals as core to classical formation. Negative signals are not evident in the materials.
    - Why this rating: The public materials present parents as engaged co-participants in formation, consistent with the classical model’s emphasis on a family-centered, communal education.

Overall Assessment

- Aggregate Ranking: CLASSICAL
    - The school demonstrates strong alignment with classical education across Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and Faculty, with robust signals in Purpose and Goal, and a solid but slightly more blended Pedagogy signal (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Element 4). Parents & Community engagement aligns with classical expectations. The combination yields an overall CLASSICAL classification with a needle-move potential focused on pedagogy (Element 4).

What Would Move the Needle

- Make upper-grade Socratic seminars and formal debates a more explicit and consistently communicated component of the pedagogy, with documented rubrics and outcomes across grades.
- More explicit articulation of the Great Books / Great Conversation framework within the curriculum notes on the site (e.g., direct references to Great Books lists and primary sources in core courses beyond history).
- Strengthen explicit parent formation offerings and documented partnerships with families as co-educators (e.g., book studies, parent sessions, and home-education alignments) with public-facing examples and schedules.
