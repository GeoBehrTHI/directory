## Executive Summary

The Good Shepherd Academy (Pomona, CA 91767) presents a clearly Catholic, classical-leaning program with strong religious and community commitments, a formation-focused mission, and a curriculum that foregrounds Western civilization, virtue, and classical formation. The public materials emphasize Catholic identity, sacramental life, and partnerships with families, alongside a traditional K–12 classical framework. However, while the school strongly signals classical aims (virtue formation, reverence for truth and beauty, Latin study, reverence for Western civilization), several explicit markers commonly associated with a full classical program (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation pedagogy and a fully articulated Trivium-focused curriculum with explicit grammar, logic, rhetoric progression across the grades) are less overt in the publicly visible content. Taken together, the school clearly aligns with classical education in a substantially Catholic frame, but there are gaps in explicit documentation of some canonical classical elements. Across the six rubric categories, the school demonstrates strong Classical alignment in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, and Community/Parents, with more partial or Emerging signals in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence base and methodology notes:
- Content from The Good Shepherd Academy’s site demonstrates Catholic identity, sacramental life, daily Mass, catechesis, and a mission centered on truth, beauty, and goodness within a Catholic framework. Specific statements include: formation in knowledge, love and service of God through a rigorous classical education aligned with the Magisterium; emphasis on daily Mass and sacraments; orthodox Catholic faith; collaboration with parents; and a classical approach to Western civilization and virtue. See multiple pages, notably the About Us and Statement of Purpose pages.  
- The site also communicates Latin instruction, a four-day school and homeschool option, and a culture of family partnership, with references to Western civilization, great literature, and the aim to form students in virtue and wisdom. 
- For rubric guidance and keyword indicators, the Six Essential Elements documents (the Classical Lexicon and the Six Essential Elements rubric) provide the explicit positive/negative keyword indicators used to classify each category. Positive signals include truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; liturgy; catechesis; Mass; sacred music; reverence; wonder; Western civilization; great books; Latin; classical education; formation; paideia; and partnership with families, among others. Negative indicators include secular humanism, values clarification, project-based learning dominance in some contexts, consumer language, etc. These lexicon sources frame how we map Good Shepherd Academy’s public materials to the six categories.
