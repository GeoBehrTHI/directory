## Executive Summary

Legacy Christian Academy CA 91355 (as represented by public materials surfaced in the crawl) presents a classical, Christian-leaning program with clear lineage to the traditional liberal-arts model. Public materials emphasize a classical curriculum (Latin, classical studies, logic, literature) delivered in a structured, community-oriented environment that partners with families. The school positions itself as a Christian, traditional, virtues-focused program and references the Great Books/Great Conversation idiom through its use of Memoria Press materials and classical pedagogy. However, explicit statements about liturgical practice, formal Socratic pedagogy in high school, or a fully developed “living curriculum” communicated directly on the site are less overt. Taken together, the school demonstrates many core classical elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books culture; parental partnership; formation of affections in a Christian frame) but with some gaps in publicly stated pedagogy and explicit liturgical/programmatic language.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Most clearly classical signals: explicit classical curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Latin/Classical Studies, use of a traditional model with parental involvement, and a focus on truth, goodness, and beauty in a Christian frame. Evidence sources point to Memoria Press-based offerings, a two-day-per-week structure, and explicit Christian formation language. See public pages describing the HLS Santa Clarita model, two-day enrollment, and the Good/True/Beautiful formulation. 
- Notable gaps for full Classical alignment: explicit liturgical practices, sustained Socratic seminars and high-school-level rhetoric coaching explicitly stated on the site, and a fully developed “living curriculum” communicated publicly. The framework exists in ancillary/classical-education literature linked in the knowledge store, but it is not consistently evidenced on the school’s own public materials. 

What would move the needle toward CLASSICAL:
- Make explicit on-site statements about liturgical/community practices (chapel, worship, seasonal rites) and the role of the church in daily formation.
- Include explicit, high-school–level Socratic seminars, rhetoric training, and public articulation of “great books” engagement and the Great Conversation in grade-level descriptions.
- Publish a concise statement of the school’s “living curriculum” and how teachers model virtue in day-to-day instruction.
- Clarify how Great Books / Western canon is integrated across grades, and provide sample course sequences demonstrating grammar–logic–rhetoric progression with direct references to classic texts.
