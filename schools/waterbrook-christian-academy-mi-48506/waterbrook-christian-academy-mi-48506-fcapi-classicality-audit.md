## Executive Summary

Waterbrook Christian Academy in Flint, Michigan presents a clearly Classical Christian model rooted in traditional liberal-arts formation. Public materials from the school repeatedly emphasize a classical framework (Hillsdale College curriculum, Great Books, Latin, logic, rhetoric) alongside a Christ-centered, liturgical-inclined community (morning devotion, worship, Psalm 42:1). The school also foregrounds virtue and formation (Love, Truth, Wisdom, Virtue) and a formation-first orientation rather than pure job preparation. Taken together with the public statements about theology, worship, and a capstone-style senior project, Waterbrook places itself within the Classical continuum, with especially strong alignment in Curriculum and Religious Foundations.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
    - Public materials emphasize a classical educational framework, including a Capstone Project in the senior year and a robust emphasis on logic and rhetoric in the upper grades. The explicit mention of “Socratic discussion” or formal seminars is not present in the public materials, though the presence of logic, rhetoric, and Capstone implies rhetoric-based schooling and likely discussion-based elements in higher grades. The absence of explicit Socratic/seminar language in the public materials yields partial alignment with the Pedagogy criteria.
    - There is reference to a structured, classical program and to a strong emphasis on memory and rote learning in the lower grades, but there is no explicit mention of singing/chanting in the lower school on the Waterbrook site (unlike some other classical schools that emphasize “Scholé” or chanting in lower grades).

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: logic, rhetoric, capstone, great books (as content to inform pedagogy), seminars/debate (implied though not explicitly named), public speaking opportunities (via rhetoric).
    - Negative keywords present: explicit references to lower-school contemplative rest, singing, or chanting are not clearly documented on the site; the site does not overtly present a restorative/scholé-based pedagogy in the lower grades, though morning devotion is included. The lack of explicit lower-grade chanting/scholé or explicit Socratic-style upper-school seminars reduces the Pedagogy alignment from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
    - Mapping to the Six Elements framework: the presence of Capstone, logic, rhetoric aligns with Pedagogy, but explicit Socratic seminars and singing/chants in lower grades are not documented in public materials, which keeps category rating from being CLASSICAL.

- Why this rating
    - Waterbrook’s pedagogy shows strong classical elements (logic, rhetoric, Capstone, Great Books) but lacks explicit documentation of Socratic seminars and lower-grade contemplative practices (e.g., singing/chanting) that some classical models emphasize. Therefore, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is the prudent categorization.

Rationale: Waterbrook Christian Academy demonstrates a robust Classical Christian orientation across Curriculum, Purpose/Goals, and Religious Foundations, with explicit elements such as Great Books, Latin, Logic, Rhetoric, Capstone projects, and a Christ-centered formation emphasis. Pedagogy shows strong classical elements but lacks explicit documentation of the full Socratic/seminar pedagogy in public materials, which places Element 4 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Taken together, the school sits firmly within CLASSICAL, with room for strengthening certain procedural articulations of pedagogy, faculty role, and parental partnership in public communications.

What would move the needle

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Implement explicit, documented Socratic seminars and graded seminars in upper grades, with a clear framework and example timetables. Add public-facing descriptions of lower-grade contemplative practices (e.g., scholé-inspired routines or singing/chanting) if part of the program, with examples of lesson formats and rubrics.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Develop and publish a formal "portrait of the teacher" (the living curriculum) that describes teacher virtues, pedagogy, mentorship roles, and collaborative professional culture. Include statements about faculty collaboration, ongoing formation, and model-virtue expectations.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Establish and publish explicit parent-partnership structures (e.g., parents as co-teachers, parent book studies, ongoing parent education, and regular parent-teacher-community events). Create a clear pathway for parents to participate in school life as co-educators, and document these structures publicly.
