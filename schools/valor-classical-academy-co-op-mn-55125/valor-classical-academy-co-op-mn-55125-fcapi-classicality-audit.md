## Executive Summary

Valor Classical Academy Co-op (Woodbury, MN, 55125) presents a clearly classical, Christian-leaning program that centers on liberal arts formation through Trivium and Quadrivium-influenced pedagogy. Public materials from Valor emphasize truth-focused schooling, Bible infusion, and Christian discernment as core aims, with explicit reference to Trivium and Quadrivium as the framework for the Liberal Arts. This yields a strong alignment in the Curriculum and Religious/Metaphysical Foundations areas, and a solid, formation-oriented School Purpose. However, the publicly available materials do not consistently demonstrate the full spectrum of classical indicators across all six categories (notably in explicit mention of Great Books/Great Conversation, Socratic seminars, visible “living curriculum” faculty cultures, and comprehensive parent partnership language). On balance, Valor is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear strengths in curriculum and religious orientation, and room for deeper documentation of pedagogy, faculty portrayal, and parent/community integration in the classical sense.

Key evidence and contours:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Bible infusion, Scripture as a plumb line for truth, and explicit Christian discernment in our mission statements indicate a grounded theological posture and transcendental moral framework. These signals align with classical positive indicators around truth, theology, and sacred formation. 

- Purpose and Goal: Formation focused on virtue and wisdom is implied through mission language (“truth-focused Liberal Arts education,” “Christian discernment”) and the aim of forming leaders, but explicit articulation of telos, true/good/beautiful as a knowledgeable end-state is less overt in public copy. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL read rather than a full CLASSICAL read for this category. 

- Curriculum: Strong alignment via Trivium and Quadrivium, with explicit mention of grammar, logic, rhetoric, and the liberal-arts framing. This category clearly meets classical expectations. 

- Pedagogy: Public content hints at a classical sensibility (structured, disciplined Liberal Arts framing; emphasis on memory and contemplation in the broader classical discourse), but explicit claims of Socratic seminars, formal upper-school rhetoric drills, or explicit scholé pedagogy are not consistently documented in the public-facing materials. This points to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL. 

- Faculty: Public-facing materials emphasize mission and philosophy but provide limited explicit descriptions of teachers as “living curriculum” or a formal faculty culture. The case for CLASSICAL faculty culture is present but not strongly documented in the public materials provided. This leans to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 

- Parents & Community: Public materials show standard family-admission channels and openness but do not consistently foreground parents as co-teachers or provide robust parent-formation programs in the classical sense. This is another area where the school is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Document explicit Socratic pedagogy and higher-level seminar practices: include statements about upper-grade Socratic discussions, seminars, debates, and a concerted rhetoric program that culminates in public speaking or oratory—complete with examples or sample syllabi.

- Publicly articulate a more explicit telos and the true/good/beautiful triad in curriculum and assessment, making the formation of virtue and wisdom as measurable outcomes through portfolio or memory work that ties back to Great Books and classical virtues.
