# ABSTRACT

Restoration Academy in Alabama 35064 demonstrates a school with strong religious foundations, virtue formation, and parental/community partnership, aligned with several classical-education principles. The evidence indicates robust catechetical/theological grounding, mission-driven formation, a public emphasis on memory-based and contemplative pedagogy, and active family/community engagement. However, its curriculum lacks explicit, visible Great Books/Great Conversation framing and an explicit Trivium-based structure in public materials. Pedagogy shows restorative, contemplative elements and memory-based practice (e.g., singing/chanting) but without a consistent, widely documented Socratic or dialectic program across all grades. Faculty are presented in a manner consistent with a virtuous, mentor-based model. Parents and community are explicitly welcomed as co-educators. Aggregated across the six classical categories, Restoration Academy would be classified as CLASSICAL, with notable strength in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, Faculty, and Parents/Community, a strong implementation of Pedagogy that is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, and a Curriculum that is NOT CLASSICAL in its public presentation. This pattern suggests a robust classical-leaning model grounded in Christian formation, with opportunities to deepen classical elements (notably in the curriculum) to achieve full classical status.

# ABSTRACT

Restoration Academy (Alabama 35064) is CLASSICAL in many respects, with robust Religious Foundations, formation-focused Purpose/Goal, strong Faculty mentorship, and active Parents & Community engagement. Its pedagogy shows clear classical influences (restful learning, memory-based strategies, and rhetoric/debate in upper grades), while its Curriculum, as publicly presented, emphasizes biblically-centered content over explicit Great Books/Trivium-based frameworks. Element 3 (Curriculum) lacks public articulation of the classical liberal-arts canon (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Great Books in RA’s public materials, placing it in NOT CLASSICAL for Curriculum. The Pedagogy category sits in the middle with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to a blend of Scholé-like practices and rhetoric/debate, but not the full Socratic-dialectic breadth across all grades. Collectively, RA demonstrates significant classical-education features rooted in Christian formation, family/community partnership, and a mentoring faculty, earning an overall CLASSICAL classification with room for explicit expansion of canonical classical elements in Curriculum and broader Socratic pedagogy across grades.
