# Classicality Audit Report: Bethel Classical Academy

School website used: https://bethelclassical.org/

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Bethel Classical Academy (Irvine, CA) presents strong textual evidence across nearly all elements of classical education: a confessedly Christ-centered telos; explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty; a Trivium-centered, Latin-forward curriculum integrated with Western Civilization and humane letters; robust memory, copywork, catechism, and discussion/debate practices; and a clearly articulated partnership with parents. The only thinner area in public-facing materials is Faculty formation/portrait language (less explicit than other categories), though teacher bios echo formation in “wisdom and virtue.” On balance, BCA’s published materials align closely with the Six Essential Elements framework and associated positive/negative keyword indicators.      

Note on method: This audit uses the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1)” keyword framework—positive/negative indicators for each category—to quantify textual signals before assigning ratings.

## CATEGORY 1: RELIGIOUS AND METAPHYSICAL FOUNDATIONS
- What we looked for (lexicon examples): truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; Scripture; worship/prayer/liturgy; chapel; catechesis; reverence; imago Dei (absence of moral relativism/values-neutral). 
- Evidence from the site (verbatim cues):
  - Christ-centered Curriculum; “all knowledge … points back to Him”; “Jesus Christ is Lord of all”; “biblical worldview” (theology integration) 
  - “true, good, beautiful and noble”; “God as Truth and the source of all wisdom” (moral realism + metaphysical claims) 
  - Statement of Faith: “study and observance of His Word, prayer, worship and the sacraments”; Holy Scripture as “supreme and final authority” (liturgical/communal practices + theology) 
  - Community practices like “Fathers’ Prayer Meeting” and “Prayers & Devotion” in curriculum sequence reinforce a worshipful rhythm  
- Keyword comparison to “Six Elements”
  - Positive hits: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue/wisdom; biblical worldview; Christ-centered; Scripture; worship; prayer; sacraments (8+ distinct)   
  - Negative hits: none observed
- Quantification
  - Positive vs. negative: +12 / -0
  - Percent positive: 1.00
  - Coverage (sub-elements present: moral realism, theology, liturgical practices): 0.90
- Rating: CLASSICAL

## CATEGORY 3: SCHOOL CURRICULUM
- What we looked for: Trivium/Quadrivium; Latin/Greek; great books/primary sources; history (not “social studies”); integrated/humane letters; wonder.  
- Evidence from the site:
  - Trivium with Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric described and implemented; “students study complete literary works instead of textbooks” (Great Books approach) 
  - Latin begins in lower school; continues through Logic School (Latin 1, Latin 2)  
  - Humane Letters sequence; Logic/Rhet (LTW, Progymnasmata) 
  - History taught explicitly as History (OT/Egypt; Greece/Rome; Middle Ages/Reformation; Explorers–Modern), not “Social Studies” 
  - Memory strand includes Bible verses, poems, catechisms (supports oral tradition/recitation) 
- Keyword comparison
  - Positive hits: Trivium, Latin, Logic, Rhetoric, Progym, Humane Letters, Great Works/Western Civilization, Memory/Catechism, Poetry; Astronomy and Music electives appear (Quadrivium touchpoints) (10+ distinct)   
  - Negative hits (e.g., “social studies,” STEM-first framing): none observed
- Quantification
  - Positive vs. negative: +12 / -0
  - Percent positive: 1.00
  - Coverage: 0.95
- Rating: CLASSICAL

## CATEGORY 4: SCHOOL PEDAGOGY OR TEACHING METHODS
- What we looked for: memorization/recitation/chanting; singing; copywork/imitation/progymnasmata; Socratic discussion/debate/seminar; restful learning (scholé); upper-school rhetoric via speeches/debates/orations.  
- Evidence from the site:
  - Lower School memory strand (Scripture/poetry/catechisms) indicates regular recitation; copywork embedded in writing sequence (imitation) 
  - Logic years: “discussion based” and “introduced to debate”; “informal logic” (dialectic)

- Keyword comparison

  - Negative hits: none observed
- Quantification
  - Positive vs. negative: +6 / -0
  - Percent positive: 0.85
  - Coverage: 0.80

## Recommendations to Strengthen Alignment (Faculty focus)
- Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate” that names virtues (prudence, justice, temperance, fortitude; humility; intellectual virtues) and commitments (lifelong learning; collegiality), aligning to classical faculty keywords. 
- Describe faculty formation rhythms (e.g., great books seminars, commonplacing, catechesis-in-faculty meetings, retreats) to make the “living curriculum” visible.
- Highlight mentorship language (teacher as “model,” “guide,” “sage”) and any peer-observation or Socratic coaching among faculty.
