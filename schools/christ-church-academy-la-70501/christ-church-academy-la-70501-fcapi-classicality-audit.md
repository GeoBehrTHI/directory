## Executive Summary

Christ Church Academy La 70501 presents a classical Christian framing in its public materials, with clear emphasis on a Christian worldview, formation of character, and the goal of education oriented toward God-glorifying aims. Public content repeatedly foregrounds a Christian lens on education (e.g., “Education Is Enculturation” and discussions of a Christian worldview and biblical knowledge) and explicitly frames the goal of education as aligning with divine purposes (e.g., “to bring glory to God”). These signals align with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose/Goal elements of a classical framework. However, explicit articulation of several core classical curriculum and pedagogy hallmarks (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, systematic use of Socratic dialogue in upper grades, and a fully developed public articulation of a classical pedagogy) is not consistently visible in the site’s public materials. When mapped against the six-element rubric, the school shows strong Classical-leaning signals in religion and mission, and in the general formation/purpose rhetoric, but more explicit textual commitments to core classical curricula and pedagogical methods are less evident in the crawl content available here. The aggregate assessment is therefore SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key takeaways
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strongly present via explicit Christian worldview framing and God-centered education.
- School Purpose/Goal signals emphasize formation, virtue, and a transcendent aim beyond information or job prep.
- Curriculum signals (grammar/logic/rhetoric, liberal arts) are present in knowledge-base materials and some public posts, but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments are not consistently evident on the site.
- Pedagogy signals (Sç Socratic, scholé, chanting, and upper-level rhetoric) are less visible in the public site content; more robust explicit articulation would push this category toward CLASSICAL.
- Faculty signals are not clearly described on the site; the knowledge-base materials emphasize “living curriculum” and modeling virtue, but direct site-based descriptions are limited.
- Parents & Community signals emphasize partnership with families and parent formation in classical education, which aligns with the classical model, though explicit on-site statements are less consistently visible.
