# Classicality Audit: Bethel Classical Academy

URL audited: https://bethelclassical.org/

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Why: Bethel Classical Academy (BCA) explicitly centers education on truth, goodness, and beauty, employs the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integrates a biblical/theological worldview across subjects, and emphasizes the cultivation of virtue and partnership with parents. Its public materials show extensive alignment with the “Six Essential Elements” positive keyword indicators, with especially strong evidence in Religious/Theological Foundations, Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents/Community. Pedagogy is solid in logic/rhetoric/discussion and imitation but shows less published evidence for scholé/restful learning and lower‑school chanting/recitation practices.     
- Notes on method: The audit compares BCA’s stated language against the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators outlined in the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” and the classicality lexicon arranged by the six elements (religious foundations, purpose/goals, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, parents/community) to quantify signal strength.  

How to read the scores
- Each category includes a keyword analysis (positive vs. negative indicators), a quantitative estimate (percent_positive and pos_neg_hits), coverage of required sub‑criteria (percent_coverage), and a rating (CLASSICAL / SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL / NOT CLASSICAL).

Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations
- What we found in BCA’s materials (keywords/phrases):
  - Positive: “truth, goodness, and beauty,” “virtue,” “glorify God,” “Christ‑centered,” “biblical worldview,” “Jesus Christ is Lord of all,” “God is Creator,” “inerrancy of the Bible,” “God is Sovereign,” “worship” (as an intellectual mandate)    
  - Negative: none observed (e.g., “value‑neutral,” “pluralistic,” “religiously neutral,” “relativism”) 
- Comparison to Six Elements lexicon:
  - Strong matches to “transcendental moral realism” (truth, goodness, beauty), and “grounded in theology” (Christ‑centered education, Christian worldview, doctrine) and some evidence of “liturgical/communal practices” via “worship” language. Less explicit is daily chapel/liturgy.  
- Quantitative estimate:
  - pos_neg_hits: +11/-0
  - percent_positive: 1.00
  - percent_coverage: 0.67 (clear evidence for moral realism and theological grounding; limited explicit mention of chapel/liturgy)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

Category 3: School Curriculum
- What we found:
  - Positive: Trivium—“Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric”; Latin; formal logic and advanced rhetoric; Western Canon; complete works over excerpts; integrated across disciplines; chronological historical study; history/government (no “social studies” substitution); modern Romance language option.   
  - Negative: none of the major signals (e.g., “social studies” replacing history, “project‑based dominance,” “STEM‑first framing”) observed in the materials reviewed. 
- Comparison:
  - Strong alignment with Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium explicitly; Quadrivium implied via mathematics and sciences), Great Books/primary texts, historical engagement, and integrated/harmonized studies (with positive language on integration and full texts). “Wonder” is not named explicitly. 
- Quantitative estimate:
  - pos_neg_hits: +10/-0
  - percent_positive: 1.00
  - percent_coverage: 0.75 (Trivium, Great Books/Canon, history vs. social studies, integration—clear; “wonder” language not explicit)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

Category 4: School Pedagogy (Teaching Methods)
- What we found:
  - Positive: “discussion based” classes (Logic stage), debate, formal logic, advanced rhetoric (upper school), imitation (“learning from and imitating faithful Christ‑followers”).  
  - Missing/weak: explicit “Socratic seminar” language (discussion is present, “Socratic” not named), little/no published mention of “memorization/recitation/chanting” in lower school, and no explicit “scholé/restful learning” language. 
  - Potential negatives present in phrasing: “Academic Excellence and Rigor” (can signal less scholé if over‑emphasized) and “hands‑on science exploration” (can lean toward “experiential learning” if dominant; here it’s a component, not the driver).  
- Comparison:
  - Matches lexicon positives for dialectic/discussion, debate, and rhetoric; matches “imitation” positively. Limited/absent signals for chanting/memory and scholé/restfulness. Some mild negative signals (“rigor,” “hands‑on”) are present. 
- Quantitative estimate:
  - pos_neg_hits: +5/-2
  - percent_positive: 0.71
  - percent_coverage: 0.40 (of the five criteria: Socratic/discussion = yes; upper‑school rhetoric = yes; imitation = yes; chanting/memory = weak; scholé/restful = absent)
- Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Overall Assessment: CLASSICAL
- Strengths: Strong theological grounding; explicit affirmation of transcendental moral realism; robust Trivium/Latin/logic/rhetoric curriculum with Western Canon and integrated studies; teacher formation language and mentoring; genuine parent partnership and parent education.    
- Growth areas (based on public materials): Publish more explicit references to scholé/restful learning, lower‑school chanting/recitation and memory work, and (if present) liturgical/chapel rhythms to round out public evidence of classical pedagogy and liturgical formation. 

Key Evidence (examples)
- “centered on truth, goodness, and beauty” and the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) 
- Christ‑centered curriculum grounded in a biblical worldview; “Jesus Christ is Lord of all … all knowledge … points back to Him” 
- Latin, logic, rhetoric, Western Canon, complete works, integrated disciplines, discussion‑based classes with debate and formal logic/rhetoric in upper grades 
- Faculty as mentors/models; discipleship and imitation of faithful Christ‑followers; teachers nurture minds, souls, hearts
