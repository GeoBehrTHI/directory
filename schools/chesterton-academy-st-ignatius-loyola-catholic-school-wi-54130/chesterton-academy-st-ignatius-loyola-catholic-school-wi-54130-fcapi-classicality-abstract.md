## ABSTRACT

Chesterton Academy of St. Ignatius Loyola Catholic School in Wisconsin (54130) exhibits a cohesive CLASSICAL profile across all six categories. Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are anchored in Catholic theology, liturgy, and a sacramental worldview, with explicit integration of faith into all subjects. The School Purpose and Goal centers on formation, virtue, and wisdom, emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty as real transcendentals. The Curriculum foregrounds the liberal arts as foundational (grammar, logic, rhetoric), with the Great Books and a braided, integrated approach across history, literature, philosophy, theology, and languages. Pedagogy blends contemplative Scholé with Socratic seminars and rhetoric, including lower-level memory work (singing/chanting) and upper-level discussion and public speaking. The Faculty are described as living curriculum-makers and mentors who form the core of the community, while Parents & Community are engaged as co-educators, with open access, formation opportunities, and active involvement. Public materials consistently reference Catholic identity (Mass, Catechesis, Newman Guide recognition) and a classical framework (Trivium, Great Books, Latin, integrated humanities).

In sum, the school achieves a robust CLASSICAL classification, demonstrating explicit alignment with the Six Essential Elements and corresponding keyword indicators across all six categories. The public portrayal reflects a credible, missionary classical education aimed at forming virtuous, thoughtful, and faith-filled leaders prepared for a life of service.
