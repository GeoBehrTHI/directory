## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Ignatius Loyola Catholic School (WI 54130) presents a robust Catholic classical education that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School. Public-facing materials emphasize a Catholic, theology-forward foundation, an integrated classical curriculum built around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a love of Great Books, contemplative pedagogy (Scholé) alongside Socratic inquiry, and a high value on virtue formation, character, and communal life. The school also foregrounds liturgical and communal practices (Mass, adoration, pilgrimages, retreats), a living-faculty model, and deep parent/community partnership (open visits, formation opportunities, family involvement).

Key signals across categories indicate a CLASSICAL orientation, with explicit references to truth, goodness, beauty; a formation-focused telos; a Trivium-based curriculum; Socratic pedagogy in upper levels; lower-school memory/repetition through chant and song; a living-faculty and mentorship ethos; and strong parental-partnering with ongoing opportunities for family formation. The presence of Catechesis and a catechetical, liturgical life further anchors the religious-metaphysical foundation.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.
