# Classicality Audit: Bishop Ryan Catholic School (Minot, ND)

Website audited: https://bishopryan.com/ 

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- What we can verify from the crawl: the school publicly frames its work around virtue and formation in a Catholic context. The homepage title says “Inspired Minds. Faithful Hearts. Lives of Virtue.” and at least one internal section is labeled “Human Formation.”  
- Strengths: Clear theological identity and moral/virtue emphasis align with classical aims in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal. These align with positive indicators such as virtue, faith/theology, and formation drawn from the Six Elements keyword lists and lexicon.  
- Gaps: The crawl (blocked by CAPTCHA in many places) did not yield public statements about the curriculum (Trivium, Great Books, Latin), pedagogy (Socratic seminar, chanting, scholé), faculty profile, or parent partnership. Absent evidence leads to low coverage and “Not Classical” ratings in Categories 3–6 for now.

Because the site copy accessible in the crawl primarily evidences Categories 1–2 (and we lack verifiable details for Categories 3–6), the aggregate is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL pending more publicly available detail.

## What would move the needle (recommended next steps)
To reach an unequivocal CLASSICAL rating, ensure the following are explicitly presented and easily discoverable on public pages:

- Curriculum
  - State the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and, if applicable, Quadrivium, as organizing principles; list Latin (and any Greek) requirements; name Great Books/primary texts and “Great Conversation” aims. 
  - Refer to “History” explicitly (avoid collapsing it into “Social Studies”) and emphasize chronological study and Western civilization engagement. 
- Pedagogy
  - Describe lower-school memory work (singing/chanting/recitation) and upper-school Socratic seminars, dialectic/debate, and rhetoric/orations/capstones; mention restful learning/scholé where relevant.  
- Faculty
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” highlighting teachers as models/mentors of wisdom and virtue—the “living curriculum.” 
- Parents & Community
  - Emphasize parent partnership/co-educators and parent education (book clubs, seminars), and hospitality/open campus norms. 

If these elements already exist internally, consider surfacing them on public pages not blocked by CAPTCHA to improve verifiability.
