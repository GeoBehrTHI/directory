## Executive Summary

Good Shepherd Reformed Episcopal School (Tyler, TX 75701) demonstrates a robust Classical education orientation across most of the rubric’s categories, with especially strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and Pedagogy signals. The school publicly emphasizes a Christian, Anglican-heritage framework, a Trivium-based curriculum, a Great-Books–styled approach to learning, and liturgical worship through daily chapel. These elements align closely with the Six Essential Elements' Positive Keywords for Religious Foundations, Curriculum, and Pedagogy, and with the School Purpose/Goal emphasis on formation, virtue, and wisdom rather than workforce preparation alone. Evidence from the school’s site highlights the medieval Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), the Great Conversation via Adler’s Paideia framework, daily Morning Prayer and creeds, and a mission centered on virtue, wisdom, and the worshiping life. See examples of these signals in the site content (Our Mission; Classical Education; Christian Heritage; daily chapel; Paideia references; Latin, history, and Great-Books–oriented curricula) with explicit quotes drawn from multiple public-facing pages.

-level takeaway: This school exhibits a clearly classical orientation embedded in a Christian, Anglican framework, with strong evidence of the trivium, Great Books approach, daily chapel, and a formation-first purpose. - The site notes communal prayer, liturgical practices, and a theology-informed worldview (Daily Offices; hymns; worship calendar).

- The materials foreground transcendental moral realism (truth, goodness, beauty) and a liturgical, communal worship framework (chapel, prayers, creeds) anchored in Anglican Christian heritage. This aligns strongly with Classical criteria for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations. The absence of secular or relativistic signals reinforces CLASSICAL rather than Not Classical.

Notes

- The daily chapel and liturgical practices are described in detail (Morning Prayer, the Lord’s Prayer, Ten Commandments, Creeds, Psalms), reinforcing liturgical commitments.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Two explicit aims emphasize formation in virtue and wisdom, not merely information or job preparation. Text describes formation in godliness and mastery of fundamental facts/skills as foundational.
- The mission articulates equipping students with tools of learning and wisdom of the ages to serve God and neighbor with love, virtue, and strength.

Positive keyword analysis

- Present: formation; virtue; wisdom; liberal education; paideia; classical mission; Great Conversation; telos; contemplation; classical heritage; education for leisure/restfulness. These correspond to Positive Keyword Indicators in the lexicon.

Why this rating

- The school's stated purpose centers on formation, virtue, and wisdom in a liberal-arts frame (paideia) rather than a narrow job- or career-metrics focus. This aligns with CLASSICAL rather than Not Classical. The public materials also emphasize the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty as real, knowable ends.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The curriculum explicitly uses the medieval Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) as the guiding framework for education.
- Subjects include Bible, Latin (Latin 1–3, with advanced options), Greek may appear as elective; history spanning ancient to modern; Western civilization; classical literature.
- Historical breadth includes Greek and Roman World, Medieval World, modern world history, and church history; emphasis on Western civilization.
- Great Books / Great Conversation signals exist (Mortimer Adler, The Paideia Proposal; discussion and debate; “great conversation” through reading and discussion).
- The curriculum emphasizes primary texts and classical works, formal Latin study, and a strong literature/history program (e.g., Medieval World Literature; Classical World Literature).

Positive keyword analysis

- Present: liberal arts; trivium; grammar; logic; rhetoric; great books; classical languages (Latin); history (Western civilization, Greek/Roman, Medieval World); Latin 3, AP Latin 4; classical literature; hymns and sacred art; art/music (fine arts). These correspond to the lexicon’s Positive Keywords for Curriculum.
- Negative keyword indicators: No overt replacement of history with social-studies framing; no dominant project-based-first pedagogy; no STEM-first framing observed in material.

Why this rating

- The content shows a textbook Classical-Curriculum orientation: Trivium, Great Books, Latin, disciplined study of history and Western civilization, and sacred art/hymnody. This is a robust CLASSICAL alignment.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Lower grades: singing/chanting and memory-formation practices; restfulness and memory-deepening elements (scholé-like feel) described in the School’s broader pedagogical narrative.
- Upper grades: Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training; opportunities to give speeches and practice rhetoric. The Paideia framework and “great conversation through discussion and debate” are highlighted.
- The four pillars (Oratio, Studium, Labor, Recreatio) imply integrated prayer, study, work, and recreation as pedagogical rhythms.

Positive keyword analysis

- Present: Socratic seminars/discussion; memorization and recitation; chanting; restfulness; scholé; contemplation; wonder; seminars; oratory; debate; memory work; liturgical calendar integration. These map to Positive Keyword Indicators in Pedagogy.
- Negative keyword indicators: No clear evidence of exclusive project-based learning or constructivist approaches; the content is consistent with a traditional, classical pedagogy.

Why this rating

- The site explicitly describes a continuum from memory/chanting in the lower grades to Socratic discussion and rhetoric in upper grades, with restfulness and contemplation interwoven. This aligns with CLASSICAL pedagogy signals rather than Not Classical or purely project-based pedagogy.

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public materials describe teachers’ roles in general terms (formation through instruction; the school’s mission, tools of learning, and virtue-formation), but there is less explicit, public articulation of teachers as the “living curriculum,” as a formal faculty culture, or a defined “portrait of teacher” per the classical-education lexicon.
- The elementary curriculum emphasizes mastery of fundamentals and formation in godliness, with teachers charged to manage time and develop discipline; this signals strong teacher engagement, but not explicit statements about faculty being the “living curriculum” or a formal faculty-development framework.
- The site references the “two aims” for elementary teachers and the “tools of learning,” but does not always foreground a formal faculty-culture description matched to the lexicon’s positive keywords for Faculty (e.g., "portrait of a teacher," "living curriculum," "great books seminars for faculty," etc.).

Positive keyword analysis

- Present: generalized references to teachers as guides and mentors within formation-focused pedagogy; "tools of learning" language; a sense of a classical teacherly role (though not explicitly labeled as “living curriculum”). The explicit, codified phrases from the lexicon (e.g., “living curriculum,” “portrait of a teacher,” “faculty of friends”) are not clearly present in the public-facing materials.
- Negative keyword indicators: None obvious in this category from the public materials (no overt anti-classical signals), but the absence of explicit “living curriculum” language lowers the strength of the Classical signal for Faculty.

Why this rating

- The evidence suggests a Classical orientation in practice, but the public materials do not robustly present the Faculty dimension with the standard classical keywords. Thus, CLASSICAL would be overstated; the best fit with the material is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, given the strong pedagogical signals elsewhere but only modest explicit faculty-language signals.

Positive keyword analysis

- Negative keyword indicators: None observed in the public materials; there is no indication of a customer-like relationship or minimal parent involvement in formation.

Why this rating

- The public-facing content demonstrates robust parent/community engagement in classical aims and formation, aligning with CLASSICAL signals for Element 6.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 5 (Faculty): Introduce and publicly document a formal “living curriculum” framework for teachers and a clear faculty-development model grounded in classical pedagogy. Publish explicit statements about the faculty’s role as mentors, exemplars, and co-educators in the classical tradition, including portraits of teacher ideals and opportunities for faculty seminars on classical methods. This would elevate Element 5 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Element 5 (Faculty) could be strengthened by showcasing teacher professional development grounded in Great Books pedagogy, classical seminars, and regular faculty discourse on Socratic/Paideia techniques.
- Element 5 (Faculty) could also include a concrete, published profile of the ideal teacher and student, aligning with the lexicon’s “portrait of a teacher” and “living curriculum” signals.
- Optional but beneficial: add explicit statements about ongoing faculty collaboration with parents to form a true partnership in the classical project (reinforcing Element 6 signals as well).

Note: The aggregate classification remains CLASSICAL due to strong signals in Categories 1–4 and 6, with Element 5’s improvement potential.
