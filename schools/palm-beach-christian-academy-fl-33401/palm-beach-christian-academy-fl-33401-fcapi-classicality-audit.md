## Executive Summary

Palm Beach Christian Academy (PBCA) presents itself as a distinctly Christian, classical education provider operating cradle-to-8th-grade with multiple Palm Beach-area campuses. Public-facing materials repeatedly couple faith formation with classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Socratic dialogue; memory work; and liturgical/community practices such as chapel). The school frames its mission around character formation, virtue, and a pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty within a community of families, students, and church partners. Evidence from PBCA materials shows explicit commitments to Scripture-centered instruction, Christ-centered values, and communal practices (e.g., chapels and worship) alongside a Trivium-based curriculum and Socratic dialogue. The presence of Great Books, Socratic discussion, and rhetoric training under a Christian framework aligns PBCA with classical education norms. PBCA’s public materials emphasize community partnership with parents and churches (Distinctively Community; partnerships with Family Church; Plan A Visit; campus networks), supporting a strong classical-leaning model in the school’s public presentation.

Overall classification by the classical rubric (Six Essential Elements of a Classical School) is: CLASSICAL.

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): CLASSICAL. PBCA foregrounds Distinctively Christian identity, Scripture-centered instruction, Christ-centered formation, and liturgical/community worship practices (chapel, worship teams). The site promotes liturgical and communal rhythms (chapel, recitals, worship) and situates education within a faith community anchored to Scripture. Evidence includes explicit statements of faith-centered education and liturgical elements. See: Distinctively Christian / Scripture at center; chapel and worship components; First Baptist/Families church alignment.   

- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal): CLASSICAL. PBCA presents education as formation of virtue, character, and wisdom rooted in Christian truth. The materials emphasize virtue, character formation, biblical truth, and formation rather than purely informational aims (with some references to college preparation as a downstream benefit). The combination of virtue/character formation with pursuit of truth aligns with the classical aim.  

- Element 3 (Curriculum): CLASSICAL. The school identifies a liberal-arts progression via grammar, logic, and rhetoric (Trivium), integrates Great Books and Socratic dialogue, and emphasizes history-informed inquiry. Campus program naming (Grammar School, Logic School) and explicit mentions of Great Books and Socratic dialogue support the curricular criteria. 

- Element 4 (Pedagogy / Teaching Methods): CLASSICAL. Pedagogy includes Socratic dialogue and rhetoric in upper levels, with opportunities for seminars, debates, and public speaking; lower levels emphasize contemplative memory and chant-like routines (via liturgical/community rhythms, recitals, and singing). The Conservatory and chapel elements further illustrate memory, chant, and communal forms of pedagogy.  

- Element 5 (Faculty): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. PBCA presents a public-facing faculty/storyline and a “Meet the Team” structure, with administrators and teachers listed. However, explicit articulations of the faculty as “the living curriculum” or as mentors who embody the classical cardinal virtues are less directly stated in the public materials. The available evidence shows a strong faculty presence and Christ-centered staffing, but without explicit language positioning faculty as the primary “living curriculum.” This yields a nuanced, mostly classical impression but not a fully explicit articulation of the “living curriculum” concept.  

- Element 6 (Parents & Community): CLASSICAL. PBCA foregrounds a strong community ethos (Distinctively Community) and explicit partnership with parents as co-participants in education; campus networks and plan-for-visits indicate openness and parental involvement. The site promotes parent collaboration, community partnerships, and opportunities for parents to engage with classical education concepts (e.g., plan visits, programs, and community events).

Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL

- The school exhibits robust classical elements across Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Community/Parents. Element 5 (Faculty) is the only area with a nuanced interpretation (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) due to limited explicit language about the faculty being the living curriculum, even though it shows a solid faculty presence and Christ-centered staff. Taken together, PBCA aligns with CLASSICAL education in its public-facing materials.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 5 (Faculty): Explicitly articulate and publish language that the faculty are the living curriculum, model virtue and formation, and function as mentors who embody the classical aims (e.g., “Our faculty are the living curriculum, modeling virtue, wisdom, and scholarly inquiry in every interaction with students.”). This would strengthen Element 5 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.

- For Element 1 (Religious Foundations): Consider adding explicit liturgical/communal routines in more disciplines beyond chapel (e.g., periodic liturgical readings in humanities or theology-integrated history modules) to deepen the liturgical dimension.

- For Element 2 (Purpose/Goal): While formation is present, a more explicit statement contrasting formation with information and job-readiness could further align messaging with the full formation-through-wisdom objective highlighted by classical education.

- For Element 3 (Curriculum): The site could further emphasize history as a through-line across the curriculum (not merely as a separate course) to strengthen the “history through the curriculum” criterion.

- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Continue to document and showcase examples of upper-level seminars, debates, and formal rhetoric assessments to provide richer public evidence of Socratic/seminar pedagogy in practice.

- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and expand opportunities for parent learning (book studies, readings on classical education) to deepen parental engagement and reinforce the co-teacher role.
