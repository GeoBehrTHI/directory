## Executive Summary

Potomac Classical Conservatory (Alexandria, VA, 22305) presents a robust classical Christian program with clear commitments to the liberal arts, the Trivium, Great Books, and a covenantal partnership with families. Public materials on the school’s site repeatedly foreground Christ-centered, scripture-integrated instruction, formation of virtue, and a rigorous yet humane classical pedagogy that blends in-home and in-class learning (a collaborative model). The site explicitly notes the Great Books, The Great Conversation, Latin study, grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, and a mission to shape students’ affections for Truth, Goodness, and Beauty to the glory of God. Taken together, these signals align strongly with classical forms of education and ecclesial articulation of education as formation, not mere transmission of information.

Overall, the school meets the core signals of classical education across all six categories, with especially strong alignment in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy. The Public/Family-Partnered model (collaborative in-home instruction and in-school instruction) is a distinctive feature that supports formation and community, though it introduces a hybrid structure that is somewhat nontraditional relative to monolithic, seat-based classical programs. Taken categorically, Potomac Classical Conservatory rates as CLASSICAL overall, with some room for growth in standardizing explicit pedagogical terms (e.g., explicit references to certain higher-level Socratic practices or formal liturgical structures) in publicly accessible materials.

Overall aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL

Rationale: Across all six categories, Potomac Classical Conservatory demonstrates robust alignment with classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements framework. The site emphasizes Truth, Goodness, Beauty; formation through the Trivium; Great Books and Great Conversation; Latin; a distinctly Christian, covenantal approach to education; and a collaborative model that involves families as co-educators. While the school’s public content describes a hybrid/home-involved model, this arrangement is itself a classical adaptation rather than a departure from classical aims. The presence of explicit catechetical/theological framing, formation-oriented aims, the Great Conversation, and a Trivium-centered curriculum places PCC squarely in the Classical camp.

What Would Move the Needle

- If PCC wanted to push the Pedagogy category toward a more uniform, explicitly Socratic-inseminated approach in all grades, the site could more consistently announce standard Socratic seminar practices in upper grades (beyond the Trivium description). This would deepen the explicit signaling of classical pedagogy to external audiences.
- Increasing explicit public references to the “Scholé” frame in lower grades (restful learning days, contemplative practices) with concrete daily or weekly examples would sharpen alignment with the orthodox classical pedagogy signal.
- Expanding public-facing teacher portraits or living-curriculum descriptions (e.g., a short profile of a representative teacher and their model of virtue or intellectual formation) would strengthen the Faculty category signals.
