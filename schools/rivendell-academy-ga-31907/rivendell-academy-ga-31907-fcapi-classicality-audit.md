## Executive Summary

Rivendell Academy (Columbus, GA 31907) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning program that centers on truth, goodness, and beauty through liberal-arts formation. Public materials emphasize a Christian worldview, a commitment to the liberal arts, and a formation-focused aim (character, wisdom, discernment) rather than purely vocational outcomes. The site explicitly frames education as a formation of character in a classical idiom, including references to truth, biblical foundations, and discernment, while also outlining a structured curricular cadence (three core days per week with Bible/history/literature/linguistics and writing) and a parental-friendly environment (Parent Portal, open admissions policies, and a faculty-driven approach). These strengths support a Classical orientation in several areas, particularly Religious/Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose/Goal, and to a substantial extent Faculty and Parents/Community. However, the curriculum and pedagogy signals are incomplete relative to a fully realized Classical program, with less explicit emphasis on Great Books, direct Socratic pedagogy, grammar-logics-rhetoric progression, and a fully articulated Great Conversation tradition within the curriculum. This places Rivendell Academy in the category of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with clear pathways to CLASSICAL through targeted programmatic expansions.

- Evidence of CLASSICAL alignment is strongest in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose/Goal, anchored by explicit statements about truth, goodness, beauty, theology, and formation through liberal-arts education. The public materials emphasize Christian discernment, joyful learning, and a Bible-centric core in the upper-structure and daily practice. See mission statements and core-days description.  
- Evidence for a robust Curriculum and Pedagogy that fully embodies classical norms is present but incomplete. The site highlights liberal-arts education and Bible/history/literature, but it does not consistently foreground Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, or explicit Socratic seminars in public-facing materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal for Element 3 and Element 4. 
- Faculty and Parent/Community signals are positive (there is a visible Faculty section and Parent Portal), supporting CLASSICAL cues in those areas, though richer detail about the living curriculum and teacher portraits would strengthen the case. 
- The aggregate profile thus skews toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong religious-metaphysical grounding and formation aims, but with room to intensify core classical methods (Socratic pedagogy, Great Books, Trivium terminology, liturgical practices) to become fully CLASSICAL.

Aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The school consistently exhibits strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1) and Purpose/Goal (Element 2), with clear formation aims and Christian theological grounding.
- Curriculum and Pedagogy (Categories 3 and 4) show classical intent but lack complete public articulation of Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, memorization/recitation, and explicit Socratic pedagogy, resulting in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals in those categories.
- Faculty (Element 5) and Parents/Community (Element 6) show CLASSICAL and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cues respectively, with public cues supporting a classical faculty model and parent engagement, but with room for more explicit “living curriculum” language and formal parent-education structures.

What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum Enhancement
  - Explicitly adopt and publicize Great Books selections and the Great Conversation framework; integrate canonical texts with clear connections to the liberal-arts framework.
  - Publicly implement and describe a Trivium progression (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across K–12, with documented milestones and sample curricula.
  - Incorporate and publicize memorization/recitation components (including classical chants or memory work) as a formal Pedagogy element.

- Pedagogy Enrichment
  - Introduce regular Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars in upper grades, with teacher-led scaffolding to develop dialectic and rhetorical skills.
  - Build in Scholé-like contemplative days or rhythms, with publicly documented restful learning moments.

- Liturgical/Communal Practices
  - Add explicit liturgical or communal practice elements (e.g., morning chapel, liturgical calendar, choral or hymnody elements) to reinforce the religious-metaphysical foundations.

- Faculty Development
  - Publish a “portrait of the teacher” and a more explicit statement of the living-curriculum model, with faculty development plans aligned to classical pedagogy.

- Parent & Community Education
  - Expand parent-formation programming (book clubs, seminars, and study groups) and publish an annual calendar of parent-education events to strengthen the partnership with families.

- Public-facing Transparency
  - Increase explicit articulation of core-classical elements (Great Books, memory work, Latin/Greek offerings) on the website to reduce ambiguity and strengthen the Somewhat/Classical boundary.
