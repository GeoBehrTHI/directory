## ABSTRACT

Dayspring Christian Academy presents a CLASSICAL-leaning, faith-centered program anchored in the Principle Approach, biblical worldview, and virtue formation. The school demonstrates strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and a Purpose/Goal aligned with formation, virtue, and classical aims. The Curriculum and Pedagogy show classical impulses (memory work, field studies, and a strong liturgical rhythm in daily life), but public materials do not consistently document explicit Great Books work or full-throated Socratic pedagogy across all grades, which places Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL tier. Faculty and Parent/Community dimensions lean toward classical ideals but lack consistently explicit classical language or structure in public materials, placing Element 5 (Faculty) in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregated assessment places the school as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear needle-moving opportunities in Curriculum and Pedagogy and additional potential in Faculty development and stronger liturgical integration.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes explicit biblical grounding, God’s Word as the starting point for instruction, and the Lordship of Christ integrated into school life. Positive keyword signals are strong (truth, biblical worldview, theology, liturgy-like practices). 

- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL
  Evidence of formation of virtue and Christian character with a classical mission; “citizens of excellence in Christian character and scholarship” signals the telos of education. Some workforce-prep signals exist (college prep), but formation remains central. 

- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows a Principle Approach, field studies, and history-driven instruction with integration of Bible and literature; however, explicit Great Books and the trivium/quadrivium vocabulary are not consistently stated in public materials. 

- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes memory-centered lower-school pedagogy and prayer/songs in daily cycles; lack of explicit, school-wide Socratic seminars or explicit rhetoric instruction across all grades lowers the classification. 

- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  The framing positions teachers as virtuous and formative but public materials do not consistently describe a formal “living curriculum” or faculty portrait as a systemic feature. 

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL
  Strong partner-with-parents language and co-teacher ideals; parent formation and open campus are highlighted. 

Cited sources from the school’s own materials and the classical lexicon underpin the above analysis. The Dayspring site demonstrates a robust religious formation and formation-centric aims, aligned with classical aims in spirit, and a strong community/parent partnership, while the explicit canonical curriculum and pedagogy signals in the public-facing materials are less fully developed than a canonical classical program would require. The analysis leverages the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) lexicon, cross-referenced with the school’s own public materials to determine category-level signals and the aggregate classification. The lexicon’s explicit terms used to compare against Dayspring’s materials include references to truth, virtue, theology, liturgy, memory work, Great Books, Socratic seminars, and parental partnership, among others. 

ABSTRACT
Dayspring Christian Academy shows a CLASSICAL orientation in its religious/theological foundations and its formation-focused purpose, with strong parent/community engagement. However, explicit classical curriculum elements (Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric triad) and fully documented Socratic pedagogy across grades are not consistently articulated in public materials, placing Curriculum and Pedagogy in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL range. Faculty language and the depth of living-curriculum theory are present but not consistently explicit in public-facing content. Taken together, the school stands as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear opportunities to move to CLASSICAL through explicit Great Books incorporation, Socratic pedagogy expansion, and a fuller articulation of faculty development within a classical framework.
