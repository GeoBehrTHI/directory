## Executive Summary

Johnson Ferry Christian Academy (JFCA) presents a strongly religious, Christ-centered educational program rooted in Scripture and a biblical worldview. Public materials emphasize discipleship, godly character, worship, and partnership with families and the church, all within a college-style, faith-centered model. Across the six classical criteria, the school demonstrates clear CLASSICAL alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Faculty, and Parents & Community; clear but not fully developed alignment in School Purpose and Goal and in Pedagogy; and limited alignment in Curriculum. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL, with some notable classical practices and several opportunities to deepen its classical formation (especially in curriculum content and pedagogy). See category-by-category details below.

Key evidence includes: clear statements of Christ-centered, biblical worldview and Scripture-guided instruction; worship and discipleship as central; a family/Church partnership orientation; a hybrid-model schedule that intertwines faith-based instruction with parental engagement; accreditation information; and explicit parental involvement requirements. Citations to the school’s materials and to the classical-education keyword framework are provided after each section.

- Core religious/metaphysical orientation and liturgical/community practices are explicit in JFCA public materials, including statements about Christ-centered education, Scripture as objective truth, and worship experiences.  
- The school’s public materials emphasize formation of character and virtue within a biblical framework, and a home-and-church extension of education, rather than a narrow vocational focus. 
- Curriculum is described as a Christian hybrid-model with limited emphasis on classical Great Books or the trivium/quadrivium; the pedagogy described centers on on-campus days and faith-based instruction with parental engagement, rather than explicit Socratic/recitation-based classical methods. 
- Parents and community are actively involved and welcomed; parents are described as partners and co-educators, with explicit emphasis on parental engagement and home-school alignment.

What Would Move the Needle

- Since Element 2 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, the needle could move toward a more robust CLASSICAL alignment by:
  - Explicitly integrating classical curriculum content (e.g., Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, and the Great Conversation) into the formal curriculum narrative, with documented course materials and reading lists.
  - Introducing explicit Socratic pedagogy (Socratic seminars, dialectic discussions) in upper grades and a structured memory/recitation program (chanting, oral narration) consistent with classical practices.
  - Embedding a formal “liturgy of learning” schedule that reflects scholé-inspired contemplative time, restful learning, and memorization routines.
  - Highlighting a formal living-curriculum approach with explicit descriptions of classical teacher training, mentorship, and exemplars for both students and faculty.

- For Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy), to reach CLASSICAL, the school could:
  - Add explicit references to classical curricular components (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a sequence of studies implementing the trivium and quadrivium.
  - Publicly document a classical pedagogy plan, including structured practice in memorization, recitation, and rhetorical exercises; increase opportunities for Socratic dialogue and formal debates in the upper grades.

- For Element 5 (Faculty) and Element 6 (Parents & Community), the school already demonstrates CLASSICAL alignment; continued emphasis on mentor-teacher models, a robust “living curriculum” approach, and ongoing parent formation opportunities would strengthen these categories further.
