## ABSTRACT

Tall Oaks Academy (Kenosha, WI 53142) presents a robust CLASSICAL orientation across most criteria. Public-facing materials emphasize a distinctly Christian and classical education, with mission language centered on faith, virtue, and liberal-arts formation. The curriculum shows strong classical anchors: Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, Paideia-inspired thinking, and an integrated, historically informed approach to subjects. The school foregrounds formation and virtue as primary aims, not merely job preparation, aligning with the classical telos of education. Parental partnership is a standout strength, described as a core pillar of the school’s mission and practice.

Category-by-category summary:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Evidence of transcendental moral realism, theology grounding, and liturgical/community practices; explicit Christian formation and chapel activities support this ranking.   
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Emphasis on formation, virtue, and life-oriented aims; deliberate rejection of education as mere job-training; reference to liberal-arts aims and Great Books.  
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Clear liberal-arts framework, Great Books/Foundation content, and integrated cross-disciplinary study; early exposure to Latin and grammar/logic/rhetoric.  
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence of memory-based, contemplative pedagogy and Scholé-like pacing in lower grades; public material references Paideia/Socratic modes, with some high-school seminar evidence less explicit.  
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Faculty are portrayed as virtuous mentors and living exemplars, but detailed public narratives of the living-curriculum or faculty development are less explicit. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong emphasis on parental partnership, co-educator role, and parent formation; open campus and visits; active family engagement. 

Overall, Tall Oaks Academy is CLASSICAL, with substantive strengths in religious foundation, purpose, curriculum, and parent/community engagement, and with room to strengthen high-school pedagogy and teacher-living-curriculum detail in public-facing materials.
