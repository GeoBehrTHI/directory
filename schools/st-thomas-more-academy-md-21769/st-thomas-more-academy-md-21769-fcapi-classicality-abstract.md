## ABSTRACT

Overall Classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. STMA demonstrates strong Catholic theological grounding and liturgical life (Mass, Eucharistic Adoration, Feast Days, catechesis) with a clear faith-formation orientation that aligns with transcendental moral realism and liturgical practices. 
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. The formation-focused mission (Forming Saints and Scholars) and the integration of classical scaffolding (grammar/logics/rhetoric) indicate a formation-and-virtue orientation beyond mere job preparation. 
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The presence of grammar/logic/rhetoric signals a Trivium-based framework, but explicit Great Books/Great Conversation elements are not clearly evidenced in the STMA crawl; thus not fully classical in this dimension. 
- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL. Public materials do not document explicit classical pedagogy methods (Socratic seminars, Scholé/restful pedagogy, memory work) in a way that meets the full classical criteria, though the Catholic formation emphasis is strong. 
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence of teacher-role modeling and faith integration is present, but a formal public articulation of the living-curriculum faculty model and faculty development is not strongly evidenced. 
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Parental involvement is present via the STMA Parents section and family testimonials; more explicit parent-formation programming would strengthen this area. 

Overall: The school demonstrates significant classical orientation in spiritual formation and Trivium-based scaffolding, with strong formation goals and community alignment. However, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation content and documented classical pedagogy across the full six elements are not consistently evident in the public crawl. As a result, the aggregate assessment sits in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete opportunities to advance into CLASSICAL through focused curricular, pedagogical, and parent-community enhancements.

What would move the needle (summary):
- Implement and publish a formal Great Books/Great Conversation component and associated reading lists.
- Publicly document and train in classic pedagogies (Socratic seminars, Scholé pedagogy, memorization/recitation).
- Publish a formal “Living Curriculum” framework for faculty and a visible portrait of teacher-formation practices.
- Expand parent-formation programming and open-campus opportunities to reinforce parental co-education.

Citations and sources used:
