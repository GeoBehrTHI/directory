## Executive Summary

St Thomas More Academy (STMA) in Middletown, MD (ZIP 21769) presents a Catholic, formation-focused program with clear liturgical and theological elements embedded in school life. Public-facing materials emphasize a vibrant faith life, Catholic worship, catechesis, and a sense of communal identity (e.g., “Vibrant Faith Life,” Mass, Eucharistic Adoration, Feast Day celebrations). The school also signals a classical scaffolding through emphasis on grammar, logic, and rhetoric in its academic structure, and it frames its mission with phrases like “Forming Saints and Scholars since 2000.” However, explicit, widely-cited references to core Great-Books-based Great Conversation and a fully articulated history-centric liberal-arts curriculum are not readily evident in the public crawl content provided. Taken together, STMA demonstrates a solid religious-metaphysical and formation orientation and a partial classical framework, but it does not consistently demonstrate all six elements at a fully classical level. On a scale of CLASSICAL / SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL / NOT CLASSICAL, the school lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear room to strengthen elements such as explicit Great Books engagement, explicit Socratic pedagogy, and deeper articulation of Great Conversation-era curricular practices.

Executive ranking by category (summary):
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
- School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
- Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL
- Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What would move the needle: To move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL, STMA could explicitly articulate and operationalize the Great Books/Great Conversation component within the curriculum, incorporate explicit Socratic or Paideia-style seminars or discussions in upper grades, enlarge the public presentation of a clearly defined “living curriculum” in which teachers function as mentors and exemplars, and deepen parent formation opportunities (e.g., book studies, parental involvement rituals, campus visits) aligned with classical education norms.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum: Explicitly articulate and publish a Great Books / Great Conversation component within the STMA curriculum, with a published list and reading plan that maps to the classical “Great Books” canon and the Great Conversation framework. This would elevate Element 3 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy: Adopt and publicly document explicit classical-pedagogy methods (e.g., Socratic seminars in upper grades, Paideia-style seminars, Harkness discussions, or mnemonic recitation as a deliberate pedagogy). Introduce and showcase Scholé/restful learning practices as a school-wide rhythm, with identifiable “Scholé days” or restful-learning curricula. This would move Element 4 from NOT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
- Faculty: Develop and publish a formal “Portrait of a Teacher” living-curriculum concept, with teacher mentorship, collegial collaboration, and sustained professional development in classical pedagogy. Publicly share faculty formation activities (seminars, study groups) that map to a classical model. This would strengthen Element 5 toward CLASSICAL.
- Parents & Community: Expand parent-formation offerings (book studies, reading groups, school-wide family catechesis, and structured campus visits) to deepen the partnership with families and actively position parents as co-educators. This would raise Element 6 toward CLASSICAL.
