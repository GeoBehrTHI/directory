## Executive Summary

Cambridge Classical Academy IL 60609 presents a public posture that is recognizably classical in impulse but uneven in the explicit articulation of core classical practices. Public materials show a faith- and character-oriented framing (e.g., “grounded in family, culture, and faith” and emphasis on forming mind and character) that signals alignment with the classical aim of virtue and wisdom, even if the site does not consistently name the classical pillars (great books, the Trivium, the Great Conversation, Scholé, etc.) by formal title. Based on the six-element rubric for classical education, Cambridge earns a cautious, but clearly present, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL profile overall. The strongest signals are in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and Faculty/Leadership (where the site foregrounds faith and a virtuous, guided teaching staff). The curriculum and pedagogy signals are weaker in explicit Great Books, Socratic pedagogy, and formal liberal-arts sequencing on the public-facing pages, and thus Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) tilt toward NOT CLASSICAL. The Parents & Community category shows some alignment with partnership and family involvement, but lacks explicit, sustained parent-education and co-teaching language in public materials. The aggregate reading across the six categories falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete needle-moving opportunities described below.

- Executive ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- What would move the needle section (included because this school is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL):
  - Make Great Books and Great Conversation explicit in the curriculum narrative (e.g., mention of reading lists, Great Books, and a sustained Great Conversation across grades).
  - Introduce explicit, documented Socratic/Paideia-style pedagogy in upper grades (e.g., regular Socratic seminars, seminars, or debates with evidence of implementation in 9–12).
  - Elevate the Liberal Arts structure on public pages (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium; Latin/Greek) with concrete course maps and sample syllabi.
  - Strengthen the Parents & Community axis with formal parent-education offerings, visible co-teaching language, and open-campus visit policies explicitly described on the site.
  - Introduce or highlight liturgical or communal practices (even if modest) to address liturgical learning and Scholé rhythms publicly.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Add explicit theology integration and liturgical/community practices on public pages (e.g., a statement of theology as a central curricular driver, liturgical rhythms in daily life, and visible communal worship or prayer practices).
- For Element 2 (Purpose & Goal): Emphasize explicit formation language (virtue, wisdom, telos) with concrete outcomes and a short, public-facing statement of formation as the primary end beyond information—e.g., “formation of character and intellect for the good” with examples.
- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate a liberal-arts framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), Great Books or Western-civilization throughlines, and the “Great Conversation” as core throughline; provide sample scope-and-sequence and reading lists.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Publish explicit evidence of classical pedagogy in practice (Socratic or Paideia seminars, memory work, recitation/chanting, and Scholé rhythms) and tie them to grade-level implementation with real examples.
- For Element 5 (Faculty): Strengthen and publish a “Portrait of the Teacher” and a public statement about the living-curriculum model (faculty as exemplars of virtue, mentors, and collaborative colleagues) with examples of professional development in classical pedagogy.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Elevate parent-partnership language (parents as co-teachers; parent formation; clubs/book studies; open-campus visits) and provide a formal plan for parental involvement on the site.

- Rationale: The current signals show foundational alignment in faith and formation but lack explicit articulation of core classical dynamics (Great Books, Trivium pedagogy, Scholé, parent partnership) that the Six Essential Elements document treats as hallmarks. Filling these gaps would push Cambridge from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
