## Executive Summary

Adelphia Classical Christian Academy (ACCA) presents a robust classical-leaning program anchored in a Christ-centered, Biblically grounded framework. Across categories, ACCA demonstrates core classical markers: the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as developmental stages; a curriculum oriented toward liberal arts, Great Books/Western civilization influences, Latin/Greek preparatory work, and a public emphasis on virtue, truth, beauty, and goodness; pedagogy that includes Socratic discourse alongside memorization, chanting, and memory-work; a faculty described as skilled teachers forming a “living curriculum”; and a strong parent/community partnership through its Private School Program (PSP) structure, which positions parents as collaborators rather than mere customers. Public materials consistently foreground virtues, theological commitments, and a shared ethos (truth, beauty, goodness) and describe structures (CLSA/WASC accreditation, NCAA/UC course approvals) that align with classical aims rather than workforce-aimed curricula.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Key strengths observed in ACCA’s public materials and the crawl content include: explicit Trivium framing and three-stage development, a curriculum that centers Classical core subjects with Memoria Press and other classical resources, Socratic and rhetoric-inflected pedagogy, visible moral formation and prayer/ethos, and a strong parental-partnership model. Some public materials also emphasize a hybrid/home-study component and 3-day on-campus structure, which reflects a distinctive PSP arrangement rather than a traditional 5-day classical school, but the classical core remains front-and-center. This combination sustains the school’s alignment with classical aims while leveraging a hybrid delivery model. See evidence excerpts cited below for category-specific justification.

### Category 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Pedagogical approach includes the Socratic method for upper levels, with opportunities for seminary-like discourse, debate, and rhetorical exercises. The public materials explicitly call out Socratic discussion and rhetoric/instruction at high school levels.
- Lower- to middle-school pedagogy includes chanting, memory work, recitation, and structured memorization, consistent with the classical pedagogy toolkit (copybooks, liturgical/catechetical memory work, etc.).
- The school acknowledges a restorative/restful dimension (scholé) in some aspects of education, while still emphasizing rigorous, high-level study—an integration of contemplative/restful elements with rigorous inquiry.
- The program documents upper-school rhetoric training, speeches, and advanced linguistic work (Latin, classical studies), indicating a full engagement of rhetoric and public expression in the pedagogy.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive indicators present: Socratic seminars; dialectic; memorization/recitation; chant/singing for memory; restfulness/scholé; rhetoric and oratory; annual public speaking opportunities.
- Negative indicators: No explicit exclusive reliance on experiential/project-based learning; the materials emphasize traditional, teacher-led, and memory-based classical pedagogy rather than a wholesale shift to student-led or project-based constructivism.

Why this rating

- ACCA demonstrates a comprehensive classical pedagogy: Socratic/seminal discourse for upper grades, chant/memory-based recitation for younger grades, and a rhetoric emphasis in high school. This is CLASSICAL.

Citations: Socratic method and upper-level rhetoric; memory/recitation and chant; scholé/restful learning language.
