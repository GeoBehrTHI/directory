## Executive Summary

Philadelphia Classical School (PCS) presents a robust CLASSICAL profile across core rubric categories. The school foregrounds a Christian, classical formation—rooted in gospel faith, the Western intellectual tradition, and the liberal arts—while explicitly committing to virtue formation, engagement with Great Books, and ancient pedagogical practices (grammar, logic, rhetoric) woven into a K–12 program. Public materials emphasize worship, liturgical rhythm, and community formation, alongside a curriculum anchored in the Great Books and unified across disciplines. Notable evidence includes: a mission statement centered on Christ and classical formation; explicit references to teaching the good, true, and beautiful; daily arts integration and liturgical practices (chapel, Christmas programs); a stated portrait of a PCS graduate oriented toward wisdom, virtue, and discipleship; and explicit examples of Great Books, humane letters, and rhetorical training at upper levels. Parental involvement is embedded (volunteer requirements, open houses), and the faculty is described as a living curriculum with a tight-knit, mission-centered culture.

Overall, PCS scores CLASSICAL across all six categories. The public materials closely align with the Positive Keyword indicators in the classical rubric (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty; formation of virtue; Great Books; integration of disciplines; liturgical/community practices; parent partnership in a shared mission). There are clear manifestations of Trivium-focused pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Great Books engagement; theology and liturgical life ground the educational project; faculty and parent community are presented as integral to the formation process.
