## Executive Summary

Cherokee Christian School (CCS) in Woodstock, GA presents a robust classical Christian program aligned with core classical criteria. Public-facing materials consistently emphasize a biblically-based, classical orientation, a mission centered on virtue, wisdom, and service, a curriculum grounded in liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with Great Books and Great Conversation, a pedagogy that combines memorization and chant in early grades with Socratic inquiry and rhetoric in the upper grades, a faculty culture described as committed, and an active, value-driven partnership with parents and the broader Christian community. Taken together, these elements support a CLASSICAL classification across all six categories of the Six Essential Elements framework. Evidence includes explicit statements about classical transition and the liberal arts, references to Great Books and the Great Conversation, explicit mention of Socratic dialogue in high school, emphasis on virtue and biblical faith, and multiple indicators of strong parent/community engagement. See representative citations from CCS materials and the classical education lexicon used for scoring. 

Overall Ranking (aggregate): CLASSICAL

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- CCS presents itself as biblically-based and Christian, explicitly describing itself as a “biblically-based, classical, Christian, independent, non-denominational, covenant school.”
- Mission and culture statements center on Christ, God’s world, and a Christian worldview. For example: “In Him, all things hold together” and emphasis on biblical truth, virtue, and the exaltation of the good, the true, and the beautiful. 
- The Primary/Lower-Elementary articulation emphasizes truth, beauty, and goodness as formative aims.
- There is explicit talk of a distinctively Christian culture and the need for students and staff to embody Christian attitudes and practices.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: biblical, Christ/Christian, truth, wisdom, knowledge, virtue, goodness, beauty, Word of God, God’s world, theology (implicit), worship/liturgical frame (explicit liturgical vocabulary appears in the lexicon and related materials, though explicit CCS liturgy terms are not loudly foregrounded on every page).
- Negative keywords observed: none in CCS public materials; absence of secular or relativistic framing is notable.
- Quantitative note: Strong presence of multiple CCS phrases that map to Positive Keywords (roughly 6–9 clear positives with minimal if any explicit negatives in CCS materials).

Why this rating

- The public materials foreground transcendental moral realism in practice (truth, goodness, beauty as real and knowable; virtue formation; Christ-centered epistemology). The language of biblical faith, virtue, and a Christ-centered culture aligns with the Positive Keywords in the Six Essential Elements lexicon. The absence of competing secular emphases and explicit references to relativism or purely secular pedagogy supports CLASSICAL rather than SOMEWHAT or NOT CLASSICAL.

Cited evidence: biblically-based/Christian identity; Christ-centered worldview; love for truth/beauty/goodness; distinct Christian culture.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

The CCS site links and documents describe classical content as foundational and historically anchored.
- Historical engagement and integration across disciplines are indicated, including interdisciplinary approaches and the aim to cultivate wonder through integrated study (as reflected in the “integration and wonder” language in the Six Elements materials and CCS materials).
- The curriculum includes explicit engagement with history, logic, rhetoric, and Great Books; upper grades employ Socratic dialogue and rhetoric training.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: liberal arts, grammar, logic, rhetoric, Great Books, Great Conversation, primary sources, history (as Western civilization), Socratic method, memory work/recitation, wonder, interdisciplinary/harmony of subjects.
- Negative keywords observed: very few if any; CCS materials emphasize classical content and avoid project-based-only framing in favor of classical structures (though some modern instruction may include activities, the public-facing materials emphasize classical methods and Great Books). The Six Elements lexicon notes potential negatives like project-based dominance; CCS materials do not foreground that as a primary approach.
- Quantitative note: Many positives (likely >6 clear indicators) with minimal to no explicit negatives in CCS public materials.

Why this rating

- CCS publicly describes its curriculum as classical and explicitly aligns with the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, Great Conversation, and historical integration, consistent with CLASSICAL criteria in the Lexicon.

Cited evidence: explicit classical/curricular terminology; Seven Liberal Arts; Great Books; Great Conversation; Socratic dialogue; memory/recitation. 

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Upper-level pedagogy explicitly includes Socratic dialogue, seminars, and debates, with emphasis on rhetoric and persuasive writing in the upper grades. This is visible in CCS materials describing high school pedagogy.
- Lower grades emphasize memory, chanting, and recitation as a foundation for learning (see classical pedagogy references and CCS materials that discuss memory-based learning in the early stages; the Six Elements lexicon notes these as core pedagogical practices).
- The CCS materials describe a continuum from contemplative/restful elements (Scholé) to rigorous Socratic inquiry in later grades, consistent with a classical progression. The CCS materials and the Six Elements lexicon both emphasize a balance of contemplation and rigorous discussion. 
- There is explicit mention of training in rhetoric and public speaking in the high school program, including debate and persuasive writing.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue, seminars, debate, rhetoric, persuasive writing, recitation, chanting, memory work, scholé/restful learning, memorization, liturgical/classical pedagogy.
- Negative keywords observed: Minimal or no explicit negative pedagogy signals; the CCS materials emphasize classical pedagogy rather than exclusively modern, project-based or inquiry-only frameworks.
- Quantitative note: Multiple strong positive pedagogical indicators; negatives effectively absent in public materials.

Why this rating

- The pedagogy matches the Classical model’s expectations: memory-based early learning, transition to Socratic dialogue and rhetoric in upper grades, and deliberate use of classical forms of discourse and memory in instruction. Therefore CLASSICAL.

Cited evidence: Socratic dialogue in high school; memory/recitation; chanting; rhetoric and public speaking; scholé/restful learning.
