## Executive Summary

Yellowstone Classical Christian Academy (Joliet, MT, 59041) (YCCA) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning educational model that centers on virtue, truth, and the integration of faith with a liberal-arts curriculum. Public-facing materials emphasize a biblically informed worldview, character formation, worship and chapel practices, and a partnership with families and the local community. The site also foregrounds a traditional, content-rich curriculum that includes Biblical instruction, literacy, numeracy, sciences, arts, and civics, all framed within a classical pedagogy.

Overall classification across the classical rubric is MOSTLY CLASSICAL but with notable gaps. In particular, Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), Element 2 (Purpose & Goal), and Element 6 (Parents & Community) show strong classical alignment (clear theology, virtue formation, and active parent/community partnership). Categories 3 (Curriculum), 4 (Pedagogy), and 5 (Faculty) show substantial classical cues but lack the explicit, comprehensive articulation of some canonical elements (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis and high-fidelity Socratic/seminar pedagogy in all grades, and a fully developed, documented faculty living-curriculum approach). The aggregate judgment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- Religious/metaphysical commitments are explicit and foundational (Bible taught, chapel, Christian worldview) and permeate mission and governance signals. Evidence includes mission statements, prayer/chapel emphasis, and a biblically anchored ethos.
- Educational aims center on formation, virtue, wisdom, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty within a liberal-arts frame.
- Curriculum is described as classical and liberal-arts oriented, with explicit mention of biblical instruction and a content-rich approach; explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language is less prominent on the public pages, though related classical resources appear in the site’s "Resources" area.
- Pedagogy signals include memory and recitation, singing/chapel, and a Restful-Scholé orientation in parts of the site; explicit Socratic/seminar practice is not consistently showcased across all grade levels in public content.
- Faculty signals (board, mission-aligned staff, and living-curriculum rhetoric) are present but not fully fleshed in terms of a documented, sustained “living curriculum” faculty profile across grades; a board-led, values-driven culture is evident.
- Parent and community partnership is a strong theme (co-teaching, parental engagement, community events, and open access to campus), aligning with classical expectations of family involvement.

This is reinforced by the site’s explicit language about biblical instruction and a biblically anchored worldview.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating

- The materials consistently present formation, virtue, wisdom, and the pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty as the educational telos, aligning with CLASSICAL norms. The presence of a biblically anchored, virtue-centered purpose supports Classic alignment.

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The curriculum is described as classical, liberal-arts oriented with content-rich instruction including Biblical instruction, literacy, numeracy, sciences, arts, and civics. This signals alignment with the Trivium/Quadrivium ethos in spirit (grammar/logic/rhetoric framework is taught in the public documents about classical education).
- The site and resources host materials that are central to classical pedagogy (Liberal Arts Tradition; Lost Tools of Learning; Great Books/Great Conversation concepts appear within the site’s “Resources” area and in classical philosophy texts linked there).

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords found: liberal arts; grammar; logic; rhetoric; classical education; Great Books/Great Conversation (via resources and classical texts); sacred tradition (through biblical/theological framing); memory work and recitation (through recitation and memory elements in classical pedagogy).
- Negative keywords: some explicit classical elements (e.g., explicit, comprehensive Great Books program) are not overtly stated in the main public pages; the presence of classical pedagogy is implied but not uniformly enumerated. The result is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

Why this rating

- The school clearly presents itself as classical and liberal-arts oriented, with explicit Biblical instruction and virtue-centered goals. However, explicit, codified articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation, and a fully enumerated Trivium/Quadrivium curriculum across all grades is less prominently documented in the public-facing materials. This keeps Element 3 in the Somewhat Classical range.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Pedagogical signals include Restful-Scholé orientation and emphasis on recitation/memory work and chant in lower grades, plus chapel-based or liturgical elements. This matches the “formation through memory and contemplation” part of classical pedagogy.
- The site describes the “classical model” with recitations, memory work, and a focus on the development of memory and the affections; in upper grades, the presence of Socratic discussion is less explicitly documented on public pages, though the classical framework is invoked.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords found: scholé (restful learning), recitation/memory work, singing/chanting in lower grades, rhetoric and memory training, Socratic discussion (referenced in classical education materials in general; direct site evidence is less explicit but implied by the classical framework), contemplative learning.
- Negative keywords observed: limited explicit evidence of sustained, school-wide Socratic seminars or upper-school dialectic seminars on public pages; the pedagogy is described, but not uniformly across grades. This reduces to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Why this rating

- YCCA demonstrates several core pedagogical elements aligned with classical education (Scholé/restful learning, memory work, chanting, reverent classroom culture) but lacks consistent, explicit documentation of formal Socratic seminars across the upper grades in the public-facing materials. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make Great Books / Great Conversation an explicit, public-facing pillar: Add a dedicated page detailing a Great Books framework, sample readings, and a public commitment to “Great Conversation” as a curricular spine, complemented by explicit references to primary sources and canonical texts.
- Publicly document a formal Socratic-seminar plan across middle- and upper-grade levels: Include teacher training notes, exemplar rubrics, and sample seminar units to demonstrate sustained Socratic pedagogy in practice.
- Publish a formal “Living Curriculum” plan for Faculty (Element 5): Provide faculty job descriptions, teacher-formation protocols, and exemplar stories of teachers modeling classical virtues and serving as living exemplars in the classroom.
- Expand explicit evidence of a Seven Liberal Arts framework (Trivium/Quadrivium) within publicly accessible materials: Include a curriculum map that shows Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric progression, Latin/Greek study expectations, and the integration of the Quadrivium in secondary grades.
- Increase explicit signaling of philosophical integration and Great Books (Element 3) through sample units and core texts to reinforce the classical identity beyond general “liberal arts” framing.
- Strengthen documentation of parent formation and community formation (Element 6) with ongoing book studies, parent-education events, and observable co-teaching activities.
