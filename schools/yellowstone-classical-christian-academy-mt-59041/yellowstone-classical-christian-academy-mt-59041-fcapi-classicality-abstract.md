# ABSTRACT

Yellowstone Classical Christian Academy (Joliet, MT, 59041) exhibits a robust classical-Christian orientation with strong signals around religious/metaphysical foundations and community/parent partnership. The public materials emphasize biblical instruction, chapel, prayer, and a Christian worldview as integral to education, aligning Element 1 with a CLASSICAL rating. The school’s mission also emphasizes formation through virtue, wisdom, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, supporting Element 2 as CLASSICAL.

Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) show substantial classical cues but lack uniform, explicit public documentation of comprehensive Great Books involvement and sustained Socratic/seminar pedagogy across all grades; the public materials emphasize liberal-arts content and restorative pedagogy (Scholé/restful learning and memory-work) but do not consistently present full canonical implementation. Thus, Element 3 and 4 are BEST characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Element 5 (Faculty) signals—living curriculum and mentor-figures—are present but not fully developed in publicly accessible material; this yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 5. Element 6 (Parents & Community) is CLASSICAL, with explicit signals about parent partnership, co-teaching, and community engagement.

Aggregate: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key sources include Yellowstone’s mission and program statements (emphasizing a classical, Christian education, with strong virtue formation and biblical instruction) and evidence of the Great Books/Great Conversation ecosystem embedded within the site’s resources, chapel practices, and community events. Public materials consistently underscore a Christian worldview, formation-oriented aims, and parental/community engagement, while explicit, codified Great Books/Socratic pedagogy across all grade levels remains less overt in the public-facing materials.
