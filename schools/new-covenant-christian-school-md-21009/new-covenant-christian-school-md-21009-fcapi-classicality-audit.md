## Executive Summary

New Covenant Christian School Maryland (NCCS-MD) presents a robust classical Christian program built around a Trivium framework, Great Books, and a piety-centered, biblical worldview. Public materials consistently foreground a Christ-centered, Reformed, classical education that emphasizes truth, goodness, and beauty and aims at character formation, virtue, and wisdom rather than mere job preparation. The school identifies its mission as glorifying God by helping parents train children to become biblically minded servant-leaders and to engage in the Great Books through a Christian Paideia. These signals align NCCS-MD with classical Christian education in most respects, particularly in Curriculum and Religious Foundations, and in overall Purpose/Goal. Some evidence is nuanced in Faculty and Pedagogy, where explicit language about a living curriculum or formal, teacher-led formation is less explicit on the site, though several faculty roles and classical disciplines are highlighted.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

- The strongest signals are in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, and School Parents & Community, all clearly aligned with classical Christian aims (theology, teleology, Great Books, Trivium, and parental partnership). 
- Pedagogy shows clear classical markers (Scholé-inspired rhythm, memorization/recitation, and Socratic-style dialogue in broader classical literature contexts), though explicit on-site statements about Socratic seminars or a fully articulated upper-school seminar program are less explicit in some pages. 
- Faculty signals are present (named teachers with classical disciplines; visible mentorship-like roles), but the public materials do not feature a formal “Living Curriculum” portrait as a stated brand, which places Element 5 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community) is strongly CLASSICAL due to explicit partnership language with families and parental formation components, plus open-door policies for visits.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 5 (Faculty) — Move to CLASSICAL: 
  - Publish explicit statements on the faculty as the “living curriculum,” including a formal portrait of the teacher and a clear articulation of faculty development in classical pedagogy (Socratic practice, memory work, and the integration of Great Books across grades).
  - Provide explicit language on teacher mentorship models and how faculty embody classical virtues (truth, goodness, beauty) in daily classroom practice.

- Element 1 (Religious/Metaphysical Foundations) — Move toward deeper liturgical/community signals:
  - Introduce explicit liturgical or communal practice language (e.g., daily or weekly chapel, liturgical rhythm, sacred music, or community worship components) to strengthen “Liturgical and Communal Practices” signals while maintaining the biblical-Christian foundation.

- Element 4 (Pedagogy) — Strengthen explicit pedagogy map:
  - Publish a formal pedagogy framework detailing when and how Socratic seminars, structured memory work, and rhetorics training are implemented in the upper grades, plus a schedule that demonstrates a Scholé-based contemplative rhythm in the daily/weekly plan.

- Element 2/3 (Purpose and Curriculum) — Further clarity and public artifacts:
  - Publish a concise, public-facing map of the curriculum showing how Great Books, Trivium, and liberal arts alignment link to grade-level objectives; include examples of canonical texts and how inquiry and wonder are cultivated across grades.
  - Expand documentation around the Great Conversation to explicitly denote the range of primary texts and authors incorporated in the curriculum.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community) — Deepen parent-education offerings:
  - Develop a formal parent-education program (book studies, seminars, and regular updates) explicitly connected to classical education topics (e.g., paideia, teleology, the Great Books tradition) to further parent formation.
