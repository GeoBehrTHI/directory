## ABSTRACT

New Covenant Christian School Maryland (NCCS-MD) demonstrates strong alignment with classical Christian education across most categories, centering on a Trivium-based curriculum, Great Books, and a biblically informed telos. The school’s public materials foreground a biblical worldview, Christ-centered, Reformed identity, and a mission to form virtuous, wise servant-leaders, emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable. The curriculum and pedagogy signals emphasize the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and memory-based practices, alongside contemplative (Scholé) rhythms and rhetoric training, pointing to a robust classical approach. The school’s parent/community framework is particularly strong, with explicit language about partnership with families and parental formation.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Clear theistic grounding, biblical worldview, and Christ-centered classical education anchor the school’s metaphysical stance. Positive indicators include theology-rich framing and Great Books engagement. There are few explicit liturgical practices in the public-facing materials, but theological orientation remains strong. Evidence: NCCS mission/vision and Great Books framing.  

- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. The school emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and human flourishing with teleological aims consistent with classical education. Evidence: Vision/mission statements and telos framing. 

- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. The Trivium, Great Books, and integrated liberal arts framing are central; history and canonical texts align with Great Books pedagogy. Evidence: The Trivium and Great Books references. 

- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL. Classical methods include memory work, recitation, Scholé-inspired rhythms, and Socratic/seminar-style inquiry implied by Great Books/Trivium approach. Evidence: The Trivium framework and memory/recitation emphasis in NCCS materials; classical pedagogy signals. 

- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Public materials show named faculty with classical disciplines but lack explicit branding of the faculty as the “living curriculum” or a formal, public portrait of teacher formation. Evidence: Faculty pages listing Bible/Latin/Logic/Theology; Head of School; team profiles. 

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong parent partnership signals, with explicit aims to partner with families and formation of parents. Evidence: Mission statements and parent-facing materials. 

Aggregate verdict: CLASSICAL.
