## Executive Summary

Coram Deo Academy (CDA) presents a robust Classical Christian educational model that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements framework. The public-facing materials portray CDA as Christian, Classical, and Collaborative, with a clear aim toward forming virtuous, wise students who pursue Truth, Goodness, and Beauty within a theology-integrated curriculum and a foundation of communal worship, parent partnership, and Socratic- and rhetoric-infused pedagogy. Across campus pages and program descriptions, CDA emphasizes a Great-Books–style, liberal-arts-informed curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; great works), a pedagogy featuring memory work, recitation, narration, Socratic discussion, and public speaking, and a family- and church-aligned community model that treats parents as co-educators. This combination supports a CLASSICAL overall rating across all six categories, with evidence of explicit classical signals (theology-integrated education, Great Books orientation, Socratic and rhetoric pedagogy, and a living-curriculum faculty/community ideal) and minimal indicators of non-classical signals in the public materials.

Key insights:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strongly present: explicit Christian framing, theology woven through academics, chapel/fellowship language, and an orthodox theological stance. This maps closely to the Positive Keywords around theology, liturgical practice, and sacred formation in the Six Essential Elements corpus.  
- Purpose and Goal: CDA’s mission centers on forming virtuous servant-leaders and wise thinkers under a Christ-centered framework, emphasizing formation over mere information and the pursuit of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty. This aligns with the Classical rubric’s telos and formation signals. 
- Curriculum is clearly liberal-arts–leaning and connected to Great Books/Great Conversation (even if the exact phrase “Great Books” is not always explicit on every page, the site emphasizes great works, classical curriculum, Latin, and historical-literary engagement). This matches the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books/Great Conversation signals and the “formation through the arts” orientation in the knowledge-base rubric.  
- Pedagogy features Socratic discussion in upper grades, rhetoric and public speaking, and memory work in earlier years, with lower grades incorporating singing/chanting and memory-rich routines. This mirrors the Classical pedagogy signals in the rubric. 
- Faculty ideal: teachers described as virtuous, living curriculum, mentors, and partners with families; a culture of collaboration and discipleship is foregrounded. 
- Parents & Community: CDA’s Collaborative Model and parent-in-home-learning emphasis position parents as co-teachers, with open-campus/semi-structured parent engagement, evidencing strong partnership with families. 

Overall aggregate rating: CLASSICAL

Note on needle-moving: No major structural changes are required to reach full CLASSICAL status; the materials already hit core signals across all six categories. If anything were pursued to push toward a more explicit “Great Conversation/Great Books” phrasing across all program descriptions, and a slightly more explicit articulation of classical vocabulary (e.g., consistent use of “Great Books” and “Solemn rites of liturgy” language in every campus page), those would further harden the signals.

- The site references theology woven into academics, Bible curricula, chapel gatherings, and biblical worldview framing. 
- Distinctives explicitly affirm Christian foundations (Nicene Creed), and a Christ-centered, biblical approach per campus materials. 
- The “pursue truth, goodness, and beauty” phrasing anchors transcendental aims within a theological framework. 

Positive keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Christian, Christ-centered, biblical worldview/theology, Bible/chapel, Nicene Creed, presence of God, sacred/traditional vocabulary, pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty, liturgical/topical worship language.
- According to the Six Essential Elements lexicon, these map to Positive Indicators like theology, liturgy/worship, reverence, wonder, and transcendental truthed values. See the Positive Keyword lists (e.g., “Theology,” “Liturgical and Communal Practices,” “Pursuit of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty”). 

Why this rating
- The school grounds its entire mission in a Christian metaphysical vision and weaves theological commitments into the curricular and communal life. In the Six Elements framework, this hits the core signals for True/Good/Beautiful, theocentric formation, and liturgical community life. The absence of explicit secular relocation signals strengthens the CLASSICAL designation.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

This pedagogical model aligns with classical traditions of formation through memory, recital, and memory-work, as well as dialogic inquiry in higher grades. 
- Some pages explicitly describe “Socratic Dialogue” and “Growing in Wisdom,” indicating a targeted use of the dialectic method in appropriate grade bands.

Why this rating
- CDA’s pedagogy explicitly uses classical methods (Socratic dialogue in high school, rhetoric training, public speaking, memory/recitation in lower grades) and includes contemplative/restful elements (scholé-like orientation) in the program materials. This aligns with CLASSICAL pedagogy signals in the rubric.

Aggregate Assessment
- All six categories are CLASSICAL based on the evidence and keyword signals present in the school’s public materials and the knowledge-base rubric. The school’s overall approach emphasizes a Christian, Classical, collaborative model, with liberal-arts curricula, Socratic/rhetorical pedagogy, a living-faculty mission, and robust parent partnership.

What Would Move the Needle
- If seeking to strengthen the CLASSICAL signal even further, CDA could consistently foreground the exact “Great Books” framing in every campus/program page (beyond references to “great works” and the classical curriculum) and uniform use of classical terminology (e.g., explicit “Great Books,” “Great Conversation” labels) across all campus sites. This would reduce any ambiguity and further align with the lexicon of the Six Essential Elements documents. Additionally, a short, campus-wide codified statement of liturgical practices (chapel cadence, liturgical calendar, and sacred-music norms) could more explicitly satisfy Liturgical and Communal Practices signals.
