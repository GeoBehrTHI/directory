# Classicality Audit Report: Bishop Louis Reicher Catholic School (Waco, TX)

Website audited: https://bishopreicher.com/

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Why: The school publicly embraces a Catholic liberal arts formation oriented toward wisdom, virtue, and the pursuit of the true, good, and beautiful, with clear evidence of liturgical life and virtue formation. However, the site offers limited explicit signals of distinctive classical curriculum and pedagogy (e.g., Trivium, Latin, Great Books, Socratic seminars), and includes some modern‑school markers (e.g., “Social Studies,” “hands‑on learning” in science). Evidence of a classical faculty profile is thin on public pages.
- Highlights:
  - Religious and metaphysical foundations are strong (virtue formation rooted in Aquinas; catechesis; regular sacred liturgy and music)  .
  - Purpose is stated in classical terms (Catholic liberal arts that “cultivates wisdom, virtue”; “forms saints, scholars, and leaders”; “foster what is good, true, and beautiful”)  .
  - Curriculum/pedagogy signaling is mixed (liberal arts claim; “classic literature” in early grades; but “Social Studies,” “hands‑on learning” language, and no explicit Trivium/Great Books/Socratic) .
- Bottom line: Strong Catholic formation and liberal‑arts intent; incomplete public evidence of core classical curriculum/pedagogy elements.  

Scoring Framework
- Method: Keyword analysis for each of the Six Elements (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose & Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community), using the positive/negative indicators provided in “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” and the classicality lexicon (e.g., truth/goodness/beauty, virtue, theology, liturgy, Trivium, Great Books, Socratic, scholé, wonder, chanting; negatives such as social studies replacing history, project‑based/experiential emphasis when it displaces classical methods)  .  
- Category ratings: CLASSICAL / SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL / NOT CLASSICAL.
- Quantification: Distinct positive vs. negative keyword hits from the school’s public pages are counted; “percent_positive” and “percent_coverage” reflect strength and breadth of classical signals on the site.

Overall Assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Strong Catholic formation (virtues, liturgy, catechesis) and stated liberal‑arts purpose (truth/goodness/beauty; wisdom/virtue) are clear strengths    .
- Public curriculum/pedagogy signals lack several hallmark classical markers (Trivium/Quadrivium, Latin, Great Books, explicit Socratic/seminar/rhetoric practices), and a few modern markers appear (Social Studies, hands‑on learning) .
- Faculty page(s) don’t yet articulate a classical teacher profile.

What Would Move the Needle
- Curriculum:
  - Publicly name Trivium/Quadrivium and show where grammar, logic, and rhetoric are taught; add Latin (even if phased) and publish Great Books/primary‑source lists by grade; relabel “Social Studies” as “History” with chronological scope notes  .
- Pedagogy:
  - Document Socratic seminars in upper school; showcase debate/orations/rhetoric capstones; highlight lower‑school chanting/recitation and memory work; add a brief on scholé/restful learning and wonder in the classroom  .
- Faculty:
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “living curriculum” statement; outline teacher formation in classical pedagogy and ongoing Great Books seminars for faculty .
- Parents & Community:
  - Add explicit parent‑formation offerings (book clubs on the great books/classical education) and a brief explainer tying home practices to the school’s classical ethos .
