## Executive Summary

Community Christian Academy (Stuart, FL, 34997) (CCA) presents a distinctly Christian, church-affiliated program with clear commitments to theology, biblical instruction, and parental partnership. The school shows strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and a robust Parents & Community orientation, along with a clearly stated formation-focused educational goal. However, in the core classical rubric used here, the curriculum and pedagogy lean away from classic liberal-arts frameworks (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy) and toward a biblically centered, Bible-based instructional model. Taken together, the school aligns more with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with strong classical-adjacent signals in certain areas (Religious Foundations, Formation-focused goals, and Family/Community partnership) but lacking full alignment in Curriculum and Pedagogy (the two central hallmarks of a traditional classical program).

Key insights at a glance

- Strong religious/metaphysical grounding: The school identifies itself as a ministry of a local church, emphasizes Christ-centered instruction, and maintains explicit statements of faith and chapel-based/missional culture. This aligns with the “Grounded in Theology” and “Liturgical/Communal Practices” strands of classical education in spirit, even if not by textbook usage.
- Formation over information: The mission and public messaging emphasize forming character and heart in a biblical frame, a hallmark of classical aims, though the site language does not consistently map to the formal classical vocabulary (e.g., “great books,” “Socratic pedagogy,” or “Scholé” are not explicit).
- Curriculum and pedagogy gaps relative to classical norms: The curriculum is Biblically-based with publishers like Abeka and Bob Jones University Press, and mentions Bible content across grades, but it does not publicly present the Great Books program, Trivium/Quadrivium structure, or explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogies, which reduces alignment with the core liberal-arts pathway of classical education.
- Parents and community engagement: The school foregrounds genuine partnership with parents and family involvement, a strong positive signal for the Parents & Community element.
- Faculty and culture: The site emphasizes faculty who anchor instruction in Christian commitments and heart-centered education, a positive signal, though explicit descriptions of the living-curriculum role or portrait-of-teacher norms are less explicit than ideal.

# ABSTRACT

The Community Christian Academy (Stuart, FL, 34997) evaluation identifies the school as CLASSICAL in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and in Parents & Community, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in School Purpose & Goal and Pedagogy (with notable formation emphasis but limited classical terminology), and NOT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy. The aggregate assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reflecting strong religious formation and parental partnership while lacking explicit classical curriculum structures and traditional classical teaching methods on public materials. The school’s public materials show a biblically-centered, Christ-centered education with a clear formation aim; however, it does not publicly articulate the full classical liberal-arts framework (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Trivium/Quadrivium) or the canonical Great Books approach in its curriculum or pedagogy. The strongest signals are Religious Foundations and Parent/Community partnership, with formation and faith-based aims permeating mission statements and Bible-focused instruction. The most significant needle-movers would be to adopt and publicly publish a canonical classical curriculum (Great Books; Seven Liberal Arts) and to implement explicit classical pedagogies (Socratic dialogue; scholé; memory work) while continuing to emphasize the faith-based identity and parental partnership.
