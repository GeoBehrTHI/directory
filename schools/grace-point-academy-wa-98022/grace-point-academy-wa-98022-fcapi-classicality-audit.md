## Executive Summary

Grace Point Academy WA 98022 (GPA) presents a publicly visible profile that strongly foregrounds classical, Christian education, anchored by Hillsdale College’s classical curriculum and a mission framed around formation, virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The school positions itself as part of Grace Point Northwest and emphasizes partnering with families to educate toward a life of purpose, faith, and service to God and others. Public materials also emphasize a worshiping, church-connected community (liturgical/communal life) and explicit parent engagement as part of the school’s ethos. Taken together, GPA demonstrates a Classical Christian orientation with clear teleology (formation toward virtue and wisdom) and a commitments to a shared spiritual milieu. However, explicit pedagogical methods associated with classical education (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholé, memorization/recitation, living curriculum) are not overtly described in the GPA materials, particularly for the lower grades. On balance, GPA is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong classical and theological framing and clear parent/community partnership, but less explicit documentation of canonical classical pedagogy in public-facing materials than the fullest classical model prescribes.

Key implications by category (high level):

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong presence of theological framing and sacred language (Classical Christian, truth/virtue/beauty in a sacred frame; liturgical/community life implied via church affiliation). CLASSICAL/STRONG alignment.
- Purpose and Goal: Clear formation-oriented telos (virtue, wisdom, life of purpose). CLASSICAL alignment.
- Curriculum: Explicit use of Hillsdale College’s classical curriculum; emphasis on classical orientation. SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL alignment (strong alignment, but with less overt Great Books/explicit Great Conversation language in public-facing text).
- Pedagogy: Public materials do not clearly articulate Socratic/seminar or scholé practices; lower grades’ pedagogy not well documented. NOT CLASSICAL (based on explicit articulation gap) but could be considered SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL if one accepts Hillsdale’s framework as encompassing classic pedagogy.
- Faculty: No explicit public-facing statements about teachers as “living curriculum” or mentorship models. NOT CLASSICAL (insufficient evidence in public materials).
- Parents & Community: Strong emphasis on partnership with families and open, welcoming community; explicit parental involvement in formation and events. CLASSICAL alignment.

What would move the needle

- Public articulation of classical pedagogy:
    - Add explicit statements or a dedicated section describing Socratic dialogue, Scholé (restful learning), memorization/recitation, and formation through imitation as core methods (especially in grades K–8, with a high-school articulation as appropriate).

- Greater Great Books/Great Conversation language:
    - Include explicit references to Great Books, great conversation, and canonical texts in the curriculum description, outlining how primary sources and classical authors are integrated across disciplines.
- Living curriculum for faculty:
    - Provide explicit language about the faculty as living curriculum, portraits of teachers, and a culture of collegial mentorship aligned with classical formation.
- Expanded evidence of integration:
    - Document the integration of the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium elements into concrete lesson structures and assessments, including memory work and recitation as a formal element.
- Strengthen parent formation programs:
    - Expand parent formation offerings (book studies, seminars) and publish a regular calendar of parent-focused classical-education events to reinforce parental partnership as co-educators.
