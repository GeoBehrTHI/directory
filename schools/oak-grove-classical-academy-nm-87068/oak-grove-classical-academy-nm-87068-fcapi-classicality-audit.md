## Executive Summary

Oak Grove Classical Academy NM 87068 presents a clearly classical, Christian-oriented program anchored in a University-Model pedagogy. Public materials from the school emphasize Christ-centered formation, biblical worldview, and virtue development (humility, courage, diligence) alongside explicit curricular elements associated with the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and upper-level rhetoric training (Rhetoric School, Latin, Senior Thesis). The school frames education as formation and character formation in a Christian context, placing substantial emphasis on parental partnership and a formation-focused mission. Taken together, these signals align most strongly with CLASSICAL, with some elements that appear more aligned with traditional or contemporary college-preparatory expectations than with a fully dialogic, Socratic, great-books-inflected classical core in every grade.

Key sources confirming the overarching orientation include the mission statement and theology-forward materials that explicitly name Christ-centered, biblical worldview formation and the Bible-based Statement of Faith, as well as the presence of virtues as central to the program. The presence of Latin, rhetoric coursework, Senior Thesis, and an explicit focus on virtue formation reinforces the classical orientation, though explicit mentions of Great Books and a continuous, school-wide Socratic/seminar pedagogy across all grades are less prominent in publicly available materials. The aggregate across the six categories remains CLASSICAL, with Element 3 and Element 4 showing SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals due to the explicit emphasis on a Trivium-and-Rhetoric trajectory and a more traditional college-preparatory framing in places.

What Would Move the Needle

- For a SCHOOL categorized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (Element 3 and 4 signals), the needle would move toward full CLASSICAL by:
    - Strengthening explicit Great Books/Great Conversation integration across all grade levels and making it visible as a consistent curricular pillar rather than primarily implied through rubrics.
    - Expanding explicit, school-wide Socratic/seminar pedagogy in more classrooms beyond the upper-school rhetoric pathway, and foregrounding restful, contemplative pedagogy (Scholé) in a more systematic way.
    - Elevating and clearly documenting the integration of liturgical practices or communal worship as a formal element of daily or weekly routines (if aligned with the school’s faith tradition) to meet liturgy-like community signals in the rubric.
