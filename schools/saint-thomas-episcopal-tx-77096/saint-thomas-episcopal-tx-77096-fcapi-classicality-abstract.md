# ABSTRACT

Saint Thomas' Episcopal School (STE) presents a Christian classical education anchored in a Christ-centered worldview, liturgical life, and liberal arts formation. The school articulates a strong religious and metaphysical foundation through worship, theology-influenced formation, and a mission focused on forming honorable men and women through classical education. It frames education as formation (not merely information) and highlights the transformative aims of wisdom, virtue, and human flourishing. The curriculum emphasizes a liberal-arts orientation with a clear path toward middle and upper-school classical studies, while pedagogy reflects a mix of liturgical/community practices and differentiated instruction designed to serve diverse learners. Faculty are portrayed as living exemplars and mentors, and the school positions parents as co-teachers within a collaborative community.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Evidence centers on truth, goodness, beauty; a Christ-centered worldview; liturgical worship; and a sense of sacred formation.
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Formation, virtue, and human flourishing are foregrounded; education is conceived as formation rather than mere information.
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Liberal arts framing and lower- to upper-school pathways point toward classical aims, but explicit canonical elements (Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts) are not consistently foregrounded in public materials.
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Contemplative liturgy and memory-centered elements appear, but explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy is not uniformly documented; there is room to strengthen public articulation of classical teaching methods.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. The living-curriculum model and mentorship emphasis are strong; faculty are described as exemplary and central to the educational mission.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. A robust partnership with families, welcoming parents, and parent formation are well established.

In short, STE is deeply aligned with classical aims in most dimensions, presenting a coherent Classical profile with specific opportunities to foreground certain canonical pedagogical and curricular practices more explicitly in public materials.

Citations and supporting content from the school’s public materials and the classical elements framework are integrated throughout this assessment.

# ABSTRACT (final)

Saint Thomas' Episcopal School (STE) in Houston, TX 77096 presents a classical Christian educational model with a strong orientation toward formation, virtue, and the liberal arts within a liturgical, faith-infused community. The school publicly emphasizes truth, goodness, and beauty; a Christ-centered worldview; weekly chapel and Bible study; and a mission of forming honorable men and women through classical education. The curriculum and pedagogy are described as liberal-arts aligned with a pathway from lower to upper grades, and the faculty are positioned as mentors and living exemplars. Parents are welcomed as co-teachers within a collaborative community. Overall, STE aligns with classical education across categories, with the strongest alignment in Religious Foundations, School Purpose, Faculty, and Parents/Community, and somewhat less explicit articulation of canonical curriculum and pedagogical methods in the public materials (i.e., Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts, and explicit Socratic pedagogy). The aggregate classification is CLASSICAL.
