## ABSTRACT

St. Thomas More Academy (STMA) demonstrates a CLASSICAL orientation across all six classical-education categories. Public-facing materials firmly position STMA as Catholic, classical, and college-preparatory, with explicit references to liberal arts, Great Books, and a Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades. The school emphasizes formation of virtue and wisdom within a Catholic framework, with a robust parental-partnership orientation and active faculty involvement as part of the living curriculum. The overall pattern aligns with the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education as detailed in the participating knowledge base lexicon, which identifies truth, virtue, theology, liturgy, Great Books, Socratic pedagogy, moral exemplars, and parent partnership as core positive indicators. The STMA materials present a coherent, integrated classical education model that meets or exceeds the stated criteria across all six categories.

Key sources include the Three Cs articulation (Catholic, Classical, College Preparatory) and the explicit use of Socratic method in the upper grades, along with Great Books language and a formation-focused mission. Catholic formation, liturgical life, and a bishop-endorsed program are repeatedly referenced, supporting the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category. The curriculum and pedagogy sections emphasize liberal arts, logic, rhetoric, and Socratic dialogue, supporting Curriculum and Pedagogy alignments. Faculty are portrayed as the living curriculum, with testimonials praising their influence, supporting the Faculty category. Finally, parent partnership and open opportunities for parent engagement are clearly described, satisfying the Parents & Community category.

Citations drawn from the STMA crawl materials show direct references to Catholic identity, the Three Cs, Mass, liturgical framing, Great Books, Socratic method, and parent partnership. For example, public materials note: “Catholic | Classical | College Preparatory” and the Socratic Method described in upper grades; Mass and liturgical language appear in admissions materials; parent-partner language appears in admissions and parent resources.    

End of Abstract.
