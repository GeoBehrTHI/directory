## Executive Summary

St. Thomas More Academy (STMA) in Raleigh, NC 27616 presents a robust classical Catholic education profile. Public materials from the school articulate a Catholic, classical, college-preparatory mission, explicit use of liberal arts and Great Books language, and a pedagogy that includes Socratic method in the upper grades. The school also emphasizes partnership with parents, episcopal endorsement, and a strong faculty identity as part of the “living curriculum.” Taken together, the public materials align strongly with the classical education rubric and indicate a CLASSICAL overall classification across the six categories.

Key signals across categories include:

- Religious/metaphysical foundations: explicit Catholic formation, liturgical framing, and worship-oriented emphasis (e.g., Ad Majorem Dei Gloriam; Mass; Bishop endorsements; patron saint). Evidence: Catholic identity foregrounded via Three Cs and patron content; liturgical language present in school materials; Mass ongoing in admissions context.
- Purpose and goals: formation of virtue and wisdom; a telos oriented toward truth, goodness, and beauty within a liberal-arts, classical frame. Evidence: explicit mention of forming character and virtues; language of formation and virtue in admissions materials; reference to “classical” formation aligned with virtue-centered goals.
- Curriculum: clear classical scaffolding and Great Books orientation; liberal arts emphasis; grammar/logic/rhetoric framing appears in public materials; explicit Great Books language appears in program descriptions. Evidence: “Three Cs” page presents Catholic, Classical, College Preparatory with a note on the content-rich curriculum and Socratic method; public text references Great Books and liberal arts language.
- Pedagogy: explicit use of Socratic method in upper grades; contemplative/restful elements referenced; memory-based recitation and ritual aspects are present in the lexicon and pedagogy claims. Evidence: Socratic method is described as part of the classical approach; the site also cites a contemplative, student-centered, traditional approach in upper grades.
- Faculty: praised as the “living curriculum” and as mentors; faculty profiles and testimonials reference virtuous exemplar teaching and strong faculty leadership. Evidence: testimonials cite faculty dedication; page structures point to a faculty-forward formation model.
- Parents & Community: strong parent-partnership framing; explicit calls for parent involvement and formation; openness to parent participation and visits; parent resources and book lists exist to engage families in classical formation. Evidence: admissions text emphasizes parent partnership; parent resources and suggested books for parents are present.

# ABSTRACT

conclusion: Based on the public materials across all six elements, STMA demonstrates a CLASSICAL orientation, with strong evidence in religious-metaphysical foundations, purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, and faculty, and solid engagement with parents and community.
