## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical Schools Of North Georgia (Veritas NGA) presents a strongly classical Christian program. Public-facing materials emphasize gospel-centered biblical formation, integration of faith and reason, a liberal-arts-inspired curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; classical languages; Great Books), and a relational, mentoring faculty model in a church-connected, family-partnered hybrid model. Across categories, the school aligns with core classical principles (truth, goodness, beauty; virtue formation; Great Books and history integration; the grammar–logic–rhetoric axis; faculty as mentors; parent partnership). The aggregate ranking is CLASSICAL, with a notable emphasis on formation, Great Books, and liturgical/community practices. The Pedagogy category shows some classical elements, but explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy is not overt in public materials, placing Category 4 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical. No category signals undermine classical aims; the overall profile remains firmly classical.

- Executive takeaway: Veritas NGA clearly embodies core classical features (catechetical grounding in scripture; Great Books; liberal-arts structure; formation and virtue emphasis; strong faculty-student mentorship; parent partnership). The principal area for needle-moving improvement would be to make explicit, high-school–level Socratic/seminar practice and explicit scholé-oriented pedagogy in public-facing materials to solidify the upper-school classical pedagogy claim.

- What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases): explicitly articulate Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades; increase explicit memory-work/chanting traditions (Scholé) in lower grades; foreground “Great Books” conversations and model dialogues in daily practice; strengthen evidence of contemplative/restful learning days in curriculum descriptions.

Evidence for these conclusions is drawn from Veritas NGA’s public materials (e.g., gospel-centered, biblical formation; Great Books; grammar/logic/rhetoric; liturgical/community practices; parental partnership) and cross-referenced with the classical education rubric and keyword indicators from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School documents.

Citations and direct textual anchors from the school materials appear throughout the category-by-category sections below to ground each finding.

- Core public statements establishing classically framed, faith-informed aims: " Rooted in biblical principles, we provide a classical Christian education in the American tradition" and "pursuing gospel-centered, classical Christian education" . 
- Scriptural and liturgical emphasis (Word of God, Scripture, chapel): "Here, your children will study God's Word, explore essential doctrines of the faith, and cultivate moral virtue through the lens of our Christian worldview" and "Through chapel, projects, and discipleship"  .
- Great Books and Great Conversation; liberal-arts emphasis: "the Great Books that have shaped Western civilization" and "grammar, logic, and rhetoric" and "classical history, literature, theology, and apologetics"  .
- Formation and virtue language; partnership with families and parents: "formation of virtue," "cultivate moral virtue," "partner with families," and "parents… primary God-ordained educators"   .

### Category 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The program emphasizes formation through faith and reason and uses a classical framework in pedagogy (grammar/logic/rhetoric; great books; history integration).  
- Public materials mention mentoring relationships and a community-centered approach, with chapel, projects, and discipleship as modalities. 
- However, explicit, public-facing statements about Socratic seminars, disciplined use of memory work, or formal Scholé days (contemplative/restful learning) are not clearly stated in the public pages. The Great Books/Conversations are referenced, but explicit Socratic seminar pedagogy or the full range of classical pedagogical methods are not uniformly articulated in the public content. This reduces the category from fully CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Negative indicators: explicit mentions of Socratic seminars, ritualized memorization/recitation, and explicit scholé/restful days are not clearly present; project-based learning is present but not described as dominant, and explicit rest/Scholé is not foregrounded.

Executive synthesis
- Veritas NGA exhibits a robust classical education profile rooted in biblical faith, the Great Books tradition, liberal-arts curriculum, and a mentorship-driven faculty model. The main gap is explicit, public-facing articulation of Socratic/seminar pedagogy and Scholé/restful learning practices in upper grades, which places Pedagogy as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical. The rest of the framework—curriculum, faculty, parental partnership, and religious foundation—strongly supports a Classical evaluation.

What Would Move the Needle
- For Category 4 (Pedagogy) to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL:
  - Publicly articulate explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy for upper grades (discussion-based inquiry, dialectic, etc.).
  - Highlight structured memory work, recitation, chanting, or other Scholé-like practices in lower grades.
  - Show concrete, day-by-day articulation of how Great Books are integrated into Socratic dialogue and how disciplines interweave in a truly integrated, dialogic classroom.
  - Emphasize contemplative/restful learning days as a regular component of the schedule, linking to the Scholé tradition.
