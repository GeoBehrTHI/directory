## Executive Summary

Faustina Academy TX 75061 appears publicly as a Classical, Christian school with a Charlotte Mason influence and a strong emphasis on inclusion and individualized supports (notably dyslexia remediation). The website content emphasizes formation of character and the moral/spiritual dimensions of education, rather than focusing exclusively on career-readiness or standardized metrics. While the school foregrounds classical language (classical tradition) and Christian worldview, explicit references to core classical elements such as Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, or formal Socratic pedagogy are less prominent in the public material. On balance, the school lands in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category across several criteria, with stronger alignment in some categories (notably Industry-standards in Family/Community engagement and clear religious/theological orientation) and partial alignment in others (notably explicit curriculum specifics such as Trivium/Quadrivium and extensive Great Books practice).

Key insights:
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are evident (Christian worldview, Scriptures at the center), supporting Element 1 at the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL level.
- The school’s stated purpose foregrounds formation, virtue, and a classical framework, placing Element 2 as CLASSICAL with caveats about explicit Great Books emphasis.
- Curriculum and pedagogy show classical commitments but lack explicit, widespread evidence of the full Great Books tradition or Trivium/Quadrivium focus in public materials, yielding CATEGORY 3 and CATEGORY 4 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty communicate mentorship and mission-aligned teaching, but public materials do not clearly articulate a formal “portrait of the teacher” living-curriculum concept, leading to CATEGORY 5 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Parent/Community partnerships are clearly emphasized (parents as co-teachers, inclusion, open access to information and involvement), resulting in CATEGORY 6 as CLASSICAL.
- Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with concrete opportunities to strengthen alignment in curriculum specifics, pedagogy, and explicit liberal-arts framing.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Explicitly articulate liturgical or communal practices (e.g., chapel/morning prayer formats, liturgical calendar, sacred music) and clearly label how virtues and transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) guide daily curriculum decisions. This would move Element 1 toward CLASSICAL by strengthening liturgical/connective practices and explicit metaphysical grounding.

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate the core classical curriculum components, including Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and if applicable Quadrivium components), Great Books or primary texts, and clear integration of history within the curriculum (not merely as a separate subject). This would raise Element 3 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL by aligning with canonical classical curricular standards.

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Introduce and describe explicit use of Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, structured seminars or debates, and a clear pathway to rhetoric training (speeches, declamation). Also publish a clear statement on Scholé/restful elements and contemplative routines to illustrate balance between rest and rigor.

- Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a formal “portrait of a teacher” and a living-curriculum framework showing how teachers serve as the core of the school’s classical formation. Include teacher professional development grounded in classical pedagogy and ongoing faculty seminars on Great Books or classical methodology.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand parent-formation initiatives (book studies, classical education seminars, family liturgies) and establish a structured open-visit policy with a visible calendar of parent-partner activities to deepen the parent-school partnership.

- Across categories: Create a consolidated, public-facing articulation of the school’s classical philosophy with explicit references to traditional classical terms (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Scholé, Socratic dialogue) while maintaining the Charlotte Mason elements and inclusion commitments. This would enhance coherence across the six categories and align the school more fully with the classical rubric.
