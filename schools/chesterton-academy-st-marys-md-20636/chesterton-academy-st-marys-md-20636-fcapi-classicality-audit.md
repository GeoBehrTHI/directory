## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Mary’s (Maryland) presents a robust, nearly fully aligned Classical Catholic curriculum and pedagogy. Public materials portray a mission centered on forming virtuous, thoughtful young people through a classical liberal arts framework, explicit Catholic theology, the Socratic method, and a culture of life. The school emphasizes a three-pillar model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality), integrates liturgical life (daily Mass, retreats) with rigorous study across literature, philosophy, history, theology, languages, and the arts, and foregrounds parental involvement and a house-system culture. Taken together, these elements indicate a CLASSICAL orientation across all evaluated categories, with strong evidence of formation, Great-Books–adjacent study, Socratic pedagogy, and intentional community practices.

Key public signals include: a mission to form joyful leaders and saints within a Catholic, classical framework; daily Mass and retreats; integration of Catholic theology across disciplines; a three-pillar approach to formation; a Socratic, integrated humanities curriculum; a robust Fine Arts program including choral singing, speeches, and debate; and active family/parent engagement through open houses and events. These indicators line up with classical-education lexicon such as the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty; formation over information; Great Books/Great Conversation notions; and the governance of a living curriculum through dedicated faculty and community practices. All six categories thus trend toward CLASSICAL. See category-by-category details below for the explicit keyword signals, supporting quotes, and the analytic rationale.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Negative keywords present: none observed in public materials (no explicit secular-relativism or values-clarification signals).

Calculation note: Positive signals (e.g., Catholic faith; theology; liturgy; virtue; wonder; sacredness) clearly outweigh any non-classical signals; no negative indicators detected in the public materials.

Why this rating

The explicit integration of theology across subjects, worship life, and a “culture of life” orientation aligns strongly with the Classical School’s Religious and Metaphysical Foundations criteria. The presence of the threefold framing (truth, goodness, beauty) and liturgical life supports a CLASSICAL assessment here (see references above).

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school explicitly follows the Socratic method of teaching (turn0file0). 
- The Humanities Program notes use of integrated curricula with a raised focus on the Socratic Method (turn0file4).
- Lower- and upper-school pedagogy references appear: lower-school activities (singing, memory work, chanting) and upper-school Socratic discussions; opportunities for rhetoric development and public speaking (turn0file7, turn0file4).
- A Restful-Scholé dimension is present in classical pedagogy discussions in the knowledge base lexicon; the Chesterton materials show a combination of contemplative/sequence-based pedagogy (memory work, recitation, chant) and Socratic seminars, debates, or rhetoric training (turn0file5; turn0file18).

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue/seminar, dialectic, questioning, memorization/recitation/chanting,Scholé (restful learning), formation through imitation and practice, rhetoric, debate, civil discourse, guided discussion.
- Negative keywords present: absence of negative signals such as exclusive reliance on experiential/project-based learning as the sole method; no explicit “constructivist-only” or non-traditional pedagogy signals in public materials.

Calculation note: The public materials demonstrate a predominant classical pedagogy signature, with strong signals for Socratic inquiry and memory-based learning, plus contemplative/restful elements.

Why this rating
- Pedagogical methods are clearly aligned with classical standards: Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, memorization/chanting in lower grades, and a rhetoric/eloquence emphasis in upper grades; contemplative/restful learning (Scholé) is also indicated. Taken together, these signals support CLASSICAL for Element 4.
