## Executive Summary

Saint Paul Catholic Classical School (SPCCS) in Richardson, TX presents a robust, explicitly Catholic classical program embedded in a long-standing parish community. Public materials from SPCCS consistently foreground Christ at the center of learning, a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), engagement with great books, Latin study, and liturgical life (Mass, adoration, catechesis). The school emphasizes formation (virtue, wisdom) over mere information, and positions parents as partners and co-educators within a living, mentorship-driven faculty culture. Across Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parent-Community dimensions, SPCCS demonstrates strong alignment with classical education tenets, particularly its theological grounding, teleological aims, liberal-arts curriculum, and Socratic-inviting pedagogy (memorizations, recitations, and thoughtful discussion). The overall assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key themes across the six categories
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Catholic identity is woven into all study; theology, liturgy, and sacramental life (Mass, Adoration, Baltimore Catechism) are integral to daily life and study. Evidence: statements about Christ as Logos; Mass and adoration; Catholic formation across subjects. This aligns with the Positive Keywords around truth, goodness, beauty, liturgy, sacred worship, and a sacramental worldview. 
- Purpose and Goal: Education is formation in virtue and wisdom, anchored in faith; pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty is explicit; the school emphasizes contemplation (scholé) and a classical telos rather than job-focused outcomes. Evidence: “formation,” “faith, wisdom, and virtue,” and the Catholic classical mission. 
- Curriculum: Liberal arts emphasis (grammar, logic, rhetoric; great books; Latin; history and Western civilization; classical humanities) with integrated religious content; explicit Great Books/primary-sources orientation and a curriculum that includes Latin and sacred arts. Evidence: Trivium description and classical curriculum; Great Books/literature focus; Latin and Western history. 
- Pedagogy: A blend of contemplative/restful elements (Scholé, memorization/recitation, liturgical integration) and active discourse (Socratic-like discussion and memory work). Evidence: recitation/memorization; emphasis on discussion and memory; occasional explicit reference to “restful learning” and “Scholé.” 
- Faculty: Faculty described as dedicated, long-tenured, highly qualified; living curriculum; strong partnership with families and parish. Evidence: School Profile & Facts notes tenure and credentialing; “living curriculum” and parental partnership language appear in admissions and academics materials. 
- Parents & Community: Explicit partnership with parents as primary educators; open-house and visitation culture; parent formation and community activities; strong parish-school alignment. Evidence: “parents are the primary educators,” opportunities for involvement, and regular open houses/tours.
