## Executive Summary

Waterloo School Austin (Waterloo School 78704) presents a markedly project- and relationship-centered approach to education, with strong emphasis on real-world learning, student agency, and narrative-driven assessments (e.g., Presentations of Learning, POLs). The public materials repeatedly foreground project-based learning, design thinking, and a portrait-of-a-graduate framework focused on communication, collaboration, and personal growth. While the school employs some elements that resonate with classical education—such as rhetoric in project work and a clear emphasis on mastery through deep student work—the curriculum and pedagogy do not consistently align with the explicit liberal-arts framework of traditional classical education (e.g., no consistent evidence of grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, no explicit Great Books or great-conversation commitments across grade levels, limited liturgical/theological programming in public messaging). The faculty structure shows a strong model-teacher orientation and a living-curriculum ethos, which aligns with classical norms in spirit, even if the outward curriculum is contemporary and project-driven. Community and parental engagement appear, with outreach through admissions, parent involvement, and fundraising (Big Cheese campaign), though the explicit “co-teacher” partnership language common in classical rhetoric is less clearly articulated in the public materials.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale: The school demonstrates several hallmark elements of classical education (notably a strong living-faculty model, a commitment to rhetoric through project-based public work, and explicit attention to character formation in a structured graduate portrait). However, its curricular content (project-based learning, design thinking, college-advising transcripts, and emphasis on real-world projects) diverges from the canonical classical framework (grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, Great Books, and explicit liturgy/theology integration). The strongest single driver toward classical is its evident emphasis on virtuous character and the cultivation of intellect through deliberate, time-tested modes of discourse (e.g., rhetoric in practice). The aggregate pattern—balanced toward modern pedagogy with classical undercurrents—places Waterloo at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle toward CLASSICAL: a formal articulation and implementation of Great Books and a formal Trivium progression (grammar-logic-rhetoric) across grades, explicit incorporation of liberal-arts history (Western civilization) and Latin/Greek offerings, a liturgical/theology integration in a clearly defined and public-facing way, and documented Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across upper grades with clear evidence of the “Socratic” culture in classes beyond rhetoric-rich public showcases.

# ABSTRACT

Waterloo School Austin’s public-facing content demonstrates a formation-oriented, project-centered educational approach that strongly emphasizes real-world learning, character development, and a graduate-portrait framework. The school’s pedagogy integrates project-based learning, rhetoric in student projects, and presentations of learning while preserving a robust faculty-led, mentor-centered ethos. These features align with classical ideals in spirit, particularly the living-curriculum faculty model and the focus on virtue and intellectual formation. However, the curricular structure does not present a sustained, canonical classical curriculum (e.g., Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, or a universal liberal-arts spine), and liturgical/theological foundations are not a public-facing pillar of the school’s program. Taken as a whole, Waterloo School Austin sits in the realm of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with significant strengths in the faculty-mentored, formation-forward approach and meaningful classical elements (rhetoric, public discourse, and a portrait of the graduate) but lacking full canonical-classical curriculum and explicit religious-metaphysical infrastructure across all grade levels.
