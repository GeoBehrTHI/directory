## ABSTRACT

Waterloo School Austin demonstrates a nuanced CLASSICAL orientation with strong CLASSICAL signals in Faculty and Parents/Community while showing more modest alignment in Curriculum and Pedagogy, and partial alignment in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and Purpose/Goal. The school’s public materials emphasize formation, virtue, and a living, mentorship-focused faculty, as well as student-driven, project-based learning and interdisciplinary inquiry. This combination yields significant CLASSICAL elements (faculty as living curriculum; strong parental/community engagement) but leaves important classical markers underrepresented (e.g., Great Books, Trivium-based curriculum, sustained Socratic pedagogy). Consequently, the overall profile is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with two explicit CLASSICAL elements (Faculty; Parents & Community) and several areas that show partial or non-classical signals (Curriculum, Pedagogy), and limited but present religious-metaphysical signals. Waterloo School Austin earns a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation, with clear opportunities to “move the needle” toward full CLASSICAL status by increasing explicit classical curriculum components, reinforcing Socratic pedagogy, and integrating more explicit liturgical/community practices in its public-facing materials.
