## Executive Summary

Providence School Santa Barbara (CA 93103) exhibits strong religious and formative aims that align with several core elements of classical education, especially its explicit theology-grounded mission, emphasis on virtue and wonder, and renders of intellectual discipline (habits of the mind) through programs like the Senior Thesis Project and a dedicated emphasis on rhetoric. However, explicit Classical curricula elements such as the traditional liberal arts progression (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the sustained use of Great Books/Great Conversation as a curricular spine are not clearly articulated in the publicly available site materials. Based on the category-by-category keyword analysis using the Six Essential Elements framework, Providence School falls between Somewhat Classical and Classical in spots, but overall trends place it in the Somewhat Classical range with clear classical-like strengths in religious/philosophical foundations and formation, and partial alignment in pedagogy and curriculum. The aggregate assessment is: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- Strengths: Clear religious/metaphysical grounding (biblical/theological framing), emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and the “habits of the mind,” and evidence of rhetoric-focused experiences (Recess & Rhetoric; Senior Thesis) that echo classical aims. The mission and motto emphasize truth, beauty, and excellence, with a Christ-centered orientation. These are well-aligned with classical aims in several respects, particularly Element 1 and Element 2. Evidence: Our Mission and Motto; Christian Education Philosophy; emphasis on formation and habits of mind; upper-school rhetoric/trial-by-speech style elements. See sources turn0file5.
- Gaps: The explicit curricular spine (grammar/logic/rhetoric trio), sustained Great Books/Great Conversation orientation, and a clearly articulated, history-saturated curriculum are not clearly present in the public materials. This tempers the overall classical classification toward Somewhat Classical. Evidence: Curriculum disclosures largely emphasize formation, spiritual aims, and rhetoric at the upper levels without detailing grammar-logic-rhetoric progression or explicit Great Books programs. See turn0file11.
- Community/family engagement is present, but explicit public statements about parents as co-teachers or structured family formation programs are less explicit, landing Element 6 in the Somewhat Classical range. Evidence: family-oriented language and openness to community involvement appear, but explicit parent-education or co-teaching language is not strongly evidenced. See turn0file11.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Make explicit the classical curriculum spine: clearly articulate grammar, logic, and rhetoric as the core sequence, and specify the Great Books/Great Conversation component as the curricular backbone (category 3).
- Publicly detail the role of Great Books and history as central to the curriculum; show cross-disciplinary integration of historical study with moral/theological reflection (category 3).
- Expand explicit pedagogy statements: explicitly describe Socratic discussion, seminars, and debates as a routine practice in appropriate grade bands, with evidence of student rhetoric outcomes (category 4).
- Clarify faculty as the “living curriculum”: publish a formal profile of the teacher-formation model, including how faculty mentorship and virtues are integrated into daily teaching (category 5).
- Make parental partnership explicit: codify and communicate a structured parent-education/co-teaching framework (category 6).
- Continue weaving theology into the daily curriculum with explicit liturgical or communal practices (category 1), including regular communal religious observances or liturgical practices if aligned with the school’s ethos (category 1).
