## Executive Summary

Tri-Cities Christian Academy (TCA) in Blountville, TN, operating within the Lakeway Christian Schools system, demonstrates substantive alignment with classical education in several of the Six Essential Elements rubric’s categories, particularly Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The school presents a robust Christ-centered, biblically integrated program, a formation-oriented mission, and a parental-partner model. Its curriculum includes biblical integration across subjects and some classical elements (e.g., Latin offerings; structured history sequencing), but it does not consistently foreground explicit Great Books, Great Conversation, or a full Seven Liberal Arts framework. Pedagogy and contemplative practices are mentioned in the broader classical lexicon, yet explicit Socratic seminars, scholé days, or a formal articulation of grammar–logic–rhetoric within a classical pedagogy are not consistently documented in public materials. The faculty portrayal emphasizes challenging, yet supportive teaching, and there is strong evidence of parent involvement and community partnership.

Overall assessment: CLASSICAL

Key insights:

- Strong religious/metaphysical grounding: chapel, biblical worldview, statements of faith, Christ-centered education.
- Formation-focused purpose: emphasis on wisdom, character, virtue, and leadership aligned with a biblical telos.
- Curriculum includes biblical integration and classical touchpoints (Latin, historiography sequencing); not explicit Great Books or universal “Great Conversation.”
- Pedagogy lacks explicit, public, high-fidelity articulation of Socratic seminars or scholé-based daily rhythm in the public materials, though there is evidence of a value system that prizes deep thinking and memory work in other classical sources.
- Faculty is portrayed as a living curriculum with intentional formation; parents and community are deeply engaged as co-educators and partners.

### Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The public materials emphasize a mix of engagement types, including chapel/memory-based experiences in lower grades (e.g., singing, liturgical framing) and upper-school opportunities for analysis and rhetoric in some Christian-school networks, but explicit statements about Socratic seminars, scholé days, or a formal emphasis on grammar–logic–rhetoric pedagogy are not clearly documented for TCA in public content. The presence of a strong teacher-student relationship and “living curriculum” language appears in some school communications, but direct references to classical pedagogical methods (e.g., Socratic dialogue, memory work, chanting) are not clearly documented in the Tri-Cities materials.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive indicators: Restful learning, memory/recitation concepts appear in the classical lexicon; limited direct evidence of Socratic seminars; some emphasis on disciplined, thoughtful discussion in upper grades exists in the broader network materials, but not explicitly in TCA pages.
- Negative indicators: No explicit, public articulation of Socratic seminars, scholé days, or explicit grammar–logic–rhetoric pedagogy for TCA; absence of clearly stated “Socratic” or “Scholé” terms in public TCA materials.

Why this rating

- The school demonstrates formation-oriented, honors-level pedagogy at times (AP/DE, honors, Latin), and some elements of contemplative and rhetorical development exist in the network, but explicit, canonical classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, scholé days, memorization as a core strategy) is not clearly documented in public content. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Citations: Evidence of public materials showing a blended pedagogy rather than explicit classical methods; no direct Socratic/seminar language found in the cited pages.

What would move the needle

- If the school seeks to move toward full CLASSICAL in Pedagogy (Category 4), it could publicly articulate and implement explicit classical teaching methods, including:
    - Adoption or clear articulation of a formal Socratic seminar framework and Paideia-style seminars for upper grades, with explicit training and teacher guides.
    - Integration of scholé days, contemplative routines, and structured memory work (recitation, chanting) as central pedagogical practices, with publicly available examples and rubrics.
    - Explicit mapping to grammar, logic, and rhetoric across the curriculum, with a public plan for integrating Aristotelian/Scholastic reasoning in core subjects.
    - Documentation of a unified classical sequence (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across K–12, with examples of student work (narration, essays, orations) tied to a Great Books/Great Conversation approach.

- To strengthen Curriculum (Category 3) toward CLASSICAL:
    - Publicly articulate a Great Books program or Great Conversation framework, including a curated list of foundational texts across grade bands and a plan for integration into history, literature, philosophy, and theology.
    - More explicit emphasis on the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and the Quadrivium) as structural pillars within the curriculum, including how they are taught and assessed.
    - A clear statement about the use of classical languages beyond Latin (e.g., Greek) or formal engagement with primary historical/philosophical texts to connect with timeless questions and wonder.

- To reinforce Religious Foundations (Category 1) and Purpose/Goal (Category 2):
    - Explicitly connect the Great Conversation and classical aims with the school’s spiritual formation and community worship rhythms, demonstrating how wonder, beauty, and virtue are experienced through liturgical practices alongside classical inquiry.
    - Provide a public articulation of how “truth, goodness, beauty” are pursued across disciplines beyond theological content, including examples of cross-disciplinary integration.
