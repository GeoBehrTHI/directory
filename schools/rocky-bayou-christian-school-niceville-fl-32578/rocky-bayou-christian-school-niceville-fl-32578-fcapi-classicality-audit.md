## Executive Summary

Rocky Bayou Christian School (Niceville, FL 32578) presents a clearly Christian, formation-focused educational ethos with strong emphasis on parental partnership and a shared mission to cultivate a biblical worldview, Christian character, and spiritual formation in students. The public materials consistently foreground faith, worship, and the integration of theology with daily learning. While the school demonstrates classical-inflected commitments (formation, virtue, truth as a transcendent good, reverence for the sacred, and a living-teacher model), its explicit articulation of canonical classical elements such as a grammar-logic-rhetoric Trivium, Great Books, or a heavy emphasis on Socratic pedagogy is less overt in the available materials. On balance, RBCS aligns with a Classical orientation in meaningful ways (true formation, virtue, theology, liturgical community, strong faculty/community emphasis) but does not consistently foreground the full canonical classical curriculum and pedagogy in public-facing materials. Thus, the aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL, with a notable potential to strengthen explicit classical-curriculum and classical-pedagogy signals to achieve a fuller Classical profile.

Key insights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are front-and-center: explicit biblical worldview, theology, catechetical content, and liturgical community (chapel, worship) are repeatedly referenced. This strongly supports Element 1 as CLASSICAL. Evidence: Mission & Vision statements and chapel/worship references.  
- School purpose aligns with formation, virtue, and the pursuit of truth as real and knowable through a biblical lens, supported by mission statements and values statements. Element 2 is CLASSICAL. 
- Curriculum signals center on Christian worldview across courses, with explicit “Biblical Worldview” in pedagogy; however, explicit canonical-classical curriculum signals (Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric) are not overtly presented in public content. Element 3 leans toward NOT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical, with some alignment toward Somewhat Classical given the formation emphasis. 
- Pedagogy appears to mix contemplative, memory-based elements (singing, memory work in lower grades) with more advanced, discussion-oriented practices in upper levels, but without explicit, uniform Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across grades. Element 4 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Faculty is described as virtuous, mentor-like, and living curriculum—strong Classical signals in Element 5. 
- Parents & community are positioned as co-educators and partners with active family formation opportunities, strongly supporting Element 6.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Make explicit the canonical classical curriculum signals in public materials, including:
  - A visible, school-wide emphasis on grammar, logic, and rhetoric (the Trivium) across grade levels, with documented syllabi and assessment aligned to the Trivium stages.
  - Public articulation of Great Books and Great Conversation commitments, including sample reading lists and integrated discussions across the humanities, as well as Latin/Greek or classical language options where relevant.
  - Clear demonstration of a history-intensive curriculum that foregrounds Western civilization, classical canon, and primary sources.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Expand explicit classical pedagogical practices by:
  - Publishing a formal description of Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars as a core upper-school method, with teacher training and assessment rubrics.
  - Increasing explicit emphasis on disciplined memory work, recitation, and oratory across more grades, with performance-based assessments and public speaking opportunities.
  - Documenting how scholé (restful, contemplative learning) is practiced on a formal schedule and how it complements rigorous inquiry.
- For Element 2 (School Purpose): While formation is strong, a more explicit articulation of the classical telos and how truth, goodness, and beauty guide daily decision-making and curriculum could strengthen overall alignment.
- For Element 1 and Element 5–6: Maintain and deepen the existing strengths in liturgical/community life, theology, and parental partnership, ensuring continued alignment with classical aims and outcomes.
