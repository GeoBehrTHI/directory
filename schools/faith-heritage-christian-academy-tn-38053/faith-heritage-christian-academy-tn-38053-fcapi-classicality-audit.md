## Executive Summary

Faith Heritage Christian Academy (FHCA) in Millington, Tennessee appears as a faith-centered institution with explicit biblical formation as a core aim. The school explicitly states a Biblical worldview as part of its mission and includes Bible coursework within its University Prep Diploma, signaling a grounding in theology and religious formation. However, the publicly visible curriculum and pedagogy do not clearly map to a full classical liberal-arts structure (e.g., explicit Great Books study, Latin, or a traditional Trivium progression), and there is no consistent, publicly documented emphasis on classical teaching methods such as Socratic seminars, memory work, or scholé cycles across all grade levels. Taken together across the six categories, FHCA demonstrates some classical elements (notably religious/metaphysical framing and formation-oriented goals) but lacks several core classical-defining practices in curriculum and pedagogy.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Element 3 (Curriculum): NOT CLASSICAL

  - Bible study is embedded in the curriculum (Bible I–IV) as part of the graduation requirements, indicating theology/Scripture is integrated into academic work. 
  - There are public links to a faith community (Faith Heritage Baptist Church) connected to the school site, reinforcing liturgical/communal religious life as part of the school ecosystem. 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis

  - Negative indicators: none evident in the public materials.
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive keywords dominate (roughly multiple occurrences of “Biblical worldview,” “Bible,” “Christ-Centered,” and God-related language with no clear secularing signals). Positive > Negative by a wide margin (approximately 4+ positive cues vs 0 negative cues in the visible material for this category).

- Why this rating
  - The school foregrounds theological formation and sacred aims (biblical worldview, Christ-centered education) and embeds Bible study in the core curriculum, aligning strongly with the “Transcendental moral realism,” “Grounded in Theology,” and “Liturgical/Communal Practices” cues in classical criteria, even as explicit liturgical routines are not extensively advertised on site. This supports CLASSICAL in this category, per the Six Essentials lexicon.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

  - Graduation and diploma structure (University Prep Diploma) indicates a college-preparatory orientation, with a structured curriculum and tests (ACT/SAT) tied to college admission. This is a modern, information-rich preparation framework rather than a purely formation-centered aim. 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive indicators present: formation-oriented language (excellence in all areas of life), telos-language implicit in forming character toward a moral/biblical ends.
  - Negative indicators: explicit classical “Great Conversation” aims or explicit articulation of the liberal-arts telos (truth, goodness, beauty as real, knowable) is not stated; focus leans toward formation with a modern diploma track.
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive cues present but not strongly anchored in explicit classical vocabulary; roughly one to a few formation-focused phrases versus a lack of explicit classical telos language; overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Why this rating

The presence of a formal college-prep track tempers the strictly classical framing.

This signals a traditional college-prep curriculum rather than a classical curriculum staged around grammar–logic–rhetoric progression and the Great Books. Bible requirements are explicit, but there is no explicit Great Books program or “Great Conversation” framing on the public site. 
  - There is mention of Monarch Online Curriculum (external program) but not a public, school-wide classical core (e.g., Trivium emphasis, Latin, or a formal Great Books sequence). 

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Why this rating

## What Would Move the Needle

- Adopt explicit Great Books and liberal-arts programming: publicly articulate a Great Books curriculum and a modern Trivium progression (grammar-logic-rhetoric) across grades, along with Latin or classical language study, and a confirmed sequence of history anchored in Western civilization. This would shift Curriculum (Element 3) from NOT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
- Signal classical pedagogy publicly: describe and implement Socratic seminars, scholé cycles, memory work, recitation, and disciplined rhetoric across upper grades; publish examples of seminars or seminars-based assessment to move Pedagogy (Element 4) toward CLASSICAL.
- Develop a formal “living curriculum” statement for faculty: publicly describe how the faculty exemplifies virtue and serves as the living curriculum, including teacher formation in classical pedagogy, and a teacher-portrait program. This would strengthen Faculty (Element 5) to CLASSICAL.
- Strengthen and publicize a formal partnership with families: publish explicit language and programs that designate parents as co-educators (parent formation, home-school partnership, family-liturgy alignment), and provide structured parent book clubs or study guides. This would move Parents & Community (Element 6) toward CLASSICAL.
- Elevate explicit theological integration in all disciplines: articulate how theology informs each disciplinary approach, including explicit integration of sacred tradition, eternal questions, and branded “great conversation” elements across the curriculum. This would further reinforce Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1) toward CLASSICAL.
