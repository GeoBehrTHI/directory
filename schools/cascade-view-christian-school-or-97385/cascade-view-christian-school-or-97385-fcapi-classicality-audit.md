# Classicality Audit Report: Austin Classical School

Website audited: https://austinclassical.org/ 

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Austin Classical School (Austin, TX) presents a robust classical, Christian identity centered on cultivating wisdom and virtue through the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The site makes explicit use of the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Latin, great books/great conversation, memory work (songs/recitation), and discussion/debate culminating in thesis defense. It also foregrounds its Christian telos (“glorify Jesus Christ”) and theological integration (“under the sovereignty of God through theology”). Evidence for faculty formation/portrait is thinner than other areas, but mission alignment with classical pedagogy is strong across the board.   

Key strengths
- Clear, repeated emphasis on wisdom, virtue, and the transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty). 
- Concrete Trivium implementation (Latin, logic, rhetoric) and “great books/great conversation” framing. 
- Lower-school songs/memorization; logic-stage debate/discussion; rhetoric-stage thesis defense/eloquence. 

Area to strengthen
- Make faculty formation explicit (e.g., a “Portrait of a Teacher,” stated mentorship model, and ongoing classical pedagogy training) to fully match classical faculty ideals in the Six Elements framework. 

Note on rubric and method: The keyword analysis and category ratings below follow the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” positive/negative indicators for each category (religious foundations, purpose/goal, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, parents/community). Each section lists keywords found in the school’s materials, compares them to the framework lexicon, quantifies positive vs. negative hits, and assigns a rating.

## Aggregate Assessment
- Category ratings: C1 CLASSICAL; C2 CLASSICAL; C3 CLASSICAL; C4 CLASSICAL; C5 SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL; C6 CLASSICAL.
- Overall: CLASSICAL

Rationale: The site explicitly embraces the classical-Christian telos (truth, goodness, beauty; virtue and wisdom), implements the Trivium with Latin/logic/rhetoric, uses memory work and Socratic discussion culminating in thesis defense, and partners with families. Faculty ideals are implied but would benefit from a more explicit classical faculty portrait and formation program.   

What would move the needle (Faculty)
- Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate.”
- Describe faculty formation (e.g., great books seminars, classical pedagogy training, mentorship/collegial culture).
- Name teachers explicitly as mentors/models of virtue and “the living curriculum,” aligned to classical virtue ethics.
