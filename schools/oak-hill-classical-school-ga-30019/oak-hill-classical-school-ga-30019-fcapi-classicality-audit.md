## Executive Summary

Oak Hill Classical School (Dacula, GA 30019) presents a robust classical Christian program anchored in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integration with a biblical worldview, and an emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and beauty. Public materials from the school’s website repeatedly foreground formation of character and formation in faith, a history-centered curriculum that includes Great Books and Western Civilization, and pedagogical practices that blend contemplative/memory tasks (Scholé) with Socratic discussion and rhetoric work. The school is explicitly affiliated with ACCS and frames its mission around wisdom, joyful learning, and love for God and others. Across six standard classical criteria, Oak Hill demonstrates strong alignment with classical education principles, with no clear signals of anti-classical tendencies in the visible public materials. Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Executive impressions by category, with keyword-based analysis and rubric-aligned scoring, are summarized below and followed by the detailed category-by-category sections. - Public-facing materials emphasize worship through music and a liturgical sensibility in communal practice; references to God as the integrating principle of understanding life and learning.

Much of this language appears in mission, philosophy, and curriculum descriptions, as well as in thematic sections on music and worship. Why this rating

Citations: Mission/Philosophy statements; ACCS membership and faith alignment; worship/music and sacred-context language.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- Lower-school practices: singing and chanting, memory work, and other contemplative/reminiscent pedagogy appear in the nature of “Scholé” and memory-deepening activities (The Body class and related sub-creator coursework emphasize musical and mnemonic approaches). 
- Upper-school pedagogy emphasizes Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training. The Rhetoric School materials describe logic, rhetoric, and a mechanism for public speaking and writing, consistent with Socratic/seminar pedagogy and rhetoric practice. 
- The public materials also show an intentional blend of contemplative, restful, and rigorous elements (as per classical pedagogy guidelines): “The lower school often engages in singing and chanting; upper school emphasizes Socratic discussion and rhetoric.” The existence of both modes is a hallmark of classical pedagogy. 
- The school’s rhetoric program includes opportunities for speeches and rhetorical training, aligning with classical pedagogy expectations. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present (sample): Socratic, seminars, debate, rhetoric, memory work, narration, chanting, scholé, contemplation, wonder, seminar, tutorial, Paideia-style elements, public speaking, classical education. The public content shows explicit mention of these methods in upper and lower grades, which maps to the Six Essentials pedagogy signals. 
- Negative keywords present: The content does not emphasize exclusive project-based/experiential-only approaches or student-directed discovery as the sole method; rather, it presents a balanced mix with explicit Socratic/rhetoric and contemplative elements. The negative signals in the lexicon (e.g., “experiential learning first” or “constructivist-only”) do not appear as dominant in OHCS pages. 

Why this rating
- The pedagogy clearly reflects classical methods: memory-focused lower-school rites and chant-like activities; Socratic seminars and rhetoric training in upper grades; structured integration of Great Books and liberal-arts pedagogy; emphasis on disciplined dialogue and public speaking. This combination supports CLASSICAL.

Citations: Lower- and upper-school pedagogy language; Socratic/seminar references; rhetoric and public speaking emphasis.
