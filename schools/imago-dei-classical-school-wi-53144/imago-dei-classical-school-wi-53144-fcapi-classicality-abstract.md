## ABSTRACT

Imago Dei Classical School Wisconsin 53144 exhibits a robust CLASSICAL orientation across all six classical categories. The religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit through biblical wisdom, virtue, truth, goodness, beauty, and a liturgical, catechetical framework anchored in Christian doctrine and the Imago Dei framework. The school’s purpose centers on formation, virtue, and wisdom—articulated as a study of the true, good, and beautiful—rather than mere information or job preparation, aligning with classical telos. The curriculum centers on the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium, integrated with Great Books and the Great Conversation, and presented as Christian enculturation. Pedagogy emphasizes memory work, recitation, chorale/memory chants, Scholé, and Socratic discourse in upper grades, with a strong emphasis on formation and imitation (mimetic learning). Faculty are portrayed as living exemplars and mentors—the living curriculum—within a collegial, faith-centered culture. Parents and community involvement is explicit, with parents positioned as co-teachers, opportunities for formation, and open invitations to engage with the school.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Evidence includes biblical/theological grounding, liturgical practices, and a Christ-centered education (imago Dei framing, Missio Dei Fellowship affiliation). Positive keyword signals (truth, goodness, beauty; theology; liturgy; imago Dei) dominate with no clear negative signals. 
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Formation and virtue-centered aims with Great Books/Great Conversation; teleological orientation toward wisdom and beauty. 
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Liberal arts focus, Great Books, historical integration, and a formation-first approach to history as humane science; emphasis on Great Conversation and Western civilization. 
- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL. Memorization/recitation, chant, liturgical memory, Scholé, and Socratic dialogues in upper grades; grammar-stage memory practices; mimetic learning. 
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. Model teachers; living curriculum; portrait of teachers; collegial culture; visible faculty bios. 
- Parents and Community: CLASSICAL. Parental partnership; co-teacher model; parent formation; open visits; church-affiliated formation.

- The school demonstrates a consistent CLASSICAL orientation across all six categories, with textual evidence of a theologically grounded, formation-centered, Great-Books-rich curriculum; a pedagogy that fuses memory work, contemplative learning, and Socratic discourse; a faculty model that embodies the classical teacher archetype; and engaged parents and community aligned with a confessional, church-centered educational mission.
