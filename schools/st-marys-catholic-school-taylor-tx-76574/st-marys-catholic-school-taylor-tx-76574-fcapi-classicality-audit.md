## Executive Summary

St. Mary’s Catholic School in Taylor, TX (76574) demonstrates strong alignment with classical education principles, especially in its Catholic identity, Trivium-centered curriculum, and adoption of Socratic and rhetoric-oriented pedagogy at the upper levels. Public materials emphasize a Catholic liberal arts framework, a Trivium-based curriculum, integration of faith across disciplines, and a community-centered approach that explicitly partners with families. Collectively, these attributes map well to a CLASSICAL ranking across most dimensions, with particular strength in religious foundations, curriculum design, and pedagogical methods. Some elements—such as the explicit articulation of a single, unified “living curriculum” model for faculty—are less directly stated on the site but are suggested by faculty credentials, long-standing church-affiliated roots, and the presence of a formal house system and Catholic networks.

Executive-grade snapshot (category-by-category) indicates CLASSICAL in four categories, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in one, and CLASSICAL or SOFTLY CLASSICAL in others, with room for enhancement in articulating a fully consolidated “faculty living curriculum” framework. The aggregate picture remains firmly CLASSICAL.

Evidence drawn from the school’s public materials shows: explicit Trivium grounding (grammar, logic, rhetoric); a commitment to a Catholic worldview and liturgical life (Mass, morning prayer, domestic church language); a history of integrating faith with reason across subjects; upper-school Socratic discussions and rhetoric training; and family-school partnership through parental involvement structures and welcoming policies. See sources describing Spirit of Truth curriculum for PK–8, the Trivium foundation, the Catholic identity integrated throughout subjects, and the emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and the true, the good, and the beautiful. Cited excerpts come from the school’s public pages and documents, including the Academics and Curriculum sections, mission statements, and program descriptions.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence

- The school presents a pedagogy blending contemplative/restful elements with rigorous inquiry, aligning with the classical balance of scholé and rigorous study. The public materials describe education as a process that includes contemplation and memory-work.
- Lower-school pedagogy emphasizes singing and chanting for memory and delight, consistent with the memory-formation dimension of classical education. (PK–8 materials indicate a rhythm of memory work and recitation in early years.)
- Upper-school pedagogy involves Socratic discussions, seminars, and debates, with explicit training in logic and rhetoric and opportunities for public speaking.
- Curriculum pages explicitly describe the Trivium’s role in shaping pedagogy and memory, with emphasis on Reading, Logic, and Rhetoric as stages.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords found: contemplative/scholé, memory/recitation, singing/chants, Socratic discussions, seminars, debates, rhetoric, public speaking, logic, Trivium, integrated religion as pedagogy.
- Negative keywords: none identified in public materials for this category.

Why this rating

- The site documents a classical pedagogy mix: contemplative/memory-based practices in early years; Socratic and rhetoric-focused experiences in high school; direct reference to the Trivium and to public speaking—aligning with CLASSICAL Pedagogy expectations.

Citations reflect the pedagogy language.

While these are externally affiliated networks, they suggest a faculty culture aligned with classical/catholic liberal arts norms.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were to move toward fuller CLASSICAL status, it would be beneficial to articulate explicitly and uniformly the faculty’s role as the “living curriculum” and to provide more direct, public-facing language and practices that frame teachers as core architectural elements of the Classical model (e.g., explicit statements that faculty are the living curriculum, and that teacher formation mirrors the Trivium stages).
- Strengthen explicit references to Great Books in public materials, with a clearly stated “Great Conversation” framework, to reinforce the classical canon emphasis beyond curricular references to philosophy and rhetoric.
- Expand and codify a visible, school-wide professional development pathway aligned to classical pedagogy (Scholé, memorization discipline, contemplation, and rhetoric training) and publish it as part of the faculty profile and strategic plan.
