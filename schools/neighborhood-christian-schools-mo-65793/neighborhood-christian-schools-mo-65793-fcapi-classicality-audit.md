## Executive Summary

Neighborhood Christian Schools (Mo 65793) presents a strong classical Christian orientation in its public materials. The branding explicitly frames the school as “Classical. Christian. Practical.” and emphasizes character formation under a theological framework (e.g., love of God and glory of God) with a clear communal/liturgical texture (singing psalms and shared worship imagery). The site structure signals a traditional classical pathway (Grammar School K-6 and Academy 7-12) and highlights associations with ACCS, reinforcing a classical lineage. Taken together, the school is best characterized as CLASSICAL overall, with robust religious/metaphysical foundations, and formation-centric aims. The site shows concrete evidence of formation-focused aims, a liturgical/community rhythm, and explicit teacher-student partnerships, but less explicit, on-site documentation of some core classical-curriculum elements (e.g., explicit Great Books curricula) in the public text. Where explicit elements are less visible, the school’s branding, governance, and associated materials imply a classical orientation. See supporting citations throughout the analysis.

- The branding and articulation of mission (Classical. Christian. Practical.) and the goal of “graduates of character who love God” establish a Religious and Metaphysical Foundations core and a Purpose/Goal core. 
- The site’s navigation and content emphasize classical education through ACCS affiliation, K-6 Grammar School and 7-12 Academy, and a public emphasis on character formation and theology. 
- Public materials also document a liturgical/communal dimension (e.g., Psalm 46 sung by K-6 students), underscoring liturgical practices and communal worship rhythms.

Why this rating
- The presence of explicit Christian framing, God-centered language, and liturgical/community practices (psalm singing) strongly supports a Classical category grounded in religious/metaphysical foundations. The ACCS affiliation and the theology-inflected mission reinforce this reading.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- The mission statement frames education as formation of virtue in service of God: “graduates of character who love God and are equipped academically, spiritually, and vocationally to understand God’s world and to live out their calling to the glory of God.” This is a formation-for-whole-person aim, not strictly employment prep. 
- The explicit emphasis on character/virtue and the teleological framing (glorifying God) aligns with the transcendental aims described in classical education—formation of the soul, virtue, and wisdom. The site also presents a broad educational aim beyond mere information transfer (formation over information). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue/character (character is explicit), wisdom (supported by the classical framing), telos (implied by purpose toward truth/beauty/virtue, via the God-centered telos phrase in mission). God-centered language reinforces teleology and moral formation. 
- Negative keywords: not evidenced; the site does not emphasize workforce-centric terms as primary goals (no strong “career-ready” framing dominates the text). 

Why this rating
- The core aim is formation (virtue, wisdom, character) and a telos oriented toward truth/goodness/beauty within a Christian framework, rather than a primary emphasis on job preparation. Despite the word “vocationally” in the mission, the surrounding context treats vocation within a broader, formation-centered Christian-humanist frame rather than a secular career-prep focus.

Citations: mission/vision statements (turn0file0); ACCS affiliation (turn1file15) as reinforcing a classical aim; liturgical/communal emphasis (turn1file15).

Evidence

The published materials about classical pedagogy emphasize Socratic dialogue, memory work, recitation, and rhetoric training; these are part of the classical pedagogy lexicon. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation (psalm singing by younger students, recitation/music-based memory work implied by the classical approach), and Socratic/seminar-style pedagogy signals in the broader canonical materials (classical pedagogy lexicon). Evidence on the site includes Psalm 46 singing and the K-6 class classical format, which supports memorization/recitation and a restful dimension. 
- Negative keywords: not overtly present in public-facing site copy (no explicit emphasis on project-based-only or purely experiential constructionist pedagogy; rather, the materials lean toward a traditional classical pedagogy). The presence of “project-based learning” as a dominant paradigm is not evidenced publicly in the site text. 

Why this rating
- The school shows classical pedagogy signals (memory-based learning, singing/chanting, and potentially Socratic-style discourse in higher grades implied by the classical framing). However, explicit, comprehensive articulation of a full suite of classical pedagogy (e.g., widely implemented Socratic seminars across grades, memorizational rigors across subjects, and explicit balanced combination with contemplative/restful learning) is not exhaustively documented in the visible site text. Citations: Psalm singing (turn1file15); Scholé/restful pedagogy references (turn1file17); Classical pedagogy terms (turn0file1).

## What would move the needle

- The Neighborhood Christian Schools material shows strong classical framing and formation aims, but explicit articulation of the core Great Books program, explicit Triad (grammar-logic-rhetoric) scope across the entire curriculum, and a clearly mapped Great Conversation across grades are less visible in public text. To move toward the fullest CLASSICAL alignment, the school could:
  - Publicly articulate explicit Great Books/Great Conversation components within the curriculum pages (e.g., listing canonical texts used, and the expectation of student engagement with primary sources across grades). This would align with the Positive Keywords for Curriculum (Great Books, classical canon) and reduce any ambiguity around curriculum depth. See the Six Essential Elements curriculum indicators. 
  - More explicitly confirm the full suite of Trivium components (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across the relevant grade bands in public pages, and provide sample scope-and-sequence illustrating how logic and rhetoric are integrated into upper grades. This aligns with the Five Liberal Arts in the lexicon and would raise Element 3 (Curriculum) from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL. 
  - Expand explicit signals for Socratic pedagogy and seminars in upper grades (Socratic discussion, seminars, or debates) with public-facing examples or testimonials. This aligns with the Pedagogy indicators in the lexicon and would strengthen Element 4 (Pedagogy) toward CLASSICAL. 
  - Deepen liturgical/cultural practices on the site (e.g., more detailed description of weekly liturgical rhythm, music, and communal ceremonies) to strengthen Element 1 and Element 4, reinforcing the liturgical dimension of classical education.
