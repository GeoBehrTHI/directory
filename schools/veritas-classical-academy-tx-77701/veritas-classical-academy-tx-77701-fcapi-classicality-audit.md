## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical Academy Beaumont (TX 77701) presents a robust classical-Christian program that foregrounds God-centered formation, virtue, and a university-model schedule. Public-facing materials emphasize a disciple-making mission, a Christ-centered worldview, and a classical curriculum that includes Latin/Greek study, a history-rich approach, and a staff-driven, professionally taught program. The school also highlights parental partnership and accreditation by classical-Christian accrediting bodies. Overall, the school aligns strongly with classical education principles across most categories, with Element 3 (Curriculum) showing some gaps in explicit Great Books/Great Conversation claims. Aggregate classification: CLASSICAL.

Evidence highlights drawn from the school’s public materials (e.g., statements of purpose, curriculum notes, and staff/testimonial material) include: a stated aim to “love God and love learning for a lifetime” and a discipleship model; explicit references to a classical curriculum, Latin/Greek study, and a strong history focus; a “university schedule” model with home-based components; and a living staff-centered approach to teaching and community engagement. The school’s accreditation by ACCS/CLSA further reinforces its classical-Christian alignment. See supporting citations throughout (selected excerpts below).

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are foregrounded: God-centered purpose, “Our heart” statement of purpose, and a Statement of Faith with God as the source and center of truth, beauty, and goodness.  
- Purpose and Goals are framed as formation and virtue, not merely job prep, with a discipleship model and lifelong love of learning. 
- Curriculum emphasizes classical elements: history-rich approach, Latin/Greek study, and a university-style schedule; explicit reference to “classical curriculum,” with a robust history focus.  
- Pedagogy is organized around a Trivium-inspired structure (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric) and upper-level rhetoric; staff/testimonials describe a rigorous, classical pedagogy, including on-campus and home-based components. 
- Faculty is portrayed as a living, guiding force and “center of the Veritas experience,” with staff highlighted in testimonials and staff pages.  
- Parents & Community are explicitly encouraged as co-educators and partners with the school; materials describe family involvement, teacher-parent collaboration, and open accessibility for family participation.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Since Element 3 is the only category reasonably landed as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to a relatively explicit Great Books/Great Conversation component not being clearly evidenced in public site text, the needle-moving step would be to make Great Books / Great Conversation language explicit on public-facing pages (e.g., a dedicated section describing Great Books reading lists, Great Conversation experiences, or internal/external reading programs tied to canonical works).
- Concrete suggestions:
  - Publish a public-facing curriculum statement detailing Great Books/GCI (Great Conversation) commitments, with examples of canonical works and a brief rationale for their inclusion.
  - Add explicit language about Socratic seminars or classical discussions in upper grades to complement the rhetoric/logic emphasis.
  - Provide a short, accessible list of the seven liberal arts in school materials, including how grammar/logic/rhetoric are implemented across grades and terms, and how Latin/Greek study integrates with history and literature.

- If adopted, these changes would move Element 3 toward CLASSICAL, and by extension strengthen the aggregate CLASSICAL rating across categories.
