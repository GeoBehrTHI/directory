## Executive Summary

St Elizabeth Ann Seton Catholic School (Keller, TX) presents a robust classical-Catholic profile, anchored in a clearly articulated mission to form lifelong Catholics through the integration of truth, goodness, and beauty. Public-facing materials emphasize a strong Catholic identity, liturgical life, and a classical liberal-arts orientation (Great Books, Latin, a historically informed curriculum) alongside a parental-partnering model and a living faculty. The school clearly positions itself within the classical-tradition spectrum in several dimensions, while also exhibiting some elements that are more Catholic-tradition focused than strictly classical in pedagogy. Overall, the materials align most strongly with CLASSICAL, with some areas (notably explicit Socratic pedagogy in lower- and middle-grades and a fully articulated Trivium/Quadrivium framework in public-facing statements) showing as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregate placement for ST. ELIZABETH ANN SETON CATHOLIC SCHOOL is CLASSICAL.

Key strengths observed across the six categories include:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: explicit theology, liturgical life, sacramental practice, catechesis, Mass attendance, and a sacramental worldview integrated into daily learning (evidence: mission statements, curriculum notes, liturgical life) .
- School Purpose and Goal: formation-focused aims—formation of character, virtue, and wisdom, with the Great Books and classical liberal arts highlighted as central to the mission .
- Curriculum: explicit emphasis on Great Books, Latin, and a curriculum integrated with Catholic doctrine, liturgy, and moral formation, with attention to “integration” and “wonder” in learning .
- Faculty: explicit statements about faculty modeling virtue and excellence; living curriculum concept; professional expectations for teachers in a Catholic-classical frame .
- Parents & Community: strong emphasis on parental partnership (Parents as primary educators), PSA involvement, and open campus, with explicit aims to educate and involve parents in classical formation .

Public content highlights the liturgical and contemplative dimensions rather than focusing exclusively on Socratic seminars in the lower grades, which aligns with a mixed pedagogy in the K–8 range.

This is acceptable for a K–8 classical profile but places the pedagogy as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in the public materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school sought to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy to FULLY CLASSICAL, it could:
  - Explicitly articulate and publicly document a Trivium/Quadrivium framework and a more explicit Great Conversation approach across the curriculum, including visible, sustained engagement with primary classical texts and a clear rationale for the sequence from grammar to rhetoric (K–8 in particular). This would strengthen Element 3 and category 4 alignment with the classical-literary tradition.
  - Provide explicit documentation of Socratic pedagogy in the upper elementary and middle grades (where applicable) or outline a staged approach to Socratic discussion in grades 9–12 if/when the school adds those grades. This would strengthen Element 4 alignment with classical pedagogy by institutionalizing Socratic dialogue and rhetorical training where appropriate.
  - Expand the articulation of “memory-work” and chant-based memorization in lower grades as part of a formal memory-culture (recitation/chant) program with public exemplars demonstrating the role of memorization in the liberal arts, potentially aligning more fully with the Positive Keyword indicators in Element 4. This would enhance the lower-grade pedagogy signals.
