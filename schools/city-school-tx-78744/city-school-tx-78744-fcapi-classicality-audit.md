## Executive Summary

City School TX 78744 (Austin, TX) presents a fundamentally formation-focused, faith-inspired private Christian education modeled on Charlotte Mason principles. The school publicly articulates a Christ-centered, holistic approach to forming minds, hearts, and communities, with explicit emphasis on character development, habits, living books, and a pedagogy centered on narration, communal discussion, and reverence for the divine order. Evidence from the school’s public materials shows core commitments to truth and God, a beautiful and orderly environment, and a curriculum designed to lead students outward toward the world, all within a diverse, faith-based community. While the school integrates many features associated with classical education (habits and formation, emphasis on moral purpose, a non-secular worldview, and a community-centered approach), several hallmark classical elements—prominent Great Books/Great Conversation mandates, explicit Trivium/Quadrivium content, and a fully articulated seven liberal arts framework—are not stated in their public materials. As a result, the overall classification is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL, with clear needle-movers available to reach full CLASSICAL status.

Key insights include:
- Strong religious and metaphysical foundations: a Christ-centered, Bible-inclusive environment, daily prayer/memory of Scripture, and explicit grounding in truth as a divine objective.
- Purpose and formation emphasis: explicit aim to form the whole person (mind, body, spirit) and to lead students toward virtue and service.
- Curriculum and pedagogy reflect Charlotte Mason influence: living books, narration, habit formation, and a worldview-led, integrative approach, but with limited explicit Great Books or Trivium/Quadrivium articulation in public content.
- Faculty and community: visible leadership and staff structure with a focus on mentorship and community involvement; robust parent/community engagement through resources, volunteering, and governance.
- The public material includes pathways for active parental involvement and community partnerships, which aligns well with classical expectations for parental partnership.

Score: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (aggregate). See category-by-category details below for category-specific keyword analysis, evidence, and rationale.

These reflect components of the Six Essential Elements’ Curriculum Content and Structure, including living books, integration, and wonder.

Why this rating
- The curriculum demonstrates strong classical-adjacent features (habits, living books, integrated approach, reverence for literature and history, and a “lead out” pedagogy), but it stops short of explicitly articulating the full Seven Liberal Arts and Great Books framework within the public materials. This places it in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL for this category.

Citations: Story of the World and living books elements (turn2file11, turn2file9); Philosophy of Education emphasis on living curriculum (turn0file18); Masterworks in arts (turn3file2).

Bible memorization and prayer are integrated into daily life, and there is a clear emphasis on forming habits as part of the learning process. The public materials speak to an approach that includes both contemplative/restful elements and active inquiry, with upper-grade discussion and rhetorical skills introduced as students progress.  

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

These align with the Positive Keywords for Pedagogy (Socratic dialogue, restfulness/scholé, memorization/recitation, formation through imitation and practice). 
- Negative indicators: explicit Socratic seminars or high-school-level dialectic in public materials are not stated; no clear evidence of formal high-school-level Socratic seminars. This is not necessarily disqualifying (the framework allows K–8 classical pedagogy to be classical even without 9–12 Socratic seminars), but it limits full CLASSICAL alignment in pedagogy for all grades. 

Why this rating
- The teaching methods strongly reflect traditional formation through memory, narration, and integrated living-text instruction, with a Miss Mason–style emphasis on the “habits” and “lead out” pedagogy. However, the explicit presence of Socratic/dialectic seminars or formal high-school rhetoric instruction is not clearly stated within the City School public content, which places it in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for pedagogy.

Citations: Narration and Bible memorization (turn0file4); Philosophy of Education and lead-out language (turn0file18).

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicit Great Books / Great Conversation articulation: The classical rubric values mention Great Books and a formal Great Conversation as central to the liberal-arts curriculum. City School uses living books and a Charlotte Mason approach but does not publicly declare a Great Books/Great Conversation framework. Adding explicit language and content around Great Books, canonical Western texts, and regular high-level dialogues would move Element 3 (Curriculum) toward CLASSICAL and reinforce the overall classical alignment.

- Clear Trivium/Quadrivium presence in curriculum: The Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) are foundational to classical education but are not explicitly enumerated in City School’s public materials. Incorporating a visible Trivium/Quadrivium pathway (even if embedded in lower-to-middle levels) would strengthen Element 3 and Element 4 alignment, and improve the overall Classical status.

- Strengthen explicit “living curriculum” language for faculty: The rubric emphasizes the faculty as the living curriculum and as mentors. Public materials could more explicitly describe faculty as living exemplars of virtue and as a core component of the curriculum (the “living curriculum” concept). This would lift Element 5 toward CLASSICAL and bolster the aggregate.

- Expand liturgical/communal practices: While City School demonstrates liturgical elements (Bible memory, daily prayer) and a Christ-centered environment, more explicit articulation of liturgical rhythms (chapel, liturgical calendar, sacred music, communal worship) would strengthen Element 1 and the overall alignment.

- Deepened historical-canonical engagement: Increasing explicit references to primary historical texts, canonical authors, and “great conversation” traditions would push Element 3 toward CLASSICAL and better reflect the knowledge-base criteria.

Note: The needle-moving recommendations are designed to push the school toward full CLASSICAL classification without compromising its current missional, Charlotte Mason–inspired approach.
