## ABSTRACT

New City Classical Academy (NCCA), Quad Cities, IA, demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL orientation across most of the classical-education rubric. The school foregrounds a theology-infused, formation-centered mission, explicitly tying education to a biblical worldview, liturgical practice, and a public articulation of truth, goodness, and beauty. Its curriculum centers on the Trivium, Great Books, and Latin, with integration of history and Western civilization across disciplines. The public materials show a strong emphasis on parental partnership, assembly-based daily formation, and a leadership structure consistent with classical education models.

Key category takeaways:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Explicit theology, biblical worldview, liturgy, creeds, and solas frame the educational mission.  
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Formation-focused aims with a telos toward virtue, wisdom, and truth, grounded in classical ideals and Great Books. 
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Trivium core, Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin, history, Western civilization; integrated humanities emphasis.  
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Memory/recitation and liturgical cadence are strong; explicit Socratic seminars are not consistently evidenced in public content; room for broader use of Socratic pedagogy. 
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Formal structure (Headmaster, board, team pages) is evident; explicit articulation of “living curriculum” and teacher-portrait language is less visible in public materials. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong parental partnership, formation, and open-campus ethos are clearly articulated. 

Overall: CLASSICAL.

This assessment draws on the school’s public materials (Latin, Great Books, Trivium, liturgical assemblies, creeds, and theology-focused language) and compares them to the Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators in the Six Essential Elements glossary. The balance of strong classical signals versus partial signals in Pedagogy and Faculty yields a CLASSICAL overall rating, with explicit opportunities to strengthen Socratic pedagogy and the formal articulation of the teacher’s living-curriculum role.

End of ABSTRACT.
