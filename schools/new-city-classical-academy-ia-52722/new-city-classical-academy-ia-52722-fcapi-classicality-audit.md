## Executive Summary

New City Classical Academy (NCCA), Quad Cities, IA, presents a clearly classical Christian orientation with strong alignment to the Six Essential Elements of classical education. Across multiple public facing materials, the school foregrounds a theology-infused, formation-driven approach anchored in the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation, and a liturgical, communal rhythm centered on Scripture and creeds. Public materials consistently emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable, a biblical worldview, and a strong partnership with families. The school’s stated curriculum and pedagogy emphasize Latin, memorization, recitation, and liturgy in daily practice, and the “Great Books” and scholarly dialogue are foregrounded through ACCS alignment and stated commitments to classical education. There is explicit emphasis on formation over information and on parental partnership.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key evidence driving this assessment includes explicit references to:
- The Trivium, Great Books, Great Conversation, and ACCS accreditation (FAQs page; ACCS language) .
- A biblical worldview, theology, and liturgical practices (nurture and admonition of the Lord; Sacred City Church location; morning assemblies with creeds, Scripture readings, and singing)   .
- Formation-focused aims and language around truth, goodness, and beauty, with explicit articulation of “we teach students to love what is good, true, and beautiful” and “biblical worldview” guidance  .
- Curriculum anchors such as Latin starting in 3rd grade, Great Books, and the Great Conversation; explicit statements about history and Western civilization as integrated across disciplines  .
- Strong parental partnership language (parents as co-teachers, formation of parents, open campus/visits) and a community-of-faith framing (board leadership, staff, assemblies)  .

Note on methodology: This evaluation uses two vector stores as requested:
- A knowledge-base vector store with classical-education criteria and the Six Essential Elements rubric (including “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1)” as a knowledge file). The rubric and positive/negative keyword indicators drive the category analyses.
- A school-specific crawl vector store containing NCCA’s public website material (FAQs, curriculum notes, statements of faith, team pages, blog posts, admissions, etc.) to identify keywords and phrases relevant to each category.

The six-category analysis below follows the mandated keyword-analytics framework, then assigns a category rating (Classical, Somewhat Classical, Not Classical) and summarizes the aggregate classification.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Memory work and recitation are explicitly present in morning assemblies (Scripture memorization and recitation; exordiums; creeds; psalms/hymns; singing), indicating a pedagogy that values memorization and liturgical-catechetical forms. 
- Contemplative/restful elements appear in daily routines (Scholé-like cadence through liturgical daily practices and contemplative rhythm). 
- Upper-school pedagogy is described in terms of classical methods (the Great Books, discussions, and discourses) in broader materials, but explicit evidence of formal Socratic seminars or dedicated high-school seminar formats is not consistently explicit in the public-facing materials. The materials emphasize Trivium and Great Books, but do not clearly demonstrate consistent Socratic seminars across all grade levels in the public materials. 
- There is evidence of a strong liturgical/recitation-focused approach (Exordiums, creeds, Scripture memorization) as part of the everyday pedagogy. 

Positive keyword indicators present (approximate): Socratic dialogue (not clearly evidenced across all grades in public materials), dialectic (not consistently evidenced), narration/recitation; chanting; singing to memorize; scholé; restfulness; liturgical calendar integration; memory work; catechesis; upper-school rhetoric; capstone orations.

Negative keyword indicators present: The explicit presence of project-based learning or experiential-learning emphasis is not evident in the public materials; however, explicit Socratic seminars in upper grades are not strongly evidenced in all public materials.

Why this rating
- The pedagogy shows strong alignment with classical practices (memory work, recitation, liturgical/narrative forms, sacramental catechesis, and scholé cadence). There is less explicit evidence of formal Socratic seminars across the entire school (especially in public content), which makes this category a partial fit rather than a full “Classical” signal. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating, with room to emphasize formal Socratic/Dialogic seminars more explicitly across high school. 

Citations: Morning assembly pedagogy (recitation, psalm/singing, exordiums) and liturgical cadence:  Creedal/memorizational elements in assembly:  Great Books/Trivium emphasis as core pedagogy:

- If the school published (or codified on the site) a clear program of formal Socratic seminars in grades 9–12 (or more explicit Harkness-style discussions) with trained teachers, this would strengthen the Pedagogy category to CLASSICAL.
- Expand explicit articulation and evidence of contemplative rest (Scholé) as a daily pedagogical norm across all grades, with documented routines and learning outcomes.

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

However, there is less explicit textual content describing teachers as the “living curriculum” or a “portrait of a teacher” in the same language as the classical lexicon’s ideal.

Why this rating

This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.
