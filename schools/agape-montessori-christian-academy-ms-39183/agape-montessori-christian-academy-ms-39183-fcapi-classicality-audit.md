## Executive Summary

Agape Montessori Christian Academy (AMCA), Vicksburg, MS 39183, presents itself publicly as a Classical Christian institution with Montessori elements and a Christian worldview. Public materials emphasize a Christ-centered, Christian-principles-based education and a classical framing of education, while also foregrounding Montessori practices in early years and a mixed-curriculum approach (A Beka curriculum alongside Montessori methods). This combination yields a mixed classical signal across the six canonical categories.

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Core insights:
  - Religious/metaphysical foundations are clearly present (Christ-centered, Biblical/Christian principles, classical framing), aligning Category 1 closely with classical aims (though not always using explicit liturgical language). Evidence includes a stated classical Christian framework and explicit Christ-centered language.   
  - Purpose/goal shows a dual emphasis on formation and preparation for ongoing schooling, with explicit aims around virtue formation and religious grounding but with college/school progression language as well. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal for Category 2. 
  - Curriculum signals are mixed: Montessori and A Beka curricula coexist, but explicit Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric liberal arts, or “Great Conversation” elements are not evident in the public materials reviewed. Category 3 is NOT CLASSICAL in explicit terms, though the classical branding is present.  
  - Pedagogy shows Montessori practices in early years and a mix of traditional and secular instruction in upper grades. Some classical pedagogical elements (Socratic dialogue, scholé, formal rhetoric) are not clearly evidenced; thus Category 4 rates SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
  - Faculty signals include references to teachers and small-class context, but a clearly articulated “living curriculum” or explicit classical teacher portrait is not overtly described in the public materials. Category 5 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
  - Parent and community engagement includes explicit partnership language with families, parent formation, and open-campus expectations; this aligns Category 6 with CLASSICAL signals. 

- What would move the needle: To achieve a robust CLASSICAL rating across all categories, AMCA could more explicitly demonstrate:
  - Category 3: Adoption or explicit integration of Great Books and the liberal arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) within a unified curriculum plan, with references to “great books” and “great conversation.”
  - Category 4: Greater use of Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades, plus explicit scholé/restful learning language in practice.
  - Category 5: A formal “living curriculum” statement and a published teacher-persona/portrait reflecting classical teacher-mentor ideals.

- Abstract takeaway: AMCA leans toward CLASSICAL in its religious/crystalized identity and community partnerships, but its curriculum and pedagogy signals are not fully classical as defined by the Six Essential Elements. The aggregate tilt is toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Executive summaries for each category follow, with evidence, keyword analyses, and rationale.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Category 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate and implement a canonical classical curriculum framework (Seven Liberal Arts; Trivium/Quadrivium) and integrate Great Books or primary sources into the curriculum. Offer explicit references to “Great Books” and “Great Conversation” in course descriptions, units, and assessment rubrics.
- Category 4 (Pedagogy): Increase explicit incorporation of Socratic dialogue/seminar styles in upper grades; frame some courses around scholé/restful learning language and practice; include structured memory work and recitation that aligns with classical pedagogy.
- Category 5 (Faculty): Publish a clear “portrait of the teacher” and a concise description of how faculty embody the living-curriculum concept, including ongoing professional development in classical-pedagogical methods and teacher formation.
- Category 2 (Purpose and Goal): Clarify and foreground formation as the primary telos, with explicit statements about virtue formation and contemplation in line with classical paideia, while acknowledging college/school progression. Reduce ambiguity around career-prep emphasis to emphasize formation-first language.
- Category 1 and Category 6: Maintain strong religious-formation signaling and robust parent-partnerships; consider expanding liturgical/cultural practices language to align with more explicit liturgical-community signals, if desired.
