## Executive Summary

The City School (Poplar Campus, Philadelphia, PA 19123) presents a distinctly Christian, college-preparatory K–12 program with a pronounced formation focus. public materials emphasize training minds and discipling hearts within a multi-campus Christian framework, including explicit references to prayer, biblical instruction, and biblically-informed counseling. This religious/ontological orientation provides a strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations core, and the school foregrounds formation of character, virtue, and values alongside intellectual development.

Judging against the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric, The City School demonstrates clear classical tendencies in theology-grounded purpose and virtue formation, along with an integrated, mission-driven community ethos. However, in key curricular specifics (explicitly invoking the Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, and explicit Socratic pedagogy), the school does not consistently present the canonical marks of a classical program. Consequently, the overall assessment lands as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: solid in religious and formation aims and college-preparatory intent, but short of a full classical articulation in curriculum and pedagogy. This evaluation integrates evidence from the school’s public materials (Admissions, Student Life, staff bios, and program pages) and aligns with the Positive/Negative Keyword indicators from the Six Essential Elements document.

Evidence and keyword-based scoring are drawn from the school’s own site content and the Classical School rubric, with explicit examples cited below. For core references to the classical rubric, see the Six Essential Elements materials and their Positive/Negative Keyword indicators. See citations throughout for the exact sources used.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

The lack of these canonical classical-curriculum markers reduces the alignment with core classical curriculum criteria. 
- The site emphasizes a Christian worldview and biblically-informed instruction rather than a traditional classical curriculum, though there are cross-disciplinary elements (e.g., STEAM) that function well in a modern integrated approach. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: interdisciplinary integration, STEAM, college-preparatory curriculum, integration and wonder (through the combination of arts/science and a holistic approach).
- Negative keywords present: absence of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation, absence of Trivium/Quadrivium language, and absence of explicit memorization/recitation as central pedagogy.

Why this rating
- The Curriculum category demonstrates meaningful classical-adjacent traits (integration, wonder, discipline) but lacks the explicit classical core elements (Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, Great Conversation). This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 3. Evidence cited above. 

Citations to the Classical Rubric

The City School materials show integration and wonder in a modern STEAM/arts emphasis but do not show canonical Great Books or Trivium/Quadrivium emphasis. 

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials describe a modern, college-preparatory environment with STEAM and arts emphasis, not explicitly aligned to Socratic seminars, formalized memorization/recitation, or the prescribed classical pedagogy (e.g., Scholé/restful learning, lower-school chant/memorization, upper-school rhetoric with formal debate). The absence of explicit Socratic-method emphasis in high school pedagogy is notable. 
- There is no explicit evidence of a deliberate “Socratic” program in the high school or a structured chant/memorization routine highlighted on public pages. The public materials emphasize formation, Christian worldview, and a modern, holistic approach rather than the classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, rhetoric training, and public speaking as a core, high-visibility practice). 
- In lower grades, the materials do reference a holistic student experience but do not indicate a classical, chant-based or routine memory work as a core pedagogy. The public-facing content aligns more with contemporary pedagogy than strict classical pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: there are references to “formation” and “discipling hearts,” which align with holistic, character-focused pedagogy but not with explicit classical pedagogical methods (Socratic, rhetoric training). 
- Negative keywords present: absence of explicit Socratic seminars, classical rhetoric training, or structured, rigorous rhetoric/public-speaking pathways; absence of memory-focused pedagogical language.

Why this rating
- The pedagogy described in public materials aligns more closely with modern, holistic, faith-informed pedagogy than with canonical classical pedagogy. This yields a NOT CLASSICAL rating for Element 4. See evidence cited above. 

Citations to the Classical Rubric
- The Classical Pedagogy indicators (Socratic method, seminars, debate, rhetoric training) are described in the Six Essential Elements document. The City School materials do not clearly present these as central, hence NOT CLASSICAL for Element 4.

Aggregate assessment

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale
- The City School shows strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1: CLASSICAL) and robust School Purpose/Goal (Element 2: CLASSICAL). However, Curriculum (Element 3) and Pedagogy (Element 4) fall short of canonical classical requirements, while Faculty (Element 5) and Parents & Community (Element 6) show partial alignment. Taken together, the aggregate is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)

- Curriculum alignment with classical canon:
  - Explicitly articulate and integrate the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and possibly the Quadrivium) and Great Books within the core curriculum.
  - Explicitly reference Great Books and the Great Conversation in course catalogs and state publicly how historical texts are studied, with primary sources and classic works included.
- Pedagogical reform toward classical methods:
  - Introduce Socratic seminars, structured rhetoric training, and formal opportunities for public speaking and argumentation in upper grades.
  - Reintroduce contemplative “scholé” rhythms in lower grades, with memory work and structured recitation as appropriate to the program.
- Faculty development and living curriculum:
  - Explicitly define and publish the living-curriculum concept, with faculty modeling virtue and intellectual habits, and professional development aligned to classical pedagogy.
- Parent and community engagement enhancements:
  - Establish formal parent co-teaching roles or structured parent education programs (e.g., book studies on classical education, workshops on virtue formation, and family catechesis) and clearly communicate these opportunities on the website.

- Evidence-cited basis for needle-moving steps: The City School already demonstrates strong formation and faith-based aims (Element 1) and formation-focused goals (Element 2). However, explicit classical-curriculum markers (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Scholé, and visible public rhetoric) are not consistently present in the public materials for Categories 3–6. This is the primary area for improvement to move toward a full CLASSICAL classification. See the public materials on admissions, student life, and pedagogy for the currently demonstrated aspects.
