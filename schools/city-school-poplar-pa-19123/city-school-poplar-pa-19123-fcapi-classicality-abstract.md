## ABSTRACT

The City School (Poplar Campus, Philadelphia, PA 19123) demonstrates a Christian, formation-forward approach to education with a clear emphasis on training minds and discipling hearts. The school's religious foundations, emphasis on virtue formation, and faith-based community life align with the Classical School rubric in Category 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Category 2 (School Purpose and Goal). However, the curriculum and pedagogy indicators do not consistently reflect canonical classical elements such as the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, Great Conversation, and Socratic pedagogy, placing Category 3 (Curriculum) and Category 4 (Pedagogy) at a NOT/Somewhat-leaning level. Public materials show modern, integrated STEAM and holistic education, which, while strong, diverge from the strict classical curriculum lexicon. The faculty and parental/community dimensions show partial classical alignment, with faculty mentorship and parental engagement present but not fully formalized in the classical sense.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes explicit Christian orientation, theology-grounded aims, and liturgical/community cues within public materials. Positive keyword alignment with formation, virtue, and sacred tradition support CLASSICAL standing in this category. 

- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL
  Formation-centric aims (formation of minds/hearts, virtue, and Christian teleology) appear prominently, supporting a CLASSICAL rating. 

- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Public materials emphasize modern STEAM and college-preparatory programming within a Christian framework, but lack explicit classical-curriculum hallmarks (Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts). 

- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL
  No consistent public evidence of classical pedagogy (Socratic dialogue, seminars, or rhetoric training as core pedagogical methods). 

- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Faculty bios show mentorship and faith-informed leadership, but the public materials do not clearly articulate the “living curriculum” model or a fully defined classical-faculty portrait. 

- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Partnership language and parental engagement are present, but explicit co-teaching structures or formal parent-education programs are not clearly delineated in the public materials.
