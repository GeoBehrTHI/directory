## Executive Summary

Coram Deo Academy (CDA) presents a strongly classical Christian education model, with public materials clearly framing the school as Christ-centered, classical, formation-minded, and committed to the Great Conversation. The school describes itself as a Classical Christian school that forms students through memorization, narration, and habituated virtue within a Trivium framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integrates Great Books and Latin, and uses Socratic discussion and rhetoric in upper grades. It emphasizes covenant-parent partnership, a Portrait of the Graduate, and real-world apprenticeships, all of which align closely with classical ideals of truth, goodness, and beauty in a Christian formation context. The public materials consistently reference virtues like wisdom, virtue, character, discernment, and other facets of moral formation, with explicit Christ-centered theology and worship as integral to the educational experience. The site also highlights faculty engagement as part of a “living curriculum” and frames the parent-school relationship as collaborative and mission-driven rather than transactional. Overall, CDA demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL orientation across all six categories of the classical rubric.

Evidence is drawn from CDA’s site content, including mission statements, curricular descriptions, pedagogy, and portraits of graduate outcomes (e.g., Contagious Christian, Compelling Communicator, Critical & Creative Thinker, Lifelong Learner, Legacy Leader). See examples of mission and curriculum language, Great Books emphasis, Trivium structure, and covenant partnership with families. The school’s materials consistently tie education to a Christian worldview, classical pedagogy, and a public articulation of formation over mere information. Relevant citations include CDA’s About/mission language, curriculum descriptions (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin; Great Books), the emphasis on Socratic discourse and memorization, and the Portrait of the Graduate.    

Executive summary category-by-category snapshot indicates CLASSICAL alignment across all six categories. The school’s religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit, its purpose and goals emphasize character and wisdom over mere vocational training, its curriculum foregrounds the liberal arts (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, Latin, history studied through a Christian lens), its pedagogy includes contemplative elements alongside Socratic discussion and rhetoric, its faculty are portrayed as mentors within a living-curriculum model, and its parent/community framework positions families as partners in a shared educational mission. The evidence supports a CLASSICAL classification overall.

## Executive Summary — Category-by-Category Snapshot

### Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- CDA presents a Christ-centered, biblical formation framework, noting that the school exists to train children biblically with “academic instruction that is distinctly and consistently Christ-centered and classical.” The statement foregrounds theology and Christian formation as foundational to the education. 
- Christian Discipleship materials articulate that the fear of the Lord governs wisdom, and that Bible reading and prayer are integrated with learning rather than segregated from secular subjects. This demonstrates a theologically integrated, liturgical/communal practice orientation. 
- The school’s discourse about “coram Deo” (in the presence of God) and identity as a Christian classical school ties metaphysical and theological foundations directly to curriculum and culture. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Christ-centered, theology, Gospel, fear of the Lord, Great Books, Great Conversation, memory/recitation within a devotional-cultural frame, liturgical/communal orientation (worship, prayer), covenant partnership with families, and a shared Gospel-centered formation (Portrait of the Graduate). These terms align with the Positive Keyword indicators for religious/metaphysical foundations (e.g., truth, virtue, worship, the sovereign role of theology in education, and a sacred orientation to learning). 
- Negative keywords present: None materially evident in public language (no explicit secular/neutral/anti-theology lexicon appears in CDA’s public materials).

Why this rating
- The school’s public materials explicitly embed theology, communal worship, and a theological frame for all study (e.g., “Christ-centered,” “fear of the Lord,” “Great Books in light of the Bible,” “coram Deo”), which squarely aligns with the Classical model’s emphasis on transcendental moral realism and the integration of theology with curriculum. The presence of liturgical/community practices and a theology-grounded program supports a CLASSICAL classification here.

CDA aligns strongly with religious/metaphysical foundations, supported by explicit language and program structures that fuse theology, virtue, and classical education. CLASSICAL.

Citations: mission/orientation language and theological integration in About Us and Christian Discipleship pages.  

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence

The Portrait of the Graduate emphasizes emerging virtues and capacities (Contagious Christian, Compelling Communicator, Critical & Creative Thinker, Lifelong Learner, Legacy Leader).  
- The school asserts education as formation, not information, through the Trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric), memorization, narration, and good habits; learning is oriented toward timeless truths and human excellence in the context of a Christian worldview. 
- Real-world purpose statements emphasize preparation for eternity and transformation of character, not solely vocational outcomes. The Covenant/Partnership framework reinforces formation through values, Habits of the Heart, and gospel-centered aims. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, truth, goodness, beauty, character, integrity, moral formation, love of learning, habits of the heart, eternal aims, discernment, self-governance, leadership, and the Portrait of the Graduate. These map to the Positive Keyword indicators for Category 2 (Formation and moral/character development; transcendental aims like truth/goodness/beauty). 
- Negative keywords present: Virtually none (the site does not signal a vocational-first goal or purely informational utility; it emphasizes formation and virtue rather than job-prep).

Why this rating
- CDA clearly positions education as formation and character development anchored in truth and virtue, not merely information or job preparation. The explicit reference to virtues and the graduate portrait demonstrates alignment with CLASSICAL aims for wisdom and human excellence. CLASSICAL.

Citations: mission statements, Portrait of the Graduate, and formation-focused curriculum language.   

### Category 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum is explicitly described as Trivium-based: grammar, logic, rhetoric; the K-12 curriculum emphasizes a classical structure with Latin and Great Books. The Lower/Upper School materials describe the three-stage curriculum with grammar (K–4), logic (5–8), and rhetoric (9–12). Great Books, Latin, logic, and rhetoric are central components. 
- History is studied chronologically and within a Christian frame; the school emphasizes reading Great Books and engaging with classic ideas within a Christian viewpoint, not “social studies” as a separate modern category. Curriculum materials also show K-2/K-12 curriculum resources and reading/literature lists. 
- The program highlights memorization, narration, participating in a “Great Conversation,” and a focus on timeless truths; it also references apprenticeships and real-world application via Guilds/Senior Thesis at the upper levels. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, Great Books, Latin, history (timelines, Christian framing), memorization, narration, contemplation/wonder, and the Great Conversation. These map directly to the Positive Keyword indicators for Category 3 (liberal arts core; history integrated; Great Books; Latin; cultivation of wonder). 
- Negative keywords present: The site uses a strongly favorable frame for classic subjects; there is little to no public-language signaling of social studies-centric or secular approaches; no explicit negative terms appear (e.g., “not” or “anti-Great Books” language is absent).

Why this rating
- CDA’s public materials strongly emphasize the classical curriculum—grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, Latin, chronological/history study, and formation through memorization and narration. This is a textbook CLASSICAL alignment.

Citations: descriptions of the Trivium and Great Books/Latin; K-12 Curriculum materials; and the emphasis on history studied chronologically.  

### Category 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogy includes contemplative/restful elements and non-rigorous moments, balanced with rigorous inquiry. The Lower School materials note that the grammar stage uses memorization, singing, and rhymes to deepen memory and delight; this reflects a contemplative, memory-based pedagogy. 
- In the upper grades, the school emphasizes Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training, including opportunities to give speeches and develop rhetorical skills. The public materials also mention the Great Conversation and a table-like Socratic discussion environment. 
- The school explicitly describes the grammar/logic/rhetoric progression and the use of memorization, narration, and “habits” in education, indicating a pedagogy designed to form memory, reason, and eloquence. 
- There is a public emphasis on a living curriculum, mentorship, and “House”/community-focused schooling that supports pedagogy through teacher-student partnerships and collaborative learning. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: contemplation, memorization, singing/chanting in early grades, grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, Great Books, Socratic/dialogue/seminars, rhetoric training, public speaking, memory-rich pedagogy, and a table-style discussion. These align with the Positive Keyword indicators for Category 4 (Socratic seminars, memory-based learning, rhetoric, and contemplative elements).
- Negative keywords present: The materials do not emphasize project-based or purely experiential learning as a primary mode; the emphasis is on classic methods rather than modern “project-based” formats. If anything, the presence of contemplative and memory-based methods counters heavy project-based norms, aligning with Classical expectations.

Why this rating
- The pedagogy shows a deliberate mix of contemplative/recitation-based lower grades and Socratic/seminar/rhetoric-based upper grades, consistent with a classical approach (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric). CLASSICAL.

Citations: Lower School grammar/memorization/singing language; upper-school Socratic discussions and rhetoric emphasis; “Great Conversation” framing.
