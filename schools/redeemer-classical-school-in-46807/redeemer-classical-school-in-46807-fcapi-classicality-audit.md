## Executive Summary

Redeemer Classical School in 46807 (Fort Wayne, IN) presents a clearly classical, Christian educational orientation rooted in Lutheran identity. Public-facing materials emphasize classical pedagogy, memory-based practice, a robust library of timeless works, and a distinctly Christian/theological frame (theology, liturgy, catechesis, hymnody) that anchors curriculum and community life. The school explicitly positions itself as a classical Lutheran education, committed to rigorous study of the liberal arts, formation of character, and integration of faith with learning. Evidence includes statements about classical education shaped by Christian theology, a strong emphasis on teachers as the primary living curriculum, and a public articulation of ancient/classical educational aims (such as classical literature, Latin, and a library of classics) as central to schooling. At the same time, explicit articulation of core classical components such as a formalized Great Books program, explicit Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades, or a sustained public claim about Great Conversation across the curriculum is less consistently explicit in public materials, though several sources reference classical literature, a large library, and a strong emphasis on memory work and recitation. The overall orientation aligns with classical education in several key respects, but some hallmark elements (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis or wide-scale Socratic seminars in upper grades) are not consistently foregrounded in the public-facing materials. The aggregate assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Executive judgment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Key drivers: strong religious/theological foundations; explicit focus on classical literature, hymnody, and memory; documented commitment to teachers as living exemplars; visible emphasis on formation and virtue; moderate but not always explicit emphasis on Great Books/Great Conversation and high-school Socratic pedagogy.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make Great Books/Great Conversation explicit: Publish a clear, school-wide statement that Great Books and Great Conversation are central across K–12, including list of canonical works and a structured plan for integration across disciplines and grade bands.
- Formalize high-school Socratic seminars: Publicly commit to regular, formal Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars in upper grades with clear rubrics, teacher training, and assessment aligned to Socratic pedagogy.
- Expand “Scholé”/restful learning and contemplative time: Publicly articulate a scholarly cadence that includes intentional periods of contemplation, memorization, and rest (Scholé) balanced with rigorous writing, logic, and rhetoric, with implementation guidelines across divisions.
- Strengthen Parent Partnership visuals and programs: Expand explicit “parents as co-teachers” language with a formal parent-education program (book studies, lectures, community catechesis) and public opportunities for parent involvement and visits.
- Strengthen Great Books library programming: Publicize a formal Great Books-reading plan and reciprocal student-guided discussions, field trips to libraries and museums, and integration of primary sources into core subjects across all grades.
- Increase explicit articulation of the curriculum’s liberal-arts structure: Provide a public, map-like articulation of grammar-logic-rhetoric across grades; show how history, literature, math, science, and languages interrelate under a Trivium/Quadrivium framework.
