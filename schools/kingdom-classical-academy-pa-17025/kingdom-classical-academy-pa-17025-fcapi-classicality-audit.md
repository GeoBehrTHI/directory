## Executive Summary

Kingdom Classical Academy (KCA) in Camp Hill, PA presents a distinctly classical, Christ-centered model described publicly as Christ-centered, classical, collaborative, and house-based. The school explicitly frames its mission around forming wisdom and virtue through a genuine love of truth, goodness, and beauty, and it emphasizes partnership with parents in a blended, collaborative program. Public materials consistently use the classical triad—truth, goodness, beauty—and foreground virtue, wisdom, and theological grounding within a community oriented toward spiritual formation and communal practices (e.g., a Statement of Faith and regular parent-school collaboration). The website presents a three-stage classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a liberal-arts orientation, with clear signposts to Great Books-era framing through the classical model, even though explicit Great Books language is less consistently foregrounded on every page. There is strong evidence for religious/metaphysical foundations, formation-oriented purposes, and a liberal curriculum framed by the Trivium and classical aims. Evidence of precise explicit Great Books language is present but less ubiquitous; the program clearly centers on formation, virtue, and a coupled community of parents and teachers.

Most categories align with classical aims, and the strongest evidence lies in a deliberately Christian, virtue-centered, Trinity-informed understanding of education and in the explicit three-stage classical approach. The notably less explicit aspect concerns some of the pedagogy signals (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars, memory-chanting, and explicit “Great Books”/“Solemn scholé” signals) which are not always foregrounded in the public materials; this is the single category that lowers the overall classification to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in that domain. The needle-moving changes described below are designed to move Element 4 toward full CLASSICAL status, with a modest impact on any edge-case interpretive assessments.

What would move the needle:
- Explicitly foreground Socratic pedagogy in upper grades (e.g., seminars, Socratic dialogues, and formal debates) with evidence in public materials.
- Increase explicit references to Great Books and Great Conversation in curriculum communications (e.g., listing canonical authors/works, reading lists, and integration into courses).
- Highlight explicit memory work, chant, and recitation practices in the lower grades as a formal, named pedagogical approach, with evidentiary description on the site.
- Tie liturgical or liturgy-adjacent practices (where present) more clearly to daily public-facing material (even if framed as “liturgical rhythm” or family liturgy) to strengthen the religious founding signals.

Note: The needle-moving changes above would push Element 4 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL without overhauling the school’s public-facing identity.
