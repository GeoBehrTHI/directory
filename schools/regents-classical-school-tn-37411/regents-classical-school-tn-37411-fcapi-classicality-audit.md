## Executive Summary

Regents Classical School Chattanooga (ZIP 37411) presents a partial alignment with classical education. The public materials clearly place a religious (Christian) foundation at the core of its identity, with explicit language like “Raising up citizens of the City of God” and “grounded in Christian faith.” This signals a theological grounding and formation-oriented aim, which aligns with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal categories of the classical rubric. The site also emphasizes a classical branding (e.g., “Classical Education” and community-oriented initiatives like Family Nights and Community Engagement), and it advertises guidance counseling and family involvement, suggesting collaboration with parents and a broader community.

However, the site does not provide explicit evidence of several core classical curriculum and pedagogy components that the Six Essential Elements articulate (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation orientation, grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, explicit Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades, or a living curriculum with a detailed, classical teacher portrait). In short, Regents shows clear virtue-oriented formation, religious grounding, and community engagement, but it stops short of the full, explicit classical curriculum and pedagogy signals described in the knowledge base. Based on category-by-category signals, the school lands in a balanced, but not fully Classical, position.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle

- Expand explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitment and curriculum details, including a stated sequence of liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and key classical texts.
- Publicly articulate a concrete classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars in upper grades, memory work, recitation, dialetic practice) and a plan for the “living curriculum” with a teacher-professional portrait that aligns with the Six Elements.
- Include explicit references to liturgical or communal practices that connect with the religious foundations (e.g., liturgical rhythm, sacred music, communal prayer) to strengthen Element 1 alignment.
- Provide a formal Parent Formation program (book studies, seminar series) with clear schedules and outcomes to intensify and document the Parents & Community alignment in Element 6.
- Publish a detailed curriculum map or annual plan that shows history instruction across the curriculum, including Western civilization and Great Books integration, to move Element 3 toward NOT or closer to Classical.
- Elevate explicit signals of virtuous-mentor faculty presence (e.g., portraits of the teacher, teacher-student mentorship language) to strengthen Element 5.
