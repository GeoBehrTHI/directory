## Executive Summary

Westminster School at Oak Mountain (WSOM) presents a robust klassical Christian identity embedded in a covenantal community and reinforced by a clearly stated formation-focused mission. Public materials consistently frame WSOM as a K-12 classical Christian school aligned with Christ-centered theology and a classical heritage, with explicit emphasis on forming wisdom and virtue, not merely delivering information. The curriculum emphasizes the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), with upper-school expectations such as a capstone thesis, and a strong articulation of forming character in tandem with academic rigor. Public materials also foreground active parental partnership and community involvement.

Overall, by the classical rubric, WSOM leans CLASSICAL across most dimensions, with Curriculum and Pedagogy showing notable alignment to classical practice (e.g., grammar/logic/rhetoric, capstone work, theology-inflected formation). Some areas show partial alignment (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language or fully documented Socratic seminar structures on every level within public materials). Based on the rubric and the public materials analyzed here, WSOM earns a CLASSICAL rating overall, with room to strengthen explicit Great Books / Socratic pedagogy and liturgical/community-practice documentation in public channels.

Key contextual notes drawn from the school’s public materials and the classical-education rubric:

- Core religious/metaphysical grounding is explicit: WSOM identifies as a gospel-centered, classical Christian school in covenantal relation with Oak Mountain Presbyterian Church (PCA) and foregrounds Christ-centered formation (evidenced in admissions messaging and about pages) .
- Formation and virtue are foregrounded as core aims, alongside intellectual growth (e.g., formation vs. information, virtuous aims, and a mission to equip with wisdom, virtue, and eloquence) .
- Curriculum signals align with classical priorities: grammar, logic, rhetoric in early stages; capstone work in upper grades; emphasis on a life of learning and the liberal arts; and a theological/affection-focused orientation in the fine-arts and visual-arts content (evident in upper-school philosophy of learning, and art programs stressing beauty as rooted in divine order) .
- Parental partnership and community life are foregrounded as core operating principles, with explicit language about partnering with parents as co-teachers and inviting family engagement in the classical project .

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The upper school’s emphasis on critical thinking, debate, and rhetoric in the capstone and in the language about learning to think clearly and express persuasively aligns with classical pedagogy (dialogic, rhetorical, seminar-like elements) rather than a uniform project-based approach. The exact public articulation of Socratic seminars or scholé pedagogy is not consistently explicit in the site copy provided, though the capstone and logic/rhetoric emphasis imply a Socratic/rigorous discourse approach in high school .
- The Fine Arts content emphasizes contemplative and aesthetic education (beauty, sacred order) and integrated learning, which can align with scholé/restful aspects in classical pedagogy, though explicit scholarly references to singing/chanting or memory work on the public pages are not readily evident in all materials .

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue (implied via logic/rhetoric emphasis and capstone research), rhetoric, dialogue/discussion (capstone thesis context), contemplative/wonderful learning (artistic framing and integrated learning).
- Negative keywords present: Instances of project-based learning dominance or exclusively experiential learning are not foregrounded in WSOM’s public materials; the absence of explicit, widely advertised Socratic seminars is a gap relative to a full canonical classical pedagogy (as reflected in the lexicon’s negative signals around overreliance on project-based or experiential-only approaches).

Why this rating

- WSOM demonstrates a classical-pedagogy orientation through emphasis on grammar/logic/rhetoric, and a capstone, but with less explicit, consistently documented Socratic seminars and scholé-day language in public materials. This results in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Cited evidence

- Upper School curriculum emphasizes logic and rhetoric and capstone theses; public statements about teaching for clear reasoning and persuasive expression .
- Lower School emphasis on grammar and logic (early Trivium), and the general classical framing across materials .
- The Fine Arts section emphasizes integrated, contemplative rather than purely instrumental pedagogy in some respects (beauty as a central aim) .
- The Six Essential Elements lexicon clarifies canonical pedagogy markers (Socratic seminars, scholé, memorization, etc.) vs. modern-project approaches; WSOM’s site shows some classical elements but not a full, explicit articulation of all canonical pedagogical practices (which informs the SOMEWHAT rating) .
