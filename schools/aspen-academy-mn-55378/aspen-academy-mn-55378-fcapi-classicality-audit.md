# Classicality Audit: Austin Classical School

Executive Summary

- School: Austin Classical School (ACS)
- Website used: https://austinclassical.org/
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Why: ACS explicitly grounds its mission in Christ (“glorify Jesus Christ”), names the transcendentals (“truth, goodness, and beauty”), centers formation in “wisdom” and “virtue,” employs the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), teaches Latin, uses primary sources and Socratic discussion, culminates in rhetoric/senior thesis defense, and implements memory work, recitation, and chants in the lower grades. It partners closely with parents via a collaborative/co-teacher model and has ACCS accreditation, all of which align with the Six Essential Elements positive indicators. Evidence: mission and TGB language; Trivium detail; Latin/logic/rhetoric; primary sources, Socratic discussion, poetry recitations, senior thesis; collaborative/co-teacher model; device ban; ACCS accreditation .

Note on scoring method: This audit follows the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” positive/negative keyword indicators and classicality lexicon. Each category includes a mandatory keyword analysis, a positive vs. negative hit count, percentage estimates, and a rating per the framework .

Overall Assessment

- Aggregate rating: CLASSICAL
- Rationale: Five of six categories present strong classical signals; faculty category is solid but would benefit from more explicit articulation of a classical “portrait” and collegial formation practices. Accreditation by ACCS further corroborates classical commitments .

What would move the needle (Faculty category)

- Publish an explicit “Portrait of a Teacher” that names intellectual and moral virtues, classical formation expectations, and faculty participation in great-books seminars or ongoing classical pedagogy training (aligns with “teachers as models,” “faculty of friends,” “lifelong learners”) .
- Describe formal collegial practices (faculty colloquia, shared inquiry, retreats), reinforcing a “faculty collegium” ideal from the classical tradition .
