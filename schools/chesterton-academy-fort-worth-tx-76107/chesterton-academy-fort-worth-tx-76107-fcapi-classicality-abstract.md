## ABSTRACT

Chesterton Academy of Fort Worth is a CLASSICAL school by design and public portrayal. It presents a Catholic, classical mission centered on truth, goodness, and beauty, with a formation-first orientation (theology, liturgy, and a Culture of Life) that permeates curriculum, pedagogy, and community life. The curriculum is described as liberal-arts-based and integrated, with canonical Great Books and a humanities core taught through Socratic methods, including Latin study and a historically grounded history sequence. Pedagogy blends contemplative/restful elements with Socratic dialogue, as well as a strong communal rhythm supported by a House system and liturgical life. Faculty roles are explicit, with named leaders and a living-curriculum ethos, while parents are positioned as partners and co-educators actively engaged in school life, events, and fundraising. Across six rubric categories—Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; School Purpose and Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community—CAFW demonstrates alignment with classical-education ideals, anchored in Catholic theology and culture, and oriented toward formation, wisdom, and virtue rather than sole workforce preparation. The public materials align with the lexicon of classical education (truth/goodness/beauty; Great Books; Great Conversation; Socratic method; scholé; liturgy; incarna-tion) and are reinforced by the network’s broader Chesterton Schools Foundation. The aggregate classification is CLASSICAL, with no immediate needle-moving changes required; CAFW already exhibits core classical features while continuing to refine and document explicit canonical reading lists, Socratic practice, and family formation initiatives to further strengthen transparency of alignment with the Six Essential Elements.
