## Executive Summary

Second Baptist School University-Model Cypress (SBSUM Cypress) presents a robust alignment with classical Christian education as defined by the Six Essential Elements. Across religious and metaphysical foundations, school purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement, SBSUM Cypress foregrounds a Christ-centered, biblically grounded, virtue-oriented approach that emphasizes formation, wisdom, and mastery of liberal arts through grammar, logic, and rhetoric. The campus explicitly markets itself as a “classical” program embedded in a University-Model framework, with published commitments to a biblical worldview, the Trivium, and a strong partnership with families. The materials consistently reference truth, beauty, and excellence; a disciplined, Socratic/Harkness-inspired pedagogy at upper levels; and active parental engagement as co-educators. Taken together, SBSUM Cypress qualifies as CLASSICAL across all six rubric categories.

Key drivers for CLASSICAL classification include: explicit articulation of classical education through grammar–logic–rhetoric (the Trivium); integration of a biblical worldview across disciplines; on-going emphasis on character, virtue, and formation; Socratic/Harkness-style discourse at appropriate levels; a living-faculty model with defined leadership roles; and a structured partnership with parents as co-teachers. These elements are found in the site content (e.g., explicit description of the Trivium and classical education, christ-centered mission, prayer/discipleship language, and defined faculty leadership) and are reinforced by the classical-lexicon indicators in the knowledge base. 

Note: Because all six categories reach CLASSICAL, there is no category requiring a “needle-moving” plan. The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL, with no categories that are NOT CLASSICAL or SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL dominating the rubric.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- The school’s mission and philosophy are explicitly Christian, emphasizing Christ-centered education and a biblical worldview across programs. Statements include “Christ-centered education that is academically excellent within a caring community” and “biblical worldview across academic disciplines and in all areas of life.” 
- Worship/communal practice is present in arts and liturgical context (singing, sacred music, praise, and reference to faith-based formation). The arts section highlights singing to the Lord and the mental/solicited aim of discerning beauty as part of a sacred framework. 
- The school frames its mission around truth, goodness, and beauty in a theologically infused context, including references to God’s Word, discipleship, and a gospel-centered community. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: truth, beauty, theology (via biblical worldview and Word of God), worship, liturgy (through communal worship and sacred music), reverence, wonder (implied in aesthetic formation and wonder in learning), logos/wisdom (embedded in critical-thinking aims).
- Negative keywords: none observed in public materials (no relativism or secular-humanist signals detected in the materials reviewed).

Why this rating
- The public materials consistently anchor the school in a Christian metaphysical frame and a clear church–family–school partnership, with language tied to truth, beauty, and virtue. In particular, references to Christ-centered education, biblical worldview, Word of God, and communal worship align strongly with the Positive indicators in the Six Elements lexicon. The absence of relativistic or secular signals supports a CLASSICAL rating. 

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- The mission expresses formation of virtue and wisdom through critical thinking and biblical living: “to think critically, live biblically and lead courageously to impact the world for Jesus Christ.” 
- The program emphasizes formation over mere information; the educational aim includes character formation, discipleship, and a biblical worldview across disciplines. 
- The language explicitly centers on truth, beauty, and excellence and a teleology toward moral and intellectual formation (not just job preparation). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, telos (conceptually present in aims like forming character and critical thinking), liberal education/paideia (through classical aims such as grammar–logic–rhetoric), great conversation and classical heritage (implied by “classical” framing and Rhetoric emphasis), human excellence (implied through pursuit of excellence and leadership).
- Negative keywords: workforce/work readiness emphasis (not central to the public materials; the emphasis is on formation and biblical worldview rather than “career-ready” language).

Why this rating
- SBSUM Cypress foregrounds formation, virtue, wisdom, and a classical telos (truth, good, beautiful) embedded in a biblical framework, far surpassing a purely information-based or vocational emphasis. The public materials consistently frame education as formation and moral development, not solely as job training, aligning with CLASSICAL criteria. 

### Element 3: School Curriculum — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- Explicit Trivium framing: The curriculum is described as “grammar, logic, and rhetoric” (Trivium). This is a core hallmark of classical education. 
- The curriculum mentions the Three Liberal Arts framing and the progression through Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric with associated subject matter (grammar: reading, spelling; logic: reasoning; rhetoric: speaking/writing). 
- Content areas include classical languages (Latin, Greek, etc.), literature, poetry, scripture, and a historical emphasis within curriculum planning. Booklists for Logic and Rhetoric are referenced (Rhetoric Booklist/Logic Booklist). 
- Great Books/Great Conversation explicit references are less visible in the public materials, though the program emphasizes classic literature and a classical humanities approach. The site highlights “classic literature,” and “literature and poetry” in the Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric trajectory, but explicit “Great Books” or “Great Conversation” language is not consistently foregrounded in all pages. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, liberal arts; Trivium; classic literature; Latin/Greek/Spanish; great books (explicit in Lexicon expectations but not always explicit on SBSUM pages); history, poetry, scripture; Socratic/ethical/moral education.
- Negative signals: social studies terminology replacing history is listed as a possible negative signal in the knowledge-base lexicon, but SBSUM emphasizes historical content within a classical framework rather than substituting history with social studies; the site’s language does not show a predominant social-studies framing.

Why this rating
- The curriculum demonstrates strong classical elements (grammar–logic–rhetoric, liberal arts framing, classical literature, languages). However, explicit, consistent mention of reading Great Books and participating in the Great Conversation is not uniformly foregrounded across all pages; the material instead emphasizes a classical-methodology and booklists. This combination supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Curriculum rather than a pure CLASSICAL rating. The evidence shows clear classical structure, with some room for more explicit Great Books articulation. 

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- Harkness-style discussion: oval Harkness table discussions are explicitly described as a method for rhetoric and truth presentation in class. 
- Socratic discussions and seminars: upper-school pedagogy includes Socratic discussion, seminars, debates as part of training in rhetoric and critical thinking. 
- Lower-school practices mentioned: singing, chanting, memory work as part of a restful or contemplative dimension (Scholé-like concepts) and to deepen memory. The arts and fine-arts education emphasize memory and musical engagement as part of formation. 
- Upper-level training in rhetoric and public speaking with opportunities to present and argue (speeches, oval discussions). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic seminars, dialectic, disputation; narration/recitation; chanting and memory work; seminar/colloquy; rhetoric exercises; public speaking; contemplation and scholé; memory work; liturgical/calendar integration.
- Negative signals: The Lexicon notes some “negative signals” around project-based or ultra-constructivist approaches; SBSUM’s materials emphasize classical Socratic/hands-on discussion rather than sole project-based learning, aligning with classical pedagogy.

Why this rating
- SBSUM Cypress clearly adopts classical pedagogy: Socratic/seminal discourse, Harkness-style discussions, memorization and recitation in early stages, and a strong emphasis on rhetoric in upper levels. The presence of contemplative/restful dimensions (Scholé-like elements) also aligns with classical pedagogy. The combination yields a CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: partnership with parents, co-teachers, parental formation, book clubs, seminars, family covenant, open campus, parent involvement, events, discipleship. 
- Negative signals: none observed in the public materials.

Why this rating
- SBSUM Cypress articulates a deep, explicit partnership with families and invites parental formation and involvement as a core component of classical education, which aligns with the CLASSICAL indicators for Parents & Community. The open-campus and family-centric language reinforces the co-educator model.
