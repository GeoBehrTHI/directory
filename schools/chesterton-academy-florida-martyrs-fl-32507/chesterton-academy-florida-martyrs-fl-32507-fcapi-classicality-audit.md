## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of The Florida Martyrs (Pensacola) presents as a clearly classical, Catholic high school. Its public-facing materials foreground a Catholic identity, a Great Books liberal-arts curriculum, and an explicit formation-oriented mission (intellectual, spiritual, and moral formation) anchored in truth, goodness, and beauty. The school emphasizes liturgical life (Mass, prayer), a three-pillar formation model, and a public commitment to forming joyful leaders and saints within a Catholic intellectual tradition. Taken together, these signals align strongly with classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements framework, particularly in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy, and complemented by clearly articulated Faculty and Parents/Community commitments.

- The public materials explicitly describe Chesterton Academy Florida Martyrs as “A Classical High School Rooted in the Catholic Faith,” with a Great Books curriculum and an emphasis on Truth, Goodness, and Beauty. This foundational framing strongly supports a CLASSICAL rating for Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal), and it sets a conservative baseline for Categories 3–6 given subsequent corroborating signals. 
- Materials repeatedly reference a three-pillar formation (Intellectual, Spiritual, and Moral/Character formation) and an explicit mission to raise up leaders and saints, underscoring formation over mere information and aligning with the classical telos. This supports CLASSICAL for Element 2 and informs Element 3 (Curriculum) as well. 
- The curricular emphasis includes Great Books, a broad liberal-arts approach, and references to studying canonical figures (e.g., Socrates, Aquinas, Dante), signalling adherence to the Trivium/Quadrivium orientation and Great Conversation concepts central to classical education. 
- Pedagogy signals include a strong emphasis on formation through the Great Books, the integration of Catholic identity across disciplines, and a classroom culture that includes liturgical practices (Mass) and communal formation, which map well to the Positive keywords in the classical lexicon. 
- Faculty presence is evidenced by named leadership (Headmaster) and a disclosed inaugural-year teaching footprint, indicating a functioning school structure with teachers who model and mentor within a Catholic-classical framework. 
- Parent and community engagement is evident through admissions materials, family handbooks, event communications (open houses, parent orientations, Mass/community life), and fundraising communications, illustrating a model that treats parents as engaged participants in the classical education project.
