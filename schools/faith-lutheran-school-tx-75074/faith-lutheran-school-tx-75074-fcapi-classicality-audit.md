## Executive Summary

Faith Lutheran School Plano (Plano, TX 75074) presents a robust alignment with classical Lutheran education as defined by the six-element classical rubric. Public-facing materials consistently foreground a Christ-centered, formation-first mission, the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), the Great Books, Latin, and a liturgical, worship-centered life of formation. The school emphasizes virtue, wisdom, and beauty as real and knowable through Scripture and the Lutheran confessions, and it situates education as formational rather than solely informational. Curriculum, pedagogy, faculty profiles, and parent/community engagement all reinforce a classical Lutheran framework. Based on the public materials reviewed from the school’s site, Faith Lutheran School Plano is CLASSICAL across all six categories.

- Core indicators include: biblical worldview, formation-focused mission, Great Books and liberal-arts emphasis (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin instruction, memorization/recitation, liturgical worship, a living-faculty model of degreed Lutheran teachers, and strong parent/community partnership (e.g., Lion Fund, admissions, campus visits). See supporting materials from the school’s site (e.g., About, Academics, Admissions, Philosophy pages, and staff profiles) for explicit language and examples.

- Evidence of a fully formed classical framework includes: “timeless lessons from the Great Books, Latin, logic, and rhetoric”; a Biblical worldview; formation of mind, body, and soul; worship as a formative daily practice; and faculty described as degreed Lutheran teachers with professional credentials.

- Aggregated assessment: CLASSICAL.

- Daily worship through historic liturgy and hymnody; theology rooted in Scripture and Lutheran confessions; emphasis on catechesis and doctrine.
- Formation of the whole person (mind, body, soul) within a Christian framework; references to “ordo amoris” and worship as shaping imagination.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: Biblical worldview, Christ-centered, Scripture, Gospel, liturgy, worship, catechesis, confessions, theology, hymns, true/good/beautiful (as ethical ideals), complete in Christ.
- Negative keywords: None evident in public materials (no explicit reliance on non-theological or secular absolutisms over theological foundations).

Why this rating

- The materials consistently anchor education in transcendent moral realism and a grounded theology, with liturgical practice and communal worship as central to formation. This satisfies the transcendental, theological, and liturgical markers of a Classical foundation. The absence of any counter-indicative signaling (e.g., secularist framing or relegation of theology to elective status) supports CLASSICAL.

Cited elements: Biblical worldview, liturgy/worship, Christ-centered formation, Lutheran confessions, Scriptural grounding, catechesis.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Recitation, memorization, and memory-building are emphasized, especially in the lower grades (learning through recitation, scriptures, catechism, hymns, poetry, etc.).
- Classical-Lutheran pedagogy is described as formation through liberal-arts practice, with “ordered” learning and the discipline of study (ordo amoris).
- Worship-based formation and liturgical rhythm ground daily learning.
- Upper school methods are described in terms compatible with classical pedagogy (Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetorical training) as part of the model, and there is explicit emphasis on rhetoric and public speaking.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: recitation, memorization, memory work, hymns, scripture, catechesis, Great Books discussions, rhetoric, logic, Latin, discipline, ordo amoris, worship, liturgy, seminars/debates (inferred from pedagogy).
- Negative keywords: Not evident; the content emphasizes contemplative and restfully rigorous aspects along with rigorous discourse, aligning with the classical model rather than a purely project-based or technology-heavy pedagogy.

Why this rating

- Pedagogical language and practices closely map to classical pedagogy: a balance of contemplative/memory-based learning in early years, with Socratic/seminar-based and rhetorical development in higher grades, anchored to liturgical formation.

Cited elements: Recitation/memorization, ordo amoris, worship as formation, rhetoric and public speaking, Great Books discussions, Latin.
