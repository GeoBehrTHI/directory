## Executive Summary

Christian Life Preparatory School (CLPS) TX 76123 exhibits a clear classical orientation embedded within a Christian-theological framework. Public-facing materials emphasize partnership with families, formation of character, and the integration of Biblical principles throughout the curriculum, with explicit references to classical methods and the Trivium. Evidence from the school’s materials shows a Christ-centered mission, a theology-driven worldview, and an emphasis on formation over mere information. While the school foregrounds classical pedagogy and spiritual formation, explicit Great Books engagement and some elements of traditional classical pedagogy (e.g., pronounced Socratic inquiry, liturgical memory work, or a well-articulated faculty living curriculum) are less explicitly documented in the public materials. Taken together, CLPS can be ranked CLASSICAL, with some aspects (notably in curriculum and pedagogy specifics) deserving the label SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key evidence anchors include:
- Statement of Faith and Biblical authority, with explicit Christ-centered theology and doctrinal foundations. 
- Public articulation of a classical approach anchored to the Trivium, with development of a formative educational axis rather than a narrow workforce-focus. 
- Mission and profiles that emphasize formation of heart, soul, mind, and body within a Christian framework and partnership with parents. 
- Explicit language describing CLPS as embracing classical instructional methods within a normative Christian worldview. 
- Documentation of family/parent partnership and community engagement as central to the school’s ethos.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school’s public materials anchor education in Biblical authority (STATEMENT OF FAITH), detailing a Trinitarian God, Jesus Christ, and Holy Spirit, with the Bible described as God’s infallible word and ultimate authority. 
- Mission and program descriptions explicitly reference Christ-centered formation and a Biblical worldview, including statements about forming students to be servants of Jesus Christ and living out the Great Commandments. 
- Public materials assert classical instruction within a Christian framework, e.g., “embraces classical instructional methods within a normative Christian worldview.” 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: theology, sacred scripture, creed, Christ-centered, Bible, biblical principles, worship/contemplation motifs, Logos, divine order, sacred tradition, spiritual formation. These align with the Positive Keyword indicators for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Transcendental Moral Realism; Grounded in Theology; Liturgical and Communal Practices). CLPS demonstrates transcendental moral realism via explicit theological authority and Christian formation; it also shows Grounded in Theology via explicit Biblical integration. The text references Christ-centered formation and a biblical worldview, corroborating liturgical/community themes (though explicit liturgical acts are not exhaustively documented in all pages). 
- Negative keywords: None of the typical secularizing signs (values-neutrality, relativism, secular humanism) appear in the public materials. The content is squarely theistic and doctrinal.

Why this rating
- The materials present a robust theological frame, with explicit Scripture, doctrinal fidelity, and Christ-centered formation, which aligns strongly with the Classical School criterion for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Positive indicators are clearly present; negative secular signals are not). Therefore CLASSICAL.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school foregrounds formation and virtue: the mission states formation of “heart, soul, mind, and body,” and the Profile of a Graduate ties formation to Christian character and lifelong growth. 
- There is explicit language about formation over mere information and the aim toward virtue and wisdom, with references to serving God and living out the Great Commandments. 
- The plan emphasizes that academe should form students to be “critical thinkers” within a Christian framework, not solely workforce-centric outcomes. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education/paideia (implicit in “classical methods”), telos, contemplation (Scholé concepts appear in the classical lexicon though not always named as such on-site), great conversation (as a classical ideal), moral imagination, character development, love of learning. The CLPS materials reference formation and virtue explicitly, aligning with Positive keywords for School Purpose and Goal. 
- Negative keywords: There is no explicit emphasis on college/career readiness or 21st-century workforce primacy in a way that would elicit negative signals for this category; the emphasis remains on formation first, with workforce implications acknowledged only as a broader life outcome.

Why this rating
- The materials emphasize formation, virtue, wisdom, and a teleological aim consistent with classical paideia rather than a narrow job-prep narrative. This matches CLASSICAL criteria for School Purpose and Goal.

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school explicitly aligns with classical education using the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and has a stated classical-methods orientation. This is a primary indicator of classical curriculum design. 
- Public materials describe a structure that supports classical pedagogy through unified, integrated instruction and a focus on formation (as part of a classical framework). The presence of “satellite work” and a formation-oriented approach aligns with a classical, liberal-arts-inspired curriculum, albeit without explicit public confirmation of Great Books engagement or a full Great Conversation mandate. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium), grammar/logical/rhetoric, classical methods, great conversation (implied by classical pedagogy), interdisciplinary/harmony of subjects (integration motif). The school’s stated Trivium-based approach and classical methods align with Positive indicators for Curriculum in the Six Essential Elements lexicon. 
- Negative keywords present: Absence of explicit Great Books engagement in the available public materials; lack of direct emphasis on the Quadrivium or primary-source “Great Books” canon in CLPS’s public statements. The “Great Books” and “Great Conversation” content is not clearly documented on the school site, though it appears in the general classical canon in the knowledge-base lexicon. This reduces the Curriculum to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

Why this rating
- The explicit Trivium-centric curriculum and classical-method framing meet substantial classical criteria, but the absence (in public content) of explicit Great Books engagement and explicit historical canon study (Great Conversation) prevents a full CLASSICAL rating for Curriculum. Therefore, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- CLPS describes itself as embracing classical instructional methods within a Christian framework, and it references formation and memory-work via a classroom approach, including structured memory and collaboration with parents, with satellite work and formation-oriented activities. This indicates a classical influence in pedagogy, even if explicit practice details (e.g., Socratic seminars) are not ubiquitous across public materials. 
- The school’s approach emphasizes active, formation-focused environments, and the use of “satellite work” to deepen understanding, which aligns with some classical pedagogy tenets (memory, recitation, catechesis, etc.) but lacks consistent on-site evidence of sustained Socratic seminars or explicit Scholé-like contemplative rhythms in public pages. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue (as a potential pedagogy in the lexicon), recitation/memory work, contemplative learning, formation through imitation, master-teacher models, and memory-based review. The CLPS materials indicate a mix of active, rigorous instruction with formation, which aligns with classical Pedagogy Indicators in the lexicon. However, explicit, consistent use of Socratic seminars or structured dialectic in daily practice is not clearly documented in the public-facing materials. 
- Negative keywords present: The public content does not clearly emphasize project-based learning-only or purely experiential learning as an exclusive pedagogy, but it also does not consistently foreground Socratic seminars or Scholé as ongoing practice. The lack of explicit, repeated Socratic/Dialectic or Scholé signals in public materials weighs toward a weaker classification in Pedagogy.

Why this rating
- Pedagogy shows clear classical orientation through explicit mention of classical methods and formation, yet the lack of recurring, explicit Socratic/dialectical practice and Scholé in public statements places it in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than Classically robust. The dual nature of formation-focused but not consistently documented Socratic pedagogy supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

### Element 5: Faculty — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials show administrative and teacher presence (Administration & Staff; Lower School/Secondary Teachers pages), but there is no explicit language indicating that the faculty themselves are the “living curriculum,” exemplars of virtue, or contain a formal portrait of the teacher as a model for students (the “portrait of a teacher” concept). The elementary/secondary team pages confirm teacher roles, but not the deeply articulated “living curriculum” or mentorship ethos. 
- The knowledge-base lexicon’s Faculty indicators emphasize a “living curriculum,” model teachers, and a strong faculty culture as central to classical education. The CLPS materials do not explicitly claim this level of faculty description in public statements. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Mentoring role, living curriculum, model teachers, portrait of a teacher. These terms are not prominently documented in the CLPS public materials; thus, there is a paucity of explicit positive faculty signals in the public-facing content.
- Negative keywords present: There is no clear strengthening of a non-classical faculty narrative; rather, there is simply a lack of explicit classical-faculty language. The absence of the “living curriculum” phrasing and other faculty-centered classical indicators in the public content points toward a weaker classification in this category.

Why this rating
- Despite having a teacher presence and official staff pages, the key classical-faculty signals (model-teachers as a living curriculum, portraits, and faculty-led formation philosophy) are not clearly documented in the public materials. This aligns with a NOT CLASSICAL rating for Element 5.

- The school provides access points and visits for parents (e.g., public information sessions, family information meetings) and maintains a calendar of events that invites parental involvement, consistent with community engagement as a core feature. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: Partnership with families; parent formation; home-school collaboration; parental engagement; family-centered learning; living tradition in the family; parental leadership; open campus; hospitality; parent in formation.
- Negative keywords: None evident in public content for this category; the CLPS materials actively promote parental involvement rather than consumer-facing or minimal-engagement models.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates strong alignment with the Community/Parents Prominence and Family Partnership indicators. The public materials emphasize partnership with parents, parental formation, and a family/church-integrated educational ethos, consistent with CLASSICAL standards for Parents & Community.
