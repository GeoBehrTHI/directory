## Executive Summary

Lafayette Academy MO 63367 presents a clearly classical orientation across most dimensions of governance, curriculum, and pedagogy, with strong emphases on virtue formation, a liberal-arts curriculum, and Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in the upper grades. Public materials frequently highlight the school’s classical identity, Great Books orientation, primary-source history, teacher-led instruction, and a four-virtue framework. There is explicit attention to truth, virtue, and wisdom as central aims, and to limited use of screens in favor of active, memory- and discussion-based learning. However, several elements that would push the school toward a fuller, traditional classical profile—explicit theology or liturgical practice, a stated trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and a more explicit “living curriculum” faculty framework—are less prominent in the public-facing materials.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key evidence driving this assessment includes:
- Clear classical framing and liberal-arts content in mission and program descriptions, including virtues and a long-standing classical posture (Fortitude, Justice, Prudence, Temperance) and a rhetoric-oriented upper school path in some materials.  
- Strong emphasis on “great books,” primary-source history, and Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades, plus explicit statements about forming minds and character through a classical liberal-arts program.  
- Explicit public statements about “teacher-led, low-tech classrooms,” strong literacy pedagogy, and the avoidance of screens, which align with scholé/restful aspects and memory-focused practices. 
- Evidence of active parental engagement and open events (e.g., prospective open houses, tours), but comparatively less explicit articulation of parents as co-teachers or a formal “living curriculum” faculty portrait in public materials.

The school’s public materials emphasize the four classical virtues, a strong liberal-arts program, true history via primary sources, and a commitment to forming minds and character.
