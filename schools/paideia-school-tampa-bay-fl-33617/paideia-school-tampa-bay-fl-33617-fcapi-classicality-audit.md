## Executive Summary

The Paideia School Tampa Bay (33617) presents a robust classical Christian educational program that aligns with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School. Across all six categories, the public-facing materials consistently emphasize formation, virtue, wisdom, and the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) taught through a structured Trivium, with explicit references to Great Books, the Great Conversation, and a Christ-centered worldview. Public materials also highlight a “Living Curriculum,” strong teacher mentorship, and a formal partnership with parents as co-educators. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL, with strongest alignment evident in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, followed closely by Purpose/Goal, Faculty, and Parents & Community.

Key insights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are intentional and explicit, anchored in Christ, Scripture, theology, and liturgical/community life. Public materials describe a Christ-centered curriculum and daily spiritual formation, aligning with Positive Keyword indicators for Element 1. Evidence includes references to Christ-centered culture, prayer, and a theology-driven worldview.  
- The school foregrounds formation over information and emphasizes virtues such as wisdom, virtue, truth, beauty, and a Human flourishing telos. Public statements regularly frame education as formation toward character and faith, with explicit references to Great Books and the Great Conversation as part of the classical mission.  
- The Curriculum and Pedagogy are explicitly classical: Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), memorization through songs/chanting in the Grammar stage, Socratic/Dialectic in the Dialectic stage, and rhetoric and public speaking in the Rhetoric stage, plus engagement with Great Books. This is reinforced by program descriptions of Grammar, Dialectic, and Rhetoric, and by explicit notes on Latin and classical Liberal Arts.   
- Faculty are framed as the living curriculum and mentors, with a village-like faculty culture and explicit emphasis on virtue and character modeling. This is visible in the site’s “Living Curriculum” language and faculty-staff emphasis. 
- Parents and community are positioned as true partners in education, with explicit language about co-teaching, parent formation, and open access for parent engagement. 

Overall rank: CLASSICAL

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

- Evidence

  - There is explicit language about theology integration and a Christian worldview guiding all subjects and community life. 
  - Wording emphasizes liturgical/community rhythm and the sacred, including references to prayer and worship as part of daily life and formation. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: Christ-centered, theology, Scripture, God, prayer, liturgy, sacred tradition, Christian worldview, Great Books (as a part of a Christian classical curriculum), worship, spiritual formation.   
  - Negative keywords observed: none evident (no secularist or relativist signals detected in public materials).
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive ~7, Negative ~0; Positive indicators comprise essentially all relevant metaphysical signals, yielding ~100% positivity.
- Why this rating
  - Public materials consistently articulate a theologically grounded, sacramental, and liturgically integrated frame for education, with explicit Christ-centered framing and theological formation as core aims. This strongly aligns with Positive Keyword indicators for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and shows no evidence of the Negative Signals described in the Six Elements lexicon.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Formation is explicit: “the purpose of education is formation, not merely information” and to cultivate wisdom and virtue. 
  - The mission declares educated students who love the Lord and learning and become virtuous citizens, i.e., true human flourishing with virtue and wisdom as telos. 
  - The Great Books/Great Conversation and the Great Ideas are positioned as central to the classical mission, joined with a Christian worldview. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, Great Books, Great Conversation, truth/goodness/beauty, liberal arts, Christian worldview, formation of affections, virtue ethics, moral imagination.  
  - Negative keywords observed: none (no emphasis on workforce-readiness or career-prep as the primary aim; college-advising is framed as post-secondary exploration rather than mere job-readiness).
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive ~8, Negative ~0; ~100% positive signals.
- Why this rating
  - The school makes formation (virtue, wisdom) and the realness of truth, goodness, and beauty central to its purpose, consistent with classical aims. The explicit inclusion of Great Books and a Christian telos supports a CLASSICAL rating.

### Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL

  - History across subjects and engagement with literature, philosophy, and the arts are embedded in the classical design, with a stated aim of forming “a whole human person” through the liberal arts.

  - Negative keywords observed: none related to modern “project-based” or purely utilitarian curricula.
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive ~9, Negative ~0; ~100% positive signals.
- Why this rating
  - Curriculum is explicitly structured around traditional liberal arts and the Trivium, with emphasis on Great Books and historical engagement, aligning strongly with CLASSICAL expectations.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Pedagogy includes Socratic discussion in the Dialectic and rhetoric-focused activities in the Rhetoric stage, with opportunities for seminars, speeches, and public discourse. 
  - Lower grades employ singing, chants, and memory work (grammars) and a disciplined approach to memory and recitation; the pedagogy explicitly specifies a restfully paced "Scholé" and contemplative elements. 
  - The presence of a living curriculum and teachers modeling virtuous conduct as part of instruction (faculty as mentors) is highlighted. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: Socratic seminar/Dialogue, Dialectic, Great Conversation, memory work, chanting, recitation, pursuit of truth, rhetoric, upper-school speeches, Scholé/restful learning, formation through imitation.  
  - Negative keywords observed: none (the site does not foreground an experiential/project-based-only approach; rather, it foregrounds classical methods like memorization and Socratic inquiry).
  - Quantitative estimate: Positive ~9, Negative ~0; ~100% positive signals.
- Why this rating
  - The teaching methods map directly to classical pedagogy: memory and chant in early years, Socratic discourse in upper years, and rhetoric/public speaking training, with contemplation and formational aims. This strongly supports CLASSICAL.

### Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL

### Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The school presents a partnership with families as essential: co-teachers, parental formation, and an open-campus ethos with opportunities for parent involvement and engagement. 
  - Admissions and parent resources emphasize collaboration, with families invited to participate (tours, admissions processes) and to engage in classical education discussions (e.g., parent guides, summer reading, book clubs). 
  - Statements describe a Christian community where parents are instrumental in forming the child and the family’s faith formation. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords observed: partnership with families, co-teachers, parental formation, open campus, parent engagement, home-school alignment, living tradition in the family, household rhythms (as reflected in the Six Elements lexicon).
  - Negative keywords observed: none (no consumer/customer framing; no minimal parent involvement signals).

- Why this rating
  - Public materials consistently describe parents as co-educators and integral to the classical formation process, with active opportunities for parent involvement and formation.

Aggregate assessment across all six categories: CLASSICAL

- The school demonstrates a comprehensive alignment with the classical education rubric: a theologically grounded, formation-centered mission; a curriculum built on the Trivium and Great Books; a pedagogy combining memorization with Socratic inquiry and rhetoric; a faculty culture that embodies the living curriculum; and a robust, family-centered community.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Based on this evaluation, no major needle-moving changes are required to reach full CLASSICAL status. If desired, minor enhancements could include:
  - More explicit articulation of liturgical practices (beyond Christ-centered community) as a formalized liturgy or chapel rhythm, to further align with Liturgical and Communal Practices criteria.
  - Additional public-facing articulation of the Quadrivium components (music, astronomy, mathematics) in the curriculum overview to reinforce the full spectrum of liberal arts beyond the Trivium.
  - Documentation of long-term outcomes (e.g., a published Portrait of a Graduate with measurable virtues and intellectual dispositions aligned to the Great Conversation) to strengthen the Element 2/perception of formation.
