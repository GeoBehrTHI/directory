## Executive Summary

Donahue Academy of Ave Maria (Donahue Academy) presents a robust Catholic, classical education profile. Public materials consistently frame the school as Catholic and classical, with explicit emphasis on Christ-centered formation, the liberal-arts Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and a curriculum and pedagogy designed to cultivate virtue, wisdom, and a lifelong love of learning. Evidence across the school’s site highlights the centrality of Catholic identity (daily Mass, sacramental life, theology, and liturgy), a formation-focused mission (raising saints, pursuing truth, goodness, and beauty), and a structured classical pedagogy (grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, original sources, and a rhetoric-inflected upper school). The combination of religious formation, formation-first aims, and classical curricular- and pedagogical structures supports an overall CLASSICAL rating, with strong alignment in all six rubric categories.

Key point: the school explicitly positions itself as Catholic and classical, highlighting the transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) and a formation-first approach across mission, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and community. This orientation is reinforced by statements about daily Mass, sacramental life, and the integration of Catholic teaching into every aspect of education, as well as a clear articulation of classical educational tenets (transmission and integration of knowledge; learning from original sources; developmentally appropriate sequencing; quest for essential truths; cultivation of wonder).   

Executive ranking (aggregate): CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogical structure aligns with the classical model: lower-school practices emphasize memory, recitation, singing, and “scholé” rhythms (restful, contemplative learning), while upper-school pedagogy emphasizes Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric training (speeches, oratory). Public materials explicitly describe the Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric progression and associated teaching modes. 
- The site describes upper-school rhetoric, with opportunities for students to give speeches and engage in rhetoric training; it also references Socratic-style elements in upper-level work. This is evident in the description of the Rhetoric Stage and rhetoric-focused activities. 
- Lower-school practices include singing, chanting, and memory work as part of the learning process, consistent with classical pedagogy. The classical lexicon and the school materials describe these elements in the pedagogical framework. 
- The Fine Arts and performance emphasis demonstrates an experiential dimension to learning, but is framed within the classical-mindset context (formation through memory, recitation, and the arts). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords observed: scholé, memory, chanting, recitation, narration, Socratic seminar, dialectic, discourse, rhetoric, debate, seminar, Harkness-style discussion, upper-school speeches, formation through practice, living books, original-sources pedagogy, and a calm or restful pace (Scholé). These terms appear across the school’s public materials and the classical pedagogy framing.  
- Negative keyword indicators: Some public materials reflect a balance with rest (Scholé) and a layered approach (not purely project-based or solely experiential), which aligns with classical pedagogy rather than the negative signals listed in the lexicon (e.g., “project-based dominant” or “experiential-only” without formation). The explicit negative signals in the lexicon are not present in the Donahue materials. 

Why this rating
- Donahue Academy’s pedagogy maps clearly to classical methods: rest-filled, contemplative “Scholé days” alongside Socratic/perimeter discussions and rhetorical training in the upper grades. The lower grades emphasize memory and chant-based retention, while the upper grades emphasize philosophical dialogue and rhetoric. This combination supports CLASSICAL in Pedagogy.
