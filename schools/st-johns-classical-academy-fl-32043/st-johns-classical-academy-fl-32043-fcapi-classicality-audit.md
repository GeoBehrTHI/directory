## Executive Summary

St. Johns Classical Academy Florida (SJCA) presents a clearly classical-inflected profile rooted in virtue, truth, and beauty, with explicit aims toward forming virtuous citizens through a classical, content-rich curriculum. Public materials emphasize a Western classical heritage, a community ethos centered on character and civic responsibility, and a motto built around the transcendental triad of truth, goodness, and beauty. The school’s stated mission and culture plan highlight formation of mind and character, rather than mere job-readiness, and portray teachers modeling virtue and students engaging in thoughtful discussion. Taken together, SJCA shows strong alignment with several core elements of classical education, especially in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, and Faculty/Community orientation. Yet some classical-signaling elements—such as explicit Great Books / Trivium pedagogy, visible liturgical practices, and documented, high-fidelity classical pedagogy across all grades—are less clearly enumerated in public-facing materials. Consequently, SJCA sits toward the middle-to-upper end of the Classical spectrum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with several robust Classical cues and a few areas where public materials could more explicitly codify all six elements of a full classical model.

Key takeaways at a glance:

- Strong Classical signaling in Foundational beliefs and goals (truth/goodness/beauty, virtue, Western classical heritage), plus explicit formation language and civic aims. This supports CLASSICAL in Categories 1 and 2.
- Curriculum signaling is present but not uniformly explicit about all classical milestones (e.g., Great Books / Trivium pedagogy is not consistently spelled out in public materials), creating a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL reading for Element 3.
- Pedagogy shows some classical signals (e.g., Great Conversations appears in a school calendar reference), but lacks consistently explicit articulation of Socratic seminars or scholé rhythms across all grade bands in the material available to us, yielding SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Element 4.
- Faculty messaging aligns with classical ideals (teachers as living curriculum, modeling virtue); Element 5 leans CLASSICAL.
- Parent/community signals exist, with emphasis on partnership and a values-centric culture, but explicit statements about parents as co-teachers or ongoing parent-formation programs are less evident, yielding SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Element 6.
- Overall, SJCA would move toward full CLASSICAL with targeted clarifications and expansions around: Great Books / Trivium emphasis, explicit Socratic pedagogy across grades, liturgical/community practices, and stronger parent-formation messaging.

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The school’s public materials emphasize a culture grounded in truth, goodness, and beauty, inspired by the Western classical tradition, and reference a nine-virtue framework (Citizenship, Courage, Honesty, Humility, Integrity, Perseverance, Respect, Responsibility, Wisdom). This is stated in the mission and culture texts, where the motto and culture statements explicitly anchor character formation in transcendent ideals.
- The motto “Learn the True, Do the Good, Love the Beautiful” functions as a lived-metaphysical orientation (transcendental ideals made concrete in daily practice). The same source notes the Western classical tradition as the cultural backbone.
- The Hillsdale-affiliated framing and the emphasis on virtuous living align with transcendental/teleological aims and moral realism in classical education lexicons.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; moral/ethical formation; classical tradition; great conversation (as an implicit aim in virtuous culture); formation of the soul (implied by character-and-mind emphasis). The public materials repeatedly invoke truth, goodness, beauty and virtue as central aims.
- Negative keywords present: minimal or no signals of relativism, secular humanism, or anti-theistic trends; the public materials lean away from value-neutral stances. The absence of explicit secular/neutral language supports the positive alignment. (Basis in the Six Essential Elements negative keyword set; no explicit alignment with relativism or secular humanism found in the public SJCA materials.)

Why this rating

- The presence of transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) and virtue-driven formation, plus a Western classical framework, strongly signals a Classical orientation for foundational metaphysical aims. The lack of explicit theology-laden or liturgical practice statements in the publicly available text lowers this from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in strict rubric terms, but the overall orientation remains solidly classical in spirit.

- Negative keywords present: There is little public indication of heavy reliance on experiential or project-based pedagogy as the dominant approach; however, the precise balance of activities (Socratic vs. project-based vs. lecture) is not fully specified in the public text. Why this rating

However, the public-facing materials do not consistently spell out a full matrix of classic pedagogical methods (e.g., universal use of Socratic seminars, memory work, chanting, or scholé across all grades). This partial signaling supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly articulate and publish a Great Books program and a Seven Liberal Arts framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across grade levels, with a clear cross-disciplinary plan. Include direct references to primary texts, canonical authors, and a regular Great Conversation sequence in the curriculum pages and promotional materials.

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide explicit, school-wide documentation of pedagogical methods (Socratic seminars, Scholé/restful learning days, recitation/memory work, and upper-school rhetoric/deliberative speech) with example units and sample teacher moves. Include a public-facing Pedagogy Guide or “Classroom in Practice” page that demonstrates how classical methods are implemented in K–12.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Develop and publish formal parent-formation programs (e.g., classical book clubs, study guides, parent lectures) and strengthen explicit “parents as co-teachers” messaging. Create a visible open-door policy and a scheduled cadence of parent education events to reinforce the partnership in formation and culture.

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): If the school intends to deepen the theologic dimension in its classical framework, publish a public theology-integrated plan (even if ecumenical) showing how theology informs the curriculum and community life, and consider occasional liturgical or contemplative practices (even if optional) to strengthen communal worship/rituals in alignment with liturgical practices.

- Element 5 (Faculty): Strengthen and publish a formal “living curriculum” portrait of the ideal teacher and a teacher-formation program that includes mentor-teaching, continuous professional development in classical pedagogy, and a documented faculty profile/performance framework.

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): While formation is strong, more explicit articulation of long-term telos (the ultimate aims beyond civic virtue) and the integration of wisdom standards across grades could reinforce the CLASSICAL orientation.

# ABSTRACT

St. Johns Classical Academy Florida demonstrates a robust classical orientation in foundational aims (truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; Western classical tradition) and a formation-centered purpose (formation of mind and character; intelligent, virtuous citizens). The faculty messaging aligns with the classical ideal of teachers as virtuous models and living curriculum. Public materials show some classical pedagogy signals (notably the reference to Great Conversations) and a generally thoughtful, discussion-focused culture. However, explicit articulation of core classical curricular milestones (Great Books; Seven Liberal Arts), comprehensive, school-wide classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, Scholé), liturgical/community practices, and formal parent-formation programs are less evident in the available public materials. Taken together, SJCA lands in the category SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with some CLASSICAL strengths in Foundational Foundations, Purpose/Goal, and Faculty, and opportunities to more fully codify and publicly publish the remaining elements of a full classical program.

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Primary strengths: Clear formation-focused mission; explicit truth/goodness/beauty orientation; virtuous-culture framing; teachers as models and living curriculum; evidence of classical elements in “Great Conversations” scheduling and Western-tradition emphasis.
- Primary opportunities for needle-moving improvements: concrete articulation of a full Great Books / Trivium curriculum; explicit, school-wide classical pedagogy; visible liturgical/communal practice; formal parent-formation and parent-co-teaching structures; and a formal “faculty living curriculum” framework.
