## Executive Summary

Heritage School TX 78624 presents a robust Classical Christian profile. Public materials consistently frame the school as “classical” and “Christ-centered,” with explicit emphasis on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, and the Great Conversation as core curricular and cultural anchors. The site foregrounds formation, virtue, and wisdom as primary aims, rather than mere information transfer, and it positions faith, character, and servant leadership as central outcomes. Across categories, the strongest signals come from explicit religious/theological framing, a liberal-arts curriculum built around classical methods, and a mission-centered faculty culture. While the website clearly articulates major elements of classical education (Element 1–3 and Element 6), some details of pedagogy and faculty portraiture are less explicit on public pages, which yields a few areas that are only partially explicit in classical pedagogy and faculty narrative (Categories 4–5). Overall, Heritage School TX 78624 aligns most strongly with classical education principles and earns a CLASSICAL rating overall.

Aggregate Assessment
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Taken together, the school aligns with classical education norms (and explicitly identifies itself as classical Christian), yielding a CLASSICAL overall rating.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide explicit public documentation of specific classical teaching methods in practice (e.g., explicit descriptions of Socratic seminars in upper grades, memory work and recitation programs, scholé days, or Paideia-style seminars). A public-facing section detailing daily or yearly pedagogical routines would strengthen the category from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a formal portrait of the faculty as the living curriculum, including statements about teacher development, mentorship roles, and collaborative professional culture (e.g., faculty seminars on classical pedagogy, teacher-mentor programs, and a stated “living curriculum” philosophy).
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Continue expanding formal pathways for parent formation (book studies, study groups, liturgical/family-learning events) and clearly articulate how parents participate as co-teachers in specific programs beyond admissions and events.
