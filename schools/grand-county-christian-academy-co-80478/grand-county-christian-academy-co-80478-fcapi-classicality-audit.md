## Executive Summary

Grand County Christian Academy (Tabernash, CO, 80478) (public-facing materials primarily hosted by Winter Park Christian Church) presents a religiously oriented program embedded within a church community. Public materials describe spiritual formation, discipling in Jesus Christ, and faith-based activities, with explicit references to weekly Bible study, worship-oriented programming, and church-led youth/children’s ministry connected to GCCA. While these elements demonstrate a strong religious foundation and communal practices, the site does not publicly articulate the liberal-arts curriculum, classical pedagogical methods, or explicit Great Books/Gaulic–style Trivium framework characteristic of a classical education. Consequently, across the six classical criteria, GCCA leans toward a predominantly Religious foundation with limited traditional classical formalism.

- Religion-based foundations: CLASSICAL
- School purpose and goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL
- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL
- Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Overall, the aggregate assessment is NOT CLASSICAL, with notable Classical-influenced tendencies in religious formation and family/community orientation. The site does, however, provide some elements that align with classical-education speech (e.g., formation through a shared liturgical life and partnerships with families), but gaps remain in traditional classical content and pedagogy.

Note: The assessment relies on explicit textual signals from GCCA’s public-facing content (primarily the Winter Park Christian Church site and GCCA-affiliated pages), and cross-checks them against the Positive/Negative keyword indicators in the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School framework.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school aimed to reach CLASSICAL status across all six categories, explicit additions would help:
 - Curriculum: articulate and publicize a classical-canon curriculum (e.g., Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, primary historical sources) alongside or integrated with biblical study, clarifying how Western civilization and classical texts anchor the curriculum.
 - Pedagogy: introduce and publicize classical teaching methods (Socratic dialogue in upper grades, Scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, and formation through imitation and practice) in line with classical ideals.
 - Faculty: develop a “living curriculum” or teacher-formation profile that presents teachers as virtuous exemplars and mentors, with visible paths for teacher development aligned with classical pedagogy.
 - Parents & Community: formalize and communicate a partnership framework with families as co-educators (parent training, classical book clubs, parent formation events) and maintain an open-campus posture that invites parental engagement.
 - Category 1 (Religious Foundations) and Category 2 (Purpose/Goal) can be strengthened by explicitly tying formation to virtues and the truth-good-beauty triad within a classical framework, while maintaining the faith-based orientation.
