## Executive Summary

Liberty Christian Classical School Mi 49106 presents a strongly classical, Christian education model focused on formation, virtue, and a biblically integrated liberal-arts program. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered worldview, Biblical truth, and a partnership with parents, together with a traditional classical curriculum that includes Latin and a scaled, grammar–logic–rhetoric orientation in spirit, supported by Hillsdale College’s K-12 curriculum framework. Evidence across the site highlights religious foundations (biblical truth, theology, worship leanings), a formation-focused purpose (virtue, wisdom, service), and a community orientation (parent partnership, ongoing communications). Some elements essential to a fuller classical pedagogy—explicit adoption of Great Books/Great Conversation, and explicit Socratic/seminar-style pedagogy in upper grades—are present in limited public-facing language, resulting in a nuanced profile with a mix of strong classical alignment and some inconspicuous gaps.

- Why this rating
  - The weight of explicit biblical/theological language and Christ-centered framing strongly supports a CLASSICAL classification for foundational religious and metaphysical orientation. The materials foreground transcendental moral realism (truth, virtue, beauty) through a biblically integrated framework. 

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence

  - The school positions education as formation rather than information and emphasizes human flourishing, virtue, and wisdom as central aims. The language about virtue, wisdom, and the pursuit of excellence is recurrent. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: virtue, wisdom, formation, goodness, truth, beauty, human excellence, moral development, service, lifelong love of learning.
  - Negative keywords present: none directly (no explicit emphasis on job-prep or career outcomes in public-facing mission statements).
  - Presence: Strong positive signal across mission and formation language.
- Why this rating
  - The explicit articulation of formation, virtue, wisdom, and a non-job-oriented educational telos supports CLASSICAL in this category. The emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty as real aligns with classical metaphysical aims. 

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The Academic Program states a traditional, classical liberal arts education, including phonics and cursive in younger grades, Latin in grades 3–12, and a broad distribution of history, literature, mathematics, science, fine arts, and physical education across grade levels. This confirms a classical curricular frame and the inclusion of several core liberal-arts elements. 
  - The school’s Hillsdale College K-12 Curriculum affiliation and the emphasis on a traditional liberal-arts structure further anchor the classical curricular orientation. 
  - The site repeatedly emphasizes "Pursue the Good, the True, and the Beautiful" and the aim to acquire wisdom and virtue, consistent with classical aims for a curriculum that integrates moral philosophy and aesthetics. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: classical liberal arts, Latin, history, literature, mathematics, science, phonics, cursive, integrated curriculum, Great Books/Great Conversation language is less explicit (public pages emphasize "great artists" rather than canonical Great Books), wonder and beauty, moral philosophy, sacred order.
  - Negative keywords present: explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language is not consistently visible on public-facing pages; no strong emphasis on seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as explicit cataloged prerequisites in the curriculum map published on the site.
  - Presence: Moderate positive signal; core liberal-arts elements appear, but explicit Great Books/Seven Liberal Arts articulation and explicit Socratic dialogue are not clearly stated in public-facing materials.
- Why this rating
  - The curriculum clearly reflects classical structure through liberal-arts framing and Latin/historic-literary emphasis, but the public articulation of the full suite of classic elements (notably explicit Great Books, Great Conversation, and the explicit grammar/logic/rhetoric triad) is not consistently published. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL in Curriculum.

The Art Teacher job description emphasizes curriculum delivery within a classical framework rather than a Socratic seminar approach. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

  - Negative keywords present: explicit Socratic dialogue, seminars, debates, and high-school rhetoric instruction are not publicly documented as standard pedagogy across grades.
  - Presence: Mixed signals; formation and contemplative aims exist, but the hallmark classical pedagogy (Socratic method, seminars, debates, rhetoric training) is not clearly documented in public materials.
- Why this rating
  - While the school presents a formation-based, classical-friendly orientation, the absence of clearly articulated Socratic pedagogy and deliberate upper-level discourse practices on public-facing materials justifies a NOT CLASSICAL designation for Pedagogy at this time. The gap suggests potential for future alignment through explicit pedagogy statements and practice.
