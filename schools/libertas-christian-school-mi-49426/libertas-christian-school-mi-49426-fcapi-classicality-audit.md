## Executive Summary

Libertas Christian School MI 49426 presents a predominantly classical framework with strong religious grounding and a clearly articulated formation-centric mission. Public materials emphasize a biblical worldview, a distinctly classical paideia, and the triadic grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, alongside evidence of Great Books-oriented reading, Latin/Greek language study, and formal memory/recitation practices. The school foregrounds formation and virtue alongside intellectual attainment, aligning with core classical ideals. While most indicators are classical, the pedagogical section shows a mix of traditional memory/recitation and chapel-driven, faith-integrated practices that are robust but less explicit about systemic Socratic/seniors’ seminar-based methods in the public-facing public materials. Overall, Libertas scores CLASSICAL, with a notable but isolated area that reads as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (pedagogy emphasis), suggesting targeted enhancements could push it toward a fully unified classical pedagogy across all grades.

Executive-level takeaways
- Religious/metaphysical foundations: Strong and explicit. The school centers on a Christian, biblical framework with worship and a stated faith–formation orientation. Evidence includes distinctive Christian branding, chapels, and explicit statements of faith, all of which align with the “theology and liturgy” dimension of classical schooling. 
- Purpose/goal: Virtue- and wisdom-centered, formation-oriented rather than job-first, with explicit aims to form the whole person through a classical framework. 
- Curriculum: Rich in liberal arts anchors (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Great Books orientation, and multilingual study (Latin/Greek). History is engaged across disciplines, and there is explicit emphasis on “great books” and “great conversation.” 
- Pedagogy: Combines contemplative/restful elements with active memory/recitation and exploratory learning; explicit Socratic practices are less clearly documented in public-facing pages, which places this dimension as the one most open to strengthen to reach a fully classical pedagogy. fileciteturn18
- Faculty: Described as virtuous, living exemplars, and as a core part of the school’s living curriculum; evidence of mentorship-oriented culture and role-modeling. 
- Parents & Community: Strong emphasis on family partnership, parent involvement, and open-campus access; a foundation for classical-parent collaboration, including community events and school visits. 

Aggregate rating: CLASSICAL

Note on needle-moving changes: Because the school already demonstrates a robust classical orientation across most categories, and the one category with relatively less explicit public documentation (Pedagogy) points to a potential area for deeper articulation rather than a fundamental misalignment, the school does not require major structural changes to be considered CLASSICAL. To push toward a fully integrated, explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across upper grades, concrete steps below could help.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any needle-moving improvements were desired to solidify CLASSICAL across all six categories, the most impactful steps would include:
  - Pedagogy (Element 4): publicly articulate and expand more explicit upper-school Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy and Great-Conversation-style discussions in a consistent, grade-spanning framework. Publish a concrete Pedagogy Map that shows where Socratic method, seminar formats, and debate are used in each grade, how students progress in rhetorical skills, and how mentorship and critique are integrated into daily learning. This would convert SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL in pedagogy.
  - Public documentation of Great-Books and Great-Conversation integration across core subjects (Curriculum/Element 3): include explicit examples of Great Books in history, literature, philosophy, and science that are actively taught and discussed in the classroom, with representative titles and canonical authors to demonstrate a truly integrated Great-Books approach across grades.

- Concrete, actionable items to consider:
  - Publish a formal Pedagogy Manifesto or handbook that details: daily or weekly Socratic seminars, memorialization strategies (recitation/chant practices) across grades, and a year-by-year progression of rhetorical training (i.e., student speeches, declamation, and public speaking opportunities).
  - Expand explicit references to Great Conversation and Great Books in the curriculum pages for each grade band, including a cross-disciplinary map showing how classic texts are used in English, History, and Philosophy/Religious Studies.

- These changes would strengthen the Pedagogy category from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL, while preserving the current foundations in faith, formation, and classic curriculum.
