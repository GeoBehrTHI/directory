## Executive Summary

Arche Classical Academy (Carlisle, PA 17013) presents a firmly Classical-oriented profile across most core categories of classical education. Public materials from the school emphasize a Christ-centered, biblical framework anchored in Classical Christian Education (CCE), a curriculum built around the grammar–logic–rhetoric sequence, the Great Books, Latin study, and an integrated view of truth, goodness, and beauty. The school also foregrounds partnership with families and a strong biblical worldview, including Sola Scriptura-style framing of knowledge and a formative, virtue-centered aim. Evidence from the school’s site includes explicit statements about biblically shaped curriculum, a Christ-centered approach, and the Great Books, along with notes on parent involvement and ACCS affiliation. 

Category-by-category signals (using the Six Essential Elements framework) indicate a CLASSICAL orientation in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. Pedagogy presents a mix that leans Classical but with less explicit articulation of Socratic/seminar-based methodologies in public-facing materials, which places Element 4 in the realm of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL. The aggregate assessment, taking all six categories into account, is therefore CLASSICAL. The school shows strong alignment to traditional classical aims (formation, Great Books, liberal arts, and a communal, faith-centered ethos), with the most notable gaps being explicit, high-profile articulation of certain Socratic pedagogy signals in public materials. The key insights are captured in the category snapshots below and summarized in the # ABSTRACT at the end.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The school foregrounds a strong classical framework in its pedagogy via the grammar–logic–rhetoric sequence and Great Books emphasis, indicating a classical approach to how students learn and engage with material. The emphasis on integrating Christ into teaching and on thinking (as opposed to rote instruction) aligns with classical aims.
- There are explicit mentions of collaborative, seminar-style work in some classical-education discussions, but public-facing materials do not consistently advertise a fully articulated Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across grades. The materials emphasize the Great Books, memory/work formation, and integrated study rather than a consistently described Socratic seminar program in every grade. This makes the pedagogical signal strong but not uniformly explicit across all public content.
- The public materials also emphasize a lively, faith-integrated classroom culture (morning prayer, Christ-centeredness, and a low-tech environment in some texts), which can include contemplative and wonder-filled approaches, but the explicit, formal pedagogy signals such as schooled Scholé, or a sustained Paideia/Socratic seminar program, are not consistently described.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords observed: Great Books, grammar, logic, rhetoric, classical education, liberal arts, memory work, recitation, integration of faith and reason, formation through study.
- Negative keywords observed: explicit signals of experiential learning, project-based-only approaches, or constructivist pedagogy signals are not evident in the public materials; however, explicit Socratic/seminar signals are not consistently described in the public-facing pages.
- The presence of core classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books) supports a CLASSICAL lean, but the lack of consistently explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy across grades makes this category slightly less than fully classical in public articulation.

Why this rating

- The curriculum and philosophy strongly align with classical aims, but explicit, scalable evidence of Socratic seminars or a consistent “Socrates-style” pedagogy across grades is not uniformly documented in the public-facing materials. Given that, the category is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.
