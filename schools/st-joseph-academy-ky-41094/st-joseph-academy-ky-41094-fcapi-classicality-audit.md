## Executive Summary

St. Joseph Academy (Walton, KY, 41094) presents as a Catholic school with a strong liturgical, faith-centered identity and a formation-focused educational philosophy. Public-facing materials emphasize spiritual formation, virtue, and community-life through Mass, traditional devotions, catechesis, and parish affiliation, alongside a structured K–8 program that includes classical homeschooling options and a traditional academic track. The school demonstrates clear religious foundations and a commitment to forming character and faith alongside academics, but its explicit classical features (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy) are not uniformly documented across all grade levels in the public materials. Overall, the school aligns with classical elements in meaningful but partial ways, yielding a ranking of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are strong: Catholic identity, Mass and liturgical life, and a clear church-school partnership are explicitly emphasized. Evidence: Catholic identity and liturgical practices are foregrounded on the public site; Our Philosophy anchors education in faith and moral formation.
- The school foregrounds formation (virtue, character, citizenship) rather than information-only aims, aligning with a core classical aim, though explicit articulation of truth-goodness-beauty or Great Books pedagogy is less evident in public materials.
- Curriculum and pedagogy show classical-adjacent elements (Classical Homeschooling option, K–8 structure, traditional coursework) but lack pervasive, explicit documentation of the full classical trifecta (grammar-logic-rhetoric, Great Books/Great Conversation, and sustained Socratic seminars) across all grades in the public materials.
- Faculty and community aspects emphasize a living-Catholic-educator model and family/parish partnership, consistent with classical education’s collegial, mentorship-friendly posture and family involvement, yet with limited evidence of the full “faculty-as-living-curriculum” framing in public content.
- Parent/community engagement is present (Getting Involved, parish connections, events, open-campus opportunities), but the materials do not strongly position parents as co-educators in the classical sense, though there are pathways for involvement.
