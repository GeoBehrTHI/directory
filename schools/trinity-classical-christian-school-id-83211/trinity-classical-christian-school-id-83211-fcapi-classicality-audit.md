## Executive Summary

Trinity Classical Christian School Idaho (Id 83211) presents a robust classical Christian program embedded in a theologically grounded framework. Public-facing materials emphasize formation of character through faith-integrated, liberal-arts education, a clear commitment to the Great Books tradition, and explicit Trivium-based pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric), including Latin and Greek study. The school also foregrounds a community-oriented pedagogy (lower vs. upper school structures, Socratic dialogue, thesis projects), a clearly defined mission of forming virtue and wisdom, and proactive parental partnership (admissions, volunteer expectations, parent education). Taken together, these signals align strongly with the classical educational rubric, especially in its emphasis on formation, transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty), the liberal arts framework, and the integration of faith with reason.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- The school presents a mission of formation (not mere information) rooted in Christ and Scripture, with explicit attention to virtue, wisdom, and the cultivation of character. This maps directly onto the core trinity of classical education emphasized in the knowledge base. Evidence includes statements about formation, Great Books, and a Christian worldview integrated into all disciplines.
- The curriculum is deliberately liberal-arts oriented, foregrounding the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), the Quadrivium, Great Books, Latin and Greek, and a history of Western civilization integrated across subjects. This is reinforced by explicit notes about paideia, memory work (songs/chanting), and a three-stage model of learning.
- Pedagogy in practice includes both contemplative/remembering (lower school songs/chanting) and Socratic/seminar-based discourse (upper school rhetoric and thesis projects), signaling alignment with classical methods. House system further demonstrates a virtue-formation orientation and community-building.
- Family/parent involvement is positioned as a partnership (co-teachers, volunteer opportunities, parental education), which supports the classical education ideal of shared formation between home and school.

What Would Move the Needle

- If a desire existed to push toward a pure, canonical CLASSICAL standard across all options, minor enhancements could include explicit, named liturgical/community worship practices in daily routines (beyond faith integration), and more transparent, published mappings of specific Great Books selections and correlating liberal-arts connections across grade levels to further demonstrate the Great Conversation framework to external reviewers. However, given the current public materials, these are not necessary to achieve CLASSICAL status; they would serve to strengthen the narrative for outsiders seeking deeper confirmation of the Great Books/Scholé tradition.

- For schools seeking to move from CLASSICAL toward an even more explicit “Fully Classical” identity, consider:
    - Publishing a formal, grade-by-grade Great Books list with integrated cross-curricular connections.
    - Adding stated liturgical or worship components (even if non-denominational) to foreground liturgical practices more overtly.
    - Providing more detailed faculty profiles showing teacher formation aligned with classical pedagogy and mentorship roles.
