## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. Joseph (Fairborn, OH, 45324) presents a robust, publicly visible model of classical education rooted in Catholic faith and the liberal arts. Public materials repeatedly describe the school as a “joyfully Catholic, classical high school,” foregrounding the integration of faith, great books, and classical pedagogy. Evidence across the site highlights a Catholic identity, the study of great thinkers, the Socratic method, a canonical humanities-based curriculum, the Seven Liberal Arts influence, a house system designed to cultivate virtue, and a strong emphasis on character formation and community life, including partnerships with families via parent liaisons and other structures. Taken together, these signals align strongly with the six elements of classical education, indicating a CLASSICAL overall classification for Chesterton Academy of St. Joseph.

Across the six categories, Chesterton Academy of St. Joseph demonstrates substantial alignment with classical criteria:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: explicit Catholic identity, theology integration, and liturgical/communal rhythm are evident (highly Classical).
- School Purpose and Goal: formation, virtue, and the aim of a substantive liberal-arts education are clearly stated (Classical).
- Curriculum: explicit Great Books/Great Conversation orientations through mentions of great thinkers and a braided, integrated humanities approach; math/science tied to philosophy/theology also signals a holistic liberal-arts framework (Classical).
- Pedagogy: Socratic/dialogical pedagogy in upper levels, with recognition of memorization/reverence in lower levels (Scholé-like elements) and a rhythm that includes spoken rhetoric and memory-based pedagogy (Classical).
- Faculty: public-facing bios and descriptions frame faculty as masterful, virtuous mentors and the living curriculum; there is emphasis on character and scholarly excellence among teachers (Classical).
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with families (parent liaisons in the house system) and community engagement; openness to parent involvement is evident (Classical).

What would move the needle
