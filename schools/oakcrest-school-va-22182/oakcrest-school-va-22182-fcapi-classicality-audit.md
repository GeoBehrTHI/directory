## Executive Summary

Oakcrest School Vienna, VA (ZIP 22182) presents a robust classical-leaning profile across its public materials. The school demonstrates a coherent integration of religious foundations, a formation-centered purpose, a liberal-arts curriculum, a pedagogy oriented toward mentoring and rhetoric, a faculty culture that models virtue, and a strong, ongoing partnership with families and the broader Oakcrest community. Across six categories, public materials consistently align with the hallmark features of classical education: a transcendental view of truth, goodness, and beauty anchored in Catholic formation; formation of character and intellect over mere information; a liberal-arts curriculum with exposure to Great Books and the Great Conversation; pedagogical practices that emphasize mentoring, rhetoric, and meaningful inquiry; a living-faculty/missional culture; and a deeply collaborative, parent-partnered community.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: The school anchors education in Catholic faith and Opus Dei spirituality, with daily Mass, a chapel, liturgical life, and theology as a core academic discipline. Evidence of liturgical practices, daily Mass, a chapel space, and a chaplain presence is strong on the site. 
- School Purpose and Goal: Oakcrest emphasizes formation of intellect, character, faith, and leadership; education is described as educating the whole person with mentoring and service as central features. The mission and philosophy explicitly foreground formation and virtue alongside liberal arts. 
- Curriculum: The site highlights a liberal-arts curriculum, Great Books/the Great Conversation, and a Senior Thesis capstone, with explicit references to a Seven Liberal Arts framework and a plan to integrate Great Books. Evidence shows emphasis on liberal-arts breadth, Great Books integration, and an integrated, cross-disciplinary vision.  
- Pedagogy: The school features one-on-one mentoring, leadership development, and rhetorical training (including senior-thesis work and opportunities for public speaking). There is evidence of a debate-oriented, rhetoric-focused ethos via the Upper School Debate Team and senior-project culture. 
- Faculty: Oakcrest emphasizes mentoring and a “living curriculum,” with faculty shown as senior role models and leaders in the school’s culture; the site frequently highlights faculty/staff holding advanced degrees and their mentorship roles. 
- Parents & Community: The school frames a strong parent partnership, parent formation through events and resources, and open campus opportunities. The “Parent Partnership” materials explicitly describe parents as co-educators and emphasize collaboration and formation, including opportunities for parent involvement. 

Overall assessment: CLASSICAL

Executive summaries of category-by-category assessments, followed by concrete evidence and keyword analyses, are provided below.

  - A public emphasis on a Senior Thesis and an integrated, rhetoric-forward culture (including public-facing work, college writing, and humanities pathways) is evident. The presence of a Upper School Debate Team signals a rhetoric- and argumentation-focused pedagogy in at least some grades. 
- Positive keyword indicators

These appear in Oakcrest’s materials (mentoring pages, Senior Thesis, Debate Team).  
- Negative keyword indicators

However, the absence of explicit, systemwide Socratic/Harkness-style pedagogy across the entire program yields a partial classical alignment in pedagogy, hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical in this category.

Aggregate assessment across the six categories: CLASSICAL
