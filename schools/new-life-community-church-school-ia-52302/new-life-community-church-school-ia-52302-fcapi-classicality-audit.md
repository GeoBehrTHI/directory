## Executive Summary

New Life Community Church School (Marion, IA, 52302) presents a distinctly Christian community identity rooted in church life and homeschooling-oriented activities. Public materials show a strong theological orientation (gospel-centric language, pastors, ministry, and family-formation programs) and concrete parental engagement (e.g., parenting seminars and home-school events). However, direct, explicit evidence of core classical elements (e.g., liberal arts curriculum, Great Books, Socratic pedagogy, memory work, and the “living curriculum” faculty model) is not observed in the public materials available through the crawl. On balance, the school demonstrates a Christian formation emphasis with some parental involvement in educational matters, but falls short on explicit classical curriculum and pedagogy signals. The overall assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear movement opportunities toward CLASSICAL if specific classical elements are foregrounded in site content (see “What would move the needle” below).

Overall Classification and Aggregate Assessment

- Aggregate Ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale: The New Life Community Church School (Marion, IA, 52302) demonstrates a clear religious/theological orientation and offers family-centered homeschooling events, which align with some classical aims (formation, virtue, community) within a Christian framework. However, it lacks explicit classical curriculum elements (Great Books, liberal arts sequence), classical pedagogy signals (Socratic method, scholé, memory work), and the traditional living-curriculum faculty model. The strongest evidence sits in Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 6 (Parents & Community), with Element 2 showing partial alignment. The absence of explicit signals in Categories 3–5 places the overall ranking in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, rather than CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicit articulation of a classical curriculum in site content, including: 
 - Clear statements about liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a plan for Great Books or classical primary sources.
 - History taught throughout the curriculum as a core discipline, not as “social studies.”
 - Expressions of Great Conversation and telos oriented toward truth, goodness, and beauty.
- Publicly described pedagogy that includes Socratic seminars, memory work, and rhetoric training (and age-appropriate deployment for K–12).
- A clearly defined “living curriculum” concept, with a visible portrait or description of the faculty as mentors and colleagues who model virtue and knowledge, and a culture of faculty collaboration.
- A formal parent-teacher partnership model where parents are recognized as co-teachers and are offered structured formation opportunities (reading groups, book studies, and classical-education trainings) beyond occasional seminars.
- Evidence of a liturgical or liturgical-inspired communal rhythm (regular chapel/mass/prayer practices, liturgical calendar integration) alongside academic instruction.
