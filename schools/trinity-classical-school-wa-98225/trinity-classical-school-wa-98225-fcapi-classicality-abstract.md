## ABSTRACT

Trinity Classical School WA 98225 (Trinity Bellingham) is classified as CLASSICAL across all six canonical categories. Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are grounded in a Christian theocentric worldview with a gospel-centered culture, liturgical practices, and a commitment to truth, goodness, and beauty as real, knowable transcendentals. The School Purpose & Goal centers on formation, virtue, wisdom, and human flourishing, rather than vocational preparation alone, with explicit emphasis on the Great Books and the integration of classical aims into character formation. The Curriculum centers on the Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium and Quadrivium), Great Books, and a unified view of history across disciplines, emphasizing wonder and the Great Conversation. Pedagogy aligns with classical methods, including Socratic dialogue, Scholé, memorization/recitation, and imitation of exemplary models, with a clearly defined progression from grammar to logic to rhetoric. The Faculty are portrayed as living curriculums and mentors, supporting a culture of virtue and professional fidelity to classical pedagogy. Parents & Community engagement is robust, with partnerships, parent-formation activities, and accessible opportunities for visitation.

Overall, Trinity Classical School WA 98225 demonstrates a comprehensive CLASSICAL orientation with explicit textual evidence in public materials supporting each category: theological grounding and liturgical practices; formation-focused aims; a Trivium/Quadrivium-centered curriculum with Great Books; a pedagogy featuring Socratic dialogue and memory-based practices; faculty serving as living exemplars; and active parent-community partnerships.

Cited materials informing this assessment include: the school’s mission and gospel-centered framing (The Gospel, Liturgy of the Day, Christian Worldview) and Trivium/Quadrivium emphasis (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; Quadrivium) as described in Trinity’s public content; Great Books and historical engagement; and explicit statements about partner-parents and community formation. These are captured across pages and documents including Trinity’s mission and grade-level descriptions, the “Our Mission” and “The Gospel” sections, grammar/logic rhetoric pages, and parent resources, and are reinforced by the Six Essential Elements lexicon of positive/negative keywords and the classical lexicon.
