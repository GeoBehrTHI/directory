## Executive Summary

Grace Classical School NC 28540 presents as a small, faith-centered classical school aligned with the Christian classical tradition. Public materials emphasize a Christian worldview, a Trivium-based curriculum, and a partnership with families. The school explicitly frames its mission in terms of truth found in God’s Word, formation of character, and a university-style schedule designed to bring structure and accountability to families. Public statements also stress the integration of God’s Word throughout instruction and the aim that students become “wise and discerning” lights for Christ. Overall, the materials strongly affirm religious/metaphysical foundations and a formation-oriented purpose, and they clearly articulate a Trivium-focused curriculum. However, certain elements central to a fully classical program as outlined in the Six Essential Elements (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation orientation, explicit Socratic pedagogy for upper grades, and a fully fleshed-out living-teacher-mentorship model) are less evident in the public materials, suggesting a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL with strong CLASSICAL lean in key areas and room for fuller alignment in pedagogy and cultural transmission in practice.

Executive posture by category (category-by-category snapshot) and overall assessment are provided below, with explicit keyword traces from Grace Classical School materials and the classical reference lexicon.

- Overall aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Why the aggregate is not CLASSICAL: While Category 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), Category 6 (Parents & Community) and elements of Category 2 (Purpose & Goal) and Category 3 (Curriculum) show solid classical alignment, several categories (notably Pedagogy, Faculty development, and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation orientation) are only partially evidenced in public materials. The school’s public materials emphasize Trivium-based curriculum and Christian worldview but provide limited explicit detail on Socratic seminars, living curriculum in faculty, robust faculty formation, and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation programming. See category-by-category breakdown for specifics.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If pursuing a fully CLASSICAL rating, Grace Classical School could strengthen:
  - Pedagogy (Category 4): Explicitly articulate and implement Socratic seminars, rigorous upper-grade discussion formats, and a clearly structured Scholé rhythm that balances contemplation with rigorous discourse. Provide public-facing statements about specific pedagogical practices (e.g., seminars, debates, dialectic seminars) to demonstrate explicit Classical methods.
  - Curriculum (Category 3): Augment the public materials with explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments and cross-curricular Great Books integration. Publicly outline a plan for Great Books reading lists and a path toward a unified Liberal Arts approach beyond Trivium alone.
  - Faculty (Category 5): Describe the faculty as “the living curriculum” with portraits of teacher ideals, mentorship, professional formation, and a visible teacher-culture that supports student formation. Provide examples of faculty development and collaborative “core of the school” practices.
  - History & Cultural Transmission (Category 3/6): Strengthen explicit signaling of cultural transmission through Western civilization curricula, primary sources, and classical canon as practiced, with clear connections to the Great Conversation.
  - Liturgical/Communal Practices (Category 1): Consider more explicit signaling of liturgical or communal practices within the school program, even grammar-school-level exposure to contemplative/prayerful routines, to deepen the liturgical dimension signaled in the documents.

- Concrete steps (short-term)
  - Publish a clarifying page detailing Socratic/seminar pedagogy, upper-school rhetoric training, and opportunities for student-led debates.
  - Introduce a publicly visible Great Books plan (e.g., list of canonical texts by grade level, with integration points across subjects).
  - Expand faculty bios with explicit statements about teacher formation, mentorship roles, and how teachers model virtue and wisdom.
  - Add a page or section demonstrating liturgical or communal practices (chapel, reverence practices, sacred spaces) to strengthen Category 1 signals.

- Concrete steps (longer-term)
  - Create a “living curriculum” initiative where faculty collectively document, reflect, and publish examples of how classical methods shape daily teaching, with a focus on formation of affections and wisdom.
  - Build a parent-education program (book studies, seminars) to deepen parental formation aligned with classical aims, further strengthening Category 6.

- Notes on evidence
  - Public materials already show clear CLASSICAL alignment in Category 1 and Category 6, with strong formation and worldview language. The gaps lie primarily in enumerated pedagogy and explicit Great Books signaling, plus richer teacher-culture signals, which would push the entire assessment toward CLASSICAL with fuller alignment.
