## Executive Summary

Uvalde Classical Academy (UCA) presents a robust CLASSICAL education profile across its publicly available materials. The school's publicly stated mission, creed, and curricular framework align closely with traditional classical Christian education: formation of virtue and wisdom under a biblical worldview; a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric); engagement with great books and history within a Western canon; and pedagogy that alternates between contemplative, memory-rich elements (chanting, memorization) and Socratic/seminar-based high school inquiry. The school also emphasizes parental partnership, a living-mentors faculty model, and a clear commitment to character formation rooted in Scripture. Overall, the aggregate impression from the materials is a fully CLASSICAL orientation rather than merely “Somewhat Classical.” Evidence across religious foundations, purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement supports this conclusion.

Most compelling signals include: explicit biblical/theological framing (Coram Deo, Scriptural worldview, Statement of Faith), a stated Trivium progression (grammar/logic/rhetoric), explicit Great Books and Western-civilization emphasis (and references to “great stories” and “great books”), and a teaching culture described as mentors living the curriculum. The parent partnership and community expectations (parents as co-teachers, open campus, involvement in classroom life) reinforce the classical-community model. The alignment with the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education (Positive/Negative keyword indicators) is strong, with only incidental use of elements that might be seen as non-classical in other contexts (e.g., occasional project-oriented activities) but not dominating the pedagogy.

What would move the needle section is not triggered here, as there are no category results that fall into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The school’s materials appear to meet the core criteria for CLASSICAL across all six categories.
