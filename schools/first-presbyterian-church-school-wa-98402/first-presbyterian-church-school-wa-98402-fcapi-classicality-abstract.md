# ABSTRACT

First Presbyterian Church School WA 98402 demonstrates a strong religious and formation-focused orientation, with explicit emphasis on Bible-centered education, character formation, and family partnership. The site evidences clear theological grounding and a robust commitment to forming virtue and faith in young children, aligning with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose/Goal categories of classical education. However, the curriculum and pedagogy as publicly documented do not fully realize classical standards (notably, the absence of liberal arts content and Great Books, and a primary emphasis on hands-on/project-based approaches). Consequently, the school ranks as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with several categories meeting classical criteria (1, 2, and 6), one category strongly NOT CLASSICAL (3), another category NOT CLASSICAL due to pedagogy (4), and one category only SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (5) due to staff signals. Concrete recommendations focus on integrating classical curriculum elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, liberal arts framing), refining pedagogy away from project-based dominance toward scholé-like, contemplative practices, and expanding explicit parental formation and a published portrait of the teacher to strengthen the “living curriculum” ethos.

Key sources referenced:

- First Presbyterian Preschool public materials describing God’s Word theology, Christ-centeredness, Bible curriculum, character formation, family partnership, and integrated STEAM programming.
- Public staff and faculty descriptions and statements of long-tenured teachers.
- Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) with associated Positive/Negative keyword indicators (categories 1–6) and the lexicon linking to the classical framework.
- Classicality Lexicon and related keyword lists for mapping site content to the six element categories.

Note: All assessments above are grounded in the specific crawl content provided for First Presbyterian Preschool in Tacoma (WP: fpptacoma.org) and in the classical-school rubric and keyword lexicon from the Six Essential Elements documents.
