## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Brookside Christian Academy (https://brooksideacademy.com) presents a clear classical Christian identity across its philosophy, mission, and curriculum pages. The site explicitly grounds learning in “truth, goodness, and beauty,” the Liberal Arts, and the Trivium/Quadrivium, and it highlights Latin and humanities with Scriptural integration—hallmark indicators of a classical school. Where the public materials are thinner is on pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, chanting/recitation, restful learning/scholé) and on faculty/parent-formation specifics. These gaps likely reflect a website content gap rather than a programmatic absence. Targeted updates (detailed below) would quickly move this school into a clear CLASSICAL designation on the public record.

Most important insights:
- Strong classical foundations and purposes are clearly articulated (truth/goodness/beauty; formation in wisdom and virtue; theological integration)  .
- Curriculum alignment is strong (Trivium/Quadrivium, Latin, primary sources, humanities)   .
- Public details about teaching methods (Socratic/seminar, chanting/recitation, restful learning) are not yet explicit; one summer-program page features “hands‑on”/STEM language that is a weak negative signal in the classical lexicon (likely peripheral to core academics) .
- Hiring copy points the right direction (teachers mature in Christian faith; enthusiastic for classical education) but could add explicit “teacher as mentor/living curriculum” language .

School website audited: https://brooksideacademy.com

Method note: Keyword analysis used the positive/negative indicators from “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” and the companion lexicon organized by the six elements (religious/metaphysical, purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, parents/community)  .

## What would move the needle (to CLASSICAL on public evidence)
Prioritize adding/clarifying the following on the website (and in parent/faculty materials) using explicit, classical keywords drawn from the Six Elements list:
- Pedagogy: Name and describe Socratic seminars/dialectic, upper‑school rhetoric orations/capstones, K–6 chanting/recitation/memory work, and restful learning/scholé rhythms; include photos/program highlights and sample prompts  .
- Great Books: Add a representative reading list and language about engaging primary texts and the “Great Conversation” .
- Faculty: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate,” mentorship/collegium language, and faculty formation in classical pedagogy .
- Parents/Community: Add “parent formation” offerings (book clubs/seminars) and language about partnering with parents as co‑educators; highlight communal/feast‑day events if present .
- Worship/Liturgy (if applicable): Name chapel/morning prayer/hymnody to complete Category 1’s liturgical dimension .
