## Executive Summary

Corpus Christi Classical Academy (KY 40065) presents a robust Catholic, classical-liberal-arts program aligned with the traditional six-element classical rubric. Public materials emphasize a Trinity of core commitments: a religious-metaphysical formation grounded in Catholic theology; a teleology oriented toward virtue, wisdom, and the formation of character; and a curriculum and pedagogy built around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books sensibilities, and Socratic practice. The school explicitly foregrounds partnership with parents, ongoing catechesis, liturgical life, and the cultivation of memory, wonder, and intellectual virtue. The public materials repeatedly tie learning to the true, good, and beautiful in a way that maps closely to the classical framework encoded in the knowledge-base rubrics. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL across all six categories.

Key takeaways:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit and central (Mass, Rosary, Magisterium, Catholic formation). Evidence is strong and recurring. 
- Purpose and goals center on virtue, wisdom, and formation in a Catholic classical mold rather than vocational preparation alone. 
- Curriculum emphasizes grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; classical history and literature; engagement with Great Books / Great Conversation; and the cultivation of wonder. 
- Pedagogy combines contemplative/Scholé elements with Socratic discussion in upper grades and memorization/recitation in earlier years; upper grades emphasize rhetoric and public speaking. 
- Faculty portrayals align with classical ideals—mentors, living curriculum, and teachers as moral exemplars within a Catholic formation context. 
- Parents and community engagement is a foundational element—partnership with families, parent formation efforts, and robust volunteer/involvement opportunities. 

Abstractly, the site reads as a disciplined, mission-driven classical school anchored in Catholic tradition, with a coherent alignment of aims, content, and methods to the six-element classical rubric.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school’s public materials consistently frame education as rooted in Catholic faith, Scripture, and the Magisterium; the mission foregrounds partnership with parents in a Catholic classical tradition. This is evident in statements such as: “In partnership with parents and guided by Scripture and the Magisterium of the Church, we educate students in the classical, Roman Catholic tradition.” and “Rooted in Truth. Formed in Christ.” 
- Liturgical and communal practices are described (Mass at least twice a week, daily Rosary, Eucharistic Adoration, regular Reconciliation opportunities, Angelus, etc.). This anchors the daily life of the school in liturgical practice and sacred devotion. 
- The curriculum and philosophy explicitly reference truth, beauty, and goodness as integrally connected to faith.

- Negative keywords: none evident in public materials (no secular/relativist signals). 
- Estimated proportion: a strong majority of religious-signaling terms are positive (roughly 8–12 visible positive signals; zero explicit negative signals in materials reviewed).

Why this rating
- The materials consistently articulate transcendental (truth, beauty, goodness) and theological grounding, with liturgical life and communal formation embedded in daily practice. This aligns solidly with the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations criteria in the Six Essential Elements rubric. The presence of Mass, Rosary, and Magisterium-centered formation indicates a robust, explicit transcendental and liturgical dimension.

Cited evidence: mission/philosophy statements; liturgical practices such as Mass/Rosary; and faith-based formation language.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum is described as a traditional liberal-arts sequence: religion, phonics-based reading, grammar, literature, Latin, mathematics, history, and science; emphasis on classical languages and foundational study. This is explicit in the Academic Overview. 
- The Grammar School materials emphasize Latin, penmanship, grammar, spelling, memorization of poetry and Scripture, and reading of classical literature; Saxon Math is used, and history emphasizes classical civilizations culminating in the life of Christ. This aligns with Trivium-focused pedagogy and classical content. 
- Upper School activities include Socratic discussions, classical rhetoric, Latin literature, and history/science studies—explicit engagement with the Trivium and Quadrivium idioms (grammar, logic, rhetoric; plus musical or mathematical topics as part of integrated study). 
- The school’s materials mention Great Books / Great Conversation framing in the broader classical canon language (evident in the emphasis on memory work, recitation, and classical literature in lower/middle levels; Socratic method in upper grades). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: liberal arts, grammar, logic, rhetoric, Great Books, Great Conversation, Latin, classical literature, history (as Western civilization lineage), memorization/recitation, wonder, sacred tradition.
- Negative keywords: minimal (no evident social-studies-as-substitute for history; no reliance on project-based pedagogy as the sole method). The site emphasizes traditional methods rather than non-classical approaches.
- Estimated proportion: high proportion of positive curricular keywords; explicit references to classical content are frequent; very few negative signals appear.

Why this rating
- The curriculum is overtly built on classical-content pillars (Trivium and Quadrivium, Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin, grammar/recitation, history as Western civilization) and emphasizes wonder and integration—core Element 3 criteria of a classical program.

Cited evidence: Academic Overview; Grammar School (3-6) details; references to Socratic/Latin emphasis; statements on classical curriculum.
