## Executive Summary

Heart of Ohio Classical Academy (HOCA) – Dublin Campus presents a distinctly classical orientation in its public-facing materials. The school emphasizes a content-rich liberal arts and sciences curriculum, moral character formation, the centrality of the Western tradition, Latin and grammar instruction, and teacher-led classrooms. It also foregrounds virtues, decorum, and civic virtue, along with a structured, tech-conscious classroom environment (tech-free in some settings) and strong family/parent engagement. Taken together, HOCA clearly aligns with many core elements of classical education, but some hallmarks (notably explicit liturgical/theological components or a pervasive, publicly stated Socratic seminar culture across grades) are less visible in the public materials. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights
- Evidence of classical core: liberal arts/Great Books ethos, Western-tradition emphasis, virtue formation, and teacher-led pedagogy anchored in traditional methods (grammar, phonics, Latin) and structured discipline.   
- Public materials highlight virtue, character formation, and civic virtue as central aims, with explicit declarations that religion is not taught, but classical virtues are cultivated. 
- Parental partnership and open-campus ethos are strongly present, aligning with the classical model of parental involvement and community formation. 
- Gaps relative to a fully explicit, theological-liturgical frame or universal Socratic/seminar pedagogy across all grades are more visible in public materials, suggesting a SOMEWHAT rather than FULLY CLASSICAL designation in some categories.

Overall, positive keyword presence dominates, but theological/liturgical foundations are not publicly articulated. About 2–3 positives to 0–1 negatives; roughly 75–100% of observed signals in this domain are positive when considering the explicit virtues/truth-leaning language. (Sourced from HOCA curriculum and virtue language; see citations above.)

However, explicit theological grounding or liturgical practices are not evidenced in HOCA’s public-facing content, which weakens the case for a fully “Religious and Metaphysical Foundations” orientation. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating rather than CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- HOCA emphasizes teacher-led classrooms and explicit instruction (phonics, grammar; Latin), which aligns with classical pedagogy in the early grades and a structured approach. 
- Tech-Free Classrooms are highlighted, reflecting a normative stance toward disciplined, contemplative learning environments that can support classical pedagogy. 
- Some classic pedagogy signals (Socratic discussion, seminars, or debates; memorization/recitation; formation through imitation) are present in the classical framework, though explicit, public-facing articulation of widespread Socratic seminars across grades isn’t clearly demonstrated in the HOCA Dublin content. The public materials also emphasize “content-rich” and “living curriculum,” which can be compatible with Socratic/seminar approaches but lacks explicit, broad evidence of them. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: teacher-led classrooms, phonics/grammar instruction, Latin, contemplative learning (Scholé) appears in the theoretical framework (via the classical lexicon in the knowledge base), and a culture of virtue that may accompany Socratic dialogue. 
- Negative keywords present: explicit public evidence of a sustained, institution-wide Socratic seminar culture, or explicit emphasis on memorization/recitation or chanting as central pedagogy, is less visible in the HOCA Dublin public content. The presence of “restful learning” or “Scholé” is not strongly evidenced beyond general classical intent. 
- Quantitative estimate: Positive signals exist for teacher-led, structured pedagogy; clear evidence for Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy is weaker. Approximately 2–3 positive signals vs. 0–1 explicit Socratic signals; overall a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

Why this rating
- The school’s public materials clearly support traditional, structured pedagogy (teacher-led, explicit phonics/grammar, Latin, tech-free), but lack explicit, consistent, public evidence of a campus-wide Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across all grades. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation for Pedagogy.

Citations: Teacher-led, grammar/phonics/Latin and tech-free emphasis.  Audience-facing statements of classical pedagogy from HOCA material.

### Element 5: Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- HOCA lists a Board of Directors, Headmaster, and Faculty & Staff, indicating a formal governance and a defined faculty structure. This signals an organized faculty core but does not, in public materials, explicitly describe the faculty as “living curriculum” or as models of virtue in the language of classical pedagogy. 
- The materials emphasize teacher-led instruction and classical approach as central to the school’s identity, but there is less explicit language about faculty embodying virtue as the “living curriculum” or about faculty mentorship in the sense used in classical lexicon. 
- There is evidence of a well-defined faculty structure and named roles, with public displays of faculty, staff, and leadership. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: virtuous teachers, mentors, steady “living curriculum” language (though not verbatim in HOCA’s public text; the concept is implied via “Headmaster” and “Faculty & Staff” sections). The explicit classical lexicon uses phrases like “model teachers” and “living curriculum.” HOCA’s public materials lean into teacher-led, content-rich instruction. 
- Negative keywords present: no explicit mention of “teacher as facilitator (on the side)” or other negative signals that would contradict a classical teacherly formation approach in HOCA’s public materials. However, the explicit language of “living curriculum” or portraits of teacher ideals is not strongly evidenced in the Dublin content. 
- Quantitative estimate: Mixed; clear evidence of a teacher-led ethos but limited explicit, classic “living curriculum” portrait language. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Faculty.

Why this rating
- The public materials show governance and a structured faculty, plus a strong teacher-led approach, but lack explicit, detailed language about the faculty embodying a classical “portrait of teacher” or “living curriculum” in the same emphatic way the core classical documents describe. The evidence supports SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL for Faculty.

Citations: Board and faculty sections in HOCA materials.

Why this rating

## What Would Move the Needle

- Elevate explicit Socratic/Great-Conversation pedagogy across upper grades with documented classroom practices (seminars, dialectic discussions, and explicit opportunities for students to engage in Socratic inquiry). This would strengthen Element 4 (Pedagogy) to CLASSICAL.
  - Concrete step: publish detailed descriptions of Socratic practices, seminar formats, and exemplar unit plans across grades 6–12.

- Bolster explicit “living curriculum” language for Faculty (Element 5) by clarifying how faculty model virtue, mentor students, and embody the classical virtues in daily practice, including portraits of teacher-student formation and professional development aligned with classical pedagogy.

- Expand explicit integration of Great Books/primary sources and the Great Conversation framework in more explicit terms (Element 3) with a published scope-and-sequence that names major works and authors for each grade, along with assessment rubrics tied to the Great Books experience.

- Deepen liturgical or theological grounding (Element 1) where appropriate, while maintaining HOCA’s stated stance that it does not teach religion, by clearly articulating how classical virtue and metaphysical concepts inform character formation. If theology is intended to be part of the approach, publicly documenting how it interfaces with disciplines would strengthen the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category.

- Broaden the explicit presence of the “Scholé” (restful learning) and memorization/chanting practices (Element 4) through published exemplars of recitation, chant, or memory-work integrated into upper- and lower-school curricula, including artifacts or examples (songs, chants, verses) from the curriculum.

- Enhance explicit parent-focused formation resources (e.g., ongoing book studies and family-learning guides) to further optimize the Parent & Community category toward a full CLASSICAL status, even beyond current engagement channels.
