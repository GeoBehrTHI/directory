## Executive Summary

Ironbridge Christian Academy (ICA) in Virginia presents a robust alignment with classical Christian schooling across multiple dimensions, anchored in a Christ-centered framework, a clearly stated classical curriculum, and active family involvement. Public materials repeatedly describe ICA as a Christ-centered, biblically sourced institution that partners with families to cultivate virtue, character, faith, and academic excellence. The curriculum is explicitly described as classical, with emphasis on Bible-integrated study and core liberal-arts subjects (grammar, literature, history, writing, etc.). The school emphasizes Chapel, biblical instruction, and theological grounding, alongside a community-centric approach to parent involvement and service. Taken together, ICA demonstrates strong alignment with classical markers in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. Pedagogy shows clear classical intent but does not always demonstrate the full suite of high-school–level Socratic/seminar practices; in light of the available evidence, the overall classification is CLASSICAL, with a note that Pedagogy contains elements that lean toward a more blended or gradually unfolding model. 

Overall aggregate rating: CLASSICAL.

Notes on the evidence base:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are foregrounded with explicit Christ-centered language, biblical instruction, and liturgical practice (Chapel) across ICA materials. Examples include explicit Christ-centered mission statements, biblical worldview framing, and chapel/bible integration in daily learning.   
- The school purpose and goal emphasize formation, virtue, spiritual growth, and character development alongside academic aims. Public materials reference following Christ while nurturing spiritual growth, academic excellence, and character development, supported by statements on virtue and moral integrity within a classical frame.  
- Curriculum is explicitly described as classical (grammar, literature, penmanship, history, math, science, writing, Bible), with biblical teachings woven throughout, consistent with a classical liberal-arts orientation.  
- Pedagogy shows classical influences (classical curriculum language, memory-oriented/recitation-like aspects in lower levels, and opportunities for structured discussion in certain programs), but the high-school Socratic/seminar emphasis is not consistently evidenced in the material available for the current grade offerings (preschool–elementary expansions are described; higher-grade pedagogy is implied but not fully demonstrated in the provided content). This yields a partial alignment in pedagogy, i.e., SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL based on the current public materials.  
- Faculty materials emphasize teachers as mentors in a Christ-centered environment, with faculty being described as living examples of virtue and character, and public materials include faculty roles and leadership (Director) within ICA’s public-facing content. This supports the Classical Faculty marker. 
- Parents & Community are actively engaged as co-teachers and partners; ICA highlights family partnership, parent involvement opportunities (Parent-Teacher Fellowship), and openness to visits and involvement, which are hallmark signals of a classical-leaning community orientation.
