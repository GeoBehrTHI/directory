## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of St. John the Evangelist (CASJE) in Charlottesville, VA 22902 presents a publicly visible classical-Catholic program that emphasizes formation of virtue within a liberal-arts framework. Public materials repeatedly foreground an incarnational Catholic identity, a curriculum rooted in the seven liberal arts and Great Books, and pedagogy that blends contemplative elements (Scholé) with Socratic discussion and rhetoric. The school highlights Mass daily, liturgical practices, and a theology-infused approach to history, mathematics, science, and the arts. Faculty profiles emphasize formation, mentorship, and living exemplars, while community engagement is foregrounded through open houses, shadow days, Mass-centric events, and parent-facing opportunities.

Based on a keyword-driven analysis aligned to the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (as captured in the knowledge base) CASJE earns a CLASSICAL rating across all six categories. The weight of explicit religious-metaphysical foundations, formation-focused purposes, a Trivium/Quadrivium curriculum with Great Books, a pedagogy combining contemplation and Socratic inquiry, a faculty modeled on virtue, and active parental/community partnership all align with the classical-education rubric. Evidence drawn from CASJE’s site (e.g., “An Incarnational Environment,” “A Focus on Truth, Goodness, and Beauty,” daily Mass, the Word as basis of learning, the Seven Liberal Arts, the Socratic Seminar, and the House System) demonstrates strong alignment with the positive keyword indicators in the Six Essential Elements documentation. See citations to Turn 0 files for the site content and Turn 1/Turn 10 documents for the classical-education keyword framework.

Key supporting materials from the knowledge framework include: the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) (positive keywords like truth, virtue, great books, seven liberal arts, Socratic dialogue, scholé, formation of affections, and partnership with families; negative signals such as project-based dominance and value-neutral pedagogy) and the Classical Lexicon (which enumerates positive/negative keywords across the six elements) ,. The school’s own materials explicitly reference Catholic formation, Incarnation, liturgical practice, Great Books and liberal arts, Socratic Seminar, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative (Scholé) and rhetorical training, which align with the classical framework (evidence cited in the executive-summary sections below). For example, CASJE states: “From history and theology to math and science, the Incarnation is the central mystery we explore across the entire curriculum” and “The study of the seven liberal arts—grammar, logic, and rhetoric—and the quadrivium is the foundation that makes fruitful the further studies…”; it also emphasizes Mass as daily worship and a Word-centered education (evidence citations).
