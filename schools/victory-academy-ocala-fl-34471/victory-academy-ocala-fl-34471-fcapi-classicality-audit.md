## Executive Summary

Victory Academy Ocala (Ocala, FL) presents as a Christian, classical-leaning program that foregrounds a Trivium-inspired framework (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) within a Christ-centered, classical curriculum. Public materials consistently describe the school as Christ-centered with a Christian worldview, integrates Scriptural study, and emphasizes a progression through the Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), Latin study, and four historical periods. There is clear attention to formation of character, virtue, and a holistic “body, mind, and spirit” mission. The school also communicates family partnership with explicit language about parental involvement and “Parental Responsibility,” along with a stated aim of forming students for college-bound futures within a Christian framework. These signals align Victory Academy with a classical education orientation, though some elements (e.g., modern programs like MakerSpace and Winterim, and explicit emphasis on college-bound outcomes) introduce non-classical features that temper a fully “pure” classical profile. Overall, the school scores CLASSICAL with a notable SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL nuance in pedagogy and program balance.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): To move toward a fully CLASSICAL orientation, Victory Academy could further articulate formation-focused language explicitly and consistently, reducing career/workforce-prep framing. A clearer articulation of telos, scholé, and the pursuit of true/good/beautiful as a primary objective (with quantifiable outcomes tied to virtue formation and humane excellence) would strengthen the classical alignment. Additionally, a more explicit emphasis on Great Books/Great Conversation and primary-source reading across curricula would push this category toward stronger CLASSICAL alignment. Concrete steps: publish a formal statement of formation goals aligned with classical education (including scholé language) and implement a school-wide, K–12 Great Books reading pathway linked to grade-level objectives.

- Element 4 (School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods): While the upper-level pedagogy shows classical elements (grammar/dialectic/rhetoric, recitation, memory work), the incorporation of MakerSpace, Winterim, and other modern programs softens the pure classical pedagogy. To move toward CLASSICAL, increase the proportion of time spent in contemplative, memory-based and literature-centered activities; elevate Socratic discourse, seminars, debates (explicitly named) as core, not peripheral, pedagogical modes; and clearly articulate a scholé-centric schedule (restful learning days, contemplative inquiry) integrated across grades. Encourage more explicit integration of primary-source inquiry, classical texts, and moral/philosophical discussion as central pedagogy rather than occasional add-ons.

- Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 5 (Faculty) could be strengthened by further foregrounding Great Books/primary sources and ensuring that faculty professional development emphasizes classical pedagogy and catechesis aligned with classical formation. A defined “living curriculum” with continuous fidelity to grammar/dialectic/rhetoric, and a teacher development program anchored in classical pedagogy would move the needle toward CLASSICAL.

Citations: Element 2/4 signals (formation-focused language; historical periods; Latin; recitation; teacher roles) and references to modern programs in the curriculum (MakerSpace, Winterim).
