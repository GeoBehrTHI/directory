# Classicality Audit: Arcadia Christian School (ACS)

Executive Summary TL;DR
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (transitioning to more fully classical).
- Why: ACS publicly affirms a theological foundation (“Scripture at every point is the foundation on which to build all knowledge,” with “biblical worldview in all subjects”) and adopts core classical structures and methods (Schools of Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; memorization; Socratic discussion). Evidence for Great Books, Latin/classical languages, chant/singing, wonder/scholé, and liturgical school practices is not (yet) explicit on public pages reviewed.  
- Strongest areas: Religious/Theological foundations; Pedagogy; Parent partnership.   
- Biggest gaps to become unmistakably classical: Publish Great Books lists; show Latin (or Greek) and quadrivium elements; make lower-school chant/recitation visible; foreground “truth, goodness, beauty” and “wonder/scholé”; describe chapel/liturgical life; post a public “portrait of a teacher/graduate.”

Method and framework
- Sources: ACS’s public site content from the provided crawl (notably the “ACS Classical Education Method” and “Moment with Mr. Limon” pages; program pages for Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; Bible curriculum/parent partnership language).    
- Rubric: The Six Essential Elements/keyword indicators (positive/negative) and classicality lexicon were used as the scoring framework (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose & Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community).

  - “Purpose… to teach the Word of God… in cooperation with their parents/guardians” (Word of God; theological centrality) 
  - Formation aim: “training and equipping them in faith, character, heart, and service” (formation language) 
- Comparison to Positive/Negative indicators:

  - Missing/low-evidence: explicit “truth, goodness, beauty”; liturgical/communal practice terms (chapel, worship, prayer, liturgy) not observed in crawl snippets.
  - No negative signals seen (e.g., “values-neutral,” “secular/nonsectarian”).
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive keyword hits (unique): 4 (Scripture, biblical worldview, Word of God, Christian/Christ-followers/formation); Negative: 0; Liturgical terms: 0 observed. Approx. 80–100% of matched signals are positive, with a gap in liturgical language coverage.
- Category rating: CLASSICAL
  - Rationale: Clear theological grounding and formation language meet most criteria for this category, even though explicit liturgical/chapel language is not yet evident.  

SECTION 2 — SCHOOL PURPOSE AND GOAL

Keyword analysis (from ACS site)
- Direct matches/evidence:
  - “Teach our students not what to think, but how to think!” (intellectual formation over mere information) 
  - Formation language: “faith, character, heart, and service” (virtue/formation emphasis; though “virtue” and “wisdom” not named explicitly) 
  - “Partner… in training and equipping our students to be Christ followers” (formation telos) 
- Comparison to Positive/Negative indicators:
  - Positives sought: virtue, wisdom, formation, pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty (TGB). ACS uses “how to think,” “character,” “faith,” “formation/ equip,” but does not explicitly state TGB or “wisdom/virtue.” 
  - No “college-and-career-ready/21st century skills/workforce prep” emphasis seen (negative list). 
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive keyword hits (unique): 3–4 (“how to think,” faith, character, equip/train); Negative: 0; Missing: explicit “virtue,” “wisdom,” “truth, goodness, beauty,” “scholé.” Estimate ≈ 70–80% of desired purpose-language present.
- Category rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Rationale: Clear formation posture and how-to-think emphasis; would be strengthened by explicit statements about cultivating wisdom/virtue and pursuing truth, goodness, and beauty. 

SECTION 3 — CURRICULUM

Keyword analysis (from ACS site)
- Direct matches/evidence:
  - Trivium structure is explicit on program pages: “School of Grammar: K–5,” “School of Logic: 6–8,” “School of Rhetoric: 9–12” (grammar, logic, rhetoric). 
  - “Reading in the classics” + “student engagement around Socratic discussion” (classics/Great Books direction; Socratic texts) 
  - Bible curriculum (explicit academic integration of theology) 
- Comparison to Positive/Negative indicators:
  - Positives matched: Trivium; classics/Great Books orientation; Socratic texts. 
  - Missing/low-evidence: Latin/Greek or quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) named explicitly; formal Great Books reading lists; “history” (vs. “social studies”) explicitly stated. No negative terms like “social studies replacing history,” “STEM-first,” or “project-based dominance” observed. 
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive keyword hits (unique): 5 (grammar, logic, rhetoric, classics, Socratic); Negative: 0; Missing: quadrivium, Latin/Greek, Great Books lists. Estimate ≈ 65–75% of the core curriculum indicators observed publicly.
- Category rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Rationale: Strong Trivium structure and classics/Socratic signals; needs explicit Latin/Greek or quadrivium language and published Great Books/history sequences for a clear “CLASSICAL” mark.

- Overall: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (strong movement toward fully CLASSICAL as of 2024–25). ACS publicly notes “ACS Journey towards Christian Classical Education” and the implementation of the “ACS Classical Education Method,” signaling an active transition.  

What would move the needle (to secure a full CLASSICAL designation)
- Name the transcendentals: Publish an explicit purpose statement affirming the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, along with the cultivation of wisdom and virtue, and “formation not information.” 
- Show the classical curriculum: Post Great Books reading lists by grade and the history sequence (explicitly “History” rather than “Social Studies”); highlight classical languages (at least Latin) and quadrivium elements.  
- Make pedagogy visible: Describe lower-school chanting/singing/recitation routines; name scholé/restful learning and wonder as pedagogical commitments. 
- Liturgical school life: Publicize school-wide rhythms of prayer, chapel/worship, hymnody, or liturgical seasons to reflect theological formation as a communal practice. 
- Faculty formation: Publish a “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and note ongoing faculty training in classical pedagogy; add a “Portrait of a Graduate.” 
- Parent formation: Offer and publicize parent book groups/seminars on classical education to deepen the home–school partnership.
