## Executive Summary

Corpus Christi Catholic Academy (CCCA) presents a robust Catholic classical-education orientation anchored in a Catholic identity, liturgical life, and partnership with parents. Public materials consistently emphasize a holistic formation of the whole person (mind, heart, soul) through a classical framework, with explicit references to Catholic liturgy, sacraments, daily Mass, and a mission framed by Truth, Beauty, and Goodness. The school promotes classical pedagogy and a classical teaching model, and it foregrounds parental partnership, staff as mentors, and community involvement.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale at a glance:

- Strengths across categories: Clear religious-metaphysical foundations; explicit Catholic identity; emphasis on formation and virtue; strong parental-partnership messaging; inclusion of classical content in curriculum (classical languages, literature, history, philosophy, etc.) and a clearly articulated classical teaching-model stance.
- Gaps preventing a full CLASSICAL rating: The website does not consistently articulate the full grammar–logic–rhetoric trinity as explicit curricular foci in a way that mirrors the Six Essential Elements’ canonical wording. Some elements of classical pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, Scholé days, great-books-heavy discussions) are mentioned more implicitly or via general statements about a classical approach, rather than explicit, cross-grade implementation descriptions. Several category-specific indicators (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references, fully articulated “living curriculum” faculty portrait) are present but not uniformly explicit across all pages.

- Mission statements frame education as anchored in Truth, Beauty, and Goodness, with formation of faith and character through Catholic formation and liturgical life (e.g., “Our Mission … Truth, Beauty, and Goodness” and liturgical practices) .
- Principal remarks highlight a classical teaching model and a goal to educate the whole person in light of Church teachings, indicating a theologically integrated, liturgical, and communal framework .

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive indicators drawn from the Classical Elements lexicon include truth, goodness, beauty; liturgy; Mass; sacraments; prayer; theology; sacred tradition; liturgical life; prayerful learning; reverence; wonder; sacred music; chant; imago Dei; sacred space; communal worship. The school’s materials consistently reference Catholic liturgy (Mass, liturgy) and the Catholic worldview, and explicitly state Truth, Beauty, and Goodness as guiding ideals.
- Negative indicators (e.g., secular, values-neutral, relativism) are not evident in the public materials; the site foregrounds Catholic doctrinal and liturgical content rather than secular ethics or relativistic approaches.

Why this rating

- The school explicitly grounds education in Catholic theology and liturgical life, and it frames character formation within a Catholic moral cosmos. This aligns strongly with Transcendental Moral Realism, Grounded Theology, and Liturgical/Communal Practices, core positive signals in the Classical lexicon. The absence of clear secular- or relativistic signals reinforces the Classical orientation in this category.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence

- The mission emphasizes formation and the pursuit of Truth, Beauty, and Goodness, with language of forming the whole person and fostering virtues (e.g., “formation,” “moral and spiritual formation,” “holistic education” in the Mercy/About pages) .
- The mission is explicitly framed in terms of a goal beyond mere job preparation, focusing on character and human excellence within a Catholic, classical framework (e.g., “pursuit of sainthood,” “Truth, Beauty, Goodness”) .
- The principal statement frames the classical approach as shaping the mind, body, and soul, signaling formation as the telos of education rather than vocational- or information-centric aims .

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty, liberal education, contemplation/scholé (as implied by classical formation), human flourishing, moral imagination.
- Negative keywords: none evident on the site (no explicit emphasis on workforce-ready framing or 21st-century-skills rhetoric in the public-facing content).

Why this rating

- The content strongly communicates formation, virtue, and the pursuit of transcendent truths (truth, goodness, beauty), with an explicit claim that education forms the person, not merely information or workforce-readiness. However, explicit articulation of the full classical telos in the same way as strict classical-education rubrics (e.g., a detailed, public statement about formation goals tied to a comprehensive Great Books-based formation) is less overt. This yields a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL, given the clear formation emphasis but incomplete publicly stated alignment with every canonical fulfillment of the classical-purposes criteria.

Citations

- Mission framing around Truth, Beauty, Goodness; formation emphasis: home/about pages ; formation/holistic development and pursuit of sainthood: About/mission language ; classical formation framing (whole-person education): From Our Principal page .

### Element 3: School Curriculum — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence

- Curriculum language emphasizes classical content areas: classical languages, literature, history, philosophy, mathematics, and the sciences; the site presents a classical education orientation and alignment with a liberal-arts approach (e.g., “classical languages, literature, history, philosophy, mathematics, and the sciences”) .
- Kindergarten through elementary pages show language-arts-focused curricula with explicit grammar/usage in later grades (e.g., 5th Grade Curriculum lists Grammar and Usage), demonstrating some presence of traditional grammar instruction within a layered classical framework .
- The site uses the descriptor “Classical Education” and references “classical teaching model” in leadership messaging, implying the Trivium-based structure (though not always stating “grammar, logic, rhetoric” outright on every page) .

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive indicators: liberal arts content (grammar/logic/rhetoric) appears in various grades (e.g., Grammar and Usage in 5th-grade Language Arts). The site uses “classical education,” emphasizes classical languages and humanities, and includes elements of history/philosophy/logic in the curriculum language.
- Negative indicators: explicit, public articulation of the full triad of Trivium components (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as a cross-grade, central organizing framework is not consistently explicit across all pages. Great Books/Great Conversation is not explicitly named on the main pages (though related ICLE materials appear in the Resources), which weakens a full “Great Books” signal.

Why this rating

- The school presents a classical curriculum orientation with substantial classical content (languages, literature, history, philosophy, liberal-arts emphasis). However, explicit, pervasive emphasis on grammar–logic–rhetoric as the central Triumvirate across all grade levels is not consistently stated on public-facing pages. Hence, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is appropriate.

Citations

- Curriculum language: “classical languages, literature, history, philosophy, mathematics, and the sciences” .
- School Life/Middle School/Elementary curriculum pages showing grade-level content and language arts emphasis (Grammar/Usage appears in 5th grade): Elementary/Middle School curriculum pages .
- Public note of “Classical Education” framing and curriculum approach: Classical Education page on site .

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence

- Leadership messaging explicitly notes adoption of a classical teaching model and a goal to educate the whole person through a classical approach (From Our Principal: “adopting a classical teaching model … classical approach aims to educate the whole person, mind, body, and soul”) .
- The presence of a holistic, formation-centered pedagogy implied by “holistic education,” “classical teaching model,” and liturgical integration (Mass, sacraments, liturgy) indicates a pedagogy that blends contemplation and rigorous study rather than a purely project-based or hands-on-only approach .
- Extracurricular and enrichment programs emphasize hands-on and experiential aspects (e.g., enrichment classes and clubs) but are presented as complementary rather than the core pedagogy, which aligns with a classical emphasis on memory, form, and the liberal-arts approach rather than a fully project-based or purely experiential model .

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive indicators: Socratic/Scholé-like signals (though not widely explicit as labeled terms), disciplined dialogue, classical pedagogy, memory/recitation concepts are signaled in the Classical Elements lexicon; the school’s own language emphasizes a classical teaching-model and formation language.
- Negative indicators: The site does not consistently feature explicit Socratic seminars, Harkness-style discussions, or formal recitation/memorization programs across all pages. There is some emphasis on hands-on and clubs, but not as a dominant pedagogy, which reduces the strength of a fully classical pedagogy signal.

Why this rating

- There is a clear top-level commitment to a classical pedagogy, with explicit reference to a classical teaching model. However, the public-facing materials do not consistently demonstrate the full spectrum of classical pedagogy signals (e.g., detailed, high-frequency mention of Socratic seminars, scholé days, or rigorous recitation protocols across grade bands). Therefore, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Citations

- Classical teaching model and holistic formation language: From Our Principal page .
- General curriculum orientation and formation: About/Home content .
- Enrichment and School Life signals (complementary pedagogy rather than primary method): Extracurricular page .

Why this rating

- The presence of a named staff and a stated classical-education orientation support a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Faculty. The absence of a robust, explicit “living curriculum” portrayal and a formal faculty-profile framework across pages keeps this category from Classic-level certainty.

Citations

- Negative indicators: The site contains occasional non-classical, general fundraising content, but the core signals emphasize partnership and parental involvement rather than customer-centric language. There is no strong negative signal in public-facing pages.

Why this rating

- The materials consistently frame parents as co-educators and emphasize active parental involvement and formation, aligning with the Positive Keywords in the Parents & Community lexicon (partnership with families; formation of affections; cultural transmission). The explicit public signals about parent engagement, parent education, and open participation justify CLASSICAL for this category.

What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): Elevate explicit articulation of formation objectives with precise, public statements about the telos of education (e.g., a published “formation framework” detailing how virtue, wisdom, and the true/good/beautiful are cultivated across grades). This could include a formal statement linking formation to specific outcomes and a more explicit alignment with the Great Conversation/two-way Great Books approach.
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate the full Trivium/Quadrivium architecture (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as a cross-grade organizing framework, and explicitly reference Great Books and the Great Conversation in the curriculum map. Include explicit language about grammar, logic, and rhetoric as core, visible pillars across grades, and evidence of their integration into assessment and instruction.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Publicly publish the core pedagogical signals (e.g., number and type of Socratic Seminars or Harkness-style discussions, scholé days, memorization/recitation protocols, and how upper-grade rhetoric and speech instruction are conducted). Strengthen the explicit presence of Scholé/restful learning and structured classical dialogue as regular elements of daily instruction.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a formal “living curriculum” portrait of teachers and a documented faculty formation plan, showing how faculty embody classical pedagogy, ongoing professional development in classical methods, and teacher-student mentorship expectations.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and expand parent-education initiatives (book studies, seminars, family liturgical/aesthetic programming) and ensure ongoing opportunities for parent visits and involvement (formalized parent-education calendars, campus-tour scheduling, and parent-educator covenants).
