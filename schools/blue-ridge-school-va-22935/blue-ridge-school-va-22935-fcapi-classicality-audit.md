## Executive Summary

Blue Ridge School (Blue Ridge School, VA 22935) presents a distinctly character- and formation-oriented program that leans solidly toward classical edges in some areas while remaining more modern or pragmatic in others. Based on a category-by-category keyword analysis drawn from public materials (the school’s website and related public pages) and anchored to the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric, the school attains a CLASSICAL rating in two categories, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in three, and NOT CLASSICAL in one category. When viewed holistically, the aggregate picture is BEST described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong virtues around character formation, disciplined community life, and a public commitment to parental partnership, paired with relatively weaker explicit anchors to the core classical triad (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and to a sustained Great Books/Great Conversation program across the curriculum.

Key insights:
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are present in character formation, honor culture, and faith-informed activities, but explicit liturgical/theological structures and liturgical practices are less foregrounded in public materials. Evidence includes references to faith-based activities and an honor-code/character-education framework. 
- The school’s stated purpose emphasizes formation, virtue, and human flourishing, with strong signals of character education and leadership development, consistent with a classical-leaning telos. 
- Curriculum signals align with Great Books-leaning through language about great works in English, small classes, and a college-prep, broadly classical orientation, though explicit “Trivium” structure (grammar, logic, rhetoric) is not prominently foregrounded on the public site. 
- Pedagogy features include a mix of rigorous academic expectations and contemplative, well-rounded experiences (arts, outdoors, memory-anchoring activities), with limited explicit mention of Socratic seminars or scholé in public pages, but with evidence of memory-based and performance-oriented elements (art, drama, and public speaking opportunities). 
- Faculty are foregrounded via named department faculty pages and public recognition of long-tenured staff (e.g., emeritus status), suggesting a strong mentoring culture and a living-curriculum ethos.  
- Parents & Community are explicitly invited as co-educators, with ongoing programs (New Family Webinars) and open-campus opportunities, indicating robust parent engagement and partnership.

What Would Move the Needle (applicable because the overall ranking is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)

Consider dedicated Great Books lists and a school-wide Great Conversation emphasis.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Expand explicit evidence of classical pedagogical practices (Socratic seminars, scholé days, memorized recitations in lower grades, and regular rhetoric/deliberation exercises) across more grade levels; maintain and publicize the balance of contemplative and rigorous instruction.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Highlight living-curriculum exemplars and teacher-mentorship programs in public materials more explicitly (e.g., portraits of teacher-mentor expectations, professional development in classical pedagogy, and a visible faculty-guided model).
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand parent-formation programming (beyond webinars) with more liturgical/educational materials that align with classical education (reading groups, book studies, and family-learning calendars) to deepen co-teaching dynamics.
