## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of Milwaukee (Menomonee Falls, ZIP 53051) presents a strong classical-Catholic profile anchored in explicit religious formation, a mission-driven purpose, and a liberal-arts curriculum delivered through a distinctly classical pedagogy. The public materials emphasize a Catholic, liturgical, and incarnational frame (daily Mass, sacramental life, and prayer), a tri-pillar formation model (Faith, Reason, Virtue), and a mentorship-oriented, house-based community model. Public-facing content also highlights parent partnership and community involvement (open houses, shadow days, WPCP participation, and parent education). Taken together, the school demonstrates the core signals of classical education in six categories, with particularly robust alignment in Religious Foundations, School Purpose, Pedagogy, and Parents/Community. The curriculum signals are clearly classical in tone (liberal arts orientation and classical formation), though explicit Great Books language is less overt beyond references to classical works and reading requirements. Overall, the school ranks CLASSICAL, with explicit strengths in faith formation, virtue-centered aims, and Socratic pedagogy, and a clear pathway for parental partnership.

- Executive insights
  - Chesterton Academy of Milwaukee demonstrates a robust classical and Catholic orientation across core pillars: formation in faith, reason, and virtue; a liberal-arts pedagogy centered on Socratic inquiry; and strong parental partnership. The strongest signals are religious-metaphysical foundations, purpose/goal, pedagogy, faculty, and parental/community engagement. The Curriculum category shows a strong classical orientation but could benefit from more explicit public articulation of Great Books-heavy elements (e.g., explicit Great Books program language or direct references to the Trivium/Quadrivium) to strengthen the explicit Classical-Curriculum alignment.

- What would move the needle
  - If the school wished to advance from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum to CLASSICAL, it could publicly articulate a more explicit Great Books framework and a clearer listing of the Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium and Quadrivium), including Latin/Greek study and primary-source reading streams across grades.
  - Increase explicit public references to universal Great-Conversation texts and canonical works beyond the occasional mention of “one classical work” to demonstrate a sustained, multi-text Great Books approach across the curriculum.
