## Executive Summary

St John Paul II Preparatory School (MO 63303) presents a robust classical-Catholic educational model anchored in a trinitarian vision of forming virtue, wisdom, and beauty. Public-facing materials on the school's site consistently emphasize a Catholic formation, a classical curriculum (notably the trivium: grammar, logic, rhetoric), immersion in Great Books and Western-Christian civilization, and a hybrid schedule designed to partner with families as co-educators. The school also highlights governance and fidelity signals (e.g., Cardinal Newman Society recognition, Catholic formation, and a three-day hybrid model) that align with the classical rubric. Across the six classical categories, the school demonstrates substantial alignment with classical criteria, with particularly strong signals in Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The Pedagogy category shows clear classical elements (notably the trivium and Great Books emphasis) but less explicit documentation of Socratic seminars or other high-school pedagogical modalities in public materials, which places Element 4 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL. Overall, the school earns a CLASSICAL designation, reflecting a coherent classical framework with a strong Catholic core and explicit engagement with the Great Books tradition and the trivium.

Citations reflect the school’s public-facing materials and the classical-education rubric/lexicon used for the analysis. Examples include explicit statements about classical education, the trivium, Great Books, Catholic formation, liturgy, and a three-day hybrid model (evidence cited below).

    - The curriculum and messaging reference the Gospels, saints, and Catholic teachings as part of the intellectual formation, tying theology and sacred tradition into learning.

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Why this rating
    - The material consistently foregrounds transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) and a Catholic theological framework with liturgical practices, a shared sacramental imagination, and communities anchored in Catholic worship. The frequency and explicitness of Catholic formation and liturgical life support a CLASSICAL rating in this category.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence

    - Great Books and a canonical, literature-rich approach are referenced, consistent with classical pedagogy.
    - The public materials emphasize a hybrid model and family collaboration, which can support classical formation but do not always spell out explicit high-school Socratic seminars, spoken rhetoric, or explicit doctoral-level seminars in the visible pages. The explicit documentation of Socratic seminars, memory work, or rest/Scholé days is not consistently shown in the public-facing materials.
    - There are references to collaborative teacher-parent co-education and a three-day in-person schedule, which aligns with a classical approach but not a full, explicit articulation of all classical pedagogical modalities in the site content.

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
    - Positive keywords present: trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric); Great Books; memory-oriented practices (narration, recitation) are implied through the classical framework; a rest-and-scholé ethos appears in classical education lexicon (though not always spelled out in the site text). The explicit use of "Socratic seminar" or "Paideia seminars" is not clearly documented in the public pages.
    - Negative keywords: explicit reliance on experiential or project-based learning, or constructivist pedagogy, is not evidenced; however, explicit statements about Socratic seminars, narration, or memory-based recitation as a routine upper-level practice are not consistently presented in the visible site content.

- Why this rating
    - The Pedagogy category shows strong classical elements (the trivium; Great Books; canonical-liberal-arts approach), but the lack of consistently explicit documentation of certain classicPedagogical practices (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars for all high-school levels) in public materials leads to a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation rather than CLASSICAL.
