## Executive Summary

Sacred Heart Academy Grand Rapids (SHA), ZIP 49504, presents a robust Catholic/classical orientation in its publicly accessible materials. The school explicitly brands itself as a Catholic, classical academy whose mission centers on forming hearts and minds in pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, with daily liturgical life, sacramental focus, and a parish apostolate structure. Public content emphasizes formation over mere information, the integration of theology with pedagogy, and a commitment to Western/Great-Books-oriented cultural formation, all aligned with classical aims. While the core Catholic/classical narrative is clear and strong (including daily Mass, liturgical life, and partnership with families), the school also features elements that are not typically associated with a pure classical model (notably the mention of Montessori in some program areas). Taken together, SHA registers as predominantly CLASSICAL, with a clearly stated aim of formation, virtue, and the pursuit of the true, good, and beautiful. The presence of Montessori programming represents a notable deviation from a strict, textbook classical pedagogy, thus a SINGLE category (Pedagogy) is assessed as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. All other categories align with classical criteria, particularly Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community.

Key insights at a glance:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong, explicit Catholic identity; daily Mass; liturgical life; theology-centered education; sacred culture framed as inseparable from learning. Evidence-rich alignment with transcendental moral realism and liturgical practice. 
- School Purpose and Goal: Education framed as formation toward virtue, wisdom, and sanctity; explicit reference to truth, goodness, beauty as real; formation over mere information. 
- Curriculum: Classical framing evident; explicit commitment to classical philosophy of education; references to Great Books and classical inquiry; some content suggests a broader Catholic-cultural formation (incarnation lens). Some ambiguity remains about explicit, ongoing Great-Books-reading requirements in all grades, but the overall orientation is classical. 
- Pedagogy: Predominantly classical in orientation, but with notable inclusion of Montessori and other approaches, which yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for pedagogy. Evidence includes a stated classical identity, enrichment courses, and other non-traditional elements. 
- Faculty: Public materials portray a structured leadership and faculty/staff presence, with a strong sense of mission and formation. The public-facing leadership and faculty pages emphasize teachers as guides in a Christian/classical formation, consistent with classical education norms. 
- Parents & Community: Explicit partnership with parents as co-educators, parent formation efforts, open-campus orientation, and welcome to visits—all hallmark features of a classical formation ecosystem.

What would move the needle

- Strengthen explicit, pervasive Great-Books engagement and Trivium/Quadrivium embodiment in the core curriculum across grade bands, with explicit public articulation of a continuous Great Conversation and primary-source reading plan.
- Reduce or clearly segregate Montessori or non-classical pedagogical components in core K–12 programming to reinforce a pure classical pedagogy (as defined by the Six Essential Elements). Public-facing statements should foreground a consistent classical-methodology in daily practice, not only in mission statements.
- Expand explicit, visible evidence of upper-level Socratic seminars, rhetoric training, and formal public speaking across grades, including sample rubrics, student rhetoric projects, and scheduled Socratic seminars in the upper grades.
- Increase documentation of the “living curriculum” with teacher portraits, classroom narratives, and concrete examples of the teacher as a model of virtue and mastery—emphasizing the "portrait of a teacher" and "teacher as mentor" as sustained public commitments.
- Ensure curriculum materials and communications consistently reference Great Books, Great Conversation, liberal arts, and the unity of truth, goodness, and beauty in all major program descriptions, not only in niche pages.
