## Executive Summary

Mayflower Christian Academy Virginia (Pulaski, 24301) presents a clear Christian formation-orientated program with strong theological grounding, small class sizing, and a mastery-based approach. Public-facing materials emphasize Bible-based curriculum, a Christ-centered mission, and a commitment to forming character and leadership in students. However, the school does not explicitly publicize classic liberal-arts features central to a rigorous Classical education, such as a formal emphasis on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), engagement with Great Books and the Great Conversation, or explicit Socratic pedagogy. On balance, the school demonstrates substantial classical tendencies in its religious foundations and commitments to formation, but it falls short of a full Classic model in Curriculum and Pedagogy as defined by the Six Essential Elements framework.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are CLASSICAL in tone due to explicit theology, Biblical framing, and liturgical elements in practice. Evidence includes a Statement of Faith, Bible-based curriculum, and explicit Christ-centered mission.  
- The School Purpose and Goal show strong formation aims (virtue, leadership, independent thinking) that align with classical ideals, but lack explicit full articulation of the Great Books/Great Conversation frame. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal. 
- The Curriculum emphasizes Christian materials (My Father’s World, IEW, BJU Press, Apologia) rather than Great Books or the Trivium framework; thus Element 3 reads as NOT CLASSICAL in its strictest sense, though it shows some classical-oriented structure. 
- Pedagogy emphasizes mastery and student-led advancement within a small-class, one-room context, but lacks explicit Socratic seminars, recitation, or formal memory work that typify classical pedagogy. This places Element 4 in SOMEWHAT/NOT CLASSICAL territory; given the prominence of mastery-based methods and a traditional classroom setting, I assess as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- The Faculty dimension is evidenced by a leadership-forward message from the head of school and a culture of mentorship, yet explicit “living curriculum” portraits or teacher-mentorship models are not clearly advertised beyond standard teacher-student roles. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signal. 
- Parents & Community show a strong partnership orientation with families and open lines of involvement (parental engagement and commitments in forms and admissions). This aligns with classical tradition and earns CLASSICAL in Element 6.

The strongest Classical signals lie in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Parent/Community, with notable classical tendencies in Philosophy of Education and Leadership Formation, but the Curriculum and Pedagogy dimensions remain incomplete relative to a full Classical model. See category-by-category notes above for specifics and citations.

What would move the needle

- Explicitly integrate Great Books and Great Conversation into the core curriculum, including a plan to introduce primary classical texts and canonical authors at appropriate grade levels.
- Adopt and publicize classical pedagogy elements beyond mastery: implement Socratic seminars in upper grades, regular recitation/memorization practices, and structured rhetoric training (e.g., formal oratory, declamation, and guided writing in the classical style).
- Reframe history as a canonical, Western-civilization-centered curriculum that foregrounds primary sources and historical interpretation in alignment with the Trivium’s grammar/logic/rhetoric progression.
- Strengthen the visible “living curriculum” concept by detailing faculty development around classical pedagogy, showcasing teacher exemplars, and highlighting portrait-driven teacher standards that model virtue and wisdom.
- Expand explicit formation language (telos, virtue formation, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty) with concrete metrics and curriculum mapping to show how students engage with these ideals in daily learning.
- Increase the explicit integration of liturgical and communal practices with a clearly defined rhythm (calendar of feasts, prayers, and worship that ties into the curriculum) to strengthen the religious-metaphysical foundations.
