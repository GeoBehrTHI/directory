## Executive Summary

Concordia Classical Academy Ky 41094 presents a distinctly classical Christian program operating as a two-day cottage school in Northern Kentucky. Public-facing materials emphasize a traditional, virtue- and character-formation approach anchored in Christian theology, the liberal arts, and classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; great books; and classical history). The school explicitly frames education as formation (virtue, wisdom) rather than mere information, and promotes the true, good, and beautiful as real ideals. It partners with parents, references a Statement of Faith, and presents a community rhythm that includes shared worship concepts and school prayer. On balance, the school demonstrates clear classical elements across most dimensions, with especially strong alignment in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, and Curriculum. Pedagogy and Community aspects show robust classical signals but less explicit documentation of the full “living curriculum” faculty model in public-facing materials. The overall classification is CLASSICAL, with a notable strength in curricular coherence and theology-centric framing. A minor needle-moving opportunity exists around more explicit articulation of faculty as the living curriculum and deeper demonstration of Socratic pedagogy in upper grades.

Overall ranking and aggregate assessment
- Aggregate classification: CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle
- For this SCHOOL: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category (Element 5) is the only non-CLASSICAL signal. The following needle-moving steps could push this toward full CLASSICAL:
  - Develop and publish explicit language and concrete examples showing the faculty as the “living curriculum” and a clearly defined faculty culture (mentorship, collegiality, shared teacher-vision) across all grades.
  - Provide explicit public documentation of Socratic teaching practices in upper grades (e.g., sample seminar prompts, teacher prompts, and student-led Socratic dialogues) to demonstrate the pervasiveness of a Socratic pedagogy rather than a primarily instructor-led approach.
  - Increase transparency around continuous faculty professional development aligned with classical education standards (e.g., CLAS/CLS–style training, classical pedagogy workshops) and publish these efforts on the website.
  - Expand explicit liturgical or communal practices on campus (e.g., daily/weekly chapel, liturgical music, explicit times of communal prayer) to strengthen Liturgical and Communal Practices documentation.
  - Consider highlighting additional Great Conversation signals (e.g., explicit reference to Great Books, original texts, and cross-disciplinary dialogue) and ensure public materials consistently connect core curriculum to the Great Books/Western canon.
