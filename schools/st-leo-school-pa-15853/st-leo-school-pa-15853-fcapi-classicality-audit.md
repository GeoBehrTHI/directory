## Executive Summary

St. Leo School, PA 15853, presents a Catholic, classically oriented program rooted in a diocesan-school framework. Public-facing materials emphasize a “classical education model” and a Catholic identity aligned with the Elk County Catholic School System and the Diocese of Erie. This yields a strong Religious/Metaphysical Foundation and a clear School Purpose & Goal oriented toward formation. However, explicit, verifiable details on several core classical elements—especially the Great Books/Great Conversation tradition, the full Trivium/Quadrivium curriculum, and explicit Socratic pedagogy—are not consistently spelled out in the publicly accessible site content. Taken together, the school leans toward a CLASSICAL orientation in spirit, but the evidence in de facto public materials places it at a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL classification across several of the six elements.

Key strengths

- Strong Catholic identity and diocesan affiliation (theology-grounded framing) clearly present in public materials. This supports a robust religious/metaphysical foundation and indicates theology-informed formation within a Catholic framework. See evidence of Catholic school system affiliation and diocese in the crawl materials.
- Explicit labeling of the school as following a “classical education model,” signaling intent to align with classical pedagogy and curriculum principles.
- Publicly available curricular materials reference a curriculum explanation and structured academic planning, including preschool-to-middle-school information, calendars, and family handbooks, highlighting organized formation and family engagement.

Important caveats

- Explicit Great Books/Great Conversation, and explicit mention of Trivium/Quadrivium or Socratic Seminar pedagogy are not clearly documented in the readily accessible crawl content. The classical lexicon and rubric emphasize those facets, but they are not consistently evidenced in the public pages we can cite. See the classical rubrics and keyword indicators used for assessment.
