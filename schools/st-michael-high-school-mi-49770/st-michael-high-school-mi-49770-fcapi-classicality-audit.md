## Executive Summary

St. Michael High School, Petoskey, Michigan (a Chesterton Academy) presents a clearly articulated classical-Catholic program that aligns strongly with core Classical education tenets. The campus emphasizes a unified integration of faith, virtue formation, and a rigorous liberal-arts curriculum anchored in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (mathematics, geometry, astronomy, music), all within a Catholic framework. Public materials repeatedly stress that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable, and that education forms character and intellect in service of the eternal rather than solely for workforce preparation. The school explicitly frames itself as a “Classical Catholic” institution with a distinctive Three Pillars model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) and a mission to cultivate “leaders and saints” through a holistic formation that interweaves faith, reason, and virtue. The public-facing materials consistently foreground liturgical life, faith formation, and communal practices, while also detailing a rigorous curriculum and Socratic pedagogy. Taken together, these signals place St. Michael High School firmly in the CLASSICAL category, with strong alignment in religious foundations, purpose, curriculum design, pedagogy, faculty orientation, and parent/community partnership.

Key evidentiary anchors include: the school’s emphasis on an “Authentically Catholic, Rigorously Classical” approach and its Catholic formation framework; the Three Pillars model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality); integration of liturgical life and faith formation into daily school life; a curriculum described as integrated, classical, and Catholic with explicit reference to the Socratic Method and a liberal arts orientation; and active parent/community engagement through the Chesterton Schools Network and family partnerships. These cues appear across multiple materials, including Our Mission statements, curriculum descriptions, and faculty pages. See excerpts and corroborating materials here: Sacred liturgy and faith life interwoven with academics; Three Pillars in action; Great-work Western-Civilization context; and explicit Catholic formation signals.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

  - Negative signals: explicit workforce-prep language is largely avoided in public messaging; the site emphasizes formation rather than job-readiness.
  - Estimated: Predominantly positive keywords; very few, if any, negative indicators.
- Why this rating
  - The school consistently frames education as formation in virtue and wisdom with transcendent aims, emphasizing a classical telos over direct workforce preparation. This maps cleanly to CLASSICAL across the purposes/goals category. See mission and pillar language.

  - Additional curricular signals reference Great Books/Western tradition and a deliberate study of classical texts.

  - Estimated: Predominantly positive keywords; minimal or no negative indicators.
- Why this rating
  - The curriculum is framed around classical core elements (Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Liberal Arts) and explicitly uses Socratic/recitation-based methods, with an integrated humanities emphasis. This strongly supports CLASSICAL categorization for Curriculum. See “Integrated, Classical, Catholic” curriculum language and the Socratic-method emphasis. 

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The humanities program uses the Socratic Method; history/literature/philosophy/theology/languages braided together with inquiry-based dialogue. 
  - Pedagogical signals include memory-work, recitation, chanting, and a rest/Scholé-oriented pace (as described in the Classical lexicon and the Chesterton network materials); upper-level pedagogy features seminars, rhetoric, and public speaking opportunities. 
  - Foundational principles emphasize a balance of contemplation/restful elements with rigorous, dialog-based learning; lower-school singing/chanting and memory-building are cited in canonical materials. 
  - The Chesterton/Chesterton-network materials emphasize a classical learning model (the Uncommon Core), with a long-term emphasis on virtue, rhetoric, and debate. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive signals present: Socratic Method, dialectic, narration, recitation, chanting, memory-work, liturgical integration, seminars, Paideia-style discussions, rhetoric and oratory, debate.
  - Negative signals: the lexicon warns against an excessive focus on experiential learning or project-based-only approaches; the site’s materials do not primarily emphasize those modes.
  - Estimated: Predominantly positive keywords; minimal negative indicators.
- Why this rating
  - The school’s pedagogy centers on Socratic discussion, rhetoric training, and memory-based learning, with a blend of contemplative/restful and rigorous inquiry. This aligns with CLASSICAL pedagogy principles. See the explicit Socratic-method description and memory/chanting practices.
