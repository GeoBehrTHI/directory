## Executive Summary

Trinity Christian School Opelika (ZIP 36801) presents a clearly classical, Christian-based program. Public materials repeatedly foreground classical foundations (trivium/quadrivium, mastery of English, classical languages, logic, rhetoric) and explicitly frame education as formation in virtue within a biblical worldview. The school describes itself as a classical Christian academy and emphasizes a Christ-centered curriculum, virtuous character formation, and a partnership model with parents. Collectively, these signals place the school solidly in the CLASSICAL category across the six classical-education dimensions.

Key textual anchors include: the claim of being “East Alabama's only classical Christian academy” and the emphasis on truth, God, and a divinely ordered whole guiding all subjects; the explicit reference to the trivium and quadrivium as the architectural backbone of the curriculum; and descriptions of virtue, wisdom, and a Christian worldview shaping pedagogy, faculty, and parent partnerships. These points are supported by multiple pages from the school’s site (classical branding, admissions language, and philosophy), and reinforced by corroborating language about formation, virtue, and a biblical worldview.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If deeper CLASSICAL signaling is desired in Pedagogy (Element 4), the school could explicitly publish and publicize a formal plan or curriculum page that names:
    - Explicit use of Socratic seminars, seminars, or debates in upper levels.
    - A formal demonstration of scholé days, contemplation, or restfulness as a defined pedagogical practice.
    - A concrete mapping of how memory work, chants, and recitation are integrated across grade levels, with sample lesson plans or rubrics.
- If the school desires to amplify Great Books/Great Conversation signals in Curriculum (Element 3), it could publish a formal Great Books list or reading sequence, a “Great Conversation” plan for the upper grades, and a pathway showing how primary sources connect with the trivium/quadrivium structure.
- For Pedagogy (Element 4), publish a public-facing document that explicitly describes seminar leadership, student-led discourse, and the rhetorical development pipeline across grades, accompanied by sample student work (speeches, debates, and rhetorical projects).
- Expand parent-facing programming (Element 6) with more explicit “parent formation” offerings (book clubs, lectures on classical education, on-site class sessions with faculty), and ensure clear opportunities for parent visits and involvement beyond annual admissions cycles.

All sections currently demonstrate CLASSICAL orientation across six dimensions; the needle-moving changes above are optional enhancements that would push Element 4 (Pedagogy) toward a fully explicit, documented Socratic/seminar model, and would further strengthen Element 3’s Great Books/Great Conversation signaling.
