## ABSTRACT

Chi Rho Classical Academy Ky 40422 demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL profile across all six classical-education categories. Religiously, the school anchors its mission in Christ, the Apostles’ Creed, Holy Scripture, liturgical practice, and Catholic tradition, with communal worship and prayer embedded in daily routines. Its purpose emphasizes formation through virtue and wisdom rather than mere job preparation, aligning with classical telos. The curriculum centers on liberal arts education (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with Latin and a recognized emphasis on memory-work and Great Books/primary texts, supported by a Cycle-based Classical Core Curriculum. Pedagogically, the institution blends contemplative Scholé-like rhythms with Socratic seminars, memory work, recitation, and rhetoric training. Faculty are presented as mentors and living exemplars, with explicit staff profiles and a culture of virtue. Parent/community partnerships are strong, featuring explicit co-teaching roles for parents, homeschool-family collaboration, parent formation opportunities, and open campus accessibility.

Key evidence includes: “Traditional Education Rooted In Christ,” Apostles’ Creed and Holy Scriptures, Mass and liturgy routines; mission statements affirming wisdom, knowledge, virtue via liberal arts and Christ’s teachings; explicit Trivium/Latin/Great Books framing; Classically oriented curriculum materials (Classical Core Curriculum, cycles, memory work); pedagogical statements (Socratic dialogue, seminars, memorization, Scholé); named faculty profiles with mentorship language; and explicit partnership with families and homeschool support. These materials map directly to the Positive Keyword Indicators and the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School, demonstrating firm alignment with classical education principles.

Cited sources and content anchors include Chi Rho Classical Academy’s public site content and program descriptions, including references to Christ-centered formation, Apostles’ Creed, liturgical practices, the trivium and Latin, memory work, Great Books framing, daily prayer and memory work in schedules, and faculty profiles.
