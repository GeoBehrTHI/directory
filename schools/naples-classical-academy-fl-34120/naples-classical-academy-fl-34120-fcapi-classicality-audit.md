## Executive Summary

Naples Classical Academy (NCA) presents a robust classical-education profile with clear pillars around virtue, the liberal arts, Great Books, and Socratic/Scholarly pedagogy. Public statements across the site emphasize formation of character and civic virtue, engagement with truth, goodness, and beauty, and a curriculum centered on liberal arts and a great-books tradition. This aligns strongly with classical aims in most dimensions, though explicit theological grounding and liturgical/community practices are less foregrounded on the public-facing pages. The school also emphasizes parental involvement (Open Houses, PTC activities) and maintains a Hillsdale K-12 / Barney Charter framework, suggesting fidelity to well-established classical-school cultures. Overall, Naples Classical Academy is CLASSICAL, with a few areas that could be strengthened to move toward a fuller, more explicit articulation of religious/metaphysical foundations.   

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Evidence centers on truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, and moral character, but lacks explicit theology or liturgical practices in public materials. Positive keywords (truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, moral character, great-books) appear, with minimal presence of theology-liturgical cues. 

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
  - The mission emphasizes formation, cultivation of virtue and wisdom, and a view of education as formation of the person (not primarily job preparation). The explicit language around truth/virtue/beauty and the aim of forming citizens aligns with classical telos. 

- Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  - Evidence includes liberal-arts orientation (grammar, geography, literature, civics, etc.), Great Books/great-conversation emphasis, and a multi-grade structure with language and historical breadth. While the site strongly supports Great Books and liberal-arts pedagogy, explicit articulation of the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a universal “Great Books in every course” framing could be stronger. 

- Element 4: Pedagogy — CLASSICAL
  - Pedagogical methods include direct instruction and Socratic questioning, frequent use of seminars, and rhetoric training; lower- and upper-school practices show both contemplative/restful elements and rigorous discourse. 

- Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL
  - Faculty pages and references depict teachers as core, virtuous exemplars in a lived curriculum, with a wide roster of named faculty and governance emphasizing character formation and mentorship. The site frames faculty as central to the living curriculum. 

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
  - Active parent involvement is evident through the Parent Teacher Coalition (PTC), open-house events, and opportunities for community engagement; parents are invited to participate and support the school’s classical culture. 

Executive summary verdict: Overall CLASSICAL, with Element 1 and Element 3 showing SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL tendencies due to explicit theology-liturgy gaps and some curriculum framing that could be strengthened to more fully meet classical norms.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations)
  - Consider making explicit theological or liturgical elements visible in public-facing materials where feasible, clarifying any nonsectarian status while acknowledging transcendentals as guiding purposes. This could involve a brief, school-endorsed statement about metaphysical commitments or liturgical rhythm that aligns with local policy for public-charter institutions.
  - Enrich the vocabulary around liturgy/reverence/worship in at least a few public-facing statements to clarify liturgical-affect formation within the classical frame.

- Element 3 (Curriculum)
  - More explicit articulation of the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across all grade bands as formal curricular pillars, with clear mappings to specific courses or modules in K–12.
  - Strengthen the explicit language around “Great Books” across the full curriculum (not just in select grade levels) to reinforce “Great Conversation” as a transversal goal rather than a high-school emphasis only.
  - Ensure that history is consistently framed as history (not social studies) across all relevant materials, reinforcing canonical/historiographic approaches.

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal) and Element 4 (Pedagogy)
  - While formation and virtuous aims are strong, consider a more explicit articulation of the telos in all program guides (e.g., a concise “Purpose of Education” statement in each major publication).
  - Maintain and expand opportunities for Socratic inquiry and guided discussion while ensuring that contemplative learning (Scholé) remains prominent, to balance rigorous inquiry with restful learning rhythms.

- Element 5 (Faculty)
  - Continue to publish clear, public-facing portraits of teaching ideals and professional development pathways that align with classical pedagogy; emphasize ongoing faculty formation in classical pedagogy for new staff and visiting instructors.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community)
  - Deepen public materials on parental co-education roles and home-school partnerships, highlighting how families participate in forming affections and classical sensibilities, not only as event attendees but as co-educators.

Notes: The Needle-Movement section reflects concrete steps to push toward fuller CLASSICAL status where categories show SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL indicators. No major shifts are required for the already-strong CLASSICAL areas.
