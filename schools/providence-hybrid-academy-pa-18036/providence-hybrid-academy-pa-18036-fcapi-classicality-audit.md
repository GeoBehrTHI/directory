## Executive Summary

Providence Hybrid Academy (Lehigh Valley) presents a hybrid Charlotte Mason–influenced program with a clear religious/theological framing, a family-centered approach, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative, memory-friendly elements with some opportunities for discussion and higher-level thinking. Public materials emphasize a Christ-centered worldview, a strong emphasis on God-centered formation, and communal practices (Bible readings, hymns, poetry) within a framework that partners with families and honors childhood. While the school clearly articulates formation and truth-seeking in a theologically informed context, it does not consistently foreground the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, or explicit Socratic pedagogy in the way a classical school typically documents. On balance, Providence Hybrid Academy aligns with several hallmarks of classical education (especially in religious/metaphysical foundations and community engagement) but falls short on explicit curriculum scaffolding and pedagogical methods (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium emphasis, and formal Socratic/seminar practices) that would push it toward full classical status. Therefore, the aggregate rating is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete needle-movers identified below.

- The school is CLASSICAL in its Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and in its Parents & Community emphasis (strong theological framing; liturgical practices; family partnership).
- It is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy (Charlotte Mason orientation; some contemplative/restful approaches; limited explicit Great Books/Socratic structures).
- It is NOT CLASSICAL in Faculty indicators and, as currently documented, in explicit, living-curriculum faculty portrayal (board/staff structures exist, but the public materials do not showcase a “living curriculum” or teacher-as-virtue-model portrait in the classical sense).
- The aggregate view remains SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear needle-movers available in Curriculum (explicit Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium) and Faculty development (living-curriculum portrayal and classical teacher profile).
