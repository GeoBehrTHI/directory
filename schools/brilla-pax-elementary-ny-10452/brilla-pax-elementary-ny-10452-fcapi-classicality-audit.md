## Executive Summary

Brilla Pax Elementary Ny 10468 shows partial alignment with classical education through its emphasis on virtue and character development, but its public materials do not consistently demonstrate foundational classical elements such as explicit Great Books study, the Trivium (grammar–logic–rhetoric), Socratic pedagogy, or a historically grounded curriculum. Public content highlights a virtue-centered, college-ready mission and a welcoming, open-community stance, yet it lacks explicit attestations to key classical practices (Great Conversation, Latin/Greek, memorization/narration at scale, liturgical or contemplative practices, and a formalized living curriculum). Therefore, the overall assessment leans toward NOT CLASSICAL, with several category-specific signals of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (notably in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations and Parents & Community) but insufficient breadth and depth to achieve CLASSICAL status across the six categories.

Key takeaways
- The school’s public materials foreground virtue-centered education and college-readiness, and they emphasize openness to families and community engagement. This aligns with some classical aims in spirit (formation of character, community involvement) but stops short of many canonical classical markers (Great Books, Trivium pedagogy, Great Conversation, formal sacred/traditional liturgy, etc.). Evidence includes statements about serving families with an academically rigorous, virtue-centered education and enrollment openness (no entrance exams) within a multi-school Brilla network.   
- Category-by-category signals: 
  - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (virtue-focused language; no explicit theology or liturgical practices). 
  - School Purpose and Goal: NOT CLASSICAL (focus on college readiness with virtue framing; limited explicit formation/true-paideia framing). 
  - Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL (no clear Great Books, Latin/Greek, or explicit liberal-arts curriculum in public materials). 
  - Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL (no explicit Socratic/seminar/rhetoric-focused pedagogy documented in public materials). 
  - Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL (public materials list qualified staff but do not present a “living curriculum” or classical faculty portrait; emphasis is on personnel rather than classical formation). 
  - Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (open enrollment, transparent access, and invitation to visit; limited explicit parent-formation or co-teaching language).

Aggregate assessment

The category-by-category mix yields a general assessment of NOT CLASSICAL, with notable SOMEWHAT signals in Element 1 and Element 6.

What would move the needle

- Adopt explicit Great Books/Great Conversation principles across the curriculum, paired with a Trivium framework (grammar–logic–rhetoric) and a Latin/Greek component for appropriate grade bands.
- Implement documented classical pedagogy, including Socratic seminars in upper grades, structured narrations/recitations, and a restful-Scholé cadence (integrating contemplation with rigorous study).
- Develop a documented “living curriculum” with portraits of teacher-student formation standards, and a formal faculty development path aligned with classical pedagogy.
- Create explicit parent formation and partnership programs (parent book studies, classical home education guidance, family liturgy/rituals) and clearly state them on the website to foreground the parental co-educator role.
- Normalize liturgical or communal practices (where appropriate to the school’s mission) to reflect a more intentional religious/metaphysical dimension that aligns with classical patterns (without compromising inclusivity or the school’s charter).
