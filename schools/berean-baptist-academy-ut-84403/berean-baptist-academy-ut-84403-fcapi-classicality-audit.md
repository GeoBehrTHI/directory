## Executive Summary

Berean Baptist Academy Utah (Berean Baptist Academy) presents a clearly classical-inflected program with a Christian theological core. The school's public materials emphasize the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin study, chant-based memory work in the grammar years, and dialectic/debate in the middle grades, with rhetoric and apologetics in the upper grades. There is explicit theological framing (Classical and Christian; Bible-informed aims; Academy Statement of Faith; Scriptures as a guiding light) and a stated aim to form virtue and wisdom in students, not merely to impart information. The public materials also stress faculty dedication, close teacher-student mentorship, parental partnership, and an emphasis on a community oriented around shared beliefs. Taken together, the school aligns with a classical, Christian educational vision across most criteria, with some room to more explicitly articulate Great Books/Great Conversation and some of the broader “wonder-integrated” elements that appear in the strongest classical syllabi.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key insight: Berean’s strong alignment with the grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, Latin study, and explicit theology weaves a coherent Classical Christian profile. The main opportunities to strengthen toward a fuller, textbook-anchored classical model would be to foreground explicit Great Books-based coursework and demonstrate more routine integration of Great Conversation across history, literature, and the other liberal arts.

Evidence and category-by-category analysis below reflect keyword-driven analysis drawn from the school’s public materials and the Classical education criteria document (Six Essential Elements of a Classical School). Citations reference the relevant public materials in the crawl of Berean Baptist Academy and the Classical education knowledge base.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The curriculum centers on the grammar, logic, and rhetoric triad; in grammar years (3rd–5th), chanting and memory work are used; Latin begins in 3rd grade; in dialectic years (6th–9th), debate and discussion and logic are emphasized; in rhetoric years (10th–12th), rhetoric and an apologetics Bible course are taught. This constitutes a strong Trivium-based curriculum.
- The school explicitly frames itself as a Classical and Christian School and prescribes the grammar/logic/rhetoric structure as foundational. This is a robust signal for the Trivium-centric liberal-arts approach.
- Latin study is highlighted, aligning with traditional classical curriculum signals.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, Latin, dialectic, debate, apologetics, Bible curriculum, chant/memory work (as part of grammar-stage pedagogy).
- Negative keywords present: There is limited explicit reference to Great Books or the Great Conversation; no explicit “Great Books” or “Great Conversation” language is shown in the public snippets. The absence of explicit Great Books engagement is a potential gap relative to a fuller classical program. (The Six Essential Elements document lists Great Books/Great Conversation as a hallmark; their explicit presence is not demonstrated in the Berean materials provided here.)

Why this rating

- The core elements of the Trivium–grammar, logic, rhetoric, plus Latin, plus a structured grade-level pedagogy (chanting for memory in the grammar years; dialectic debate; rhetoric/Apologetics) strongly support a Classical orientation. However, the curriculum does not explicitly demonstrate Great Books/Great Conversation or an explicitly continuous, history-spanning Great Books sequence in the available materials. Because of that absence, the category is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: it is solidly Trivium-based and Christian, but the Great Books/Great Conversation dimension is not clearly evidenced in the public materials we have.

Citations

- Public curriculum description with Trivium emphasis: Berean materials.
- Latin, chant/memory, grammar-year pedagogy; dialectic/rhetoric progression: Berean materials.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Pedagogy is explicitly described as a tripartite movement: grammar-stage chant/memory in lower grades, dialectic debate in middle grades, rhetoric and apologetics in upper grades. This is a textbook articulation of the classical pedagogy (Scholé/restful memory work and Socratic/seminar-style discussion in higher grades).
- Grammar years employ singing/chanting to commit material to memory; dialectic years emphasize debate and questioning; rhetoric years emphasize clear communication and formal rhetoric courses, including an apologetics component. This directly corresponds to the Classical pedagogy arc.
- The public materials also indicate a goal of forming students through virtue and mastery of the material, with a cognitive arc from memory/repetition to critical discussion and persuasive speaking.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: chanting, memory work, restfulness, Socratic-style discussion signals (dialectic/debate), rhetoric/persuasion, formal rhetoric courses, apologetics, seminar-style engagement. These map to the Positive Indicators around Scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, and Socratic/seminar style pedagogy.
- Negative keywords: The materials do not emphasize purely project-based or experiential learning at the expense of these traditional methods. There is no explicit “experiential-learning-dominant” signal in the public materials. (The Negative Keywords in the classical lexicon emphasize experiential or project-based approaches as potential markers of non-classical pedagogy; Berean’s language does not foreground that as the primary approach.)

Why this rating

- The explicit articulation of the Trivium pedagogy and the concrete invocation of grammar, dialectic, and rhetoric, along with chant/memory methods in early grades, strongly support a CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy. The presence of Scholé-like memory and contemplative rhythm in the lower grades, plus dialectic/seminar-style upper-grade instruction, aligns with classical pedagogy signals.

Citations

- Grammar/Dialectic/Rhetoric progression and chant/memory in lower grades: Berean materials.

- Not applicable (the aggregate rating for Berean Baptist Academy Utah is CLASSICAL). No major needle-moving changes are required to move this school into a higher tier within Classical education according to the rubric. If desired, the following could further strengthen the Classical profile:

# ABSTRACT

: The six-category synthesis places Berean Baptist Academy Utah squarely in CLASSICAL territory, with strong religious/theological foundations, a clear formation-oriented aims in School Purpose and Goal, a robust Trivium-based Curriculum and Pedagogy, a clearly defined Faculty model, and strong Parent/Community engagement. The main area for potential strengthening lies in the explicit incorporation of Great Books/Great Conversation across the curriculum, which would push Element 3 to a more robust CLASSICAL alignment. The overall program presents a coherent Classical Christian model with a strong formation emphasis and a well-structured Trivium pedagogy.
