## Executive Summary

Leman Academy of Excellence – Mesa, AZ (85212) presents a clearly classical-inflected public charter model. Public materials emphasize a tuition-free K–8 classical education that centers wisdom, virtue, and character development, with repeated claims of a “classical curriculum,” small classes, and a close, partnership-oriented school community. These signals align strongly with the Classical education ethos in the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal categories, and to a meaningful extent in Curriculum and Faculty. However, there is less explicit evidence of several core pedagogical features (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholé/memory-retentive practices, or a highly articulated Great Books/Great Conversation program) on the publicly accessible materials, which tempers the alignment in Categories 3–4–6. Taken together, the school earns a GENERAL RANKING of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong footing in foundational virtue-oriented aims and a recognizable classical framework, but lacking explicit, consistently articulated elements in several hallmark categories.

- The site states a tuition-free K–8 classical education that “nurtures wisdom, virtue and academic excellence,” emphasizing character formation and a close, partnership-based school community with dedicated teachers. These signals support CLASSICAL in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal, and imply a classical approach in Faculty.
- Public signals of Curriculum and Pedagogy are present but not uniformly explicit about the full Great Books ideology, Trivium/Quadrivium framing, or Socratic/seminar-based teaching in the Mesa program, which leads to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Curriculum and NOT CLASSICAL/NOT enough for Pedagogy by strict rubric, depending on interpretation.
- The Parents & Community signal exists (partnership with families, active parent involvement concepts in classical education literature) but the site does not always spell out parents as co-teachers in the MESAsphere; this yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 6.

Aggregate Assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale: The school demonstrates strong CLASSICAL signals in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal (virtue, wisdom, formation), plus clear Faculty signals (dedicated teachers, close partnerships). However, explicit Pedagogy (Socratic/seminar-based practice, scholé restfulness), and thorough Curriculum (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, history through the curriculum) articulation are not consistently evidenced in the Mesa public materials. Parents & Community show engagement signals but lack of explicit co-teaching or formal parent-formation programs keeps the aggregate classification at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Pedagogy: Publicly document and implement explicit classical pedagogy elements on the Mesa campus, including Socratic seminars at appropriate grade levels, regular seminars or debates in upper grades, and structured memory/chants where appropriate (scholé-inspired cadence). Demonstrate how grading integrates with memory, memory work and recitation, and how rhetoric develops in upper grades.
- Curriculum: Provide explicit statements and curriculum maps that highlight the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium elements, explicit Great Books selections, and consistent integration of history across the curriculum rather than a “social studies” framing. Highlight Great Conversation commitments and primary texts to illustrate a coherent Great Books/Great Conversation approach.
- Liturgical/Religious Foundations: If alignment with liturgical or communal practices (where appropriate for the school’s identity) is part of the ethos, clearly articulate liturgical or communal practices and theology integration in the curriculum and daily routines.
- Parent & Community Partnerships: Expand explicit language and structures to position parents as co-educators, with formal parent formation resources, reading groups, and opportunities for parental involvement in curriculum development and classical pedagogy adoption. Publicize open-campus policies, parent-focused activities, and home-school partnerships more explicitly.
- Faculty Development: Emphasize teacher formation in classical pedagogy; publish a “portrait of the teacher” and the people who embody the school’s core virtues as part of the living curriculum, including professional development aligned to classical methods.

# Abstract

Leman Academy Mesa (85212) presents a CLASSICAL orientation in its public materials, with strong signals in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal due to emphasis on virtue, wisdom, character formation, and a classical framing. The Curriculum and Faculty signals are present but less explicit about full Great Books commitments, Trivium/Quadrivium structure, and a rigorously articulated scholé-based pedagogy. Pedagogy, in particular, shows room for more explicit classical teaching methods. The Parents & Community dimension demonstrates partnership and engagement but would benefit from clearly articulated co-educator roles and formal parent-education infrastructure.

Overall, the school falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with solid foundations and a clear classical vision, but with notable opportunities to strengthen explicit classical pedagogy, curriculum mapping, and deeper parent-formation practices.
