## ABSTRACT

Overall Classification: NOT CLASSICAL (with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL elements in several dimensions)

Key insights:

- The Draper 2 campus emphasizes virtue and character formation via a values-based Reading University program and mission statements, which signals formation-oriented aims (a partial alignment with classical education’s formation goal). Public content explicitly centers on forming students to be “Smart & Good,” rather than presenting a full classical curriculum. Evidence of this formation emphasis is visible in mission statements and Reading University materials.
- There is clear evidence of strong parent/community engagement (PAC and family involvement), which aligns well with classical practices of partnership with families. This category shows a strong classical signal.
- However, explicit classical curricular components (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, Great Conversation) and formal Socratic pedagogy are not visible in the public Draper 2 materials. The pedagogy appears more contemporary/reading-centered than strictly classical, though Scholé-related concepts appear in related scholarly materials. Thus Category 4 is SOMETIME CLASSICAL rather than classical in pedagogy.
- Faculty portrayal as the living curriculum and explicit faculty culture (mentorship, virtue modeling) is not clearly evidenced in the available public content, leading to a NOT CLASSICAL designation for Category 5.
- Category 3 (Curriculum) is NOT CLASSICAL due to the absence of a described classical curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books) in the public materials.

Aggregate assessment: NOT CLASSICAL, with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL aspects (notably Category 2 and Category 4 to some extent, and Category 6 in strong alignment with classical community practices). The evidence base is largely oriented toward formation and values-based literature, with less emphasis on explicit classical curricular architecture.
