## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of Our Lady of Hope in Rhode Island (Warwick, RI 02886) presents a robust, self-described classical-Catholic program that aligns strongly with the classical education rubric. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered, Virtue- and Wisdom-forming mission, a curriculum centered in liberal arts and Great Books, a pedagogy that blends Socratic dialogue with traditional memory-work, a faculty culture described as mentors and living exemplars, and a deep partnership with families and parish life. Taken together, the school exhibits clear signals across all six classical categories, with Catholic liturgy, a focus on truth-goodness-beauty, a liberal-arts curriculum emphasizing Trivium/Quadrivium and Great Books, Socratic-based pedagogy, a faculty-as-curriculum ethos, and an active parental/community partnership. Based on the evidence from the school’s public materials and the classical rubric in the knowledge base, the aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Executive support for this assessment comes from multiple public materials and statements, including explicit Catholic identity and daily liturgy, a mission to form joyful saints through a classical education, demonstrations of Great-Books-era study (Homer, Dante, Shakespeare, The Canterbury Tales, etc.), evidence of Socratic pedagogy and rhetorical training, and strong parental/community engagement (Open Houses, Shadow Days, Founding 100 donations, Gala, tours, etc.). The knowledge-base lexicon confirms the language and practices that constitute a Classical program (truth, virtue, beauty; theology; liturgy; Great Books; Trivium/Quadrivium; Socratic dialogue; formation; partnership with families; and a caregiver/community orientation) and provides explicit criteria for evaluating each category. See cited sources for the core evidence and keyword mappings.
