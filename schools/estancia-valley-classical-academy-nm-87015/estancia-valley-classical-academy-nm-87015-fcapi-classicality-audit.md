## Executive Summary

Estancia Valley Classical Academy (EVCA) presents a clearly classical orientation across most rubric categories, anchored in a classical liberal arts framework, virtue formation, and a pedagogy that foregrounds traditional classical methods. Public materials show a mission-and-culture built around character formation, the liberal arts tradition, and a deliberate emphasis on a great-books oriented curriculum and Socratic-style pedagogy. Evidence from EVCA’s own materials points to a strong alignment with the classical aim of forming virtue and wisdom rather than merely imparting information, with explicit references to classical pedagogy (Scholé, Socratic dialogue, memorization/recitation) and a governance structure steeped in parental and community involvement. Still, some categories reveal room for stronger explicit articulation of theology, liturgical/community practices, and explicit faculty modeling as a “living curriculum.” Overall, EVCA is best categorized as CLASSICAL, with a few areas that are SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully classical in the published materials.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Public EVCA materials foreground virtue, character, and moral formation within a classical framework, including explicit mentions of virtues and habits of character (honesty, discipline) and a mission oriented toward the classical liberal arts. 
  - The materials do not show a robust, explicit theology or liturgical framework in the public-facing text, though classical education literature cited by EVCA includes theological and transcendent themes in the broader classical lexicon. This limits the category to somewhat classical rather than fully classical. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: virtue, moral formation, character, habits of character, honesty, discipline. 
  - Negative keywords present: none clearly evidenced in public materials (no explicit secular ethics or value-neutral framing shown in the extracts). The absence of theology-liturgical framing keeps this within “SOMEWHAT.” 
- Why this rating
  - The school clearly aims at virtue and character, aligning with transcendental aims of classical education, but lacks explicit theological and liturgical articulation in the accessible public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL placement rather than fully CLASSICAL in this category.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - EVCA positions education as formation and cultivation of virtue and wisdom, not simply information or job-ready training. The founders describe a mission to provide a content-rich, rigorous classical liberal-arts education aimed at forming character and excellence. 
  - Mission statements emphasize fidelity to the classical tradition and “habits of character” as part of education, signaling a telos oriented toward truth, goodness, and the beautiful. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, human flourishing, great conversation (implicit in Great Books/moral discourse), classical mission, virtue ethics. 
  - Negative keywords present: minimal explicit emphasis on workforce/job-readiness; public materials do not foreground “career-ready” as the primary aim. Some elements (e.g., a later College & Career event) appear, but they don’t dominate the stated telos. 
- Why this rating
  - The school’s stated purpose emphasizes formation and virtue within the classical liberal-arts framework, aligning with the classical aim of human flourishing and the pursuit of truth. While college/career preparation is incidental (evidenced by events like a College & Career Fair), it does not appear as the primary goal in the public mission statements. This justifies CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

The Founders describe an education in "humanities, languages, history, government, geography, and the first principles of philosophy" and sciences in math and natural philosophy, with arts including visual arts and music. 
  - Public materials repeatedly tie EVCA to classical education norms: Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books/Great Conversation, and integrated history-laden inquiry implied by a cross-disciplinary approach, as discussed in the broader classical lexicon used by EVCA. 
  - Pedagogy section reinforces alignment with traditional classical curriculum modalities (recitation, memorization, and canonical/historic texts via classical methods). 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium: arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), Great Books/Great Conversation, history integrated with literature/philosophy, interdisciplinary unity, wonder/holy integration. 
  - Negative keywords present: none in public text signaling a departure from classic curriculum norms (no explicit project-focused or purely STEM-first framing as a dominant approach). The public descriptions align strongly with classical content and approach. 
- Why this rating
  - EVCA’s curriculum statements and public materials align with core classical curriculum tenets (Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, interdisciplinary integration, wonder, and unity of truth/history/philosophy). This warrants CLASSICAL positioning for Curriculum.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Pedagogy section explicitly names Socratic dialogue and dialectic, Scholé (restful learning), memorization/recitation (singing/chanting, memory work), and formation through imitation and practice; upper-level rhetoric, speech, and debate opportunities are noted. This directly mirrors classical pedagogy expectations. 
  - The public materials describe lower-school practices (singing/chanting, memory work) and upper-school structures (Socratic discussions, seminars, debates), which match the classical pedagogical progression from memory work and contemplation to argumentation and rhetoric. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue, Scholé (restful learning), recitation/memorization, singing/chanting, imitation, masterful rhetoric, speeches, seminars, and debates. 
  - Negative keywords present: none evident in public materials; no overt emphasis on purely experiential, constructivist, or project-based-only pedagogy in these extracts. 
- Why this rating
  - The school’s pedagogy map aligns closely with classical teaching methods (Socratic, memorization/recitation, restfulness, and rhetoric). This justifies CLASSICAL.

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

  - Founders’ narrative emphasizes formation and character in teachers’ and leaders’ roles, and governance includes community/parent involvement, suggesting a living, mentoring culture rather than a purely disciplinary or transactional faculty model. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

  - Negative keywords present: explicit statements of “faculty as living curriculum” and detailed teacher-profile portraits are not robustly shown in the extracts; some classical markers exist but not in a fully explicit, codified way (e.g., a formal “portrait of a teacher” or explicit faculty formation program). 
- Why this rating
  - EVCA demonstrates strong classical sensibilities in terms of faculty roles and mentorship implicitly through mission and pedagogy, but the publicly accessible materials do not present a fully explicit, codified “living curriculum” faculty framework. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL in this category.

- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: partnership with families, co-teachers, parental engagement, home-school collaboration, open campus/inclusion of families in school life. 
  - Negative keywords present: none evident in the extracts; the school’s own materials foreground family partnership and community involvement rather than consumer/customer standpoints. 
- Why this rating
  - The public materials strongly reflect a classical ethos of family partnership and community integration, which aligns with the “Parents and Community” category as CLASSICAL.
