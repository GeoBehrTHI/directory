## Executive Summary

Wisdom Truth Classical Academy (AR 72713) presents a clearly Christian, classical framing in public materials, with explicit emphasis on biblical truth, virtue, and wisdom. Public statements stress formation of character within a classical Christian framework and a communal aim to “glorify God.” This yields strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal signals (Categories 1 and 2), aligning with classical ideals in those dimensions.

However, the publicly available materials do not consistently foreground the explicit liberal-arts curriculum anchors (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Conversation) in the way the standard classical rubric expects. As a result, Element 3 (Curriculum) is only partially evidenced. Similarly, explicit pedagogy (Socratic dialogue, scholé, chanting, memory work), faculty living-curriculum portrayals, and formal parent/community partnerships are not clearly documented in the public-facing content available in the crawl. Taken together, the school demonstrates several classical signals but does not meet the full, category-wide criteria in every dimension based on the available public materials. Overall assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key takeaway: WTCA is clearly rooted in a Christian classical ethos (Element 1 and Element 2 carry strong signals), but it lacks explicit, publicly stated evidence of the full liberal-arts curriculum structure, pedagogy, faculty portrayal, and parent/community partnering that would push it into CLASSICAL on every category. Concrete steps that would strengthen its case toward full CLASSICAL include explicit statements about Great Books/Great Conversation, articulation of Trivium/Quadrivium within the curriculum, publicly documented pedagogy (including Scholé and Socratic methods), a stated living-curriculum faculty model, and robust parent/community partnership initiatives.

What would move the needle (flagged for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL schools)

- Publicly articulate a Great Books/Great Conversation program and explicitly name the Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) as core pillars of the curriculum, with explicit examples of texts and historical breadth.
- Describe pedagogy in more concrete classical terms (Socratic discussions, Scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, training in rhetoric) and provide examples from grade bands.
- Publish a clear “living curriculum” portrait of the faculty and a formalized, visible partnership with parents (co-teachers, home-school alignment, parent formation opportunities).
- Highlight explicit evidence of liturgical or communal practices as part of the school day or year, to meet the Liturgical & Communal Practices criterion.
