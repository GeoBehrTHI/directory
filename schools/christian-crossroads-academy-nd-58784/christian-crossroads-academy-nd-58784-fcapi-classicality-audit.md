## Executive Summary

Christian Crossroads Academy (ND 58784) presents a robust alignment with classical Christian education, anchored in a Christ-centered mission, the liberal-arts foundation, and a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric). Public materials emphasize forming character and intellect together—placing virtue, wisdom, and love of truth, goodness, and beauty at the heart of learning. The school foregrounds Latin study, biblical centrality, and a mission to cultivate disciplined, thoughtful students who pursue knowledge in service to Christ. While explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language is less visible in the material reviewed, the program consistently reflects core classical notions (grammar, logic, rhetoric; history and literature studied in a Christian framework; contemplative, memory-based pedagogy; and a strong partnership with families). Overall, the assessed materials classify the school as CLASSICAL, with minor gaps in explicit enumeration of certain classical practices (e.g., explicit Great Books emphasis and, in pedagogy, explicit Socratic/seminar framing). Evidence drawn from the school’s own pages and FAQs, as well as the classical lexicon for coding positive/negative indicators, supports this determination. 

- Executive summary category-by-category snapshot indicates strong CLASSICAL orientation in Religious Foundations, Purpose & Goals, Faculty & Community, and Parents/Community. Curriculum and Pedagogy show substantial classical alignment but with fewer explicit references to some hallmark elements (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and explicit Socratic pedagogy in every program level). This yields an overall CLASSICAL assessment. See below for category-specific keyword analyses and evidence.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school presents a Christ-centered, theologically integrated model: “Educating the mind, forming the heart, and shaping the soul for Christ” and a mission framed by a Classical Christian approach (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with theological grounding. 
- Biblical centrality and theological framing are explicit, including statements that the Bible is central to all learning and that the school aims to form students in a biblical worldview. 
- Worship-oriented and liturgical-influenced practices appear in the context of music and reverence for sacred truth, beauty, and worshipful aspects of education. 
- The Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) is foregrounded as the structural backbone of the curriculum, with Latin highlighted as a foundational language reinforcing grammar and historical-theological study. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; liturgy; reverence; wonder; wisdom; history and language rooted in Latin; Christian doctrinal framing. 
- Negative indicators: no explicit signals of relativism, secular humanism, or morally neutral stances in the public materials identified. The lexicon lists possible negatives, but the school materials reviewed do not show them. 

Why this rating
- The public materials consistently map to a transcendent moral framework (truth, goodness, beauty) grounded in Christian theology and liturgical/community life, with a clear aim of character formation aligned to virtue and wisdom. The explicit emphasis on Christ, the Bible, and a liturgical/communal dimension supports CLASSICAL rather than merely historical or secular approaches. The use of Latin and the Trivium further aligns with classical categories. 


### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Language around virtue, wisdom, and human excellence, along with the idea of education as formation rather than information, aligns with classical aims.

- The public statements center on character formation, virtue, wisdom, and a classical mission, with explicit rejection of education as merely information. The stated telos and the paideia-oriented language align with CLASSICAL in purpose. 


### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum foregrounds liberal arts with grammar, logic, and rhetoric as organizing principles (the trivium) and Latin as a foundational study. This is a strong classical anchor. 
- History is studied throughout the curriculum in a textual, historical sense (not simply “social studies”), with emphasis on primary sources or classical/hegemonic Western civilization narratives. Public materials mention history and literature in a traditional sense, including the aim to study stories and moral questions through primary works. 
- There are explicit references to literature and "classical tools" (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and to language study (Latin). Public materials emphasize reading and language as vehicles for understanding truth and virtue. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: liberal arts; trivium; grammar; logic; rhetoric; Latin; history; primary sources/literature; classical tools; Western civilization. The content explicitly names grammar, logic, rhetoric and Latin, and frames literature/history in a classical way. 
- Negative indicators present: no explicit reference to Great Books or explicit Great Conversation on the public-facing materials; no explicit Socratic-seminar or seminar-through-Socratic pedagogy in all grade levels is stated in the accessible pages (pedagogy is less explicit beyond overarching classical framing). The material does not clearly state an explicit Great-Books-reading push. This constitutes a partial gap relative to a fully classical curriculum as traditionally defined in some classical schools. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum is strongly anchored in the liberal-arts Trivium and Latin, with historical and literary study, and a classical framing. However, explicit statements about Great Books/Great Conversation and ubiquitous Great-Books pedagogy are not clearly evident in the public materials provided, which colors this category toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL. 


### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogy emphasizes a mix of methods aligned with classical aims: memory-rich teaching, recitation, and a contemplative dimension (scholé-like) appear in classical teaching lexicon; the lower-school emphasis on memory and ritual learning sits alongside upper-school Socratic possibility in classical education frameworks. Public materials show a rhythm of rhythm, structure, and a memory-forward approach (e.g., Latin, grammar, and textual study). 
- The presence of a “learning cohorts” model implies a move away from traditional age-graded pedagogy toward a more personalized, mastery-based progression, which can be compatible with classical memory and recitation practices but is not equal to explicit Socratic seminars in every grade. 
- The public materials emphasize a Christ-centered, moral formation with a focus on understanding and wisdom rather than rote testing alone, which aligns with a contemplative, non-frantic pedagogy typical of scholé. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: narrations/recitation, chanting, memory work, restfulness/scholé, seminar/Paideia-style discussions are listed as classical methods in the lexicon; the school demonstrates a pedagogy that values memorization, structured language study, and a contemplative approach (scholé). The materials support a contemplative and memory-based dimension (even if not always described with the exact classical labels). 
- Negative indicators: explicit project-based learning dominance or experiential learning bias is not evident in the public materials; however, the explicit articulation of Socratic seminars or upper-school rhetoric/competitions is not consistently documented in the pages we reviewed, which softens the frame toward a full classical pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The program presents several core classical pedagogy elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric intake; memory-based learning; contemplative/restful elements; potential for Socratic discourse at higher levels) but does not consistently articulate all hallmark mechanisms (e.g., explicit, pervasive Socratic seminars across grades). This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.
