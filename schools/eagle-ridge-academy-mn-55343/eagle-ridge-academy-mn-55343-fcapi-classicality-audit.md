## Executive Summary

Eagle Ridge Academy (Minnetonka, MN 55343) demonstrates strong classical orientations in its public materials, with several explicit signals of a classical liberal arts approach, virtue formation, and family partnerships. The school’s public messaging emphasizes time-tested Classical Liberal Arts education, character formation, and a structured pillar system (CIPHER: Citizenship, Integrity, Perseverance, Honor, Excellence, and Respect). It also organizes its K–8 programs around a grammar-logic-rhetoric progression and cites a focus on foundational Western texts and critical thinking. Overall, the evidence places ERA as CLASSICAL with some room to deepen certain classical practices (notably explicit Great Books engagement, liturgical/theological grounding, and fully articulated Socratic-seminar pedagogy across the upper grades). Aggregate rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key signals from ERA materials include:
- Declarative classical emphasis: “a demanding college-preparatory, Classical, liberal arts curriculum” and a mission to engage students in a time-tested Classical Liberal Arts education (evidence of a strong classical frame) , .
- Explicit core values and pillars: Veritas, Pulchritudo, Probitas (Truth, Beauty, Goodness) as central tenets, plus a public articulation of CIPHER values (Citizenship, Integrity, Perseverance, Honor, Excellence, Respect) .
- Curriculum scaffolding aligned with classical pedagogy (K–4 School of Grammar; 5–8 School of Logic; Core Knowledge integration with 6–8) showing progression through the Trivium-like structure and liberal-arts orientation, with emphasis on foundational texts and cross-disciplinary study in the middle years , , .
- Public-facing statements of formation over information and a mission to develop character and virtue, not merely academic achievement (e.g., “strong character” and formation language) .

Overall classification and synthesis

- Why the aggregate is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: 
  - Strong CLASSICAL signals exist in Curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric scaffolding; liberal arts framing) and in Parent/Community alignment (partnerships, open visits).

These gaps pull the overall profile toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL. See evidence above and citations to ERA materials. 

- What would move the needle (to become fully CLASSICAL):

Include a clear articulation of how classes are designed to cultivate wonder and contemplation as a regular practice.
  - Faculty (Element 5): Publish a formal “living curriculum” statement and a teacher portrait that clearly describes the aspirational teacher profile, mentorship roles, and professional development in classical pedagogy. Include examples of faculty collaboration and teacher-led seminars or master-teaching models to demonstrate the living-curriculum approach in action.
  - Religious/Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1): If desired by the community, publish explicit theological grounding or liturgical-life components as part of a public-facing section (even in a limited, inclusive form appropriate for a public charter) to strengthen the Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices signals. This would move the grade toward a more overt Theological foundation, if aligned with the school’s broader mission and public policies.
  - Curriculum (Element 3): Expand and publicly highlight Great Books and primary-text engagements across more grades, including explicit reference to “Great Books” discussions and the Great Conversation, and demonstrate how the Trivium/Quadrivium framework is explicitly integrated across the curriculum with cross-disciplinary coherence (e.g., history-texts, philosophy, and literature connected within a unified program).
