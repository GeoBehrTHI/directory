## Executive Summary

Delaware Valley Classical School (DVCS) in New Castle, DE 19720 presents a robust, clearly classical trajectory aligned with the core Six Essential Elements of Classical Education. Public materials describe a Christ-centered, classical curriculum built on the grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, a strong emphasis on Great Books and the Western canon, intentional formation of virtue, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative, memory-rich lower grades with Socratic discourse and rhetoric in the upper grades. The school highlights Great Books, classical languages (Latin, Greek), a house-system culture, and formal mentorship, all under a Biblical worldview with explicit theology components. These features collectively map strongly to the classical rubric, suggesting a CLASSICAL overall evaluation.

Key signals across the six categories include:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations anchored in Christendom and theology, with explicit Bible memory, hymnody, liturgical rhythm, and systematic theology emphasis. Evidence spans kindergarten “Christ-centered education,” Bible memory, and theology-forward upper-level instruction.  
- Purpose and goal centered on formation, virtue, and the good life, not merely job prep, with crisp statements about training to love truth, goodness, and beauty, and to pursue Christ and His Kingdom. 
- Curriculum built on the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium, plus Great Books/Great Conversation, history-driven across grades, and an emphasis on wonder. Latin/Greek instruction and integration with theology are highlighted.  
- Pedagogy explicitly includes contemplative/restful learning, memory- and singing-rich lower grades, and upper-grade Socratic discussion and rhetoric training. 
- Faculty described as model teachers, living curriculum, and mentors; there is a formal leadership structure (Head of School, Logic & Rhetoric Principal, Grammar Principal) and ACCS/NCPSA accreditation reference. 
- Parents and community engagement framed as partnership with families, co-education, and ongoing education for parents (e.g., blogs, resources, and grandparent-focused content).

What Would Move the Needle

- The school currently presents a strong classical profile with limited signs of deficit in the rubric. If a needle-moving step were considered, it would be to more explicitly document and quantify the Great Books list and the specifics of the Quadrivium components in the public-facing materials, to further demonstrate the depth of Great Books/Quadrivium engagement and the breadth of the Great Conversation across grades. Additional public statements about history/humanities integration across disciplines (beyond explicit mentions) could further reinforce the “integration and wonder” criteria at scale.
- Concrete steps (if aiming for a more explicit CLASSICAL alignment across all potential rubrics):
  - Publish a publicly accessible, cross-grade Great Books syllabus with primary-source selections and corresponding discussion prompts.
  - Provide a concise articulation of how the Quadrivium is structured within the middle/upper school programs (arithmetic/geometry/music/astronomy) and how these are integrated with other subjects.
  - Expand parent education programming with more systematic, regular offerings (book studies, lectures) to reinforce the partnership with families as co-educators.
