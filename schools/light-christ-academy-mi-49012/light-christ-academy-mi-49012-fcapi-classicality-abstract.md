## ABSTRACT

Light of Christ Academy (Augusta, MI) exhibits CLASSICAL alignment across all six classical-education categories. The Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are anchored in Catholic formation and liturgical life, with catechesis and the Catholic moral framework shaping character formation. The School Purpose and Goal emphasizes formation through truth, goodness, and beauty, not mere vocational preparation. The Curriculum integrates liberal-arts foundations (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, and the Great Conversation, with integrated history study and a robust Latin/poetic/chanted dimension. Pedagogy combines contemplative, memory-rich Montessori practices in the lower grades with Socratic seminars, debates, and rhetorical training in the upper grades. Faculty portrayals underscore virtue and mentorship, and Parents & Community are positioned as co-educators, with explicit language about parental partnership, open visits, and church-community life.

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) = CLASSICAL. Evidence includes daily Mass, catechesis, liturgical life, and explicit metaphysical framing (the True, the Good, and the Beautiful). 
- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) = CLASSICAL. Evidence shows formation and virtue-centric aims, not just job prep. 
- Element 3 (Curriculum) = CLASSICAL. Evidence includes Great Books, Great Conversation, and grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, plus memorization, poetry, and Latin. 
- Element 4 (Pedagogy) = CLASSICAL. Evidence shows lower-grade contemplative elements and upper-grade Socratic/rhetoric pedagogy. 
- Element 5 (Faculty) = CLASSICAL. Evidence includes named staff with virtue-formation signals and leadership roles. 
- Element 6 (Parents & Community) = CLASSICAL. Evidence includes parent-as-educator framing and open-enrollment/family engagement. 

General assessment: CLASSICAL

The school’s public-facing materials coherently embody classical education’s core motifs: formation in truth, goodness, and beauty; a triadic curriculum (grammar-logic-rhetoric) built around Great Books and classical discourse; and a liturgical, virtuous, family-centered community that actively integrates parents as co-educators. While some rubric-precise language (e.g., explicit “living curriculum” phrasing) could be bolstered in public communications, the overall alignment across all six categories is robustly CLASSICAL.
