## Executive Summary

Covenant Classical School Sd 57108 presents a robust alignment with classical education principles, anchored in a distinctly Christian worldview and a formation-focused approach. Public materials describe a collaborative, parent-partnered model, a commitment to virtue formation, engagement with Great Books and the “great conversation,” and attention to the liberal-arts framework through elements such as Latin study and history as the organizing backbone of curriculum. The school emphasizes virtue, wisdom, and beauty, while keeping faith formation at the center of its mission. Overall, the school’s materials meet the core criteria of a classical education in most categories, with particularly strong signals in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Faculty, and Parents & Community. A few curricular/pedagogical details appear less explicit (e.g., explicit articulation of grammar/logic/rhetoric as formal terms in the curriculum, and Socratic pedagogy in upper grades), resulting in a tendency toward CLASSICAL with some elements that are clearly classical and others that are still developing toward a full, explicit Trivium-centered framework. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Curriculum and Pedagogy show robust classical signals (Great Books, history-forward curriculum, Latin), though explicit articulation of all Trivium components (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and some Socratic pedagogy elements are less overt in the public materials. What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly name and demonstrate the full Trivium framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric) in public materials and on the site, and provide concrete examples of how each component is implemented across grade bands.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide explicit descriptions of Socratic seminars, seminars, or debates in upper grades and demonstrate how upper-grade rhetoric and oratory are cultivated (e.g., sample capstone oration prompts).
- For Element 1 (Religious Foundations): While the Christian/theological framing is strong, additional explicit articulation of liturgical practices, communal worship, or liturgical life within the school’s weekly rhythm could strengthen signals in the “Liturgical and Communal Practices” area.
- For Element 2 (Purpose and Goal): Continue to emphasize formation/wisdom ascent with more explicit articulation of “unfolding true, good, beautiful” as a daily telos in classroom practice and assessment.
