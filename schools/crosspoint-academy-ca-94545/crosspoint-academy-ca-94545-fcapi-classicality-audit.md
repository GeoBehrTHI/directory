## Executive Summary

Crosspoint Academy California (CCA) presents a notable integration of Christian formation within its K–12 program, with explicit religious instruction, devotional practices, and a Christian worldview embedded across school life. Public materials emphasize Bible study, chapel, community service, and a Christian ethos in athletics and academics. While these elements strongly reflect a religious foundation and formation-focused aims, explicit classical-pedagogy signals (e.g., a structured Trivium, Great Books program, or Socratic seminars) are less evident in the available materials. Taken together, Crosspoint Academy CA 94545 lands primarily in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL zone across the six-category rubric, with clear CLASSICAL alignment in some domains (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; School Purpose and Goal) and more modest alignment in other domains (Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community), plus a cautious notch toward NOT CLASSICAL in Pedagogy due to limited explicit classical teaching methods in public-facing materials.

Key evidentiary notes:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are strongly present: Bible classes, daily devotions, chapel, and a Christian worldview permeate curricular and co-curricular life (examples include daily prayer/devotions and integration of faith with study)    .
- The school’s stated purpose leans toward formation, virtue, and a teleology tied to truth/beauty within a Christian framework (formation via Bible-based instruction and community service) .
- Curricular signals show limited explicit Great-Books/Trivium/Quadrivium language, though there are occasional classical references (e.g., a summer program reference to The Odyssey and Greek history) rather than a formal Great Books framework .
- Pedagogy signals point to liturgical and devotional practices and community life, with limited explicit evidence of Socratic seminars, rigorous memory work, or the full Schola/Scholé pedagogy typically associated with classical education in public-facing content  .
- Faculty materials show credentialed leadership and a staff structure (Academics Director, Principal roles, etc.) within an affirmed Christian-education milieu (ACSI alignment noted) rather than a fully articulated “living curriculum” classical profile publicly stated  .
- Parent and community engagement exists (family portals, open houses, and opportunities for parent involvement), though explicit classical-parent partnerships and formal parent-education programs are less visible in the public content (some signals of family involvement through admissions and events) .
