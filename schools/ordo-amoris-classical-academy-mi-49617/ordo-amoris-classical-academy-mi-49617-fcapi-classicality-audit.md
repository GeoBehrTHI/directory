## Executive Summary

Ordo Amoris Classical Academy (OAC) in Beulah, Michigan (MI 49617) presents a clearly classical Christian education model, delivered as a homeschool-hybrid with strong parental partnership. Across public-facing materials, the school foregrounds its classical aims (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, liberal arts), its telos toward virtue and wisdom, and a covenantal, community-based approach that centers families as co-teachers. In short, the materials demonstrate robust alignment with core classical education signals, including a strong religious-metaphysical frame, a formation-focused purpose, a curriculum anchored in liberal arts and Great Books, explicit classical pedagogy practices (narration, memory work, imitation), and a family-centered community ethos. The public content also emphasizes a distinctive identity—Ordo Amoris (Latin for Rightly Ordered Loves)—and a visible commitment to catechetical and liturgical framing within a Christian classroom ethos. Overall, the school is rated CLASSICAL, with only minor indications of pedagogical practices that are less explicitly documented as classical (see Element 4). The aggregate assessment reflects strong classical alignment across categories, with Element 4 showing partial alignment rather than full classical pedagogy, thus a CATEGORY-BY-CATEGORY picture that reinforces an overall CLASSICAL classification.

- Executive judgment: The school demonstrates clear and consistent alignment with classical education foundations across most dimensions, including religious-metaphysical grounding, formation-focused goals, a Great Books-informed curriculum, and a family-centric, teacher-mentored instructional model. The aggregate ranking is CLASSICAL.

- What would move the needle? For a strictly CLASSICAL designation with perfect alignment, nothing major is required. The strongest opportunities to tighten alignment would be to make explicit any Socratic/seminar pedagogy in upper grades and to foreground explicit statements about Socratic dialogue, Paideia-style seminars, or explicit rational rhetoric pedagogy across the upper grades to close any remaining gaps with the canonical Pedagogy quadrant.

Negative keywords from the lexicon (relativism, secular humanism, values-clarification, morally neutral) are not evidenced in the public materials. Estimated: Positive matches approx. 6–9; Negative matches approx. 0. Classification justification: The materials articulate transcendentals and sacred formation, with explicit theology-integration and liturgical/communal cues evident in the school’s language and mission. 
  Why this rating: The religious-metaphysical foundations are central and explicit, aligning with the Classical model’s emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty as real and knowable, and the integration of theology and liturgy into the school life.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The materials emphasize formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, and the belief that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable; the curriculum aims at human flourishing rather than mere job-readiness (e.g., “true education should be directed toward the telos… of the cultivation of persons in wisdom, virtue and godliness”; avoidance of an exclusive emphasis on workforce preparation). 
  Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include formation, virtue, wisdom, telos, classical mission, Great Books, and liberal education; Negative signals from the lexicon (college/career readiness, 21st-century skills) are not prominent in the public materials. Estimated: Positive approx. 5–7; Negative approx. 0–1. Classification justification: The school presents education as formation toward human excellence and moral imagination, consistent with classical aims rather than a narrow vocational focus.
  Why this rating: The stated purpose centers on virtue and wisdom, not solely job prep, and integrates a formation-oriented teleology (telos) aligned with classical education signals.

- Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The school’s materials reference liberal arts education, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, primary sources, and Western civilization; explicit mention of “Great Books,” “Great Conversation,” and the liberal-arts framework appears in governance and curricular materials (e.g., portraits and curriculum references). The curriculum is described with references to classical content and aims (grammar, logic, rhetoric; history; Latin/Greek; classical literature; and other classic humanities).  
  Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include liberal education, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, Western civilization, Latin/Greek, history as discipline, and poetry/literature; Negative signals (social-studies re-labelling of history, STEM-first framing) are not evidenced. Estimated: Positive approx. 6–9; Negative approx. 0–1.   Why this rating: The explicit curriculum language and structure map onto classic liberal-arts and classical texts, with clear emphasis on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Great Books.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The site describes master-apprentice mentorship, imitation, narration/recitation, and memory-work as foundational pedagogy (e.g., “Instructing is mentoring, and is essentially a master-apprenticeship relationship.”; emphasis on imitation; recitation; memory). There are explicit references to contemplative/restful learning and to the use of memory devices (chanting, singing) in lower grades; upper grades are said to use Socratic discussion and rhetoric training in some traditions, though explicit, classroom-wide Socratic seminars are not consistently documented across the site.  
  Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include narration, recitation, chanting, memory, master-apprentice, imitation, seminar-style discourse in some contexts, and rhetoric; negative signals in the lexicon include projects-focused, experiential-only approaches and constructivist trends. The public materials show a mix: strong traditional methods (narration/recitation/memory) but less explicit, universal documentation of Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars across all grades. Estimated: Positive matches approx. 5–7; Negative matches approx. 0–2 (depending on whether one counts the absence of explicit “Socratic seminars” as a negative indicator). Classification justification: The pedagogy strongly reflects classical traits (memory, recitation, imitation) but lacks explicit universal documentation of Socratic/Seminar pedagogy in every grade, suggesting a partial but not complete alignment with the Pedagogy category.
  Why this rating: The school employs several core classical pedagogy methods (memory, narration, imitation, master-apprentice teaching) and a contemplative/restful dimension, but the absence of explicit, universal Socratic seminar documentation lowers it from full CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The school emphasizes partnership with parents as co-teachers, parental formation through websites, book studies, gatherings, and parent involvement in school life; there are references to open campus access and parent covenant language. Aggregate Assessment
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Rationale: The school demonstrates strong classical alignment across five of the six categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community) with Pedagogy showing substantial classical alignment but not uniformly documented as full Socratic seminar pedagogy. The aggregate indicates CLASSICAL with a single area (Pedagogy) that is MOSTLY classical rather than fully classical in every grade, hence still CLASSICAL overall.

What Would Move the Needle
- If aiming for a stronger, explicit universal classical pedagogy across all grades, the school could:
  - Document and implement uniform Socratic/Paideia-style seminars or seminars in upper grades, with clear scaffolding and assessment.
  - Expand explicit reference to classical seminar methodology (Socratic discussion, Paideia-style teaching) in public-facing materials for all grades to reinforce the Pedagogy alignment.
  - Continue to foreground “memory work” and “chanting” in lower grades with explicit connections to classical pedagogy (and potentially integrate them with more explicit Latin/Greek ritual or recitation cycles).
