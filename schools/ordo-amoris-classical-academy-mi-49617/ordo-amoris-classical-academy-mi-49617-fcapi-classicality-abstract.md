## ABSTRACT

Ordo Amoris Classical Academy (OAC), Beulah, MI (49617), is classified as CLASSICAL. The public materials demonstrate a robust classical Christian orientation with a strong teleology toward virtue and wisdom, a curriculum anchored in liberal arts and Great Books, and a family-centered model that treats parents as co-teachers. The religious-metaphysical foundations are explicit (nurture and admonition of the Lord; Ordo Amoris as a guiding philosophy; Covenant language). The school’s purpose centers on formation and human excellence rather than mere job preparation, aligning with classical aims. The curriculum cites grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, and Western civilization; this is reinforced by portraits of a graduate emphasizing telos, virtue, and imitation. Pedagogy features strong classical elements such as narration, memory work, and master-apprentice mentorship, with some documentation of contemplative/restful pedagogy. Faculty are depicted as living curriculum and mentors, and parents are explicitly integrated as co-teachers, with open community and covenant language. Where pedagogical explicitness (Socratic seminars) is less documented across all grades, the strongest aspects of classical education are still evident.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
  Evidence includes explicit Christian formation, covenantal language, and the Ordo Amoris philosophy; keywords include nurture and admonition of the Lord, telos, and Rightly Ordered Loves.  

- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows formation, virtue, wisdom, and teleology as guiding principles; the school emphasizes a formation-based education over mere job prep. 

- Curriculum: CLASSICAL
  Evidence emphasizes liberal arts content (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Great Books, Latin/Greek, and Western civilization as core content; curricular references to Great Books/Great Conversation appear in school materials. 

- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence centers on narration, memory work, imitation, and master-apprentice models; lower grades use chanting and singing to aid memory; upper grades suggest Socratic-style elements, but explicit universal documentation of Socratic seminars across all grades is not consistently evident in public materials. This yields a strong classical orientation with partial documentation of full Socratic pedagogy. 

- Faculty: CLASSICAL
  Evidence describes faculty as mentors and living curriculum, with a focus on character formation and a collegial culture. 

- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows explicit partnership with parents as co-teachers, parental formation opportunities, and an open-community posture, including covenant language. 

Overall, Ordo Amoris Classical Academy demonstrates a strong CLASSICAL orientation with well-developed religious, teleological, curricular, and community aspects, and a pedagogical approach that is predominantly classical but with room to more explicitly codify universal Socratic pedagogy across all grades.

Note: All assessments above rely on the school’s public-facing materials accessed via the Ordo Amoris Classical Academy site (crawled content) combined with the classical-education rubric and keyword lexicon from the knowledge base. See sources cited within each category for textual evidence.
