## Executive Summary

Cornerstone Academy Florida (CA) presents a clearly religiously anchored classical program. The school foregrounds a biblical worldview, discipleship, and formation of character alongside a structured Trivium-inspired curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and associated language study (Latin, Greek). Public materials repeatedly frame CA as a classical Christian school with a mission to form virtue and wisdom in students, not merely to prepare them for college or careers. Evidence from the site emphasizes a theology-infused, liturgical-leaning culture (weekly chapels, apologetics, biblical worldview courses) and a governance/ownership structure aligned with Christian formation, parental partnership, and a living-mission orientation. Some classical criteria—most notably the explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis and certain socratic/seminar pedagogy signals—are less openly documented in CA’s public content, which lowers certainty in categorizing CA as fully classical across every rubric criterion.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Executive notes: CA demonstrates strong religious/metaphysical foundations, a virtuous formation aim, and a robust Trivium-based curriculum with evidence of parental partnership and a mission-driven faculty. However, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments and widespread Socratic/seminar pedagogy signals are not overtly documented in public materials, which prevents a full CLASSICAL rating across all six categories. See category-by-category details below for the keyword analysis and categorical justifications. Citations indicate where these observations are drawn from CA’s public content and the classical rubric sources.

- What would move the needle (for a full CLASSICAL rating):
  - Add explicit, school-wide Great Books reading and Great Conversation commitments with documented value statements and assigned texts.
  - Introduce more explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy in secondary grades and publish concrete descriptions of these practices.
  - Expand liturgical/community practices to more formally articulate and document contemplative time (Scholé) and rest in daily routines.
  - Strengthen visibility of the “living curriculum” concept in faculty profiles and teacher-student mentorship language.

- Abstract takeaway: Cornerstone Academy Florida demonstrates a strong classical Christian orientation with clear formation and Trivium-based curriculum, solid family partnership, and a committed faculty. Its public materials affirm a CLASSICAL orientation in most dimensions, with some missing explicit attestations to classic elements like Great Books and Socratic pedagogy that keep it from a full CLASSICAL rating at this time.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly commit to and publish Great Books / Great Conversation commitments within the curriculum (and provide a public roster of primary texts and reading lists).
- Introduce and document structured Socratic/seminar pedagogy for upper grades, including example transcripts, rubrics, and outcomes to align with classical pedagogy.
- Expand and clearly label contemplative/scholé elements (restful learning, liturgical rhythm) in daily schedules and public communications to fulfill the more restful dimension of the classical model.
- Strengthen faculty professional development around teaching methods that foreground living books, memorization, and classical memory work as a consistent part of the lower school experience.
- Increase explicit articulation of “living curriculum” concepts in faculty profiles and public communications, including teacher portraits and expectations for mentors and role models.
- Further articulate and document the partnership with families as co-educators, including formal parent education programs, reading groups, and structured opportunities for parental formation, to solidify Element 6 as a deeply integrated feature of CA’s model.

These steps would push Categories 3 (Curriculum content extended to Great Books and Great Conversation), 4 (Pedagogy with more Socratic/Scholé elements), and 6 (Parents & Community deeper integration) toward CLASSICAL, thereby elevating the overall assessment toward CLASSICAL.
