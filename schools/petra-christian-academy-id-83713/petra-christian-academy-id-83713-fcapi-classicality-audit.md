## Executive Summary

Petra Christian Academy Id 83713 presents itself publicly as a tuition-free, Christian classical school that emphasizes Christian formation and a classical branding. Public materials explicitly label the school as “Tuition Free Classical Education” and “Live Online Christian Classical Education,” signaling a classical framework paired with a Christian worldview. The school also advertises a Statement of Faith (We believe the Bible to be the only inerrant, authoritative Word of God) and a mission to develop Christian leaders, suggesting a purpose-oriented focus on character and virtue within a theological frame. Evidence from the site includes explicit mentions of “Christian education,” “classical education,” and a vision of formation (e.g., formation of Christian leaders) alongside a substantial reliance on volunteer teachers to support tuition-free options. These signals point to a Classical/Christian integration, but with limited explicit articulation of several traditional classical curriculum and pedagogy elements (e.g., Great Books, Great Conversation, seven liberal arts, or structured Socratic pedagogy) in the public-facing materials. Overall assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear strengths in religious/theological grounding and formation-oriented aims, but lacking explicit, comprehensive public articulation of core classical curriculum and pedagogy markers.

Key public signals observed from Petra’s materials:
- Explicit classical Christian branding and online delivery: “Live Online Christian Classical Education” and “Tuition Free Classical Education.” 
- Clear theological anchor via a Statement of Faith: “We believe the Bible to be the only inerrant, authoritative Word of God.” 
- Admissions language framing a vision of forming Christian leaders in a Christian life. 
- Prominent reference to “classical Christian education” and a philosophy of thinking and well-rounded graduates. 
- Provision of tuition-free options and volunteer-driven teaching as a model for accessibility.

What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum clarity and expansion
  - Explicitly articulate the curriculum’s classical components: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; and Quadrivium as applicable), Great Books program, and the Great Conversation. Include a publicly visible matrix mapping courses to Trivium/Quadrivium phases and the Great Books canon.
  - Introduce a Great Books sequence (with primary texts) and a formal Great Conversation initiative for upper grades, with teacher-guided discussion rubrics and assessment anchored in dialectic reasoning. Evidence sources: the Six Essential Elements emphasize Great Books and Great Conversation as core markers of classical education. 

- Pedagogical method enhancements
  - Integrate explicit SCholé (restful learning) pacing and opportunities for memorization/recitation in early grades, with documented upper-school Socratic dialogue or Harkness-style seminars in line with classical pedagogy. Publish examples of daily or weekly schemata for memory work and Socratic sessions. Evidence sources: the Six Essential Elements outline "Socratic Dialogue," "Scholé," and related pedagogical signs. 

- Liturgical/communal practices
  - Include explicit, structured liturgical or communal practices (e.g., morning prayers, liturgical rhythm, communal music) as an explicit part of the daily life, with protocols and outcomes. This would address the liturgical/communal dimension of the religious-metaphysical foundation category. Evidence: Positive signals exist in the “Liturgical and Communal Practices” criterion of classical education literature (the Six Essential Elements), and adding explicit practice would strengthen alignment. 

- Faculty as living curriculum
  - Develop and publish a formal “faculty profile” that describes teacher virtues, professional development, and how faculty embody the school’s classical aims. Clarify how teachers function as mentors and co-learners with students, and highlight exemplary teacher portraits. Evidence: The Six Elements emphasize “the living curriculum” and the portrait of teachers; public materials should align with this. 

- Parent and community partnerships
  - Expand formal parent-involvement programs (e.g., classical parent study groups, home-school alignment initiatives, parent education about classical education). Formalize “parents as co-educators” language and provide ongoing opportunities for parent engagement beyond volunteering. Evidence: The Six Elements emphasize partnership with families and community involvement. 

- Public articulation of the Great Conversation and canon
  - Publish a public synopsis of the “Great Books” canon and a rationale for literature choices, including how they connect to the school’s theological commitments. This would satisfy the curriculum criterion and reinforce the “formation” aim through canonical texts.
