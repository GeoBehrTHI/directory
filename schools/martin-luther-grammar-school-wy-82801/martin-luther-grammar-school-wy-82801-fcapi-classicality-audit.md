## Executive Summary

Martin Luther Grammar School (Wy 82801) presents a robust Classical Lutheran educational model embedded in a explicit Christian worldview. Public materials from the school’s site frame the program as classical (grammar–logic–rhetoric, Great Books/Great Conversation, Latin, and a liberal-arts orientation) and sacramental in its life (Chapel, liturgy, catechesis, gospel-centered instruction). The faculty and leadership appear oriented toward the classical tradition, with documented credentials and a clearly defined “living curriculum” ethos. Parent involvement and community integration are foregrounded, including open visitation and family-time chapel culture. Taken together, the school aligns strongly with core Classical elements and with the six-category rubric used in this evaluation.

Overall, the MLGS materials read as CLASSICAL across all six categories. The public materials repeatedly reference truth, goodness, beauty; a formation-oriented aim; the trivium and great books; Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy elements in the broader classical-literature tradition; a faculty-led living curriculum; and a partnership model with parents and church community. Where explicit statements are needed, the sources emphasize theology, liturgy, catechesis, and church-centered formation as integral to the educational mission. ### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The materials describe a pedagogy consistent with classical education: grammar-stage memory devices, recitation (as part of the classical memory-work ethos), and formation via imitation of masterworks. The Great Books and Trivium framing implies Socratic discussion and rhetoric in appropriate grade bands, with upper grades emphasizing seminars, debates, and public speaking. Direct references to Socratic dialogue, memory work, and rhetoric are found in the broader classical-education lexicon and in MLGS’s own materials.  
- The school’s own pages emphasize a balance of restfulness (Scholé) and rigorous study, and the lower grades’ engagement with songs, chants, and memory work align with classical pedagogy. The CCLE/Marks of a Classical Lutheran School content supports a pedagogy that is both contemplative and rhetorically active as appropriate to grade level.  
- There is explicit mention of upper-grade Socratic discussion and rhetoric training in the classical Lutheran materials and in the MLGS materials that discuss the path from grammar to logic to rhetoric. Public pages describe the classical path and public-facing descriptions emphasize the Trivium and Great Books.  

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue; seminars; discourse; rhetoric; memory work; recitation; scholé; wonder; contemplation; examination; debate; public speaking; master-teacher model. These align with Positive keyword indicators in the lexicon. 
- Negative keywords present: The MLGS materials do not foreground excessive project-based, student-led-only formats; rather, they emphasize structured memory work, teacher guidance, and classic texts—the signals in the public materials align with classical pedagogy rather than the non-classical negations. (No strong negative indicators observed in public MLGS content.) 

Why this rating
- The pedagogical approach aligns with classical method expectations (grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, memory work, Socratic/seminar traditions, and rhetoric training in upper grades). The school's own materials and the CCLE-aligned documents provide robust support for CLASSICAL pedagogy.
