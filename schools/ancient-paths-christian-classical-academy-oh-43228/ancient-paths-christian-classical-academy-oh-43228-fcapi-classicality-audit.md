## Executive Summary

Ancient Paths Christian Classical Academy (Columbus, OH 43228) presents a distinctly Christian, classical framework that foregrounds virtue, wisdom, and the liberal arts within a Biblically rooted worldview. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered, classical approach, partnership with families, and a commitment to Western civilization through the seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; with Latin, literature, history, and the other Trivium/Quadrivium components). The school explicitly identifies its mission as educating in excellence, equipping students to engage the world, and empowering them to endure in faith (Colossians 3:23; Matthew 28:18-20; Proverbs 22:6; Jeremiah 6:16). This combination signals a robust Classical orientation with clear religious foundations and parent partnership.

Overall, the school demonstrates a strong Classical frame in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goals, and Parent Community, while its public materials show solid alignment with core Classical content (liberal arts, Latin, literature, history) and a Biblically anchored formation. However, some pillars of a full classical program—notably explicit references to Great Books/Great Conversation, and detailed Socratic pedagogy/seminar practices for the upper grades—are less directly evidenced in the available materials. Taken together, Ancient Paths aligns closely with Classical aims but does not consistently demonstrate every hallmark of a canonical “full Classical” practice in every category. Therefore, the aggregate assessment is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale: The school aligns strongly with Classical aims in Religious Foundations, Purpose/Goal, and Parents & Community, and it demonstrates robust liberal-arts curriculum and biblically integrated formation. However, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis and fully articulated Socratic pedagogy (especially in upper grades) are not clearly evidenced in the public site texts. The faculty portrayal is present but not fully developed as a “living curriculum” framework in the public materials. Taken as a whole, the school is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong Christian formation and classical content, but not a complete, canonically explicit Classical model across all six categories.

What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum (Element 3): Publicly articulate explicit Great Books/Great Conversation components and a defined list of primary canonical works. Include reference to classical texts alongside Bible and Latin, and show cross-disciplinary integration demonstrating the unity of truth across disciplines.
- Pedagogy (Element 4): Add explicit documentation of Socratic/ Seminar-based learning in upper grades, Paideia-style seminars, and structured rhetoric/debate opportunities to demonstrate a full upper-school classical pedagogy.
- Faculty (Element 5): Develop and publish a clear “living-curriculum” profile for teachers, including mentor-model practices, ongoing faculty formation in classical pedagogy, and a public statement of teacher-student relationships as the core of the curriculum.
- Liturgical/Communal Practice (Element 1): If not already explicit, provide publicly accessible descriptions of liturgical cycles, prayer life, and communal worship or liturgical practices to strengthen the religious-metaphysical foundation and communal identity.
- General Public Narrative (Cross-cutting): Highlight Great Books, historical canon, and classical virtues in marketing materials and school communications to strengthen the perception and articulation of a fully classical program.

# ABSTRACT

Ancient Paths Christian Classical Academy demonstrates a clear Classical-Christian orientation with strong religious foundations and a robust liberal-arts curriculum framed within a Biblically grounded formation. The school emphasizes virtue, wisdom, and a canonical Western educational heritage, while stressing parent partnership and independence from state education. The six-category assessment finds the school CLASSICAL in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, and Parents & Community; SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty. The aggregate stance is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reflecting strong classical elements but with room for more explicit Great Books/Conversations, Socratic pedagogy, and a more formalized “living curriculum” portrayal of faculty. The school’s public materials substantiate a culturally coherent Classical-Christian program that centers faith, virtue, and the liberal arts as a coherent pathway for forming wise, virtuous, and eloquent students.
