## ABSTRACT

Founders Classical Academy – Prosper (Prosper, TX) demonstrates a classical-education orientation with a stated emphasis on virtue, truth, beauty, and goodness within a liberal-arts framework. The school presents a formation-focused mission and public commitment to classical education, with accreditation in the public-school system (ResponsiveEd/TEA). Across the six classical elements, the school shows strong alignment in School Purpose and Goal (CLASSICAL) and notable alignment in several other areas but partial alignment in Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The overall assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete opportunities to move toward full CLASSICAL alignment through explicit articulation of Great Books and Great Conversation, explicit Socratic/pedagogical practices in upper grades, a publicly stated “living curriculum,” and formal parent-education/co-teaching initiatives.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence: emphasis on truth/beauty/goodness and virtue; lack of explicit theology or liturgical practices in public materials. 
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Evidence: formation of virtuous citizens; pursuit of truth/ goodness/ beauty; formation-based framing. 
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence: liberal-arts frame; absence of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references in public materials. 
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence: general classical approach and liberal-arts pedagogy; lack of explicit Socratic/seminar practices in public materials. 
- Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL. Evidence: no “living curriculum” language or teacher-portrait framework in public materials. 
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence: partnership with parents; small campus environment; limited explicit parent-teacher-co-teaching language.
