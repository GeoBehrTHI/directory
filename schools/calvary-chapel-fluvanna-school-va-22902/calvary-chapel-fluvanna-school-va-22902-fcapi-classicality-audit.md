# Classicality Audit: Berean Christian Academy (Katy, TX)

Website used: https://www.bereanchristianacademy.org

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Why: Berean Christian Academy (BCA) explicitly grounds its mission in a Christ-centered worldview and the transcendentals of truth, goodness, and beauty; employs the seven liberal arts and the Trivium (Grammar–Logic–Rhetoric); emphasizes Great Books and primary texts; and gives clear evidence of classical pedagogy (Harkness/Socratic-style discussions, debate, rhetoric training, and a culminating senior thesis). The school articulates parent partnership (including parent interviews and required classical reading) and publishes a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate” that foreground virtue and wisdom. These align closely with the positive indicators in the Six Essential Elements framework and lexicon for classical schools      .

Highlights
- Mission: “Christ-centered worldview” to recognize “Truth, Goodness, and Beauty” and a stated partnership of “faculty and parents” .
- Curriculum: Seven liberal arts; Trivium named and described; Great Books and original texts; Latin through upper levels; structured history sequence (Ancient–Medieval–Modern); Rhetoric I/II; Senior Thesis; Debate/Mock Trial; Apologetics and Theology  .
- Pedagogy: Harkness Table conversation, debate, formal logic, oratory, oral presentations, senior thesis defense—classical rhetoric in practice  .
- Community/Parents: Required parent interview and classical-reading orientation; tours; ECC integrates family worship/church life; housed within a community of faith; active parent association noted in the handbook   .

Note on framework: Positive and negative keyword indicators and category criteria are drawn from “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” and the classicality lexicon (e.g., truth/goodness/beauty; theology; Trivium/Quadrivium; Great Books; Socratic/Harkness; scholé; chanting/recitation; parent partnership; and negative signals like “values-neutral,” “project-based learning” dominance, “college and career ready” emphasis)   .

## Overall Assessment
Aggregate result across the six categories: CLASSICAL

Rationale: Strong matches to the positive keyword indicators and criteria for (1) religious/metaphysical foundations, (2) purpose/goal, (3) curriculum, (5) faculty, and (6) parents/community; category (4) pedagogy is solid but lacks explicit public statements on chanting/recitation and scholé/restful learning. Even with that, the presence of Harkness/Socratic discussion, debate, rhetoric training, and a senior thesis keeps pedagogy clearly within the classical orbit  .

---

## What would move the needle (for Category 4: Pedagogy)
Although the overall rating is CLASSICAL, pedagogy was scored SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to missing explicit signals.

- Publicly articulate early-grades memory work practices—name “chanting,” “singing,” “recitation,” and “copywork/dictation” where used .
- Publish language affirming scholé/restful learning and “wonder-based education,” indicating times/contexts for contemplation in the school week or year .
- Share examples of Socratic seminars across grades (not just Harkness in upper school) to show progression.
