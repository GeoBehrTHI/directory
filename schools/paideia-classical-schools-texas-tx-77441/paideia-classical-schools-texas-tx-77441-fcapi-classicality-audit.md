## Executive Summary

Paideia Classical Schools of Texas (Fulshear, Spring Campuses) presents a clearly Christian, classical framing in public materials and explicitly references its Paideia-programmed approach, small class sizes, and a four-day-on-site schedule. The site emphasizes spiritual formation, virtue-oriented aims, and a partnership with families, all of which align with classical education narratives. However, explicit, widely articulated classical pedagogy and curriculum elements (e.g., Great Books, grammar–logic–rhetoric sequence, or documented Socratic seminars across grades) are not consistently stated in the public-facing content. On balance, the school demonstrates a classical orientation with notable Christian/theological emphasis, but it does not consistently demonstrate the full breadth of the classical rubric in its public materials. Therefore, the overall classification is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key public-materials evidence includes: the campus self-identification as a “Christian, classical, hybrid school” and mention of the Paideia program, small classes, and a four-day schedule (with explicit reference to spiritual, academic, social, and emotional education) (Paideia Classical School of Fulshear public materials). This is complemented by direct references to Paideia in the content and by a small but present faculty-staff section. See sources describing the Fulshear campus, Headmaster commentary, and staff pages for context. 

In addition, the knowledge-base materials (Six Essential Elements of a Classical School) provide the comprehensive rubric (positive/negative keyword indicators) used to evaluate each category, including explicit mentions of theology, liturgy, great books, scholé, Socratic pedagogy, and family partnerships. These elements frame the scoring rubric used in this assessment.

- Most categories show SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL alignment with the classical rubric; only Element 5 (Faculty) leans fully Classical, Element 4 (Pedagogy) clearly Not Classical, and Categories 1, 2, 3, and 6 show partial Classical alignment. Aggregate ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle (for a move to CLASSICAL)

- Curriculum clarity in public materials:
 - Explicitly state and map the full classical curriculum: grammar, logic, rhetoric; history through the Western canon; Great Books or Great Conversation; Latin/Greek where appropriate. Link these to the school’s Paideia framework and its mission.
 - Publicly articulate the role of Great Books and the Great Conversation in the curriculum, with sample scope-and-sequence.
- Pedagogy clarity and signaling:
 - Publicly commit to classical teaching methods: Socratic seminars, memory work/recitation, chanting or hymnody tied to the curriculum, and the restfulness/scholé approach in appropriate grades.
 - Demonstrate upper-school rhetoric training and opportunities for public speaking.
- Liturgical/communal language and practice:
 - If applicable, include explicit reference to liturgical practices or a community prayer rhythm (even if aligned with the school's Christian identity) to meet the Liturgical/Communal Practices criterion.
- Formation and parent partnership:
 - Highlight a formal parent-formation program (book studies, lectures, parent seminars) and opportunities for parent involvement as co-teachers, with clear expectations and pathways for involvement.
- Public evidence of the “living curriculum”:
 - Provide narrative and examples showing faculty modeling virtue and mastery, with quotes or stories about teachers embodying classical virtues, and explicit “portrait of a teacher” language in public materials.
