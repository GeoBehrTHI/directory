## Executive Summary

Twin Peaks Classical Academy (Longmont, CO; 80501) presents a robust classical orientation across multiple domains, anchored by a charter-school model and a teacher-led, standards-aligned program that centers on truth, beauty, and goodness. Public materials emphasize character formation, a liberal-arts-inflected approach (grammar, logic, and rhetoric), and a strong parental partnership. Overall, the school reads as CLASSICAL, with strong alignment in Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents/Community, and clear but partial alignment in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations and Pedagogy. The executive snapshot below details category-by-category signals, supported by explicit textual evidence from the school’s site and the classical-education rubric.

- The school foregrounds truth, beauty, and goodness, and highlights character formation (Six Pillars of Character) as core aims. These align with the classical formation mindset, though explicit theology and liturgical practices are not evident in the public materials. ### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The site emphasizes a teacher-led classroom model and a strong emphasis on character formation, with lower-level content that includes singing, memory, and recitation elements (e.g., preschool singing and memory-driven activities). The public materials emphasize classroom structures as teacher-led rather than student-directed. This aligns with the classical emphasis on teacher-guided instruction and memory work in early years. - Explicit upper-school Socratic seminars, debates, or rhetoric-training opportunities are not clearly documented in the available public pages. The classical-pedagogy rubric values Socratic discourse and formal rhetoric in upper grades, which is not strongly evidenced in the visible site content. The publicly visible pages emphasize teacher-led instruction and foundational classical pedagogy rather than a consistent, high-frequency Socratic/seminar approach. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present include: teacher-led classroom; memory work; singing/memory in lower grades; engagement with great works of literature; classical pedagogy signals (grammar/logic/rhetoric). The Lexicon for Pedagogy lists Socratic seminars, narration, and rhetoric training as classic features; the site has some alignment but not a consistent high-frequency depiction of Socratic seminars. Positive signals: teacher-led, memory/recitation-like practices in early years; mention of classic texts and memory-based learning. 
- Negative indicators appear to be minimal in public materials; there is limited evidence of modern, project-based, or student-centered, highly autonomous pedagogy in the publicly visible materials. The absence of heavy emphasis on experiential/project-based learning as the primary mode signals a partial alignment rather than full alignment with the more expansive “pedagogy” indicators. The absence of explicit Socratic seminars in public pages yields a weaker alignment with the strongest classical pedagogy signals. 

Why this rating
- The pedagogy leans toward classical (teacher-led instruction with memory-based learning in early grades) but lacks explicit, consistently documented Socratic, seminars, and rhetoric-learning at high school levels in the publicly visible content. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation, with room to strengthen upper-grade Socratic and speech-rhetoric pedagogy in publicly available materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Introduce explicit theology or liturgical/community practices in publicly accessible materials, or provide a clearly labeled pathway showing how faith perspectives inform core curriculum and character formation. This would strengthen the Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices signals and convert SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL into CLASSICAL in this category.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Expand explicit upper-grade (9–12) Socratic seminars, Paideia-style discussions, or rhetoric-focused projects, and document these practices on public pages to strengthen the Pedagogy dimension from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) alignment: More explicit articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation as a structured, school-wide curricular thread across grades (K–12) and align upper-grade instruction with consistent Socratic dialogue and evidence of rhetoric training, to deepen the programmatic alignment with classical ideals.
- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) and Element 5 (Faculty) emphasis: Continue to articulate and document explicit markers of moral formation (virtue ethics, character formation in the faculty living curriculum) and maintain a visible, public-facing portrait of teacher-mentors with evidence of ongoing faculty formation in classical pedagogy.
