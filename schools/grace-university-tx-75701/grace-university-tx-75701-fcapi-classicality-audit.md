## Executive Summary

Grace Community School (Grace Tyler; Tyler, TX 75701) presents as a distinctly Christian, Christ-centered private school community that emphasizes formation of the whole person through a Biblical worldview, character formation, and athletic and arts excellence. Public materials consistently foreground teaching Jesus, God’s Word as the foundational framework, and partnership with Christian families. While the materials demonstrate strong religious foundations and a clear formation-oriented purpose, explicit classical elements such as Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades, or a fully articulated living curriculum are not prominently documented on the public site. The school’s public signals align with several classical-education ideals (formation, virtue, Christian worldview, a call to study and excellence), but they do not comprehensively demonstrate the full Great Books/Great Conversation, rigorous canonical curriculum, or Socratic pedagogy emphasized by the classical-school rubric.

Overall classification: NOT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are explicit and strong (theology, teaching Jesus, God’s Word as foundation, Christ-centered mission) and recur across multiple public materials. This is a core, positive indicator for Element 1.
- The school’s stated purpose centers on formation, virtue, and a Christian worldview, with explicit warnings against reducing education to job/workforce preparation. This supports Element 2 as CLASSICAL in spirit, though some classical vocabulary is not formally stated.
- Public materials show a Christian liberal-arts posture and some evidence of high academic achievement, but explicit Great Books, canonical readings, Trivium/Quadrivium, or explicit Socratic pedagogy are not clearly documented in the public materials. Element 3 and Element 4 indicators are weaker or not clearly present in the accessible site content.
- Faculty and parent/community signals exist (e.g., Head of School leadership, admissions processes referencing faith), but explicit modern classroom portraits of faculty as the “living curriculum” or a formal “parents as co-teachers” program are not clearly documented on the public pages.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community) shows meaningful partnership signals (faith-based admissions criteria, family involvement, church connections) but would benefit from more explicit parent-formation programs, book clubs, or open-campus signals as framed in the classical lexicon.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate and implement canonical classical elements, including explicit Great Books readings, Trivium/Quadrivium alignment, and canonical historical literature. Publish a public curriculum map that shows Great Books usage, Latin/Greek, and primary sources in core disciplines.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Publicly endorse and describe classical pedagogy practices (Socratic seminars in upper grades, structured memory and recitation traditions, scholé days, and ritualized contemplation) and provide concrete examples from classrooms.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a “portrait of the teacher” and a robust statement about the faculty as the living curriculum, including professional development in classical pedagogy, ongoing faculty formation, and a living-curriculum framework.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand explicit parent-formation programming (classical book clubs, home-catechesis guidance, family liturgy alignment) and provide structured open-campus or family-partner events that reinforce parent-as-co-teachers. Increase transparency about family partnerships with public-facing evidence of home-school synergy.
