## ABSTRACT

Cornerstone Classical School Round Rock, TX (77429) demonstrates a strong CLASSICAL orientation across five of six categories, with Curriculum showing partial alignment. The school’s public materials reveal a distinctly Christian, virtue-formation-centric mission rooted in biblical-theological foundations, a clear telos toward wisdom and virtue, and a classical pedagogy progression from memory/chanting in the Grammar stage to Socratic seminars and rhetoric training in the Logic/Rhetoric stages. Faculty are portrayed as the living curriculum and core of the school, and parents/community are actively engaged as co-educators and partners. The one domain with partial alignment is Curriculum, where the Trivium is evident and classical teaching resources are used, but explicit language about Great Books/Great Conversation is not consistently foregrounded in public messaging.

Key evidence includes:
- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: Christ-centered mission and biblical/theological grounding (Mission; Statement of Faith; Faith-centered motto).  
- Purpose & Goal: Formation in virtue and wisdom, not merely job-readiness, with a teleological framing around truth, goodness, and beauty.  
- Curriculum: Trivium emphasis (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a variety of classical/Christian curriculum resources; partial explicit Great Books/Great Conversation framing in public text. 
- Pedagogy: Memory/chanting in lower grades; Socratic/seminar/rhetoric in upper grades; contemplative and restfulness elements alongside rigorous discussion. 
- Faculty: Faculty described as partners with families, mentors, and living curriculum; leadership development involves teacher-mentee relationships. 
- Parents & Community: Strong parent partnership, family-discipleship, open campus, and community-life programs. 

Overall, Cornerstone Classical School Round Rock sits firmly within CLASSICAL, with the Curriculum category representing a manageable area for enhancement to achieve full canonical status. The needle-moving step would be explicit Great Books/Great Conversation framing within Curriculum messaging, complemented by a clearer articulation of liturgical/community practices as part of daily life in public materials.
