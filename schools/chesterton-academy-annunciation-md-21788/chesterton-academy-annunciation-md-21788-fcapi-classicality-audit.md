## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy Annunciation MD 21788 (Annunciation High School, Emmitsburg, MD) presents a clearly classical Catholic program in public-facing materials. Public materials emphasize a Catholic, liturgical life (daily Mass, Rosary, Adoration, Examen), a theologically grounded identity, and a classical, formation-driven mission (formation of virtue and wisdom through a classical curriculum and Socratic inquiry). Evidence indicates engagement with the liberal-arts framework (classical curriculum, Socratic method, memorization/recitation, and a Great-Books orientation implied by references to the Great Conversation and classical texts). Faculty portrayals present teachers as virtuous models and mentors, and the school emphasizes partnership with families and active parish/community life. Tuition-related information and MSDE/NAPCIS recognition further anchor its identity as a Catholic classical school. Overall, the school qualifies as CLASSICAL across most dimensions, with particularly strong alignment in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. Element 4 shows substantial classical pedagogy signals (Socratic methods and formation-focused practices) but appears to lack explicit public documentation of every pedagogical hallmark (e.g., some elements of Scholé/restful learning or full cataloging of all classical pedagogy practices). The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key signals contributing to CLASSICAL status:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations: daily Mass, regular liturgical life, Catholic formation, Magisterium-aligned teaching, daily prayer, Rosary, Examen, adoration, novena practices. 
- Purpose and goals: explicit aim to form virtue and wisdom through study of the true, the good, and the beautiful; formation over mere information. 
- Curriculum: public notes of a classical curriculum rooted in Catholic faith; Socratic method cited for pursuing truth; stated emphasis on classical education and Great Conversation. 
- Pedagogy: Socratic dialogue appears in public materials; a classical, formation-driven approach is implied by the curriculum and liturgical integration. 
- Faculty: faculty pages present teachers as virtuous models and the “living curriculum.” 
- Parents & Community: emphasis on partnership with families, open communications, and community/stewardship through church-affiliated life and fundraising; MSDE/NAPCIS recognition cited publicly. 

Abstract: CLASSICAL

- There is explicit reference to a classical curriculum and a foundation in the Catholic tradition, with teachers who model virtue and mentorship (see Faculty section). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue/seminars, dialectic, great conversation, guided discussion, logical reasoning, rhetoric, memorization/recitation signals (through public mention of a classical pedagogy), scholé/restful learning (implied by liturgical and contemplative practices; see Six Elements’ Pedagogy signals). 
- Negative keywords: The public materials do not emphasize purely project-based or experiential-only pedagogy; however, the public materials do not always explicitly catalog the full spectrum of classical pedagogy signals (e.g., dedicated notes on memorization chants, formal scholé days). The absence of explicit exhaustive descriptions means signals are partial rather than comprehensive.

Why this rating
- The clear inclusion of Socratic/Dialogic pedagogy and classical aims places the school solidly in the classical pedagogy domain, but the public documentation does not exhaustively demonstrate every facet (e.g., explicit scholé days or full recitation/memory work practices). Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL at this moment, given the public documentation. 

Note: If more explicit evidence of full scholé days or complete memorization/recitation routines were provided publicly, the category would shift toward CLASSICAL.
