## ABSTRACT

Veritas Classical School Florida (St. Augustine) demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL alignment across all six categories of the classical education rubric:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Clear Christian grounding with biblical worldview, Christ-centered pedagogy, chapel life, and theology integrated into curriculum.
- School Purpose and Goal: Formation in virtue and wisdom; truth/goodness/beauty as real telos; liberal education orientation permeates aims.
- Curriculum: Trivium-based structure (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric); Great Books/primary sources; integration of history with a classical canon; Latin study present.
- Pedagogy: Mix of contemplative (Scholé-like) and rigorous Socratic/seminar formats; memorization, recitation, and rhetoric training across grade levels.
- Faculty: Mentorship model, living curriculum, and faith-informed teacher leadership; collegial culture embedded in core values.
- Parents & Community: Strong emphasis on family partnership, parental formation, and open-campus engagement.

Qualitative keyword alignment shows a deliberate emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, Great Books, Trivium, and the formation of character, with appropriate communal and liturgical practices. The evidence from Veritas Classical School’s public materials—Mission, Christian Education, Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric, and community pages—supports CLASSICAL classification consistently. The school also holds ACCS accreditation, reinforcing its classical identity. No category indicates a shift toward non-classical signals.

Notes on methodology
- The analysis integrates two sources: (1) the school-specific crawl content (Veritas Classical School site) to extract public-facing curriculum, pedagogy, and community practices, and (2) the knowledge base’s Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) document to identify positive and negative keyword indicators and to ground the scoring framework.
- Positive keyword indicators drawn from the Six Essential Elements corpus include Trivium, Great Books, formation, liturgy, theology, memory work, Socratic dialog, and partnerships with families, among others. Negative indicators (e.g., excessive emphasis on job-readiness, project-based learning without formation) were not evident in the Veritas materials.
