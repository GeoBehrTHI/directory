## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical School Florida (St. Augustine, FL 32084) presents a robust classical Christian program organized around the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) with explicit commitments to a biblical worldview, virtue formation, and engagement with Great Books within an integrated curriculum. Public materials from the school's website emphasize Christ-centered formation, a two-campus grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, and a mission to cultivate truth, goodness, and beauty through a classical education rooted in faith. The school repeatedly frames itself as a classical Christian institution with three distinct educational phases and a clear stance on faith-integrated pedagogy, including chapel participation and family partnership. Evidence across pages such as the Mission and Christian Education sections, the Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric structure, and references to accreditation and faculty supports a strong Classical orientation with explicit religious foundations. The aggregate assessment across the six classical elements is CLASSICAL.

Key inflection points
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are explicit: biblical worldview; Christ-centered pedagogy; weekly chapel; the fear of the LORD as the starting point for wisdom; theology integrated into curriculum.
- Purpose and Goal emphasize formation and moral imagination over mere job preparation; a teleology oriented to truth, goodness, and beauty.
- Curriculum centers on the Trivium, Great Books/Great Conversation, and the liberal arts framework, with explicit mention of grammar/logic/rhetoric, classical pedagogy, and Western civilizational cross-curriculum integration.
- Pedagogy includes contemplative elements (Scholé-like restfulness), memorization/chanting in the Grammar stage, and Socratic/seminal discourse as students advance, culminating in rhetoric and public discourse in later years.
- Faculty are positioned as mentors and living curriculum, with statements about virtue, character, and collaboration; there is a defined “portrait” or sense of what a teacher should be and what a student should become.
- Parents & Community are foregrounded as partners—co-educators with visible calls for parent formation, open campus, and frequent parental involvement.
