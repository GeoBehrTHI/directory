# Classicality Audit: Candeo Schools (Peoria, AZ)

Website audited: https://candeoschools.com 

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Candeo Schools presents a robust classical mission and language across its site: repeated commitments to “wisdom,” “virtue,” and “truth, goodness, and beauty”; identification with the Trivium; explicit statements about formation over information; and strong partnership-with-parents language. Curriculum pages explicitly ground the program in classical thought and the Trivium while using Core Knowledge for content. Pedagogy hints at wonder and rhetorical formation, but lacks explicit evidence of lower-school chanting/recitation and upper-grade Socratic seminar (the school appears K–8). Religious/theological integration and liturgical practices are not evident (as expected for a public charter). Faculty pages emphasize alignment to a classical philosophy and ongoing mentoring, but do not articulate a “portrait of a teacher” or the faculty as a “living curriculum.”

Most classical criteria are met in Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, and Parents & Community. Evidence is thinner (though positive) in Pedagogy and Faculty, and the Religious/Metaphysical category shows transcendental moral realism without theological/ liturgical grounding. Result: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall.

- Key textual signals:
  - “Grow wisdom and virtue… through rigorous, knowledge-based schooling in the classical liberal arts” (mission/landing) 
  - “Grounded in classical thought and the Trivium… Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric” (Curriculum & Instruction) 
  - “Western Tradition… good, true, and beautiful” (Curriculum & Instruction) 
  - “Parents are the primary educators… partnership with parents” (About/Philosophy) 

Note on framework: Keyword comparisons and category judgments are grounded in the Six Essential Elements lexicon of positive/negative indicators (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, Trivium, great books, Socratic, scholé, chanting; and divergences like 21st‑century skills, project‑based learning dominance, etc.)  .

## What would move the needle (from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL → CLASSICAL)
- Pedagogy (K–8):
  - Publish explicit practices for memory work: “chanting,” “recitation,” and “singing” in lower grades; name them as such to align with classical pedagogy language in the lexicon .
  - Name “Socratic discussion” (age‑appropriate forms) and “narration, copywork/imitation (progymnasmata)” where used, and show exemplar lesson snapshots .
- Curriculum:
  - Publicly list a core reading/“Great Books” spine and primary sources by grade; consider explicit Latin (even exploratory) in K–8, or articulate the rationale if not offered .
- Faculty:
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate”; state teachers as the “living curriculum” and “models of virtue,” and outline faculty classical formation (seminars, great books colloquia, mentoring) .
- Parents:
  - Add explicit parent‑formation opportunities (book clubs on classical education, talks on the Trivium, etc.) and highlight them on the Parents page .
