## Executive Summary

Cathedral School of Saint Mary (Austin, TX 78701) demonstrates a robust classical-Catholic orientation across curricular content, pedagogy, and community life. Public materials emphasize a historically grounded Catholic identity, a commitment to a classical curriculum, and a structured progression through the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and liberal arts, including Latin and great-books-influenced study. Evidence of liturgical life, communal worship, religious formation, and collaboration with families reinforces a coherent Classical Catholic model. While category-specific signals are overwhelmingly Classical (with one category showing only partial alignment), the aggregate impression is CLASSICAL.

Key strengths include: explicit Catholic formation and liturgical practices; a stated classical curriculum with grammar/logic/rhetoric foundations; emphasis on Great Books and the Great Conversation; a pedagogy that blends memorization and chant in early grades with Socratic/seminar-style inquiry in later years; and active partnership with families as co-educators. Notable elements that temper an otherwise uniform Classical stance include the more explicit articulation of formation and faith life over job-prep rhetoric in public-facing materials and a faculty narrative that, while aligned with virtue-formation, does not consistently foreground the “living curriculum” language seen in some classical-program descriptions (explained further in the category-by-category notes).

What would move the needle? If the school further foregrounded explicit language about the living curriculum and teacher-as-virtue-mentor role, and if it broadened public statements to consistently articulate the Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) with accompanying Great Books/readings commitments beyond the current Catholic-classical framing, it would push any remaining room toward a fully explicit CLASSICAL label across all six elements.
