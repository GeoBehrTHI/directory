## Executive Summary

Sterling Classical School TX 78641 presents a robust classical education orientation with a clearly Christian, Trinity-aligned foundation. Public-facing materials describe a school that explicitly integrates a Christian worldview with classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Charlotte Mason-inspired practices, while inviting active parent partnership as co-educators. The school emphasizes living books, narration, memory work (including singing/chanting in the lower grades), and Socratic-style inquiry in the upper grades, all framed within a college-preparatory program. The identified materials show a coherent alignment with several core elements of classical education, including formation of character, engagement with Great/ living books, and a clear trivium structure, plus deliberate parental partnership and communal faith life. Based on the available public materials, Sterling Classical School TX 78641 is CLASSICAL in orientation across all six rubric categories, with the strongest alignment in Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Parents & Community, solid alignment in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, and clear alignment in School Purpose & Goal and Faculty, albeit with some tension between “formation” and college-preparatory/workforce emphasis in public-facing wording.

Key notes from the school’s materials include: emphasis on Jesus Christ and biblical truth as foundational, a mission and vision built around a Christian worldview and classical methods, explicit articulation of the trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Charlotte Mason-inspired living books and narration, deliberate teacher-student partnerships and a visible emphasis on parent involvement as co-educators, and an admissions/process framework anchored in faith and formation as well as academics. These elements collectively point to a CLASSICAL orientation with limited signs of the negative signals that would push the rating toward Not Classical; the presence of college-preparatory language alongside formation language places the aggregate in CLASSICAL with no broad disqualifying signals.

- The Focus/Foundation explicitly frames education as forming students “with the foundational truth of Jesus Christ and His word,” indicating a liturgical/communal dimension anchored in faith. 
- A Statement of Faith and explicit Christ-centered language are present in the site materials, reinforcing theological grounding. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Jesus Christ, biblical truth, Christian worldview, theology, prayer, worship, liturgy, sacred. The site text repeatedly frames education through a Christian lens and biblical truth. 
- Negative keywords: No explicit relativism or secular-humanist indicators detected in the public-facing materials; there is no overt signaling of “morally neutral” approaches. 

Why this rating
- The school’s public materials consistently anchor education in Christian theology and a theologically grounded worldview, with explicit references to Christ, biblical truth, and a foundation in Christian faith, which aligns strongly with the positive indicators for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations in the classical rubric. The presence of liturgical/community elements is visible, though more explicit liturgical practices are not as prominent as the affirmed theological grounding. Overall, the evidence supports a CLASSICAL designation for this category.

Why this rating

Overall rating for this category is CLASSICAL.

- Lower grades incorporate singing/chanting as part of memory and engagement; upper grades emphasize seminars, debate, and rhetorical work; the program includes a focus on critical thinking and writing across the curriculum. 
- The school highlights the integration of classical methods with Christian, Charlotte Mason-inspired practices and a strong emphasis on literature, hands-on learning, and inquiry (e.g., The Young Scientist) that aligns with a liberal-arts orientation. 
- Specific sections outline School of Logic (5th–8th) and School of Rhetoric (9th–12th) with structured opportunities for argument, rhetoric, and public speaking. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, Great Books / living books, narration, dictation, Socratic/seminar style, memory work, chant/singing, liberal arts, Western civilization, history as a throughline, Great Conversation (implicit through classical canon language), and the incorporation of classical literature and inquiry (The Young Scientist, nature-based inquiry). The site explicitly uses the trivium framework and living books/narration language. 
- Negative keywords: there is little to no emphasis on project-based-only instruction at the expense of classical forms; the site presents a balance that includes contemplative/restful elements and traditional memory/recitation methods. The explicit negative signals in the lexicon (e.g., “project-based learning dominant,” “experiential-only approaches”) are not strongly present as the primary pedagogy described. The site emphasizes a variety of methods, not exclusively project-based, maintaining alignment with classical pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum is explicitly organized around the trivium, employs living books and narration, integrates Charlotte Mason elements with classical methods, emphasizes classical literature and inquiry, and presents a throughline of history and literature across the curriculum. This combination aligns strongly with CLASSICAL expectations for Curriculum.

This aligns with classical expectations rather than signaling, e.g., “experiential learning focus” as a sole approach. 

Why this rating
- The pedagogy integrates contemplative/restful elements (scholé), memory-based learning (singing/recitation), and upper-level Socratic/seminar/rhetoric methods, closely matching the classical teaching method spectrum. The overall pedagogy alignment is CLASSICAL.
