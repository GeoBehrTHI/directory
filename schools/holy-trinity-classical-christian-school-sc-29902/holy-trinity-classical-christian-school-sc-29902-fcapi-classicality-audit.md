## Executive Summary

Holy Trinity Classical Christian School (Beaufort, SC, 29902) demonstrates strong classical-CChristian alignment across all six canonical categories. Public materials from htccs.org repeatedly emphasize a distinctly classical framework integrated with a Christian worldview, committed to truth, wisdom, virtue, and the love of learning; a curriculum that foregrounds liberal arts, Great Books, Latin, and Western civilization; pedagogy that includes Socratic discussion, memory work, and disciplined rhetoric; a faculty model that seeks a living curriculum; and an active partnership with parents and the broader community. The school explicitly describes its mission as “distinctly classical and distinctively Christian,” with the Scriptures as a foundational element of learning, and it foregrounds Great Books, memory work, and classical modes of inquiry throughout its published materials. This combination supports a broad, cohesive CLASSICAL rating.

Key indicators throughout HTCCS materials include:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations: emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty; biblical worldview; chapel and liturgical life; theology integrated with learning.
- Purpose and goal: formation through virtue and wisdom; teleology (pursuit of truth, goodness, beauty); education as formation, not merely information.
- Curriculum: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) invoked in public materials; Great Books and the Great Conversation; Latin study; Western civilization as a throughline.
- Pedagogy: contemplative elements (Scholé), memory work, recitation, chanting; Socratic discussion and seminars in upper grades; rhetoric and public speaking opportunities.
- Faculty: living, mentoring teachers; emphasis on character formation; public portrayal of faculty as part of the school’s “living curriculum.”
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with families; parent formation through book groups and gatherings; open campus and inclusive parent involvement (e.g., PAW, admissions guidance).

Evidence from the public HTCCS site and related school materials is cited throughout this report. See the cited sources for direct wording demonstrating mission, curriculum commitments, pedagogy, and community partnerships. ## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Mission & Vision articulate a distinctly classical and Christian identity with a biblical worldview and a foundation in Holy Scriptures for all learning. The mission states: “The Mission of the School is to be distinctly classical and distinctively Christian, entering into a partnership with parents as we educate students in grades Preschool through 12 with a Biblical worldview. The school will be committed to the pursuit of truth, wisdom, scholastic excellence, and the love of learning. With the Holy Scriptures as the foundation for all they learn, our students will be ambassadors of the Kingdom of God, prepared to defend and advance the apostolic Christian faith.” 
- Distinctively Christian framing reiterates a theology-grounded approach to education. 
- Liturgical/communal life is present (Chapel/Houses; liturgical cadence and communal worship), indicating liturgical practices as integral to formation. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; liturgy; chapel; worship; wonder; sacred; Logos; gospel; biblical worldview.
- Negative keywords detected: minimal or no explicit relativism or secular-humanist phrasing in public materials.
- Quantitative note: Positive indicators clearly outnumber negative indicators in the public materials here (roughly multiple terms across mission, theology, and liturgical life; few if any negative signals found in the public pages).

Why this rating
- The school’s core materials foreground transcendental qualities (truth, goodness, beauty) and a theologically grounded, liturgical life. This aligns with the classical model’s Religious and Metaphysical Foundations criteria, and is reinforced by explicit statements about theology, liturgy, and biblical foundation. Conclusion: CLASSICAL.

Cited evidence
