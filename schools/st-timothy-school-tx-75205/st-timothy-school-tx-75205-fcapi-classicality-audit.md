## Executive Summary

The Saint Timothy School (Dallas, 75205) presents a clearly classical Christian program anchored in Anglican parish life. Public materials describe a K4–12 school that explicitly embraces the classical trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a robust spiritual life (daily prayers, chapel worship, Psalms and hymns), and a mission to cultivate wisdom and virtue through truth, goodness, and beauty. The school’s Anglican identity is explicit through its affiliation with the Anglican School Association and its status as a parish school under The Chapel of the Cross (Reformed Episcopal Church). The faculty are framed as mentors forming character, and the curriculum integrates classical subjects (Greek, Latin, music, arts, fencing, etc.) within a spiritual formation context. Senior leadership and faculty are presented as aligned with the school’s mission, including a clearly identified Head of School.

Key public signals of classical orientation include:

- Explicit articulation of the trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and a K–12 program designed to advance through those stages.
- A foundational commitment to worship, daily prayer, and chapel life as part of the school day.
- An emphasis on virtue, wisdom, truth, goodness, and beauty as central aims of education. This is directly echoed in the school’s mission statement, which foregrounds truth, goodness and beauty alongside virtue.
- A parish/Anglican identity, including governance under the parish and affiliation with Anglican organizations.
- A faculty focus on mentorship and virtue, with statements about teachers acting as mentors and the living curriculum.

Overall classification across the six categories: CLASSICAL. The Saint Timothy School exhibits strong religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose and goals aligned with classical aims, a clearly defined classical curriculum, pedagogical structures consistent with classical education, a faculty described as morally and academically gifted, and community/family engagement rooted in its parish and Anglican affiliation. Some nuances (e.g., explicit, ongoing parent-education programs or broad parent-as-co-teacher routines) could be strengthened, which places Element 6 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL, but the aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL.
