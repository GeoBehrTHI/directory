## Executive Summary

Packinghouse Christian Academy (PCA) is a K–12 Christian school in Redlands, CA that publicly presents a biblically integrated, Christ-centered form of education. Evidence from PCA’s site shows explicit emphasis on spiritual formation, biblical worldview, discipleship, and partnerships with families, including a house-system built around Biblical virtues. The school is accredited (WASC) and affiliated with the Association of Christian Schools International (ACSI), underscoring its commitment to Christian educational standards. In terms of the classical rubric, PCA clearly exhibits religious/metaphysical foundations and a formation-focused mission, and it integrates community/family partnership. However, explicit classical components such as a stated commitment to the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), Great Books/Great Conversation, and explicit use of Socratic pedagogy are not clearly advertised in the public materials.

Overall classification (aggregate): CLASSICAL

Key insights:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are robust: biblically integrated instruction, Christ-centered mission, and explicit theology-focused language per site materials. Evidence: “Education and Discipleship… In Jesus’ Name,” biblically integrated instruction, and Ephesians 4:4–6 framing on the About page. These themes align strongly with Positive Keywords related to theology, liturgy, and sacred tradition from the classical lexicon.
- School purpose and goal emphasize formation and virtue within a Christian frame, consistent with “formation” and “pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty” in classical theory, though explicit statements about “Great Books” or the Trivium/Quadrivium are not visible in PCA public materials. PCA’s learner goals emphasize biblical formation, virtue, and a biblically-informed worldview. This supports CLASSICAL with caveats about the absence of explicit Great Books/Trivium in public messages.
- Curriculum: Limited public documentation of classical core content (Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, Great Conversation) on the site. The public-facing materials emphasize biblical integration, discipleship, and virtue training, but do not clearly advertise Great Books or grammar/logic/rhetoric as curriculum pillars. This suggests a gap relative to a fully explicit classical curriculum, contributing to a mixed signal on Element 3.
- Pedagogy: Public materials show emphasis on discipleship, community, and structured student formation (house system, Biblically-based education). Direct references to Socratic seminars, scholé, or classic memory/recitation pedagogy are not evident in the public-facing pages. This suggests Element 4 is not fully Classical, though the school’s approach to formation and communal learning aligns with some classical-method sensibilities.
- Faculty: The school emphasizes staff as ministry and living examples of Christian character, with explicit language about servant/minister posture and a staff-application emphasis. This aligns with the classical emphasis on virtuous teacher models and the “living curriculum.”
- Parents & Community: PCA explicitly positions parents as partners in formation and discipleship, seeking family involvement and offering opportunities for parent engagement (book studies, gatherings, visits). This aligns well with classical expectations of family-school partnership.

Why this rating

The absence of explicit classical curricular anchors lowers Element 3 from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
