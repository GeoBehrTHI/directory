# ABSTRACT

Calvin Christian School (Escondido, CA, 92008) demonstrates a robust, biblically grounded educational mission with classical affinities centered on formation, virtue, and a biblical worldview. The school’s public content strongly signals Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Trinity, Scripture, liturgical language, and a covenantal partnership with family and church) and a Purpose/Goal framed around virtue, wisdom, and life formed by God. Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 5 (Faculty) show partial alignment with classical education—rooted in liberal-arts language and credentialed faculty—but public materials do not consistently present explicit Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium structures or a fully articulated living curriculum pedagogy. Element 4 (Pedagogy) appears NOT CLASSICAL in public materials due to the lack of explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy language in posted pages. Element 6 (Parents & Community) is CLASSICAL, given clear parental partnership and ongoing community formation.

Overall, the school sits at CLASSICAL with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL elements. Its strongest features are religious foundations, formation goals, and parent/community engagement, while explicit classical curricular components and pedagogy require more explicit public articulation to move all six categories into full CLASSICAL standing.

Notes on sources (highlights)

- Religious Foundations and Mission: Content emphasizes a biblical worldview, the Trinity, and Scripture as the authoritative norm; “We believe the Bible is the only inspired, infallible, inerrant, and authoritative standard” and mission statements stressing a life of Christ-centered service.
- Portrait of a Graduate and Formation: Public materials describe identity formed by God’s love and a life of wonder, worship, work, and wisdom (paideia).
- Curriculum and Liberal Arts Orientation: The site references the liberal-arts tradition and paideia language; explicit Great Books/Trivium/Quadrivium references are not consistently posted in public content.
- Pedagogy: No explicit, consistently described Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in public-facing content; some rhetoric-bearing aims exist in mission language.
- Faculty: Public pages list faculty counts and credentials (MA degrees, certification) but do not articulately describe a “living curriculum” or classical-teacher portrait.
- Parents & Community: Explicit emphasis on partnership with home and church, with historical examples like the Mothers’ Club and visible parent engagement in school life.
