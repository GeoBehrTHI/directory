## Executive Summary

Spokane Classical Christian School (SCCS) presents a robust classical Christian program that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric. Across all six categories, SCCS evidences a consistent classical orientation: explicit religious/theological foundations; a purpose centered on formation of virtue and wisdom; a curriculum built around the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a historically-grounded humanities core; pedagogical practice anchored in memory, recitation, disciplined study, and (in upper grades) rhetoric; a faculty culture described as mentors and living exemplars; and a deeply family- and church-partnered community ethos. The public materials emphasize a ministry-of-education posture, Great Commission-centered formation, covenant-community framing, and a four-day school model designed to balance family, church, and school life. There are no strong signals of secularization or a predominant shift toward non-classical, job-focused education in the public materials. The aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL.

Key themes that drive the assessment
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are foregrounded (Distinctly Christian Ministry of Education; Gospel-centered; Word of God; Great Commission; liturgy of life)  
- The school purpose emphasizes formation, virtue, and the real/true/beautiful (formation over mere information; virtue formation; truth/goodness/beauty) via a classical frame 
- Curriculum centers on the liberal arts and Great Books-like structure (grammar, logic, rhetoric; historic Christian humanities; biblical-trivium; classical history sequences)  
- Pedagogy employs memory work, recitation, liturgical/Contemplative elements, and upper-school rhetoric; lower-school singing/memory; and a Biblically-informed Trivium  
- Faculty concept emphasizes mentors as living curriculum and model teachers; a “ministry of education” culture supports virtuous pedagogy 
- Parents & community are central: partnership with parents, open to visits, book/gatherings, giving, and family-church-school alignment
