## Executive Summary

Cornerstone Christian Academy NM 87120 presents a clearly Christian Classical orientation, anchored in Biblical truth and a Trivium-based approach to learning. Public-facing materials emphasize Biblical authority, virtue formation, and a mission to cultivate spiritual growth through a Classical Christian framework. The school is explicit about its Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and its aim to integrate theology into daily learning, with a strong emphasis on memory work, communal values, and character formation. However, explicit, documented evidence of fully articulated upper-school Socratic pedagogy, a formal “living curriculum” description for faculty, and defined, observable parent-as-co-teacher partnerships are less evident in the public materials. Taken together, the school demonstrates many hallmarks of classical Christian education, but some elements of the Six Essential Elements rubric require more explicit or comprehensive articulation to reach a fully CLASSICAL standing. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong classical Christian foundations and clear trajectory toward fuller classical practice as the program grows.

Pre-school materials emphasize Biblical principles and structured curricular themes, and Kindergarten notes the launch of preschool/JK initiatives. This satisfies the criterion that the curriculum includes grammar, logic, and rhetoric, and indicates history-oriented study implicit in the classical approach, with a focus on wonder and meaningful content.
 Why CLASSICAL: Trivium explicit; emphasis on memory, structure, and classical aims; progressive pre-K/K program aligned with classical aims.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
 Evidence shows lower-school emphasis on memory, singing, and contemplative/restful learning, and upper-school potential for dialogue and rhetoric training. Explicit Socratic/seminar methods are not clearly documented in public materials; the materials emphasize memory-based and contemplative learning with moves toward rhetoric and public speaking as students advance. The absence (in public content) of explicit, repeated Socratic/seminar practices suggests room for growth toward full classical pedagogy in the high school years.
 Positive indicators: memorization, contemplative learning, singing/engagement in lower grades; rhetoric training implied by Trivium.
 Gaps: explicit upper-school Socratic/seminar approach, regular seminars/debates, and public-speaking opportunities are not clearly documented in website materials.
 Why SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: foundational classical practices are present (Trivium, memory, wonder), but explicit, high-school Socratic/seminar pedagogy and a fully articulated “Scho­le” culture are not yet clearly evidenced on public pages.

- Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
 Evidence shows a staff structure (administration, teachers) and a faith-based governance context (Sole Ministries/Acacia Worship Center) that frames mentorship and role modeling. However, explicit language describing faculty as the living curriculum, or a formalized “portrait of teacher” and “portrait of student” are not clearly presented in public materials.
 Positive indicators: staff roles (Administration, Secretary) and church-affiliated governance; mission emphasis on virtuous formation.
 Gaps: explicit statements about faculty as living curriculum, teacher character portraits, and closely linked mentorship culture are not readily visible in public content.
 Why SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: the school context is strongly shaped by teachers/administrators in a faith-based setting, and virtue-centered aims are present, but explicit “living curriculum” framing of faculty is not clearly documented.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
 Evidence includes a culture of partnership through church/ministry channels and fundraising efforts, and public content inviting involvement in donations and school development. However, explicit, systematic parent-teacher partnership language (parents as co-teachers, formal parent education, and open-door access for visits) is not robustly documented in public-facing materials.
 Positive indicators: faith-based community, parent involvement via giving, visible church-school connection.
 Gaps: formal parent-as-co-teachers language and structured parent education initiatives are not clearly present in the public content.
 Why SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL: community/parent involvement exists in practice (donations, church affiliation), but explicit, formal parent-partnering language and programs are not fully documented.

What would move the needle

- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Implement and publicly document explicit upper-school Socratic seminars, regular formal seminars/debates, and rhetoric-focused public speaking opportunities to align with the full classical pedagogy in Grades 9–12.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Develop and publish a formal “Living Curriculum” framework for faculty, including a teacher-portrait and a student-portrait, and articulate mentorship as a core role of the faculty.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Establish and publicize explicit parent-as-co-teachers partnerships (teacher training for parents, co-curricular co-planning, and structured parent education programs), plus open-door policy statements with clear guidelines for visits and involvement.
