## Executive Summary

The Fletcher School (Southwest Florida) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning educational model. Public materials consistently frame the school as classical and Christian, emphasizing formation, virtue, and a God-centered view of knowledge. The curriculum intentionally centers on liberal-arts structure (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a traditional ordering of subjects, with explicit theological grounding and emphasis on the formation of character. However, certain pedagogical elements typical of a full Classical model (notably explicit Socratic pedagogy and a fully articulated “living curriculum” in faculty practice) are less clearly demonstrated in the publicly accessible materials. Taken as a whole, the school sits squarely in the Classical spectrum in several core domains, while showing partial alignment in others.

Overall classification by category:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The public materials emphasize classical pedagogy elements (logic, rhetoric, grammar progression; upper-level rhetoric; debate; speech; and rhetorical training) with a track to “Rhetoric” in high school and a focus on classical disciplines; this points to a pedagogy that embraces disciplined reasoning and public speaking (e.g., logic, composition, classical studies, debate, and rhetoric) , .
  - There is explicit mention of a traditional, integrated curriculum and focus on formation and virtue, aligning with the classical pedagogy ethos (e.g., “formation through the liberal arts”; “the goal is formation, not mere information”) , .
  - Distinctive pedagogical practices like Socratic seminars, scholé/restful learning, memorization/recitation, and deliberate “living curriculum” are present in the classical lexicon, but explicit adoption by Fletcher (e.g., schedule, explicit Scholé days, singing/chanting for memory, or Harkness-style dialogues) is not consistently documented in the public pages. The public materials do, however, emphasize rhetoric and debate as an upper-school emphasis, which is a Classical hallmark but not necessarily a full Socratic pedagogy across grades , .
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive indicators: Socratic dialogue (not explicit in Fletcher’s public pages, but the lexicon signals its importance; the site does show rhetoric/debate and classical reasoning in the upper grades); scholé/restful learning (mentioned in the lexicon, but not clearly evidenced in Fletcher’s site as a formal program); memorization/recitation (not explicitly described in Fletcher’s pages, though Latin/Bible and memory work are common in classical curricula).
  - Negative indicators: Lack of explicit, uniform Socratic seminars or a fully documented “Schólé-based” restful pedagogy; absence of detailed lower-grade singing/chanting (as a formal practice) in the public materials.
  - Net: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The school demonstrates classical elements (grammar-logic-rhetoric; rhetoric, debate, logic; integrated curriculum) but does not consistently present explicit Socratic pedagogy, Schólé days, or fully documented memory/singing practices across grade bands in public-facing content.
- Why this rating
  - The presence of a clear classical track (grammar-logic-rhetoric; high-school rhetoric; Latin/Bible; debate) aligns with CLASSICAL pedagogy in spirit, yet the explicit cadence of Socratic discussion, restfulness, and daily contemplative learning is not consistently documented in the public site content. That mix yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.

The public materials do show explicit parental involvement language and a call to align home-life with school formation.
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis

  - Negative indicators: None explicit in the Fletcher public materials; no consumerist language or reduced parental access is evident; the site invites parental involvement and visits through program content and vision statements.
- Why this rating

The content strongly aligns with the Six Essential Elements’ Positive Keywords for this category (partnership with families; formation of affections; cultural transmission), and the absence of disqualifying signals supports CLASSICAL here.

What Would Move the Needle

- Concrete curriculum clarity around Great Books/Great Conversation and primary-source reading explicitly stated in public materials, including a published Great Books list and a timeline showing reading across grades.
- Explicit articulation of Socratic pedagogy or Harkness-style discussions in high school and explicit restfulness Scholé days or contemplative practices in lower and middle grades, with public-facing language and examples.
- A clearly articulated “living curriculum” section for Faculty that showcases teacher mentorship, professional development in classical pedagogy, and the living-curriculum model.
- Greater explicit documentation of a robust, two-way partnership with parents, including regular parent education programs (books clubs, gatherings) and open-campus policies with explicit benefits for parental involvement.
- More explicit Great-Books canon references, primary-source integration, and historical-civilizational context as a consistent curricular thread across grades, not only in select pages.
