## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy of the Rogue Valley (a Catholic, classical high school located in Medford, Oregon) presents a robust classical-education profile with clear signposts of the six classical elements. Public materials consistently emphasize Catholic theological formation, liturgical life, and virtue formation; a mission carved around the cultivation of intellect, character, and spirituality; integration of liberal arts, history, and Great Books; a pedagogy that foregrounds memory, chant, and Socratic/seminar-style inquiry; and strong parental engagement as part of a living, communally formed education. Multiple public pages describe a curriculum and culture built on the classics, the great conversation, and the integration of faith and reason, with explicit instances of liturgy, prayer, chanting, and a strong emphasis on virtue.

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are foregrounded through explicit Catholic liturgy, theology, prayer life, and a sacramental worldview, including daily prayer, Mass, liturgical rhythm, and the imago Dei framing of education. 
- The school’s stated aim centers on formation (virtue, wisdom, human flourishing) rather than merely information or career outcomes, with the Three Pillars (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) highlighted as the core objective. 
- Curriculum and Great Books/Great Conversation ideas are present (integrated liberal arts approach, Latin, history, philosophy, and the humanities), with explicit reference to reading original texts and engaging in a classical dialogue. 
- Pedagogy features lift-and-separate elements of contemplative/restful learning (Scholé) and Socratic/seminar-style inquiry; memorization and liturgical/chanting practices are invoked as part of memory-work and formation. 
- Faculty signals include named leadership (Headmaster) and a culture of mentorship and formation, with emphasis on a coherent school identity; however, explicit language about the faculty as “living curriculum” is less prominent in public materials. 
- Parent and community partnership is a stated priority (parents as co-educators, parent formation, open campus/visits, and family involvement), with ongoing opportunities for engagement described in public materials. 

Overall assessment: CLASSICAL. The school demonstrates many core indicators of classical education (Catholic, moral-virtue formation; Great Books orientation; traditional pedagogical modes; liturgical life; parental partnership). The strength is clearest in religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose/goal, curriculum, and pedagogy. The faculty portrayal is present but less explicitly framed as the “living curriculum” in public materials, and while parental partnership is evidenced, the depth of that partnership (as co-teachers and ongoing formation) could be strengthened in public-facing communications. The aggregate picture remains solidly CLASSICAL, with Element 5 (Faculty) the most nuanced element (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL).
