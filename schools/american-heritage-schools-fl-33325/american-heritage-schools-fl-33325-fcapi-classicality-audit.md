## Executive Summary

American Heritage Schools Florida (AH Florida) presents a strong private, college-preparatory program with notable strengths in faculty quality, fine arts, and pre-professional pathways. Public-facing materials emphasize high academic outcomes (AP performance, National Merit/Presidential Scholar candidates) and a family-oriented community, signaling clear virtues such as integrity and compassion. However, the school’s public materials do not explicitly foreground the core classical markers—liberal arts foundations (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation, or a distinctly Socratic pedagogy—nor do they overtly articulate a theological or liturgical metaphysical program. Taken together, AH Florida sits at the intersection of high-quality, college-prep private schooling and some elements aligned with classical aims, but it stops short of a full classical education model in its published materials.

Overall classification (aggregate): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Strengths aligned with classical cues: virtue-related language in mission statements (integrity, compassion), strong teacher dedication, and a robust, college-prep culture with extensive AP offerings and pre-professional programs. This supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating in several categories, particularly Faculty and Parents/Community. Evidence: mission statements emphasizing integrity/knowledge/compassion and a family-oriented community; dedicated faculty; AP and pre-professional programs; strong arts and STEM pathways.  
- Moderate to no explicit classical curriculum signals: absence (in public-facing content) of explicit liberal-arts scaffolding (grammar/logical/rhetorical tracts), Great Books/Great Conversation, or clearly stated Scholé/quiet-wonder pedagogy; minimal reference to liturgical/metaphysical underpinnings. This weighs toward NOT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy categories. Evidence: descriptions of college prep, arts, and STEM, but little on Great Books, classical trivium/quadrivium, or Socratic seminars. 
- Category-by-category balance: Religious Metaphysical Foundations (some virtue-based language; not theology-focused) and Curriculum/Pedagogy show the strongest misalignment with classical criteria, while Faculty and Parents/Community show meaningful alignment. The aggregate tilt is toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear opportunities to advance to CLASSICAL through explicit curriculum and pedagogy changes. The classical-keyword framework (from the Six Essential Elements) provides concrete targets AH Florida could pursue to reach CLASSICAL status across all six categories.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly adopt and publish a classical-curriculum framework
  - Introduce grammar, logic, rhetoric as core through-lines in early grades and publicly document their through-lines across K–12, with a clear rationale that ties to Great Books and Great Conversation where appropriate. This aligns with the Positive Keywords (grammar, logic, rhetoric, great books) and directly addresses Curriculum (Element 3). 
- Integrate Great Books and a canonical Western canon into the curriculum
  - Implement a classical “Great Books” program or a “Great Conversation” series across grades, and provide publicly accessible exemplars of canonical readings mapped to grade-level expectations. This directly targets Element 3 and Element 2 and would strengthen Element 1 by elevating philosophical/metaphysical grounding through canonical texts. 
- Implement classical pedagogy components (Scholé, memory work, narration, Socratic discussions)
  - Publicly document and pilot Socratic seminars, memorization-based learning, narration, and chanted or sung learning where appropriate (especially in lower grades where memory and repetition are helpful). Align with the Pedagogy signals and the Classical Lexicon’s expectations. 
- Elevate the teacher role as a “living curriculum”
  - Develop and publish a “portrait of a teacher” and a “core teacher profile” that exemplifies classical teacher-mentorship and virtue formation. This would move Element 5 toward CLASSICAL and reinforce Element 1’s foundations. 
- Deepen family-partnered homeschool/community formation (Parent formation)
  - Expand parent formation programs (book clubs, study groups, liturgical or moral formation activities) and explicitly frame parents as co-educators in the classical sense. Publicly outline opportunities for parental involvement and home-school alignment to advance Element 6 toward CLASSICAL.
