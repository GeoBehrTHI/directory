## Executive Summary

Columbus Classical Academy (CCA) demonstrates strong, explicit alignment with classical education principles across the key Rubric categories. The publicly visible materials portray a school anchored in virtue, truth, goodness, and beauty, with a curricular and pedagogical program that centers on the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books and primary-source engagement, and a mix of Socratic seminar and dialogue-based pedagogy in the upper grades. The school presents itself as Hillsdale College Curriculum School and, in some materials, emphasizes a biblical-Christian foundation while maintaining a civic, non-proselytizing stance in formal curriculum. Taken together, the evidence suggests a CLASSICAL orientation with robust fidelity to foundational elements such as formation of character, integration of liberal arts, and a coherent classical pedagogy, paired with community and parental engagement. The analysis below triangulates public materials from CCA’s site with the classical education rubric drawn from the knowledge-base lexicon.

Key signals from the knowledge sources indicate:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations: explicit biblical-Christian framing appears in sanctuary-like statements about a biblical foundation and Apostles’ Creed alignment in some materials, with a Christ-centered moral horizon, while avoiding catechesis in the curriculum per stated policy. This supports a strong religious/metaphysical baseline. 
- Purpose and goals: the school foregrounds virtue formation, character formation, and a mission to cultivate truth, goodness, and beauty, not merely job-readiness; there are explicit statements about forming minds for virtue and civic life.  
- Curriculum: clear articulation of grammar/logic/rhetoric (Trivium) and a curriculum scaffold connected to Great Books/primary sources, Latin, history, and integrated study across subjects. The Hillsdale curriculum framing is repeatedly cited.  
- Pedagogy: a blend of contemplative/restful moments and rigorous Socratic-style engagement in upper grades, with lower-school singing/chanting/memory-building, and upper-school seminars/debates.  
- Faculty: emphasis on teachers as mentors, the living curriculum, and a culture of virtue and care; faculty are described as intentional, virtuous models, and as core to the school’s ethos. 
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with families, parent engagement through information packets, parent-education opportunities, and open campus/involvement; a shared mission with families is repeatedly highlighted. 

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.
