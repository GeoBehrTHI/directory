## ABSTRACT

Columbus Classical Academy (CCA) presents a CLASSICAL profile across all six rubric categories. Evidence from the school’s public materials points to a biblically informed, virtue-centered foundation (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), a purpose oriented toward formation of wisdom and character (Purpose & Goal), a rigorous curriculum built on the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, primary sources, and Hillsdale College pedagogy (Curriculum), a pedagogy that combines contemplative elements and Socratic/interrogative methods with early-stage memory work and choral activities (Pedagogy), a faculty described as mentors and living exemplars who model virtue and knowledge (Faculty), and a strong emphasis on partnership with families and active parental involvement in the school’s life (Parents & Community).

- Category 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes biblical foundation language and creed-aligned governance, with Hillsdale-based foundational framing. 
- Category 2 (Purpose & Goal): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes explicit formation/virtue aims and a mission built around truth, goodness, and beauty. 
- Category 3 (Curriculum): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes Trivium progression, Great Books/primary sources, Latin, and Hillsdale curriculum alignment. 
- Category 4 (Pedagogy): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes singing/memory work in lower grades and Socratic/seminar approaches in upper grades.  
- Category 5 (Faculty): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes living-curriculum faculty emphasis and bios of faculty leaders.  
- Category 6 (Parents & Community): CLASSICAL. Evidence includes parent partnership language and opportunities for parental education and visits. 

Aggregate: CLASSICAL.

Note: The evaluation draws on two sources of textual data: (1) the school-specific crawl vector store (public pages and documents about CCA) and (2) the knowledge-base rubric and “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1)” corpus. The analysis shows alignment with classical education across all six categories, anchored by formation of virtue, Great Books and primary-source engagement, and a coherent Trivium-based curriculum, taught through a mix of Socratic and memory-focused pedagogy, with strong faculty and active parental partnership.
