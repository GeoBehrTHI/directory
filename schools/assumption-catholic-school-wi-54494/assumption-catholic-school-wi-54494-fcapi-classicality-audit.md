# Classicality Audit: Augustine School (Jackson, TN)
Website audited: https://www.augustineschool.com 

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Why: Augustine School’s public materials strongly affirm classical aims in their religious and metaphysical foundations and in their stated purpose (formation toward truth, goodness, beauty; virtue and wisdom). They clearly identify as “Biblically-Based,” “Gospel-Centered,” and “Classically-Informed,” and emphasize partnering with families. However, the crawl data contains limited explicit evidence for the classical curriculum specifics (Trivium/Quadrivium, Latin, Great Books) and explicit pedagogical markers (Socratic seminar, chanting/memory work, scholé) required to award a fully CLASSICAL rating using the Six Elements keyword framework. With modest additions to public-facing pages (see “What would move the needle”), Augustine could readily exhibit a CLASSICAL profile. Key site signals include “form students who love goodness, truth, and beauty,” “forming wise and virtuous young men and women,” “Biblically-Based,” “Gospel-Centered,” and “partner with families”  . Community-facing posts such as an ACCS accreditation announcement and a “Season of Prayer Guide” further reinforce mission alignment and communal devotion  .

Scoring below follows the required Positive/Negative Keyword Indicators from “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” and the lexicon groupings (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose & Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community)  .

## What would move the needle
To present a fully CLASSICAL profile via public materials, Augustine School should:
- Curriculum page updates
  - Name the Trivium and Quadrivium explicitly; list grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy; show sample reading lists and “Great Books/primary sources” for upper grades; highlight Latin/Greek study and “history” (use “history,” not only “social studies”)  .
- Pedagogy page
  - Describe K–6 memory work, chanting, singing, recitation; note upper school Socratic seminars/debates; mention rhetoric orations/thesis capstones; consider adopting/mentioning scholé/restful learning language as appropriate  .
- Faculty formation
  - Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher/Graduate,” emphasizing teachers as mentors and the “living curriculum,” plus any classical pedagogy training the faculty receives .
- Parent formation
  - Centralize ongoing parent education (book clubs, talks, classical education explainers) and highlight these on the website (you already run family-facing events like “Mommy + Me”) .
