## Executive Summary

Eastwood Christian School (Montgomery, AL) presents a clearly classical Christian orientation. Its public materials frame the school as Christ-centered, biblically grounded, and committed to a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) delivered within a biblical worldview. The school explicitly registers its mission as glorifying God through a classical Christian education, emphasizes formation of virtue and wisdom, and positions parents as primary partners in education. Evidence across mission statements, statements of faith, admissions materials, and history documents shows robust religious foundations, a formation-focused purpose, a Trivium-based curriculum, and an explicit emphasis on family-school partnership. While the school markets a classical approach with explicit emphasis on theology and virtue, it does not consistently spell out every element of the Great Books/Great Conversation tradition in public-facing content. Overall, Eastwood Christian School scores CLASSICAL across most categories, with two categories showing strong classical alignment but more room to explicitly document certain pedagogical practices.

ABSTRACT
Eastwood Christian School presents a strong CLASSICAL orientation, grounded in a Christ-centered, biblically-based educational philosophy. The school’s public materials emphasize biblical theology, a robust Statement of Faith, and liturgical/corporate worship as core to the educational process. The curriculum centers on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and classical literature as the backbone of a liberal-arts approach in a Christian worldview, with explicit mission statements about truth, life, and vocation. The school clearly positions parents as primary educators’ partners, endorsing a family-centric model of education. While the overall alignment is CLASSICAL, two categories—Pedagogy and Faculty—could benefit from more explicit articulation of certain classical practices (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholé/restful learning, and explicit “living curriculum” language for faculty). The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL, with actionable steps offered to move the remaining categories toward full Classical alignment.
