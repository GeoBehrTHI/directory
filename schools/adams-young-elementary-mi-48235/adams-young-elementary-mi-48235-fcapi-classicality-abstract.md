## ABSTRACT

Adams-Young Elementary (Adam-Young Academy) demonstrates partial alignment with classical education, notably in its emphasis on character formation, career awareness, and strong faculty/parent engagement. The public materials, however, do not articulate the full classical program—specifically the Seven Liberal Arts (Trivium/Quadrivium), Great Books/Great Conversation, Socratic pedagogy, or liturgical/theological formation—leading to a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL classification across several categories rather than CLASSICAL. The category-by-category ranking yields: RMF NOT CLASSICAL, SPG SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, Curriculum NOT CLASSICAL, Pedagogy NOT CLASSICAL, Faculty SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, Parents & Community SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregate rating is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Category outcomes reflect the school’s strong emphasis on character formation and family/community involvement (Categories 2, 5, and 6) but a lack of explicit classical curriculum and pedagogy (Categories 3 and 4) and limited RMF alignment (Element 1).

- The school would move toward CLASSICAL with explicit adoption and public articulation of: a Great Books/Great Conversation framework, Trivium/Quadrivium elements, Socratic seminars or Paideia-style inquiry, scholé/restful learning, and a liturgical/theological formation component, complemented by formal teacher development in classical pedagogy and a stronger framing of parents as co-educators.

Citations refer to Adams-Young Academy public materials describing character formation, career awareness, faculty quality, and parent/community engagement. Representative quotes include: “Building good character is infused in everything we do,” “We expose students to career options from an early age, so that they may explore occupations that align with their purpose and passion,” “Academic achievement, excellent character, and career awareness provide our students with the tools they will need to pursue complete lives,” and “Strong parent and community engagement.”    

References to the classical-education keyword framework are drawn from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) corpus, including positive keywords such as wisdom, virtue, truth, goodness, beauty, formation, Great Books, and the Trivium/Quadrivium, and negative signals (e.g., a focus on career-workforce readiness or secular pedagogy).
