## Executive Summary

Adams-Young Elementary (Detroit, MI 48235), operating under Cornerstone Schools’ Adams-Young Academy branding, demonstrates several features aligned with classical aims (notably character formation, a focus on community/family engagement, and a strong faculty emphasis). However, the public-facing materials from the school emphasize character development, career awareness, technology integration, and parent/community engagement rather than explicit classical hallmarks such as robust Great Books study, the Trivium/Quadrivium, intensive Socratic pedagogy, or liturgical/theological formation. Taken together, the school most closely aligns with a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL profile rather than CLASSICAL. This assessment reflects the presence of formation-oriented messaging and strong faculty/parent engagement, contrasted with the lack of explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, and traditional scholé pedagogy in the public materials examined.

Key insights:

- The site emphasizes character formation and career awareness, with claims of a robust character-and-culture program at every grade level and strong faculty engagement. This points to virtues-centered aims, albeit without explicit classical lexicon (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or liturgical/theological pedagogy).
- There is clear evidence of parental and community involvement, which aligns with classical models’ emphasis on pp. partnership with families and formation of affections, but the materials stop short of detailing parents as “co-teachers” in a formal sense.
- The absence of explicit Great Books, Socratic seminars, scholé days, or liturgical practices in the public materials lowers the score for several categories, pushing the overall classification toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle

- Explicitly articulate and implement a Great Books-oriented, liberal-arts-driven curriculum (even at the elementary level where feasible), including primary-source engagement and historical texts aligned with a Great Conversation approach.
- Introduce guided Socratic dialogue or Paideia-like seminars in upper elementary and middle school grades, along with structured opportunities for rhetoric and public speaking.
- Incorporate scholé-focused, contemplative learning days (restful, memory work, and deliberate reflection) into the schedule.
- Integrate a clearly defined liturgical/theological dimension (even if ecumenical) in line with traditional Christian classical education, including communal practices, liturgical rhythms, and sacramental or worship-informed elements.
- Formalize the “living curriculum” by codifying teacher mastery of classical pedagogies (copywork, dictation, imitation of master texts) and making teacher development a pillar of the program.
- Expand explicit family formation initiatives (classical book studies, parent study groups, home-school alignment) and continue promoting parents as co-educators within a classical framework.
