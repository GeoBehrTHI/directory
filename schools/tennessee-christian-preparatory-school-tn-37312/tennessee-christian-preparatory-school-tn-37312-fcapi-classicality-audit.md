# Classicality Audit: Tennessee Christian Preparatory School (TCPS)

Website audited: https://www.tennesseechristian.org/

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Why: Strong, explicit Christian religious foundations and regular liturgical life (daily Bible, weekly chapel, worship; integration of “Biblical truth” across subjects) align well with classical metaphysical and theological grounding. Middle School curriculum requires Latin and Logic; Upper School explicitly uses Harkness discussions (Socratic-style). However, the school’s stated purpose and public framing are dominated by “college preparatory” language, there is no public articulation of “truth, goodness, and beauty” (beyond a chapel letter touching “truth”), no visible Great Books list or trivium/quadrivium structure, and little evidence of lower-school chanting/memorization pedagogy, published rhetoric/thesis work, teacher-formation language, or parent formation.
- Most important insights:
  - Religious/metaphysical: Chapel and worship are central; mission and leadership letters emphasize integrating Biblical truth across the curriculum; Upper School includes apologetic theology, and chapel culture is formative (positive)    .
  - Curriculum/pedagogy: Latin and Logic in Middle School; Harkness (discussion) in Upper School (positive), but no public great books/trivium/rhetoric thesis promises and limited evidence of chanting/recitation or scholé (gaps)  .
  - Purpose/goal: Repeated “college preparatory,” “college credit” signals weigh against a classical aim framed around virtue/wisdom/beauty; only scattered “truth” language appears in a chapel letter, not as a curricular aim (mixed)    .

Method note: The keyword criteria and scoring follow the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” indicators and lexicon (positive/negative signals such as truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, chapel, Socratic/Harkness, scholé, wonder, chanting vs. project-based, career readiness)  .

What would move the needle (actionable next steps for TCPS)
- Purpose and goal: Publicly reframe your telos in classical terms—virtue and wisdom as ends; explicitly name “truth, goodness, and beauty” (and, if appropriate, “wonder” and “human flourishing”) as guiding lights across divisions, not just in chapel letters  .
- Curriculum:
  - Publish a Great Books/primary texts spine per grade bands and explicitly reference “joining the Great Conversation” and the seven liberal arts (Trivium/Quadrivium) .
  - Show Upper School rhetoric sequence (formal Rhetoric, orations, senior thesis/defense) and the continuity from Logic to Rhetoric .
- Pedagogy:
  - State Socratic/seminar methods across Upper School (Harkness already present; broaden to “Socratic seminar”) and add evidence of chanting/recitation/memory work in the Lower School and Logic School (songs, chants, catechisms, poetry recitation) and a posture of scholé/restful learning where appropriate .
- Faculty:
  - Publish a “portrait of a teacher” and “portrait of a graduate,” and highlight teachers as mentors/living curriculum; note teacher formation in classical pedagogy and faculty colloquia on Great Books .
- Parents & Community:
  - Add parent formation offerings (book studies on classical education, evening talks, “classical 101” seminars) and articulate a parent partnership/covenant that names parents as primary educators in harmony with the school .
