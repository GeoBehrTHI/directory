## Executive Summary

Legacy Academy (Georgetown, DE, 19947) presents a clear classical Christian orientation across most dimensions of its public materials. Its public communications emphasize Christ-centered formation, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), memory-driven pedagogy (recitation and poetry from memory), and engagement with Latin and Shakespeare through camps and programs. The school frequently frames education as formation of character and virtue within a spiritual framework, and it maintains visible partnerships with families and a culture of community events (e.g., Open Houses, Grandparents Breakfast, pastors’ and community gatherings). The most explicit gap relative to a fully classical rubric is in the explicit articulation of Socratic seminars and some traditionally scholé (restful, contemplative) pedagogical rhythms in core public materials; however, concrete evidence of traditional elements such as the Trivium, Great Books practices (as part of “Great Conversation” concepts) and a living-mission emphasis is present.

Overall classification (aggregate): CLASSICAL

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
 Evidence includes explicit Christ-centered theology, integration of faith with learning, and liturgical/community rhythms mentioned in school materials. Positive signals far outweigh any negative signals (no clear secularization indicators found in the public material).

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
 The school emphasizes formation, virtue, and a transcendent sense of truth/goodness/beauty as guiding aims, not mere job prep. This aligns with a formation-first, humane-excellence orientation.

- Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL
 Strong evidence of Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin, and classical culture through camps (Latin Boot Camp, Shakespeare Camp). Great Books lineage and “Great Conversation” framing are implied by the classical-curriculum emphasis.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
 Evidence shows robust memory/recitation and chanting practices (e.g., Scripture, poetry recitation) and a strong emphasis on Christian formation; however, explicit, pervasive documentation of Socratic seminars (the high-school hallmark) is less evident in public pages. The presence of memory-based and liturgical/repetitive pedagogy is clear, but additional Socratic/dialectical pedagogy is not as prominently documented in the public-facing content.

- Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL
 The school presents teachers as mentors and models of virtue, with documented faculty profiles and explicit emphasis on teacher leadership and formation (e.g., individual faculty pages and biographical notes). This aligns with the classical ideal of teachers as living exemplars and part of the curriculum.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
 The school foregrounds partnership with families and churches, parent engagement, and open campus/community events. Public materials portray parents as co-educators and active participants in school life.
