## Executive Summary

Public materials consistently foreground a classical framework integrated with Christian formation, emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty, and a Trivium-based curriculum. The school emphasizes partnerships with families, a liturgical/spiritual dimension (chapel/worship), and a Great-Books–leaning posture through classical resources. This alignment is repeatedly echoed in mission/vision statements, philosophy of education, and the publicly published resources and governance pages. What Would Move the Needle
- Pedagogy (Element 4): Explicitly document and implement a more robust Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades, including regular Socratic discussions, guided seminars, and rhetoric-centric activities. Add structured memory work and recitation where appropriate, and articulate a concrete plan for Scholé/restful learning days to complement rigorous instruction. This would reinforce Element 3’s CLASSICAL standing.
- Documentation of liturgical cadence: If not already explicit, publish a formal liturgical calendar and evidence of communal worship/chapel practices integrated with the academic day, to strengthen the Liturgical/Communal dimension of Element 1.

Note: The needle-moving recommendations above target Element 4 primarily, with ancillary reinforcement in Curriculum and Liturgical/Communal dimensions.
