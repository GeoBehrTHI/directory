## Executive Summary

Faith Christian School (Port St Joe, FL, 32456) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning educational program conducted within a Christ-centered environment. Public-facing materials emphasize a Biblically integrated curriculum, a Trivium-based framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and a stated mission to educate the mind, body, and spirit. The school also foregrounds theology and doctrinal commitments through a formal Statement of Faith, a church affiliation, and explicit articulation of biblical authority. Taken together, these signals establish a framework that is strongly religious and oriented toward classical formation, though some classical elements (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and some faculty-community formation signals) are less clearly stated. The aggregate impression from the public materials is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear CLASSICAL commitments in Religion, Curriculum, and portions of Pedagogy, and more modest emission in Faculty and explicit Parent/Community formation.

Executive-level takeaway per category:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
- Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What would move the needle: To tip toward full CLASSICAL, the school could foreground explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language, demonstrate the living-curriculum teacher model (faculty as mentors and living curriculum), formalize parent formation and school-wide liturgical or communal practices, and more clearly document classical pedagogy signals (e.g., scholé days, chanting, or memory work as a staple across grades). See “What Would Move the Needle” after Category 1 and throughout Category sections.

Note: All category signals below are grounded in the school’s public materials and cross-walked with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric. Citations reference the school content available in the crawl and the knowledge-base lexicon.

Negative keywords such as relativism or secular humanism do not appear in these passages.
- Estimated hits: multiple explicit positives (Word of God, Bible, Trinity, Holy Spirit, creed/theology) and no explicit negatives in the core religious-definitional passages. 4–6 distinct positive signals vs 0 negative signals observed in the core passages.

- Negative keywords present: none explicit in the core public-facing materials (e.g., “workforce readiness” or “21st-century skills” language is not used in the provided public-facing content).

Quantitative estimate

Why this rating

However, explicit articulation of virtue/wisdom as telos and a fully elaborated formation framework (beyond the phrase “Educating the Mind, Body, and Spirit”) is less overt than in some canonical classical schools.

The public-facing content references the memory-centered aspects of the grammar stage. 
- Upper school pedagogy mentions structured rhetoric and the ability to present and argue (rhetoric-focused training). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, recitation, memory work, rhetoric training, seminars/debates signals (through the rhetoric emphasis). Note: explicit “Socrates/Scholé/paideia/seminars” language is not widely shown; the content does not clearly describe Socratic seminars or Paideia-style seminars within the public-facing materials.
- Negative keywords: lack of explicit experiential learning- or project-based-only pedagogy signals in the core materials; no strong negative indicators.

Quantitative estimate
- Positive keyword hits: grammar, logic, rhetoric, recitation/memory work, rhetoric-training signals = 4–5.
- Negative keyword hits: 0 explicit in core pedagogy passages.

Why this rating
- The program is clearly built on a classical pedagogy (grammar-logic-rhetoric with memory work and upper-level rhetoric). The absence of explicit Socratic seminars or “Scholé days” in public-facing content lowers it from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Publicly describe Socratic discussions, seminars, or Paideia-style seminars in upper grades; include examples of student-led inquiry or collaborative inquiry (e.g., Socratic dialogues or Declamations) in the high school program.
- Include a formal articulation of contemplative or scholé-friendly cycles (restful learning periods, memory rituals) across the academic calendar.

Citations
