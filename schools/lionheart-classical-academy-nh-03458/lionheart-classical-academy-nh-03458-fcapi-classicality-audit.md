## Executive Summary

Lionheart Classical Academy NH 03458 (Peterborough, NH) presents a clearly identified classical-education orientation anchored in virtue formation, liberal-arts curriculum, and Hillsdale College alignment. Public-facing materials foreground a mission to train minds and inspire hearts through a classical Liberal Arts and Sciences education that emphasizes virtuous living, responsible citizenship, high academic standards, and cultural literacy. The school describes itself as tuition-free, serving grades K–8, with a view toward a K–12 classical identity and a vision of forming “virtuous” citizens with intellectual curiosity and a love of learning. A Hillsdale-affiliated curriculum is explicitly marketed, alongside a commitment to a content-rich, classical approach and a focus on virtuous character and civic virtue. However, there is no explicit articulation of religious/theological foundations or liturgical/community practices on the public-facing materials (and the FAQ notes that charter schools are not religious), which affects Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations). The public materials also signal ongoing enrollment practices (lottery/waitlist), open access for NH residents, and active parent engagement, which supports Element 6 (Parents & Community).

Overall classification by category (using the Six Essential Elements lexicon as the rubric):

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
- Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 4: Pedagogy — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 5: Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key insights:
- The school’s public materials robustly articulate virtue, moral character, and cultural literacy as core aims, and the Hillsdale-aligned liberal-arts curriculum is central to its classical identity (Element 2, Element 3 to some extent).
- Element 1 reflects a secular orientation with explicit charitable statements about being “not religious” in the charter context, which prevents full alignment with religious/metaphysical foundations.
- Pedagogy, faculty, and some curricular details are present but do not consistently demonstrate all classical-method markers (e.g., explicit mention of Socratic seminars, rigorous memory-work, or the full Great Books framework appear only indirectly or not at all in public-facing text), yielding a mixed (Somewhat Classical) signal across Categories 3–5.
- Element 6 shows stronger classical-community alignment through parent partnership, formation activities, and open-access policies, aligning with Classical indicators.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public-facing descriptions emphasize a classical approach and big questions, with a general sense of a content-rich program. Explicit pedagogy terms such as Socratic seminars, chant/singing, or liturgical/scholastic memory work are not clearly described in public materials, but the phrase “asks big questions” appears in the classical-education framing.
  - What is a classical education? frames education as addressing intellectual, moral, and civic virtue, with questions and beauty; emphasizes a content-rich approach and big questions. 
  - The Academics content describes a liberal-arts, content-rich approach as a foundation for learning and memory of curriculum; but explicit lower- vs upper-school pedagogy markers (Socratic, seminars, singing, chanting, etc.) are not enumerated in the public text. 
  - Enrollment and other pages emphasize structure and mission, not explicit spacing for contemplative/restful pedagogy beyond general virtue formation. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: contemplation and wonder language from the classical-education framing; emphasis on big questions and a content-rich approach.
  - Example: “asks big questions” and emphasis on a classical approach. 
- Negative indicators present: lack of explicit statements about lower-grade singing/chanting, Socratic seminars, or upper-grade rhetoric/debate in public-facing materials; absence of detailed contemplative/restful pedagogy markers beyond general scholé framing. 

Why this rating
- The school clearly identifies as classical and emphasizes big questions and content-rich pedagogy, but explicit, codified evidence of the full pedagogical methods typical of a classic program (Socratic seminars, memory work, chanting, and formal rhetoric/debate) is not clearly documented in public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.

The signals thus align with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for the Faculty category.

### Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school emphasizes partnerships with parents, parent formation, and active family engagement; open campus and public events are referenced; parent information and enrollment materials indicate active parent involvement and communication about classical education.
  - Parent Information pages and enrollment materials indicate a proactive approach to parent engagement and community involvement. 
  - Enrollment and information sessions are described as opportunities for prospective families to learn about classical education, with multiple communications channels (email, newsletters, events). 
  - The public materials show open access for visits and involvement, with parent-facing sections (FAQ, enrollment, events, board information) and “Training Minds and Inspiring Hearts” messaging that invites family participation. 
  - Addressing the public: “Lionheart Classical Academy is a licensee of Hillsdale’s K-12 Program” helps build community credibility and a shared classical framework. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: partnership with parents, co-teachers concept (as found in the classical lexicon), parent formation, reading groups, seminars/book clubs, open campus, family involvement, and parent communications.
  - Examples in public text include parent information, enrollment sessions, and events. 
- Negative indicators present: none significant in public materials; the school presents a proactive, welcoming posture toward parents rather than a consumer-model approach.
  - The public-facing FAQ explicitly rejects religious discrimination in admissions but does not present consumer-focused language. 

Why this rating
- Lionheart’s public materials consistently frame parents as partners and co-educators in a classical model, with ample opportunities for parent involvement and education about classical education. This aligns strongly with CLASSICAL for Parents & Community.

What would move the needle

- For Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Include explicit language about transcendent moral order, shared liturgical or communal practices (even if non-denominational), or theological grounding to demonstrate deeper metaphysical alignment. A cautious, secular articulation could be maintained, but adding a stated tradition or contemplative practice would push Element 1 toward CLASSICAL.

- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly name and demonstrate the presence of Great Books, primary sources, and the grammar/logic/rhetoric sequence; clarify the role of classical languages (Latin/Greek) and explicit historical narrative (not just Western civilization). Incorporate language about not substituting history with social-studies framing and reduce or contextualize STEM-first narratives with balanced emphasis on humanities.

- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Add explicit mention of Socratic seminars, memory work (recitation, dictation), and a formal sequence of rhetoric and oratory in upper grades; demonstrate daily or weekly practice of contemplation/scholé, and show examples of student-led seminars or debates.

- For Element 5 (Faculty): Strengthen and publicly articulate the living-curriculum concept, a formal portrait of teacher expectations, and a clearly defined teacher-professional-development pathway aligned with classical pedagogy; emphasize mentor-model teacher profiles and a “faculty as living curriculum” statement in public materials.

- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): While this area already reads CLASSICAL, the school could strengthen forms of parental formation (book clubs, study groups), formal family liturgy alignment, and explicit home-learning partnerships to deepen the co-teaching model.

- General needle-movers (cross-category): Publish a concise “Classical Education Playbook” on the website that explicitly lists the six elements (grammar/logical/rhetoric sequence, Great Books/Great Conversation, memory work, contemplation/scholé, rhetoric (upper grades), and living curriculum) with concrete, public examples from Lionheart’s program. This would provide clearer alignment across all six categories and help visitors see how the school implements classical education in practice.
