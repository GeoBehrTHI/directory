## Executive Summary

Pinnacle Classical Academy (AR 72209) presents a robust Classical Christian program that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education. The public-facing materials consistently frame the school as a private, classical Christian K4–12 institution that centers faith, virtue formation, and a liberal-arts curriculum built on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), extensive use of Latin, and engagement with the Great Books/Great Conversation approach. The school emphasizes that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable, that education forms character as its telos, and that pedagogy weaves together memorization, liturgical sensibilities, and Socratic-style discourse as students advance. This evaluation synthesizes explicit statements from PCA’s site and blog content with the knowledge-base rubric for a fully consistent CLASSICAL ranking across all six categories. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key evidence includes: PCA’s mission to form students in virtue and love of God, the explicit Christian-theological framing of all knowledge as God’s truth, the grammar-logic-rhetoric progression with Latin, and the articulation of pedagogy that blends memorization, contemplative elements, and rhetoric-based expression. The Parent-Teacher Alliance and faculty pages further reinforce a living-curriculum model anchored in virtue, mentorship, and a community of faith. Positive keyword indicators from PCA materials align strongly with the Positive Keyword Lists in the Six Essential Elements document, while negative indicators are notably absent or minimal in PCA’s public materials. See sources cited in-context below.

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- PCA describes itself as a private classical Christian school and emphasizes Christ-centered education; public materials state that “All truth and knowledge are rooted in the person of Jesus Christ” and that truth is interconnected to God’s truth across subjects.  
- The Headmaster and faculty materials frame education as formation, oriented toward forming students’ character in a Christian framework, with explicit references to a “community of faith and learning” and to a Statement of Faith governing faculty. 
- PCA’s communications emphasize worship/faith-life integration (e.g., “the grace of the gospel” in PTA, and Christian formation elements embedded in culture), supporting liturgical/communal sensibilities, even if formal liturgical events are not enumerated in every page. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive indicators present in PCA materials (per the Six Elements lexicon): truth, theology, liturgy/worship, wonder, imago Dei, sacred text/scripture, catechesis, gospel-centered formation, reverence, sacred music, and the Logos. Examples in PCA content include “All truth and knowledge are rooted in Jesus Christ” and statements about forming virtue in Christ-centered ways.  
- Negative indicators (e.g., relativism, secular humanism, values-neutral education) are not evidenced in PCA materials; the public materials consistently affirm a doctrinal stance and theological grounding. The Six Elements lexicon shows these as potential negatives, but PCA materials do not foreground them. 

Why this rating
- PCA explicitly grounds education in Christian theology and a theologized view of knowledge, with mission emphasis on forming virtue within a sacred framework and a community of faith. The presence of catechesis, explicit theology, and liturgical/communal orientation (where described) aligns with the Positive keywords for this element. There is no significant presence of secular or relativistic signals; rather, the public materials foreground objective truth anchored in Christian revelation. Therefore, this category is CLASSICAL.

Citations in support: PCA’s Christian framing and theology integration are evident in “Christian” and “Classical” descriptions on the site, including the claim that “All truth and knowledge are rooted in Jesus Christ” and the Christian college-like ethos described by leadership.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum is described as the classical liberal-arts model with the Trivium: grammar, logic, rhetoric. PCA materials explicitly present a three-stage progression: Grammar (Pre-K4–6th), Logic (7th–9th), Rhetoric (10th–12th). Latin is highlighted as part of the curriculum.  
- There are direct references to classical education concepts, including classical, Christian framework, and a focus on the great books and Western civilization through a classical approach. The site discusses “great books of Western civilization” and curriculum integration under classical education. 
- Multiple PCA pages emphasize the integrated curriculum, with content showing a focus on “the stages of education” (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the role of memorization, catechesis, and Latin in building foundational learning. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: liberal arts, trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), great books, primary sources, Latin, Western civilization, history as a longitudinal discipline, and a focus on education as formation (not purely informational).
- Negative keywords (per the Six Elements lexicon) include terms that would signal a departure from classical aims, such as “social studies terminology replacing history,” “STEM-first framing,” or “project-based learning dominance.” PCA’s materials emphasize history within a liberal-arts frame and explicitly highlight classical methods (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books), with no strong emphasis on a social-studies-centric or purely project-based approach; thus, negative signals are minimal.

Why this rating
- PCA’s curriculum is squarely built on the classical liberal-arts framework (grammar-logic-rhetoric, Latin, Great Books, and historical study as core through all grades), clearly aligning with CLASSICAL criteria.

Citations in support: Descriptions of the Trivium progression and Latin emphasis, plus Great Books framing, are present in PCA’s site and blog content.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Pedagogy combines contemplative/restful elements with rigorous inquiry: “Acts of rest and scholé” are described as part of the learning approach; lower grades emphasize memorization through songs and chants, while upper grades move into rhetoric and persuasive expression. The grammar stage uses memorization with songs; the logic and rhetoric stages emphasize reasoning and communication. This aligns with the classical pedagogy described in the lexicon (Scholé, chant/memory, Socratic-style discussion, and rhetoric). 
- The rhetoric stage emphasizes expressing wisdom, with aims to persuade and to form character, consistent with upper-level seminars, debates, ororatory. The Rhetoric page explicitly describes shaping students to express truth persuasively and to influence culture for God’s glory. 
- The school explicitly references the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as a pedagogical framework and discusses the use of catechesis, memorization, and memory-work as part of lower-level pedagogy. This aligns with the positive pedagogy indicators in the Six Elements lexicon. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: scholé, contemplation, wonder, memorization, chanting, recitation, narration, dictation, seminar, colloquy, rhetoric, memory-work, catechesis, liturgical integration, and discussion-based learning.
- Negative keywords (per the Six Elements lexicon) to watch for would include exclusive "project-based learning dominance" or purely experiential/constructionist approaches. PCA materials emphasize classical dialogue, memorization, and rhetoric over project-based-only pedagogy, with no overt signaling of constructivist-only pedagogy. The lexicon flags these as negatives; PCA’s materials do not emphasize them as primary modes.

Why this rating
- PCA’s pedagogy harmonizes with classical education: contemplative/restful aspects (Scholé), memory-based lower-grammar pedagogy, and upper-level Socratic-style dialogues and rhetoric instruction. The explicit articulation of the grammar/logic/rhetoric progression and rhetoric outcomes supports CLASSICAL.

Citations in support: Pedagogy descriptions for grammar/logic/rhetoric, memory/chanting, and rhetoric outcomes are on PCA pages and blog posts; the formal Pedagogy lexicon (Socrates/Scholé/dialectic) is also embedded in the knowledge set.

Why this rating
- PCA’s public materials depict a strong, intentional partnership with parents, with explicit programs and language around parental formation and ongoing involvement. This matches the CLASSICAL orientation for Parents & Community.

Citations in support: PTA description, open-campus language, and statements about parental partnership with instruction.
