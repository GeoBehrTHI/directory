## Executive Summary

Summit Classical Christian School WA 98027 presents a robust alignment with classical education as described in foundational classical-education criteria. Public materials on the school's site consistently frame education as formation within a Christian, transcendent framework (truth, goodness, beauty) and emphasize a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) grounded in Biblical theology. The school’s mission, philosophy of education, and statements of belief articulate a deliberate Christian worldview, liturgical/cohesive community practices, and a vertically integrated PreK–12 classical program. There is strong evidence of virtuous character formation, Memorial/communal worship, and a culture of partnership with families. The faculty pages reinforce a professional, virtue-informed teaching staff, and parent-facing materials highlight ongoing engagement, transparency, and opportunities to align at-home learning with school aims. Taken together, Summit WA 98027 is CLASSICAL overall, with some nuanced areas that could be strengthened to reach full “fully CLASSICAL” status in every category.

Executive takeaways by category, with a concise justification:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Clear theology-driven, Bible-centered basis; liturgical/community practices; explicit Christian worldview.
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Emphasis on formation of virtue and wisdom, Christian formation as the core aim, not merely job prep.
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Strong Trivium emphasis and history focus; explicit lower/upper-level grammar–logic–rhetoric structure present; lack of explicit, public Great Books language in site content, though related Great-Conversation rhetoric appears in the broader classical conversation; history integrated across curriculum.
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Evidence of memory- and singing-based lower-school pedagogy and Socratic-style leadership in upper grades is implied through rhetoric-focused sequences, but explicit mention of regular Socratic seminars/debates is not consistently explicit on the site.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. Public bios and institutional language emphasize virtuous, skilled teachers who form part of the living curriculum and partner with families.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong parent-partner stance, explicit Statement of Faith review for admissions, open-house and interview processes, ongoing family engagement.

What would move the needle: Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) would benefit most from explicit public articulations of Great Books/Great Conversation and explicit Socratic/seminar-based practices in the high school grades, respectively. Also, more explicit language about continuous parent education and structured home-school alignment could further strengthen the Parents & Community category, though current materials already reflect a healthy partnership.

- What would move Summit WA 98027 toward FULL CLASSICAL status:
 - Element 3: Publicly articulate a Great Books approach and specify Great Conversation commitments in the curriculum scope and sequence; demonstrate explicit integration of Great Books across grade bands.
 - Element 4: Describe explicit use of Socratic seminars or debates in upper grades, including sample rubrics, outcomes, and alignment with the Trivium stages.
 - Element 6: Expand parent-education offerings (beyond statements of faith and admissions) with ongoing book studies, parent workshops, and a structured “co-teacher” program with measurable expectations.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum (Element 3)
 - Publicly articulate and publicly commit to a Great Books/Great Conversation component across grade levels (explicit language and scope/sequence). This would align more clearly with the “Great Books and Great Conversation” hallmark of classical education as described in the knowledge base.
 - Provide a transparent, published cross-grade Great Books reading list and a public-facing statement about how Great Conversations are integrated with the Trivium progression.

- Pedagogy (Element 4)
 - Add explicit, published descriptions of regular Socratic seminars or debate formats in upper grades (with rubrics, outcomes, and alignment to logic and rhetoric stages) to complement the rhetoric-focused curriculum and demonstrate concrete high-school pedagogy in action.

- Parents & Community (Element 6)
 - Expand formal parent-education opportunities (e.g., periodic book studies, parent evenings, and structured “co-teacher” training) with a published schedule and outcomes to deepen the home-school partnership.

- All categories (as needed)
 - Consider more explicit language around contemplative/scholé moments in daily lessons (refl ections, quiet time, or memory-focused activities) across grade bands to reinforce the contemplative dimension of scholé as called out in classical education literature and the accompanying literature in the ICE materials.
