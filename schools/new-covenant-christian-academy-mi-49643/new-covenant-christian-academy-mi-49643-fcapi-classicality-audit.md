## Executive Summary

New Covenant Christian Academy (Interlochen, MI 49643) presents a clear classical Christian orientation in its public-facing materials. The school identifies itself explicitly as a discipleship-driven, evangelical program that integrates theology, worship, and biblical worldview into daily life and study. It also emphasizes classical pedagogy through Trivium-based learning (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) and a focus on forming wisdom and virtue, with mission language such as “Glorifying God through Wisdom and Virtue.” In curriculum, the school foregrounds grammar-logic-rhetoric and the Trivium, with explicit attention to language study (Latin begins in second grade) and the Quadrivium in later study. Pedagogical practices suggested by the site align with some classical elements (e.g., chanting/memory and/or Socratic-style engagement are not explicitly described on the site, but the grammar-logic-rhetoric structure and Latin emphasis point toward classical methods). The faculty material suggests teachers function as living exemplars and mentors, and parents are positioned as partners in a Christian formation process. Taken together, the school ranks as CLASSICAL overall, with some category-level caveats where explicit Great Books, explicit Socratic pedagogy, or a more detailed, explicit Great-Conversation orientation are not clearly documented on the site.

Key drivers for CLASSICAL classification:

- Religious/metaphysical foundations are explicit (discipleship, evangelical identity, liturgical practices, weekly chapel, prayer and Scripture integrated into classes, theology-informed worldview)
- Purpose/goal centers formation, virtue, wisdom, and God-centered aims (Glorifying God through Wisdom and Virtue; formation over mere information; tools of learning to become independent learners)
- Curriculum foregrounds Trivium/Quadrivium and language study (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; Latin beginning in Grade 2)
- Pedagogy mirrors classical structures in naming the Trivium and the goal of forming thinkers, with a clear emphasis on classical aims (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric; some aspects of memory/recitation implied by classical approach, though not extensively documented on site)
- Faculty portrayals evoke mentors living the curriculum and modeling virtue; staff are described as aligned with Christian faith and content knowledge, bridging content and biblical belief
- Parents and community are positioned as partners in formation; open invitation and active involvement are indicated (parents as co-teachers in the classical formation project)

The presence of a classical framework without explicit documented Socratic methods lowers the certainty to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Why this rating

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly document and publicize the Great Books/Great Conversation framework as part of the curriculum, including representative canonical texts, a stated plan for Great Books reading lists, and explicit connections to the Great Conversation within the Western canon. This would strengthen the category from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
    - Concrete steps: publish a formal “Curriculum Overview” that names Great Books or core primary sources, a cross-curricular plan, and a stated progression from Grammar to Logic to Rhetoric with explicit historical/Western-canon anchors. Cite canonical texts (e.g., Homer, Plato, Augustine, Shakespeare) and show how Great Conversation informs along with the Trivium/Quadrivium.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Make explicit the use of Socratic dialogue and scholé-based practices in upper grades, plus a documented memory work/chanting component in lower grades, to align more fully with classical pedagogy signals.
    - Concrete steps: publish a pedagogical framework page that describes how Socratic seminars, structured dialogues, and memory/recitation activities are implemented across grades, with sample lesson sequences.
- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) / Element 1 (Religious Foundations): Continue to foreground explicit discussion of “truth, goodness, beauty” as real transcendentals in daily academic work and in chapel/classroom integration; connect these transcendentals to specific course objectives and assessment rubrics to reinforce the formation narrative.
    - Concrete steps: publish a reflective statement or course-embedding document tying truth/goodness/beauty to specific learning outcomes and assessment criteria across disciplines.
