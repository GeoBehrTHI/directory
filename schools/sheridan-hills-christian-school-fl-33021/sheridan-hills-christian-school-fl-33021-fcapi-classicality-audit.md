## Executive Summary

Sheridan Hills Christian School (SHCS) in Florida presents a coherent classical Christian education model with strong religious foundations, a Trivium-centered curriculum, and active parental and community engagement. Public-facing materials consistently frame education as formation in virtue, wisdom, and faith, anchored by a Christ-centered worldview, weekly chapels, and mission work. The school explicitly promotes the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), history taught through a Biblical lens, liberal arts emphases, and classical pedagogy, including elements such as memory work, recitation, and opportunities for rhetoric and public speaking. Faculty are portrayed as experienced, purpose-driven educators, and there is clear institutional partnership with a church community and a robust parental/community role through missions, events, and open engagement. Taken together, the evidence supports a CLASSICAL classification across all six categories of the classical-school rubric.

Key evidentiary threads include: a formal claim of being “A Classical, Christian Education” with Christ-centered, biblically-based framing; explicit references to the Trivium and stages of grammar/logic/rhetoric; history taught through Biblical lens; Great Books/Great Conversation framing in philosophy of classical education materials; commingling of faith, worship (chapel), and formation (virtue, wisdom); and sustained mission and family-partnership language (Gospel emphasis, House System, parent involvement). Public materials also highlight a strong faculty emphasis and community partnerships with SHCS’s church network and mission initiatives. Citations reflect the primary SHCS materials and the knowledge base lexicon that defines classical indicators.      

Executive highlights by category (category-by-category snapshot below) show SHCS aligning closely with classical education principles, with all six categories rated CLASSICAL. The analysis also identifies explicit textual cues that align with each category’s criteria (Religious foundations, formation-centered goals, Trivium-based curriculum, contemplative/recitation-based pedagogy, mentor-teacher model, and robust parent/community partnership).

- The school emphasizes the Gospel and Kingdom-conscious framing in its mission and programs (Missions page and partnership with the church). 
- Weekly chapels, Bible as a required course in the Upper School, and mission trips embed liturgical, communal, and theological practice into daily life. 
- The public materials explicitly connect education to divine purposes (e.g., a Christ-centered worldview), aligning with liturgical/community practice and theology as foundational elements. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Christ-centered, biblically-based, Biblical worldview, theology-related concepts (via Biblical worldview), gospel, weekly chapels, covenantal, Kingdom Conscious. These reflect the Positive Keywords cluster for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (theology, liturgy/worship, sacred texts, and reverence/wonder aspects appear across the lexicon as signals of classical orientation).  
- Negative keywords present: none observed in public materials (no explicit signaling of relativism, secular humanism, or morally neutral stances in the SHCS materials). The absence of negative indicators supports a Classical alignment in this category.

Why this rating
- The materials present a clearly devotional, theologically integrated framework with liturgical/community practices (chapels, missions, Gospel emphasis) and a worldview formed through Scripture. This strongly aligns with the Positive Indicators for Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and avoids negative indicators, yielding a CLASSICAL rating.

- History is taught through a biblical lens and integrated across disciplines (e.g., “History is taught through a Biblical lens including the study of major Bible stories,” and Lower School materials emphasize literature and history within a classical frame).  
- The school references great books/classics and a classical education tradition; the public materials frame reading primary sources and classical works as part of the curriculum. The public-facing “What is Classical Education?” materials discuss great books and the Trivium, embedding canonical classical content. 
- Specific content highlights include Latin, Saxon Math (in Lower School), and classical-capstone projects; AP and honors options exist in the Upper School, reflecting a rigorous, liberal-arts-informed curriculum that sustains the classical tradition. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The SHCS materials explicitly map to these classical curriculum markers. 
- Negative keywords present: few if any; the SHCS materials emphasize classical content rather than “social studies” terminology replacement, STEM-first framing, or project-based dominance, which would be negative signals per the lexicon. The absence of those explicit negative indicators supports a CLASSICAL reading.

Why this rating
- SHCS demonstrates core curriculum elements of the classical suite: Trivium in structure, biblical-history integration, engagement with great books/classics, and a classical-language/intellectual-historic emphasis. The presence of Latin, classical literature, and historically grounded pedagogy substantiates a CLASSICAL rating.
