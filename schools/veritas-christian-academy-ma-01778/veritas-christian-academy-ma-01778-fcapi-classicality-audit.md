## Executive Summary

Veritas Christian Academy (Wayland, MA, 1778) presents a biblically centered, formation-forward approach to schooling in Wayland, MA, serving Pre-K through 8th grade. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered mission, faith-integrated learning, and a culture of excellence, community, and hands-on learning. Evidence ranges from explicit statements of authentic faith in Christ and faith-informed learning to descriptions of accelerated math, enrichment programs, and student-led demonstrations of learning. These elements strongly align with the religious and communal aims common in classical models, but the school’s public materials do not clearly demonstrate the core liberal-arts foundation, Great Books engagement, or explicit Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy that typically mark a classical school. Taken together, the school shows substantial classical-influenced features (especially in virtue-formation and faith-informed pedagogy) but lacks several hallmark classical practices. Cumulatively, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key signals driving the assessment:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Clear emphasis on authentic faith in Christ, Christ-centered learning, and faith-informed formation. Evidence includes statements such as “authentic faith in Christ” and “Christ-centered Learning.” These components indicate a transcendent moral framework and theology-infused pedagogy, which the classical rubric recognizes as a strong alignment with religious grounding. See evidence of faith-centered framing in school materials.
- Purpose and Goal: The school foregrounds formation through faith and reason, a sense of striving for character and wisdom, and a mission-oriented language around truth, goodness, and beauty. Public materials emphasize thinking deeply and wisely, faith-informed excellence, and character-faith integration. This aligns with the classical aim of formation, even as explicit statements about “Great Books” or the classical telos aren’t evident.
- Curriculum: The site highlights acceleration, enrichment, STEM/Hands-on learning, and history projects but does not show explicit Great Books, Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), or Quadrivium components, nor a pervasive history-throughout-curriculum approach. Absence of explicit Great Books or Trivium rhetoric reduces the degree to which the curriculum mirrors classical content.
- Pedagogy: Pedagogical notes emphasize experiential and hands-on STEM learning, and student-driven enrichment, with limited evidence of Socratic seminars, dialectic inquiry, or formal rhetoric instruction described publicly. Public-facing statements emphasize reflection and faith-informed reasoning, but explicit classical pedagogy (e.g., scholé, Socratic seminars) is not clearly documented for the school.
- Faculty: Public materials describe the school as a Christ-centered community with a focus on character and faith, but explicit language about “living curriculum” or teacher portraits is not evident. This suggests alignment with classical aims in spirit, but not in explicit classical faculty framing.
- Parents & Community: The school demonstrates robust parental involvement and community engagement (e.g., family-friendly events and social-media content showcasing community activities). This aligns with the classical emphasis on partnership with families, even if not explicitly framed as “co-educators” in policy language.

Overall aggregate: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Adopt explicit Great Books and Great Conversation practices (even in a K–8 structure) and foreground the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) in age-appropriate sequences. Publicly articulate and document a Trivium-focused curriculum, including primary texts, classical discussions, and memory-work, to shift Element 3 from NOT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
- Introduce and document formal Socratic seminars or dialectic discussions, aligned with scholé (restful learning and contemplation) in lower/middle grades, and rhetoric-focused capstone opportunities in upper grades. This would elevate Element 4 to CLASSICAL or at least SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Develop and publish a formal “portrait of a teacher” and “living curriculum” framework for faculty, including ongoing professional formation around classical pedagogy, and publicly display a faculty-formation narrative. This would strengthen Element 5 toward CLASSICAL.
- Expand liturgical and communal practices within the school’s public materials (e.g., explicit chapel/mass rhythms, liturgical year events, sacred music) to strengthen Element 1’s liturgical dimension and demonstrate a fuller integration of theology into daily schooling.
- Deepen the integration of history and the Western tradition across disciplines, explicitly incorporating primary sources, canonical texts, and cross-disciplinary connections to support a stronger "Cultural Transmission" signal in Element 3 and Element 6.
- Formalize and document a parent-formation program (book studies, lectures, and classical-language training for parents) to strengthen Element 6 and reinforce partnership with families as co-educators, per the Six Essential Elements framework.
