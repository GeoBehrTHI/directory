## Executive Summary

Westminster Academy Tampa (33609) presents a coherent classical Christian program that foregrounds character formation, virtue, and faith-integrated inquiry. Public materials consistently tie education to truth, goodness, and beauty, with a stated aim of forming wise, virtuous ambassadors who root knowledge in a biblical worldview. The school positions itself as classical in orientation, emphasizes classical literature and primary sources, and describes teaching as a “living curriculum” informed by Christian faith. There is clear evidence of strong parental involvement and active community engagement (Open Houses, SALT Day, and various parent-partnering activities). While the site emphasizes classical methods and virtue-centered aims, it does not always spell out every specific hallmark of a full six-element classical rubric (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and some Socratic pedagogy in every grade). Overall, Westminster Academy Tampa aligns with the Classical/Christian rubric more strongly than not, and the aggregate evaluation is CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: biblical worldview, theology-infused instruction, and a stated aim to form hearts and minds by truth, beauty, and goodness. Positive signals dominate; theological framing is explicit.
- Purpose and goals center on formation of virtue and wisdom, not primarily job training; the school emphasizes formation and a teleology toward truth, goodness, and beauty within a Christian framework.
- Curriculum highlights classical elements: classic literature, historical texts, and primary sources are foregrounded; explicit Great Books language is not always stated on the site, which tempers the "classical" completeness in this category.
- Pedagogy shows elements of classical teaching (memory, rhetoric, and disciplined, contemplative aspects via scholé-like rhythms in places), though explicit Socratic seminars across the upper school are not repeatedly stated in the public materials.
- Faculty portrayal leans into model teachers and mentors, with named faculty and a head of school who frame learning as a faith-informed, virtuous enterprise.
- Parents and community engagement is a strength: documented partner-ship with parents, open houses, and active volunteerism (Chapel, Dad’s Curb Service, etc.) alongside community service (SALT).

- Founding statements emphasize thinking biblically in all areas of life and a biblically grounded worldview.
- Official value statements foreground Biblical Fidelity, Devotional Vitality, and Intellectual Integrity.
- Public materials articulate a biblical, theology-informed approach to education and faith integration (Scripture as the guide for faith and life).

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present in public materials: truth, goodness, beauty; theology; biblical worldview; Scripture; devotional vitality; intellectual integrity. These terms appear across the Welcome/About pages and value statements.
- Negative keywords (from the lexicon) such as relativism, secular humanism, values clarification are not evident in the public materials. The content instead foregrounds a biblically grounded approach.

Why this rating

- The combination of explicit references to truth/beauty/goodness, explicit theology/ Scripture integration, and a stated Christian mission anchors the school clearly in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations in a classical sense. Therefore: CLASSICAL.

Why this rating

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Curriculum emphasis is on classic literature, historical texts, and primary sources as central elements; public materials state: “curriculum includes classic literature, historical texts, and primary sources” and that classical teaching methods are used.
- The site describes a classical approach to thinking deeply, engaging with ideas, and developing analytical skills.
- There is mention of a robust classical framework in core sections (Classical, Christian) and in Fine Arts referencing study of classical artists and plays with memorization and rhetorical skills.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: classical teaching methods, classic literature, historical texts, primary sources, modern emphasis on deep engagement with ideas.
- Notable gaps relative to the strict six-element rubric: explicit reference to Great Books and the Great Conversation is not consistently stated on the site; nor is there a clear, repeated listing of grammar, logic, and rhetoric as canonical trinity in the curriculum within public-facing content. (One can infer rhetoric-related work in Fine Arts, but explicit triad grammar-logic-rhetoric is not repeatedly named in public copy.) This makes the category weaker than a full “Classical” designation based strictly on the rubric’s curriculum bullets. See public content: “classic literature, historical texts, and primary sources” and “classical teaching methods.”

Why this rating

- The school strongly foregrounds classic, liberal-arts content and primary sources, which aligns with classical education. However, the explicit framing around the traditional triad of grammar-logic-rhetoric, and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language, is not consistently documented in the public materials. Therefore, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- No major needle-moving changes are required given the aggregate CLASSICAL designation. The school already demonstrates strong alignment across most categories and robust parental/community integration. If one sought to advance to a fuller, explicit alignment with the strictest classical rubric in Curriculum and Pedagogy, it could consider:
    - Explicitly signaling Great Books/Great Conversation as core components (e.g., naming canonical Great Books texts and describing student participation in Great Conversation-style discourse in appropriate grades). Evidence would include explicit language about Great Books and Great Conversation across grade levels.
    - More explicit articulation of grammar, logic, and rhetoric as the core triad of classical pedagogy in public materials, with examples of graded activities (grammar-school Latin, logic puzzles, rhetoric exercises, or formal declamation) clearly described on the site.
    - Additional depiction of scholé/restful learning days or contemplative-isolated study rhythms to align with scholé language in the classical lexicon.
