## Executive Summary

Stella Maris Academy San Francisco (SMA) presents a clearly Catholic, classical-inspired program for PK–8, with explicit emphasis on a liberal arts curriculum framed by a Catholic worldview. The school markets itself as academically excellent, classically aligned, and catechetically integrated, with a strong emphasis on forming virtue and character in a Catholic-context environment. Public materials highlight a classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integration of faith and reason, and a parental-partnered approach to education.

Overall rating: CLASSICAL

- The religious/metaphysical foundations are robust, anchored in Catholicism and liturgical life, with explicit language about prayer, sacraments, and a Catholic integrated education model. This aligns strongly with the transcendental moral realism and communal practices elements of classical education. Evidence includes references to integrated Catholic education, morning prayer, and mission language centering on truth, goodness, and beauty.
- The school purpose and goal emphasize formation, virtue, and wisdom over vocational preparation, with explicit statements about forming minds and hearts, seeking truth, goodness, and beauty. This is a strong match for the classical formation objective.
- The curriculum is explicitly described as a classical curriculum with grammar, logic, and rhetoric, and it situates learning within a Catholic, integrated framework. There is clear articulation of these liberal-arts phases and related pedagogy, including language studies (Latin) and arts/music integration. This supports a CLASSICAL designation for Curriculum.
- Pedagogy shows a staged approach (grammar–logic–rhetoric) and a blend of contemplative elements with active, seminar-like elements as students advance, aligning with classical pedagogy expectations for a PK–8 program. While explicit Socratic seminars are not described for middle grades in public materials, the structure remains consistent with classical pedagogy appropriate for the school’s grade span.
- Faculty is portrayed as mentors and living curriculum, with references to clergy and faculty alignment with mission, and to a collaborative, virtuous-teacher ideal. This supports a CLASSICAL reading of the Faculty category.
- Parents & Community are positioned as partners, with enrollment processes framed as a partnership with parents, and public-facing invitations to visits and information sessions. This aligns with the classical model of parent partnership and a welcoming community.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The pedagogy rests on a triadic progression (Grammar → Logic → Rhetoric) consistent with classical pedagogy, with public materials describing this progression and how students move through stages.
- Lower grades emphasize a contemplative and wonder-filled approach (e.g., fostering wonder, moral imagination) and integration of memory-friendly formats (singing, review). The curriculum mentions arts/music and memorization strategies in early years.
- Public materials describe upper-level pedagogy as engaging in discussions and seminars aligned with rhetoric development (the classical trajectory). While explicit “Socratic” language is not repeatedly stated for SMA’s 9–12 (since SMA is PK–8), the high-level classical pedagogy is preserved through grammar/logic/rhetoric emphasis and opportunities for rhetorical expression.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords observed: contemplative, wonder, memory, recitation/chanting, singing, circle/school assemblies, logic, rhetoric, seminars, discussion, moral imagination.
- Negative keywords observed: None clearly present in public materials; there is no emphasis on non-classical modes like purely project-based or test-driven rote drilling without context.

Why this rating

- The curriculum’s explicit grammar/logic/rhetoric progression and emphasis on wonder and formation align with the classical pedagogy model. Although specific high-school Socratic seminars aren’t applicable (the school is PK–8), the pedagogy remains true to classical methods appropriate for SMA’s grade span, qualifying as CLASSICAL.

Citations: Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric progression; lower-level wonder and memory emphasis; upper-grade rhetoric/seminar-like opportunities described in curriculum/cultural pages.
