# ABSTRACT

Trinity Knox Classical Academy (TKCA) demonstrates solid Classical Christian alignment, particularly in its religious and metaphysical foundations (Christ-centered, biblical worldview) and its curriculum (Trivium framing, liberal-arts orientation, Great Ideas). The school explicitly presents formation-centered goals, including the shaping of character and virtue, and frames education as a formation of souls guided by biblical truth and Western intellectual heritage. Public materials emphasize a Christian classical mission and a goal of forming virtuous, thoughtful students who engage with truth, goodness, and beauty.

- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): CLASSICAL
    - Evidence includes Christ-centered language, biblical worldview, theology integration, and formation of souls. Positive keywords include truth, beauty, virtue, theology, and formation.
- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): CLASSICAL
    - Evidence includes formation-focused aims and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty as central.
- Element 3 (Curriculum): CLASSICAL
    - Evidence includes liberal-arts framing and Trivium reference; Great Ideas/Great Conversation framing is present.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Evidence shows classical foundations but lacks explicit, public articulation of Socratic seminars, scholé, or a fully documented pedagogy plan.
- Element 5 (Faculty): NOT CLASSICAL
    - Public materials do not clearly present the “living curriculum” faculty narrative or a robust teacher-formation framework.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Evidence suggests community and parental involvement potential, but explicit parent-partnership language and formal parent-education programs are not clearly documented in public content.

Notes on the evidence base: The evaluation integrates TKCA’s public-facing site content with the classical education rubric from the knowledge base. The school’s stated mission and curriculum strongly align with classical Christian education (Trivium, liberal-arts, Great Ideas, formation). The explicit pedagogical methods, faculty-curriculum integration, and formal parent-partnership language are less clearly documented in the public materials, which shapes the overall classification toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
