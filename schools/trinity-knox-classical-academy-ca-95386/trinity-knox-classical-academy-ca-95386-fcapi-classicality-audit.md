## Executive Summary

Trinity Knox Classical Academy (TKCA) demonstrates a clear classical-Catholic-Christian orientation in its public materials, with a strong emphasis on Christian formation, biblical worldview, and a liberal-arts framework. The school explicitly positions itself as Christ-centered and classical, articulating aims around forming souls, virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The curriculum references the liberal-arts foundation (including the Trivium) and stresses a long-standing classical vision of education, including engagement with Western intellectual heritage and Great Ideas. However, while TKCA presents many elements aligned with classical education (especially in religious foundations and curriculum), explicit pedagogy and faculty-community dynamics characteristic of a fully developed classical model (e.g., dedicated Socratic/seminar-style pedagogy, a living-faculty-as-curriculum approach, and robust parent-partnership structures) are less clearly documented on the public site. Given the evidence, TKCA sits toward the Classical end of the spectrum but exhibits notable gaps in explicit articulation of all six classical elements in public-facing materials.

- Strong classical and religious foundations are evidenced by Christ-centered language, biblical worldview integration, and explicit formation goals (Element 1: CLASSICAL; Element 2: CLASSICAL).
- The curriculum aligns with liberal-arts tradition (Trivium and Great Ideas), with specific reference to the Trivium and a classic curriculum framing (Element 3: CLASSICAL).
- Pedagogy and upper-school practices are less explicitly documented, yielding a more mixed (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) signal (Element 4: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL).
- Faculty engagement and explicit “living curriculum” framing are not robustly evident in public-facing content (Element 5: NOT CLASSICAL, given available text).
- Parent/Community partnerships are suggested more implicitly (e.g., campus openness and community links), but explicit co-teaching or formal parent-formation language is not clearly documented (Element 6: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL).

Overall classification and synthesis

- Aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Rationale: TKCA demonstrates strong alignment with classical education in its religious foundations (Element 1), formation-oriented purpose (Element 2), and curriculum (Element 3). Pedagogy (Element 4) and Faculty (Element 5) show some classical signals but lack explicit public articulation of core classical practices. Parent/community (Element 6) shows potential alignment but isn’t fully codified in public content. Taken together, TKCA sits toward the classical end but does not fully meet every criterion in a way that would classify it as CLASSICAL across all six categories.

What Would Move the Needle

- Pedagogy (Element 4)
    - Publicly articulate and implement explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades, including structured inquiry, dialectic discussions, and opportunities for student-led rhetoric and debates.
    - Highlight scholé/restful-learning components and contemplative practices (e.g., Scholé days) as part of the lower and middle school pedagogy.
- Faculty (Element 5)
    - Publish a clear “living-curriculum” narrative for faculty (portrait of a teacher, teacher-mentors as core to the school, and professional development in classical pedagogy).
    - Include statements about teacher formation, classical pedagogy training, and community of practice among faculty.
- Parents & Community (Element 6)
    - Explicitly describe a formal partnership model with parents (parents as co-teachers) and offer structured parent-formation opportunities (book studies, seminars, and voluntary involvement).
    - Publicly detail a campus-visitor/open-house policy and ongoing opportunities for parent education about classical education.
