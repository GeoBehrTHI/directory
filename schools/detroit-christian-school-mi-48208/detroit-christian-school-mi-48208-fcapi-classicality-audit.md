## Executive Summary

Detroit Christian School (DCS) in Detroit, MI presents a robust Christian classical model. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered, classical education anchored in Biblical truth, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), memorization and recitation, the study of great books and Western literature, and a strong partnership with families in formation and character development. The school signals a clear teleology of forming virtuous, thoughtful graduates who glorify God, understand Scripture, and engage the Western intellectual tradition. Overall, the school exhibits strong alignment with the classical framework across most categories, with faculty and curricular practices closely tethered to classical methods and a community ethos that positions parents as essential partners. This evaluation uses the knowledge-base rubric for classical education alongside DCS’s own public materials to score each category and provide keyword-based justification.

Overall rating: CLASSICAL

- Key drivers: explicit Christ-centered and classical identity; Trivium framing; emphasis on virtue and character; engagement with Great Books/Western tradition; emphasis on authority of Scripture and doctrinal/theological formation; deep parental partnership.
- Potential nuance: explicit, high-visibility articulation of faculty living-lesson/“living curriculum” language is present in spirit, but some of the finer faculty-specific descriptors are less directly asserted in public copy. The school’s emphasis on parental partnership and liturgical/theological commitments is strong and stabilizes the overall classical orientation.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 5 (Faculty): Increase explicit public articulation of faculty as the “living curriculum” and provide a formal portrait of the ideal teacher and student in public-facing materials. Include explicit language about faculty professional formation in classical pedagogy, collaborative faculty work, mentoring, and professional development aligned with classical pedagogy frameworks.
- For Element 2 (Purpose/Goal) and Element 3 (Curriculum): Consider clearer articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation as a formal, named curricular element (e.g., a published Great Books sequence, required canonical readings list, and a stated Great Conversation goal) to reinforce the explicit Great Books element in the curriculum and to strengthen the language of formation toward “true, good, beautiful” through canonical works.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Maintain and expand parent formation offerings, and publish examples of specific family-liturgy resources or household-formation practices to deepen public perception of parents as co-educators with a clearly defined program.
