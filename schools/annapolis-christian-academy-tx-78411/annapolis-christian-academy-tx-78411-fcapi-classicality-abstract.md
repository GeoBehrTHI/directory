## ABSTRACT

This report evaluates **Annapolis Christian Academy (Corpus Christi, TX)** using the **Six Essential Elements of a Classical School** keyword framework and the associated classicality lexicon.   The school’s website crawl reveals a consistently classical and Christian orientation.

Across **Religious and Metaphysical Foundations**, the school explicitly embraces **classical Christian education**, emphasizing **spiritual formation, character formation, and godliness**, with language about students being formed “to God” through the “spirit of the school” and “domestic godliness.”  This aligns with transcendental moral realism and theological grounding.

In **School Purpose and Goal**, the stated aims focus on **knowledge, understanding, and wisdom**, and on the holistic formation of students rather than on narrow college or career metrics.  This matches the classical emphasis on the cultivation of **wisdom and virtue** over mere information transfer or job training. 

The **Curriculum** is explicitly organized around the **Trivium**—Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric—with corresponding school divisions and **senior thesis presentations** in the School of Rhetoric, indicating a robust rhetoric program and likely engagement with Great Books and primary texts.  No evidence suggests a social-studies- or STEM-first, skills-only curriculum.

In **Pedagogy**, the Trivium staging, logic and rhetoric divisions, and formal speech/oratory structures (including a detailed speech meet rubric) demonstrate classical teaching methods that culminate in rhetorical excellence.   While explicit terms like “Socratic seminar” and “scholé” are not visible in the snippets, there is also no sign of project-based or constructivist paradigms dominating instruction.

Regarding **Faculty**, the head-of-school letter emphasizes that “more is caught than taught” and that the spiritual atmosphere and “domestic godliness” of the school community form students, implying that teachers and staff are understood as **models of virtue and the living curriculum**.  This aligns with classical expectations for faculty as mentors and exemplars.

Finally, **Parents and Community** are clearly engaged through numerous events (gala, service projects, Parents Night Out, giving campaigns) and through direct theological communication about the nature of education, indicating a **shared mission and active parental involvement** rather than a consumer model. 

Across all six categories, positive classical keywords and concepts are frequent, while negative indicators are absent. The aggregate classification for Annapolis Christian Academy is therefore **CLASSICAL**.
