## Executive Summary

El Paso Classical Academy (El Paso, TX 79907) publicly presents as a classical, liberal-arts oriented school, emphasizing virtue, truth, beauty, and a traditional curriculum. The site foregrounds a classical approach, with explicit references to the liberal arts, Great Books, and a partnership with families. However, the publicly available materials do not consistently demonstrate the full range of classical practices across all six canonical categories (notably explicit liturgical/theological foundations and some pedagogy/faculty articulations). Based on the six-category rubric drawn from the classical-school lexicon and the Six Essential Elements framework, the school demonstrates solid classical alignment in several domains but is not uniformly exemplary across all six. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:

- There is a strong articulation of classical aims (truth, goodness, beauty; virtue formation; liberal arts orientation) and explicit mention of Great Books, Greek/Latin, and a joint public/charter mission. This supports a Classical orientation in multiple categories. Evidence includes explicit statements about a classical education, liberal-arts curriculum, and Great Books (as part of the school’s commitments) on the public site and in site materials.
- The site lacks explicit, sustained evidence of liturgical/theological foundations or a clearly described, ongoing “living curriculum” faculty culture in the public-facing materials, which are hallmarks of a fully Classical program per the Six Essential Elements lexicon. This lowers the score in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and Faculty categories.
- The school does present a structured approach to pedagogy (a threefold instructional method) and a strong parent/community partnership, which support a Classical orientation in Pedagogy and Parents & Community, respectively, though some pedagogical details (Socratic/seminar practices, Scholé rhythms) are not consistently documented across all public materials.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
    - The program emphasizes orientation toward truth, beauty, and goodness and virtue as core ends of education. This aligns with transcendental moral realism and virtue-centric aims. For example, the program states: “a traditional liberal arts and sciences curriculum and whole-group teaching methodology with an orientation towards truth, beauty, and goodness,” and the end goal of forming virtuous citizens.
    - The site repeatedly references virtue and moral formation as outcomes of education.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: truth, beauty, goodness, virtue, moral formation.
    - Negative keywords: not evident in public materials (e.g., relativism, secular humanism) in this category’s context.

- Why this rating
    - The school foregrounds transcendental ideals (truth, beauty, goodness) and virtue formation, which maps to Transcendental Moral Realism and Virtue-focused aims. However, there is no explicit documentation of theology, liturgical practices, or other theological grounding in the public materials, which prevents a full alignment with Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 1.

- Keywords anchor
    - Core terms observed: truth, beauty, goodness, virtue, formation, moral imagination. See evidence above.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
    - The public materials articulate formation of virtue and human flourishing as the ultimate aim, not merely job-readiness. The Welcome/mission passages frame education as forming virtuous citizens who flourish in a free society.
    - The materials emphasize formation over information, and the ability to know truth, goodness, and beauty as real; the language centers on virtue, wisdom, and human excellence in several listings (e.g., “pursues knowledge, promotes virtue” and “formed responsible citizens and virtuous people”).
    - Greek/Latin and Great Books commitments are highlighted in the school’s related materials, reinforcing a knowledge-rich, classical formation focus.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, human flourishing, truth, goodness, beauty, classical, liberal education.
    - Negative keywords: none evident in public materials for this category (no overt emphasis on work-readiness or purely utilitarian aims).

- Why this rating
    - The school explicitly centers virtue formation, human flourishing, and recognition of truth/goodness/beauty as real—core aims of classical paideia. Given the explicit articulation of formation-centered aims and a classical mission, Element 2 earns CLASSICAL.

- Keywords anchor
    - Notable terms: formation, virtue, wisdom, human flourishing, truth, goodness, beauty, classical education.

### Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL

    - The school’s materials reference Greek and Latin as foundational for training in grammar and critical thinking, and highlight Great Books as a curricular element. The 12 Commitments document explicitly mentions “Greek and Latin” and “Great Books” as curricular anchors.
    - The presence of Great Books/Great Conversation and the emphasis on cultural literacy (Western tradition) are highlighted in the materials.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: liberal arts, grammar, logic, rhetoric (three liberal-arts triad implied by “classical approach” and the associated public materials), Great Books, Greek, Latin, Great Conversation, Western tradition, cultural literacy.
    - Negative keywords: none detected that would undermine curriculum classicism (no strong pivot to social studies-as-education or project-based-only frameworks in public materials).

- Why this rating
    - The explicit identification of liberal-arts curriculum and foundational classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books; Greek/Latin) supports CLASSICAL under this category. The explicit mention of Great Books and a Western-civilizational curriculum aligns with the Great Conversation criterion. Therefore, Element 3 is CLASSICAL.

- Keywords anchor
    - Core curricular terms: liberal arts, grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Great Conversation; Greek/Latin; Western tradition; cultural literacy.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
    - The site emphasizes a classical threefold approach to pedagogy via “an instructional method” that recognizes a hierarchical progression of learning: systematic knowledge, dialectic/logic, and rhetoric. This mirrors the classical pedagogy, particularly in upper-level practice.
    - There is clear mention of a contemplative/restful dimension in classical pedagogy (Scholé) within the broader Classical Education lexicon, and the Schole-focused materials (The Schole Way, Scholé-related texts) are present in the knowledge store; however, explicit evidence of Scholé-based practice on the El Paso site is not consistently documented in public pages. This reduces full alignment with the Scholé/memory-mastery elements in the Pedagogy category.
    - There is evidence of a public, collaborative, and teacher-led structure (Headmaster, Upper School teachers) but without a consistently described program of Socratic seminars, formal memory work, or direct recitation/chanting on site materials. The public site emphasizes classical aims and a threefold method rather than a full menu of classical pedagogy modes.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue/seminar (mentioned in the knowledge-lexicon rather than explicitly on El Paso’s public pages), dialectic, rhetoric, mastery through repetition, and the threefold method. The explicit “threefold method” (systematic knowledge, dialectic/logic, rhetoric) is present on the Our Approach page.
    - However, explicit evidence of lower-school singing/chanting, memory work, or Scholé-infused routines is not consistently documented in the public pages. This reduces the scale of alignment for Pedagogy.

- Why this rating
    - The presence of a threefold classical pedagogy confirms significant Classical alignment, but the absence of consistently documented Socratic seminars, memory-chanting, or Scholé rhythms in the public-facing materials reduces the Pedagogy score to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Keywords anchor
    - Key terms tied to pedagogy: dialectic, logic, rhetoric, threefold pedagogy, memory/recitation (Scholé-related) not consistently evidenced on the site.

### Element 5: Faculty — NOT CLASSICAL

- Evidence

The “faculty as living curriculum” and “portrait of a teacher” concept are not clearly evidenced in the public pages.
    - The public pages show individual staff listings (headmaster and teachers) but do not describe ongoing faculty formation, collaborative pedagogy culture, or a portrait of teacher ideals that align with classical living-curriculum concepts.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present in the lexicon include: "portrait of a teacher," "teachers as models of virtue," "collegial," "living curriculum." These are not explicitly found in El Paso Classical Academy public pages; thus, negative signals predominate because the site lacks explicit alignment with these terms in the public-facing copy. The lexicon shows these as standard Positive Keywords in Element 5; the absence in the site yields a negative signal for this category.
    - Negative keywords (from the lexicon) such as “teacher as facilitator” or “subject-area silos” are not evidenced as the school’s stated model in public content.

- Why this rating
    - Given the absence of explicit evidence that the faculty function as the living curriculum, mentors, or exemplars in the public-facing materials, Element 5 is NOT CLASSICAL under the rubric.

- Keywords anchor
    - Observed public-site content does not demonstrate a teacher-portrait, living-curriculum approach, or faculty-mentorship culture as described in the classical lexicon.

The “12 Commitments” include a line that explicitly frames education as a partnership between parents and professional educators.
    - The school signals openness to parent involvement (e.g., collaborative announcements, enrollment materials, and events calendar), and the About/Calendar materials suggest active family engagement and campus accessibility for parent involvement.

These map directly to the Lexicon’s “Partnership with Families” and “Formation of Affections” and “Cultural Transmission” sub-areas.
    - Negative keywords: none evident in public materials for this category; the school actively promotes parent involvement rather than consumer-driven parent roles.

- Why this rating
    - The school demonstrates strong parent/community partnership and parent engagement, aligning well with Classical principles in Element 6. The evidence in the public materials supports CLASSICAL for Parents & Community.

- Keywords anchor
    - Core terms: partnership with families, parent formation, home-school collaboration, parental engagement, school as co-educator, open campus, parental leadership.

What would move the needle

- Strengthen explicit liturgical/theological foundations and communal practices documentation:
    - Add clear public-facing language showing theological grounding or liturgical/community rhythms (even as a charter school, many classical programs highlight liturgical or communal practices). This would move Element 1 toward CLASSICAL and align with the “Liturgical and Communal Practices” and “Grounded in Theology” indicators.
- Deepen explicit pedagogy with Socratic/seminar culture and Scholé rhythms:

- Elevate the faculty narrative as a living curriculum:
    - Publicly articulate a “portrait of a teacher,” faculty mentorship, and a living-curriculum approach with visible teacher modeling and collaborative professional development. This would move Element 5 toward CLASSICAL.
- Expand explicit Great-Books/Great Conversation commitments in site copy:
    - Fortify explicit, public mentions of Great Books, Great Conversation, and canonical Western texts in daily coursework, so Element 3 shows more robust evidence of Great Books and a unified Great Conversation across grades.
- Continue and expand parent-education initiatives:
    - Publicly document parent-book clubs, faculty-led parent seminars, and structured pathways for parents to participate as co-educators, reinforcing Element 6 further.
