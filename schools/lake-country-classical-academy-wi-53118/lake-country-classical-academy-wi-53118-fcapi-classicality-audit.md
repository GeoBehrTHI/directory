## Executive Summary

Lake Country Classical Academy (LCCA) presents a clear orientation toward classical education, with manifest strengths in its mission, curriculum framing, and parent engagement. Public materials emphasize pursuit of truth, the love of beauty, and the formation of virtue, notably in statements about fostering moral character and civic virtue, and in a mission that foregrounds the flourishing of each student in heart and mind. The school identifies itself as Hillsdale College–aligned, describing Hillsdale as a partner that provides access to a classical curriculum and training, which signals alignment with a recognized classical framework and resources. There is also concrete evidence of robust parental involvement (e.g., parent book clubs and testimonials) and a commitment to civics and character formation. However, explicit, visible articulation of several core classical criteria—such as a named Great Books program and explicit use of Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy, structured Socratic discourse in upper levels, and a formal “living curriculum” portrait of the teacher—are less clearly demonstrated in the public-facing materials. Taken together, these factors place the school firmly in the realm of CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with room to strengthen explicit articulation of several hallmark practices.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What would move the needle:
- Make explicit on the website (Curriculum and Pedagogy pages) the presence of Great Books/Great Conversation, and any formal Trivium/Quadrivium elements in the lower and upper grades.
- Elevate and publish explicit statements about Socratic dialogue, seminars, and other classical discussion formats (Paideia/Harkness-style practice) in the upper grades.
- Feature a visible, written “Portrait of a Teacher” and a concise description of the living curriculum in action (how teachers embody and transmit the curriculum daily).
- Expand liturgical or communal practices (even if nonsectarian, clearly articulate any liturgical rhythm, communal rituals, or shared cultural practices that align with the classical tradition).
- Provide a clear, publicly accessible articulation of a pedagogy progression from K–8 to 9–12 that moves from contemplative/recollected learning (Scholé/restful days) to more rigorous rhetoric and public speaking, with concrete examples (speeches, debates, oratory opportunities).
- Strengthen parent education offerings (e.g., more formal parent formation opportunities tied to classical education principles) and widen open-campus opportunities.

ABSTRACT
Lake Country Classical Academy demonstrates a strong classical orientation anchored in truth, goodness, and beauty, with formation-focused goals and a Hillsdale-aligned curriculum. Public materials reveal clear commitments to character formation, civic virtue, and the flourishing of each student, along with active parent engagement. Core classical hallmarks such as Great Books, structured Socratic discourse, or explicit Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy are less visible in public content, placing the school in a SOMEWHT CLASSICAL stance overall. Strengthening explicit articulation and implementation of Great Books/Great Conversation, Socratic pedagogy, and a visible living-curriculum portrait would push the school toward a CLASSICAL rating across more categories.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) were to shift from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL, LCCA could explicitly publish and publicly document: 
  - A named Great Books program and a documented Great Conversation approach, with a clear year-by-year list of canonical texts and discussion formats.
  - A public articulation of Trivium/Quadrivium elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as part of the K–12 curriculum map.
  - Clear statements about the use of Socratic dialogue or Harkness-style seminars in upper grades, with examples of weekly or monthly cycles of seminars and student-led discourse.
  - Explicit “Scholé” days or contemplative learning cycles in Lower/Upper school and a public description of restful learning as a pedagogy component.
  - A publicly accessible “Portrait of a Teacher” and description of the living curriculum: how faculty embody and transmit the curriculum, professional development in classical pedagogy, and a framework of teacher collaboration/mentoring.
- Element 5 (Faculty) could be strengthened by publishing a formal faculty-portrait page that describes the ideal teacher archetype in the school’s Classical tradition and by detailing professional development in classical pedagogy.
- Element 6 already shows strong classical alignment; sustaining and expanding parent formation opportunities (e.g., regular book clubs, lectures, and family liturgical-type rhythms) would further consolidate CLASSICAL alignment.
