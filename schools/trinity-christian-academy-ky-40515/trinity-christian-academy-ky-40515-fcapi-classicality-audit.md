## Executive Summary

Trinity Christian Academy (KY 40515) presents a discernible classical Christian orientation in public-facing materials, foregrounding Christ-centered education, virtue formation, and a caregiver-partnered community ethos. The school markets itself as Central Kentucky’s “premier classical Christian private day school,” and its admissions/communications emphasize partnership with families and engagement with church life (including a stated ministry connection to a Presbyterian church). These signals align with elements of the classical model, particularly in its stated aim of forming character and fostering a virtuous, Well-ordered education within a Christian framework. However, while the public materials clearly advertise classical language and frameworks (e.g., “classical Christian private day school”), explicit curricular components that are hallmarks of a full classical curriculum—such as a stated seven liberal arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric; the Trivium; the Quadrivium), explicit Great Books/Great Conversation pedagogy, and explicit Socratic pedagogy across grades—are not consistently spelled out in the school’s publicly accessible content. On balance, the school exhibits a classical Christian orientation and robust parent/community alignment, but with gaps in explicitly documenting certain core classical curriculum and pedagogy tenets across all levels.

Overall rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Executive observations and key takeaways:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are present, but strongly framed through a Christ-centered, Christian-school identity, with explicit church-ministry alignment. This signals a robust religious-metaphysical orientation, but the materials do not consistently foreground liturgical practices or explicit theological program beyond the Christ-centered framing. Evidence includes explicit Christ-centered language and a church-affiliation note.  

- School Purpose and Goal align with classical-pedagogical aims (formation, virtue, wisdom), anchored in virtue and character formation within a Christian framework. The capital campaign language emphasizes character development and education that forms the person, not merely information. 

- Curriculum signals a classical orientation (public materials describe a classical Christian school), but there is limited explicit documentation within the public site content of the canonical Trivium/Quadrivium structure or Great Books enrollment. The absence of explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language in the visible public content is a notable gap for a fully classical taxonomy. See evidence under Element 3. 

- Pedagogy is not uniformly documented in classical terms (e.g., Socratic seminars, memory work, scholé days, or extensive upper-school rhetoric programs are not consistently described in the public-facing pages). The lack of explicit Socratic pedagogy as described in the classical rubric contributes to the overall classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL. 

- Faculty presence appears, with named faculty pages and visible teacher profiles and signs of a mentorship/cultivation culture; however, explicit language about “living curriculum” or classroom practices aligned with classical pedagogy is not consistently documented in the public materials. This again supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 5. 

- Parents & Community are foregrounded as partners, with explicit emphasis on family involvement, parent formation, and open-campus/visits. This aligns with the classical element of a strong partnership with families and a shared mission.

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The public materials emphasize a Christ-centered education and classical Christian identity, signaling a religious-metaphysical orientation. For example, Capital Campaign language explicitly frames the school as a “Christ-centered” institution and a “premier classical Christian private day school.” This reflects transcendental aims and a theological framing of education. 
- A church-affiliation note appears in the employment materials, indicating the school operates within a faith-community context (ministry of a Presbyterian church). This supports a theological grounding in practice. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators from the Six Essential Elements lexicon include truth, goodness, beauty; theology; liturgy; worship; chapel; worship-related culture; and sacred tradition. The visible public content emphasizes Christ-centered and classical Christian identity; however, direct textual instances of terms like theology, liturgy, or worship in the exact keyword lists are not consistently foregrounded in the public-facing pages. This yields a partial positive alignment but not a full, explicit liturgical-theological program on the site. See classical lexicon guidance for the taxonomy of terms. 

Why this rating
- The religious orientation is undeniable and central to the school’s self-definition (Christ-centered, classical Christian). Yet the absence of explicit liturgical practices, overt theological course structures, and direct usage of certain canonical keywords (e.g., “theology,” “liturgy,” “worship”) in the public-facing materials reduces it from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in this rubric.

What would move the needle
- Add explicit references to liturgical practices (e.g., regular chapel/worship schedule, liturgical calendars) and direct theology integration across core disciplines (with explicit mentions of theology as the mistress science). Include enrollment language for theology-focused courses and a clear doctrinal statement.

Citations
- Christ-centered and classical Christian framing on Capital Campaign page. 
- Ministry connection to a Presbyterian church (employment page).

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

The site emphasizes formation, virtue, and character, but does not provide explicit, category-contained evidence of Socratic dialogue, large-scale Socratic seminars, or upper-school rhetoric training in the public-facing materials. This lack of explicit classical pedagogy documentation is a key determinant for NOT CLASSICAL in this rubric. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The Trinity materials do not consistently showcase these teaching methods; the public-facing pages do not present a robust, documented pedagogy aligned with the classical method, which yields a low positive keyword signal in this category and aligns with NOT CLASSICAL status for Element 4.
- Negative keywords in the document emphasize experiential learning, project-based-only approaches, or student-directed pedagogy that undermines classical methodology. While the Trinity site emphasizes formation and virtue rather than project-based extremes, the lack of explicit classical pedagogy documentation supports the NOT CLASSICAL designation.

Why this rating
- Without explicit evidence of Socratic pedagogy, memory work, or formal rhetoric training as a sustained, documented approach, Element 4 does not meet the rubric for a Classical pedagogy.

Citations
- General pedagogical framing (no explicit classical pedagogy on public pages).

## What Would Move the Needle

- If this school seeks to become CLASSICAL across all six categories, it should explicitly document and publish:
  - Curriculum with explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium alignment, and a clear Great Conversation framework (Element 3).
  - Pedagogy with explicit Socratic seminars, memory work, and Scholé-inspired contemplative days (Element 4).
  - A defined “living curriculum” approach with faculty development programs and explicit teacher-formation language (Element 5).
  - Increased explicit liturgical practices or liturgical-year integration (Element 1).
  - Additional explicit articulation of “truth, goodness, beauty” as core transcendental aims (Element 1) and expansion of theology integration across disciplines (Element 1).

- Strengthen textual alignment with the classical keywords by incorporating explicit language such as “great books,” “Socratic dialogue,” “Scholé days,” and “Trivium/Quadrivium” across curriculum and marketing materials, not only in internal documents.
