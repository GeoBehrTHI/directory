## Executive Summary

Christ Covenant School La 70808 presents a Christ-centered, play-based, gently classical approach that foregrounds spiritual formation, virtue, and a reverence for truth, goodness, and beauty. Public materials emphasize Christ-centered formation, weekly chapel, Scripture memory, and a liturgical rhythm, alongside a curriculum described as “gently classical” with references to liberal arts and great texts. This yields strong alignment with several elements of classical education—most notably Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, and Parents & Community—while certain hallmark classical-methods (explicit Trivium/Quadrivium study, robust Socratic pedagogy, and a fully explicit Great Books program across grades) are less clearly demonstrated in the public-facing materials. Overall, the school falls into SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear directions toward fuller classical coherence through targeted enhancements.

Key takeaways
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations strongly align with transcendental realism, theology, liturgy, worship, and communal prayer, anchored in a Christian worldview. Evidence is explicit in mission statements, chapel practices, and catechesis-oriented activities.  
- Purpose and Goal emphasize formation, virtue, and contemplation in the service of truth, goodness, and beauty, with explicit language about affirming the true, pursuing the good, and enjoying the beautiful. 
- Curriculum signals liberal arts and engagement with great texts, though explicit references to the Trivium/Quadrivium and a full Great Books program are not consistently explicit in the publicly visible materials. Still, the presence of “great texts” and “great books” plus liberal-arts framing keeps Element 3 at a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL level. 
- Pedagogy includes play-based, memory-friendly elements with liturgical rhythm and Scripture memory, but explicit Socratic/seminar methods and high-school rhetoric seminars are not clearly evidenced in the public site. Element 4 is therefore SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Faculty portrayal is less explicit in public materials; while the site describes mentors and spiritual formation, a fully developed “living curriculum” faculty identity is not consistently foregrounded in the same terms used by classical-literature lexicons. Element 5 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Parents & Community are clearly valued as co-educators, with a partnership orientation, family formation, and opportunities for parent involvement, aligning Element 6 with CLASSICAL.

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Mission and framing emphasize Christ-centered formation, theological grounding, and liturgical practices (weekly chapel, Scripture memory, catechesis). 
- Public materials describe worship, prayer, and communal religious life as integral to education, with explicit references to Christ and biblical foundations. 
- Descriptions of school identity include “Christ-centered” and references to God’s word guiding practice and curriculum. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: Christ-centered, Christ-exalting, theology, liturgy, weekly chapel, Scripture memory, catechesis, worship, sacred language, reverence.
- Negative indicators present: none apparent in public materials (no explicit secular-relativist signals noted).

Why this rating
- The school’s publicly stated foundations align strongly with transcendental/moral realism, theology, liturgical life, and communal practices described in the classical rubric. However, the presentation is primarily framed through a Christian, devotional lens rather than a fully explicit philological or liturgical tradition canon. This yields CLASSICAL alignment in spirit, but because the evidence of a broader canonical liturgical tradition and a deeper, non-denominational theological framework is not as extensively spelled out as some classical schools, the rating sits at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than unequivocally CLASSICAL.

- Explicit reference to truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable, and to forming affections aligned with Christian faith.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating
- The school explicitly embraces formation over information and foregrounds the transcendent aims of truth, goodness, and beauty. The emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and the cultivation of human excellence is consistent with classical aims. While the overall context is Christian and devotional, the core orientation toward transcendent purposes justifies a CLASSICAL rating for Element 2.

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials describe liberal arts framing and “great texts/great books,” indicating engagement with canonical works and a humanities-centered approach. 
- The curriculum is described as “Christ-centered, gently classical, and play-based,” with emphasis on history content, nature study, Bible memory, and literature-based learning. 
- The materials signal ongoing engagement with Western civilization through literature and history study (e.g., “great texts,” “great books”). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: liberal arts, great books, great texts, history as a through-line, name-drops of classical pedagogy (though not always by explicit Trivium/Quadrivium terms).
- Negative indicators present: explicit references to grammar/logic/rhetoric (Trivium/Quadrivium) are not consistently highlighted in the public-facing curriculum pages; no strong evidence of a formal, high-school-level Great Books sequence across grades in public materials.
- Net: several strong classical cues (great books, liberal arts, integrated history), but lacking explicit, universal articulation of the Trivium/Quadrivium and a fully developed Great Books program in the visible materials.

Why this rating
- The curriculum shows classical scaffolding and a clear emphasis on canonical texts and liberal arts, but lacks consistently explicit references to the complete classical trifecta (grammar/logic/rhetoric) and a comprehensive Great Books pipeline across all grades. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL for Element 3.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school is described as “play-based” and “gently classical,” with attention to wonder, delight, and memory-based practices (Scripture memory, singing, and formation).  
- Public materials emphasize a contemplative, joyful pace, and a pedagogy that integrates spiritual formation with learning (e.g., Scripture memory, weekly chapel, liturgical rhythm). 
- There is explicit emphasis on the “work of childhood is play” and on wonder as a core driver, with a focus on delight in learning and moral formation. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: play-based, wonder, delight, memory-based learning, catechesis, chapel, scriptural memorization.
- Negative indicators present: explicit Socratic seminars, rigorous rhetoric training, and full upper-school rhetoric/speaking programs are not clearly evidenced in public materials; experiential/project-based instruction is suggested by some classical neighbors but not clearly the dominant method.
- Net: The pedagogy leans toward classical virtues and contemplative learning, but without robust evidence of full Socratic/rigorous rhetoric seminars and grammar-logic-rhetoric sequences in regular practice across the upper grades.

Why this rating
- The materials strongly emphasize a gentle classical approach with memory, singing, and contemplative rhythms, but lack explicit, widespread evidence of the full traditional classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, rhetoric training, and a formal, grade-spanning engagement with the Trivium/Quadrivium) in publicly accessible content. This places Element 4 in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make the Trivium/Quadrivium explicit in public materials: clearly articulate how grammar, logic, and rhetoric are taught (scope, progression, and assessment) across grades, with concrete examples, syllabi, and sample lessons.
- Illuminate the Great Books pathway: present a public-facing, grade-spanning Great Books/Great Conversation sequence, with representative authors, reading lists, and discussion formats to show alignment with “great books.”
- Expand Socratic and rhetoric-focused pedagogy evidence: provide public examples of seminars, debates, or formal rhetoric training in upper grades, including student speeches, rhetorical exercises, and assessment rubrics.
- Strengthen the Living Curriculum language for Faculty: publish explicit statements about teacher as mentors, portrait of a teacher, professional development in classical pedagogy, and faculty collaboration as core to the school’s identity.
- Deepen public documentation of culture of scholarly wonder: maintain robust evidence of contemplative moments, scholé-inspired rhythms, and wonder-driven inquiry in daily practice beyond memory work and liturgical routines.
- Expand explicit integration of Latin or classical languages and a broader D.C.-style historical-philosophical integration, if aligned with the school’s mission, to strengthen the liberal-arts dimension.
