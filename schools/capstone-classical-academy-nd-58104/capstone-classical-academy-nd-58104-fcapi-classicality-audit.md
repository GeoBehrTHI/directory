## Executive Summary

Capstone Classical Academy Nd 58104 exhibits a clearly Christian classical orientation in its public materials, with explicit emphasis on virtue formation, theology-informed growth, and a deliberate emphasis on rightly ordered affections. The site foregrounds character formation through its public articulation of “The Gryphon’s Weapons” (Integrity, Humility, Temperance, Fortitude, Charity, Reverence, and Prudence) and anchors those virtues in Biblical texts. This strongly aligns Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal) with a Classical profile. However, explicit, school-wide statements about the canonical Classical Curriculum elements (e.g., Great Books, Trivium, and Quadrivium) and associated pedagogies (Socratic dialogue, memory work, scholé) are less directly shown on the public site, which places Element 3 (Curriculum) and Categories 4–6 (Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community) in a more tentative, “Somewhat Classical” to “Not Classical” range based on the available materials. The aggregate assessment places Capstone Classical Academy Nd 58104 in the “Somewhat Classical” neighborhood overall, with strong classical-inflected religious formation but incomplete, explicit public articulation of full classical curriculum and pedagogy on the site.

Aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- While Capstone Classical Academy Nd 58104 shows strong religious and virtue-centered foundations consistent with classical aims, the publicly available site content does not uniformly demonstrate explicit canonical curriculum and pedagogy signals (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic dialogue, living curriculum statements about faculty, and explicit parent-partner programs). The balance of evidence suggests a strong classical orientation in religious foundations and formation goals, with partial alignment in curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parents/community.

What Would Move the Needle
- Make explicit the Great Books/Great Conversation stance on the website (e.g., publish a page detailing canonical authors, expected reading lists, and how Great Books shape the curriculum).
- Publicly document the Trivium and Quadrivium components of the curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music) and how the school sequences these arts across grade bands.
- Describe concrete pedagogy: include explicit statements about Socratic discussions, scholé days, memory work, narration, and other classical teaching practices on the site.
- Provide a clear portrait of the faculty as “living curriculum” with stated teacher standards, mentorship roles, and ongoing classical pedagogy training.
- Expand parents & community offerings publicly: outline a formal program for parent formation, book studies, partnerships with families as co-educators, and clear visitation policies to welcome parents to the campus.
- Add evidence of liturgical or communal practices in daily life or school routines (worship/music/communal ceremonies) with explicit ties to the school’s theology and classical formation ethos.
