## Executive Summary

Heritage Preparatory School (Atlanta, GA, 30324) presents a robust classical Christian program focused on formation of character, virtue, and wisdom within a Christ-centered framework. Public materials emphasize a Trivium-based curriculum, the Great Books approach, frequent use of recitation and living books, strong faculty mentorship, and active parental partnership. Across categories, the school demonstrates explicit alignment with core classical criteria—most notably religious/metaphysical foundations, purpose/goal oriented toward virtue and wisdom, curriculum grounded in grammar–logic–rhetoric and Great Books, pedagogy that blends contemplative and Socratic dimensions, a faculty culture described as mentors and living curriculum, and an engaged, co-teaching style with parents and community. Taken together, these elements support a CLASSICAL assessment for Heritage Preparatory School.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Key strengths: explicit Christ-centered formation; Trivium curriculum; integration of Great Books and Socratic discourse; strong emphasis on habits, virtue, and character; active parental partnership.
- Areas that would further strengthen if pursued: deeper articulation of wonder as an explicit experiential component across the full K–12 spectrum; explicit histories and syllabi showing sustained Great Books exposure in upper grades; continued evidence of liturgical practices beyond chapel (e.g., formal liturgical calendar) across all divisions.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were to move toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, concrete improvements could include:
 - Element 1: Provide explicit liturgical calendar communications and more frequent liturgical/ritual references beyond chapel to demonstrate a broader liturgical rhythm in everyday life.
 - Element 2: More explicit articulation of virtues and telos in student outcomes beyond character formation, including demonstrable evidence of universal formation across all grade levels.
 - Element 3: More explicit mention and documentation of Great Books readings across upper grades with direct course mappings and syllabi to show sustained exposure beyond foundational references.
 - Element 4: Expanded opportunities for Socratic seminars and debates in the middle and elementary grades (where feasible) to reinforce the continuum of Socratic pedagogy.
 - Element 5: A formal, published faculty portrait and living-curriculum narrative with example teacher-mentors and demonstrable professional-development pathways that align with classical pedagogy.
 - Element 6: Additional parent/community programming that specifically documents parent-teacher co-curricular projects and home-school integration frameworks.
