## Executive Summary

Smith Preparatory Academy Florida 32752 (Smith Prep) presents a clearly classical, Christian-language model of education rooted in the liberal-arts tradition. Public-facing materials describe a Christian classical framework with Cottage and Traditional programs, emphasis on virtuous formation, and deep integration of faith, pedagogy, and community life. Core elements such as the grammar-stage approach, Common Arts offerings, Latin emphasis, and a stated mission to recover Christian humanism align with classical rubric expectations. The school also foregrounds partnerships with parents and a donor-supported mission to sustain the program through faith-infused resources.

Overall, the school demonstrates strong alignment with Classical education across all six rubric categories. The presence of explicit religious foundations, a purpose oriented toward virtue and wisdom, a curriculum that centers grammar/logic/rhetoric and Great-Conversation style learning through Western-Christian heritage, contemplative and participatory pedagogy, clearly described faculty expectations, and a robust parent/community partnering ethos supports a classification of CLASSICAL. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Given the aggregate CLASSICAL rating across categories, no major needle-moving changes are required. If the school sought to reach a surer “CLASSICAL” designation in every sub-criterion, it could enhance explicit articulation of:
  - A more explicit Great Books/Great Conversation articulation in program pages (beyond Western tradition and Latin) to foreground the Great Conversation as a classroom practice.
  - More explicit references to Socratic dialogue/university-level seminars and demonstrable opportunities for extended Socratic discourse in upper grades (where not already explicit) to strengthen the Pedagogy category further.
  - Clear articulation of a full, graded integration of Latin and logic/rhetoric across the upper grades as well as explicit mention of classical memory work across the curriculum to reinforce Curriculum and Pedagogy signals.

- Concrete examples (potential steps):
  - Add a page section detailing Great Books/Great Conversation commitments and representative book lists.
  - Provide examples of Socratic seminars and student-led debates in upper grades with sample rubrics.
  - Publish a teacher-mentorship portrait and a formal “living curriculum” description with examples of how faculty model virtue and mastery across disciplines.
  - Create a formal parent-formation series (book studies or lectures) that aligns with classical aims and documents participation metrics.
