## Executive Summary

Valor Kyle, a free K-12 classical charter school in Kyle, Texas, presents a solid foundation in classical education but with notable gaps in explicit religious/metaphysical framing and some classical elements that are either implied or only partially evidenced on the public site. Public materials emphasize formation of the whole person, memory-work, liberal-arts orientation, and family partnerships through a robust Parent Service Organization (PSO). They also showcase a curriculum that includes classical touchstones (Latin, history, reading lists, poetry memorization) and a governance/leadership structure that positions teachers and administrators as mentors and stewards of a tradition-focused education. However, explicit references to theology, liturgical practices, and sustained Socratic pedagogy are not consistently evident in the publicly available site content. Taken together across the six classical criteria, Valor Kyle leans toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear strengths in curriculum design and family/community engagement, but lacking in consistently explicit theological/metaphysical framing and fully explicit high-school Socratic/seminar pedagogy.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make religious/metaphysical foundations explicit on public materials:
    - Include a stated theology or Christian formation framework (if aligned with the school’s mission) and describe liturgical or communal practices (even if distinct; e.g., morning prayers, liturgical calendar, or contemplative moments) to align with the Positive Keywords in the Six Essential Elements lexicon.
    - Add a transparent section describing liturgical practices or sacred moments in the student day to demonstrate liturgical rhythm and reverence for truth, goodness, and beauty.

- Strengthen explicit Great-Books/Great Conversation articulation in Curriculum:
    - Publicly certify and narrate a formal Great Books program (or a clearly defined Great Conversation track) across grades, with examples of canonical texts used in K–12 and a plan for progressive engagement with classical authors.
    - Highlight how the Trivium/Quadrivium framework informs the curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) and give examples of how this suite shapes units across subjects.

- Expand explicit Socratic and Scholé pedagogy:
    - Publish a public, school-wide statement describing when and how Socratic dialogue, Harkness-style discussions, or seminars occur (especially in high school), along with teacher training and assessment methods (e.g., evaluating reasoning, rhetoric, and virtuous inquiry).
    - Include descriptions of contemplative/restful learning days (Scholé) in the public-day schedule and how such days balance with rigorous content.

- Deepen “living curriculum” and teacher formation:
    - Present a formal “portrait of a teacher” and “living curriculum” language publicly (as a norm, not just internally) to satisfy the Faculty category’s Positive Keyword expectations.
    - Highlight mentorship programs, teacher collaboration, and faculty development that emphasize virtue, character formation, and lifelong learning.

- Maintain and grow Parent-Public engagement:
    - Sustain and expand opportunities for parent education and outreach (e.g., classical book studies, Great Books discussions, and parent formation events) to strengthen the Parent-Community alignment.

- Concrete next steps (practical):
    - Publish a public, explicit statement of the curriculum’s classical aims (Trivium/Quadrivium alignment; Great Books/Great Conversation; formation ethos).
    - Create a teacher-portrait page that describes the model teacher and mentorship expectations.
    - Provide a public calendar of liturgical/contemplative events (even if modest) to demonstrate Scholé and liturgical rhythm in campus life.

- Targeted metrics to track progress:
    - Percentage of curriculum units clearly mapped to Trivium/Quadrivium topics.
    - Documentation of Great Books/primary-source readings in K–12 units.
    - Public postings describing Socratic seminars and Scholé days (frequency and outcomes).
    - Parent engagement metrics (participation in PSO events, book clubs, and family-education sessions).
