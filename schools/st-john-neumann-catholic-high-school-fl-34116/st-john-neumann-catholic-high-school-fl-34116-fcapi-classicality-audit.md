# Classicality Audit Report

Executive Summary
- School: Barry County Christian School (BCCS)
- Website used: https://bccsk12.com/
- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Why: The crawl shows strong, explicit alignment with classical Christian convictions about theology (e.g., “Theology is the queen of the sciences”), formation in virtue and wisdom, and core curricular markers (Trivium, Great Books, ad fontes, primary sources in history). However, the public materials provided do not yet surface classroom pedagogy signals (e.g., Socratic seminars, chanting/recitation, scholé/restful learning), faculty formation/portrait language, or sustained parent-formation programming. Strengths cluster in Categories 1–3; Categories 4–6 show partial or missing evidence from the crawl. Evidence: “Theology is the queen of the sciences,” “biblical worldview (Paideia),” “cultivates the seven Christian Virtues,” “train student reasoning through the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric),” “Great Books,” “history using original sources,” “ad fontes,” and “home, church and school” partnership language appear on the site’s Classical page and related content surfaced in the crawl . Positive/negative keyword comparisons are anchored to the Six Essential Elements lexicon and lists (e.g., Trivium, Great Books, moral realism, theology, liturgy; and negative signals like “project-based,” “21st-century skills,” “social studies” replacing “history”)    .

What would move the needle
If BCCS wants to present as fully CLASSICAL in public materials:
- Pedagogy: Publish explicit descriptions and examples of classroom methods aligned to classical pedagogy: “Socratic seminar,” “dialectic,” “debate/orations,” “narration,” “copywork/dictation,” “chanting/singing/recitation,” and “scholé/restful learning” (each are named as positive indicators in the Six Elements)  .
- Faculty: Add a “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Portrait of a Graduate,” and describe faculty as mentors and models of virtue; include brief notes on faculty formation in classical pedagogy (book seminars, Great Books colloquia, classical training) .
- Religious practices: If applicable, share liturgical/communal practices (chapel, Morning Prayer, hymnody, sacred music, liturgical calendar) and their role in formation; these are strong classical Christian signals .
- Parents & community: Add parent-formation touchpoints (book groups on classical education; parent seminars; classical philosophy primers), and articulate an open/hospitable campus posture where feasible .
- Curriculum detail: Consider publicly naming Quadrivium elements and any Latin/Greek study to round out the Trivium emphasis already present, and add language about “wonder” and “integration” across disciplines .
