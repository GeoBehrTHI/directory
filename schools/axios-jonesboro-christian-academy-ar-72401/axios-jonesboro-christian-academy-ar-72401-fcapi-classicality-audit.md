## Executive Summary

Axios Jonesboro Christian Academy (Axios JCA) presents a clear orientation toward classical Christian education. Public materials consistently describe Axios as a private Christian school employing a distinctly classical framework, with emphasis on formation, virtue, and the integration of faith with learning. The school foregrounds a Christian metaphysical lens, a formation-oriented purpose, a curriculum that leans toward classical topics (Latin, logic, classical subjects), and pedagogy that blends contemplative elements with Socratic or seminar-style engagement. Evidence also points to robust partnerships with parents and a family-centered community ethos, consistent with classical education norms. While the school shows strong classical signals in several areas, certain elements (e.g., explicit liturgical practices, explicit Great Books / Great Conversation language, and a fully fleshed articulation of all six classical criteria across every strand) are present but not as fully explicit as in some benchmark classical schools. Overall, Axios JCA is CLASSICAL with some opportunities to deepen or surface additional classical indicators in a few categories.

- Most categories show strong classical signals (pedagogy, curriculum signals with Latin/logic, faculty modeling, and a robust parent/community partnership). 
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are evident through explicit Christian framing and values like pursuit of Christ and Non Sibi (Not for Self). 
- A few areas could be strengthened by more explicit articulation of liturgical practices and deeper Great Books/Great Conversation language, which would push the school further toward a fully classical lexicon.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Axios explicitly presents itself as a private Christian school, embedding faith in its identity. 
- Public materials articulate pursuing Christ and a faith-centered formation (e.g., “a heart to pursue Christ”) as part of the student formation framework. 
- The school emphasizes knowledge and goodness as a linked objective and references “Not For Oneself” (Non Sibi), signaling virtue and moral formation.  
- Statements about learning within a Christian framework and the integration of faith with learning appear (e.g., “Christian, Private, Classical,” and a commitment to leading purposeful lives). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: truth, goodness, beauty (in the sense of pursuing true/good/beautiful), virtue, theology, and reverence in the public narrative, plus wonder (lower-school wonder).  
- Negative indicators: few explicit negative signals in the public materials; the core potential issue is whether liturgical/communal practices are clearly described (not strongly evidenced as liturgical in the public materials). No explicit relativism/secular-humanism signals are evident in the public materials. 

Why this rating
- The school’s Christian framing and emphasis on Christ-centered formation, virtue, and the integration of faith with learning align with a religious/metaphysical orientation. However, explicit liturgical structures or a robust, explicit Great-Conversation liturgical culture are not consistently foregrounded in every public-facing text, which places this category toward the high end of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school emphasizes formation and character development as a core aim, including a focus on virtue and a biblical worldview (pursuit of Christ; Non Sibi; faith-integrated formation).  
- Language around shaping leaders and equipping students to influence culture reflects a telos beyond mere job preparation; there is explicit emphasis on character and formation. 
- Public statements tie knowledge and goodness with Christian formation, reinforcing a classical telos toward wisdom and virtue. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: formation, virtue (via Non Sibi), telos, pursuit of Christ, classical mission (via explicit mention of classical framework).  
- Negative indicators: no explicit emphasis on 21st-century workforce preparation or college-and-career readiness as primary goals; rather, formation and virtue are foregrounded. This aligns with the classical emphasis, not a negative signal. 

Why this rating
- Axios JCA’s stated aims center on formation, virtue, wisdom, and shaping human excellence within a Christian/ classical frame, not narrow job-preparation goals. The presence of telos-oriented language and Non Sibi supports CLASSICAL rather than only SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The curriculum signals a classical orientation by naming Latin (begins in 3rd grade) and formal logic (begins in 8th grade) as core elements, plus a focus on “classical subjects.” 
- Abeka is used for core subjects, with instructor-developed curriculum for AP and classical subjects, indicating a classical grounding alongside traditional Christian resources. 
- The public materials emphasize a strong integration of classical topics and a worldview aligned with classical Christian education. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: Latin, logic, classical subjects, classical education model, great books / classical conversation language is referenced in the broader classical framework (explicit mention of classical education and “classical model”). 
- Negative indicators: explicit Great Books / Great Conversation language is not consistently foregrounded in all public texts (the site focuses on classical methods and Latin/logic explicitly, but the “Great Books” language is not strongly foregrounded in every piece). No pervasive project-based-first framing is evident; the materials emphasize a traditional, classical approach rather than a purely project-based mode. 

Why this rating
- The curriculum clearly aligns with classical education markers (grammar/Latin, logic; classical subjects; intentional emphasis on classical elements). However, explicit, repeated surface references to “Great Books” and a sustained Great Conversation framework are not always foregrounded across all public materials, which makes this category SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school speaks to a pedagogy that blends contemplative/restful elements with rigorous inquiry and discussion (Socratic style inquiry is implied via “the power of asking the right questions” and disciplined inquiry). 
- There is emphasis on hands-on, experiential learning in some passages and on engaging students in exploration; lower grades emphasize play-based learning and wonder, and upper grades emphasize discussion and rhetoric training. 
- The public materials explicitly describe a classical approach (e.g., “classical model of education,” Latin, logic, and classical subjects; opportunities for rhetorical training and speech in higher grades). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: Socratic seminar / dialectic / disputation (implied by “how to think by honing the skill of asking good questions”), narration/recitation, chanting/memory work, scholé/restful learning, seminar, rhetoric, and debate. Hands-on learning and inquiry are also present. 
- Negative indicators: there are no explicit signals of project-based learning dominating pedagogy; the emphasis leans more toward classical methods than purely experiential or constructivist approaches. The public materials do not indicate a sole reliance on project-based pedagogy. 

Why this rating
- The Pedagogy category shows robust classical signals (Socratic-style inquiry, rhetoric preparation, memory work, and a balance of contemplation with active discourse). Thus CLASSICAL is justified, with some room to surface additional explicit mentions of specific classical practices (e.g., explicit semester-by-semester Socratic seminars in upper grades) to reach full CLASSICAL across every text.

### Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Faculty are described as model teachers and mentors who form the living curriculum and partner with students; the school emphasizes teachers as a central, virtuous, and skilled core of the school community. 
- The public statements highlight faculty passion, partnership with parents, and a teacher-centered culture of virtue and intellectual leadership. 
- The school references a portrait of a teacher and a living curriculum concept, aligning with classical pedagogy norms. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: model teachers, living curriculum, portrait of a teacher, collegial culture, mentorship, and virtue in teacher formation. 
- Negative indicators: no explicit negative signals about faculty quality; there is no indication of alternative hiring models that diverge from classical mentorship ideals. 

Why this rating
- The materials describe a faculty as a central, virtuous, and nurturing substrate for classical formation, consistent with CLASSICAL categorization.

### Element 6: Parents and Community — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Axios emphasizes partnering with parents and viewing parents as co-teachers, with active involvement in the educational process. 
- There are explicit references to parent formation (web content and community activities) and opportunities for parents to engage (tours, parent information packets, etc.). 
- An open-campus ethos and emphasis on family involvement align with classical education’s communal and familial formation model. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: partnership with parents, co-teachers, parent formation, classical book clubs/gatherings (as indicated by parent formation language), open campus, and family involvement. 
- Negative indicators: none evident in public materials; no customer-service language or minimal parent involvement signals are observed; rather, the opposite is clearly indicated. 

Why this rating
- The explicit emphasis on parental partnership, parent formations (reading groups, gatherings), and open-campus style activities strongly align with CLASSICAL expectations in this category.

Aggregate Assessment
- The majority of categories are CLASSICAL (Categories 2, 4, 5, and 6). Element 1 and Element 3 show SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals but are not as fully explicit in every classical keyword area as the other categories. Overall, Axios JCA leans toward CLASSICAL with solid, mission-aligned signals across multiple categories.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle
- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Increase explicit surface-level emphasis on liturgical/communal practices (e.g., daily or weekly liturgy, chapel, sacred music, and formalized liturgical calendar) to strengthen the liturgical dimension of the model. This would push this category toward a stronger CLASSICAL alignment by tightening the religious/ritual dimension.
  - Concrete step: Add or clearly articulate liturgical calendar integration, morning prayer, and chapel activities as part of the daily/weekly rhythm, with mentions on the website and admissions materials. Reference for alignment: liturgical and communal practice indicators in the classical lexicon. 

- Element 3 (School Curriculum): Surface a more explicit and consistent Great Books / Great Conversation framework and ensure clear articulation of grammar (broadly understood in classical education as foundational language/grammar study) in the published materials.
  - Concrete step: Explicitly name Great Books or primary classical works in curricular pages and show examples of Great Conversation activities; confirm or highlight grammar components beyond Latin (e.g., classical grammar and composition exercises) in public materials. This would strengthen the explicit Trivium articulation. 

- Optional: Continue to surface additional explicit mentions of classical Great Books, explicit Socratic seminars in upper grades, and the full grammar/logic/rhetoric progression in public-facing descriptions to push Element 3 and Element 4 further into the core CLASSICAL zone.
