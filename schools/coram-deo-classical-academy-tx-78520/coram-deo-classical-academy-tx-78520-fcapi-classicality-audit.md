## Executive Summary

Coram Deo Classical Academy TX 78520 presents a clearly classical-leaning program anchored in Christian faith and a traditional, formation-first approach to education. Public-facing materials emphasize Christ-centered instruction, Scripture as absolute truth, a Catholic/Protestant Christian worldview, and a classical method that includes memory work and character formation. The school describes its mission as forming virtuous, God-centered students through a classical teaching method, small classes, mentorship, and partnership with families. The curriculum highlights Bible, Literature, History, and later Latin and Logic, consistent with a liberal-arts orientation. Taken together, these signals point to a CLASSICAL classification across all six categories, with strong alignment to religious/metaphysical aims, a formation-focused purpose, a curriculum anchored in biblical-literary-historical study and classical disciplines, pedagogy that includes memorization and gradual reasoning, a faculty model of mentorship and living curriculum, and an active partnership with families and the broader Christian community.

Key public materials reviewed include:
- Strong religious/metaphysical anchors (Christ-centered instruction, Scripture-based worldview, and daily prayer) and explicit theology-language in mission statements and core values.  
- Clear purpose rooted in formation, virtue, and wisdom, with explicit statements about character development and a Christian worldview as telos. 
- Curriculum emphasis on Literature, Bible, History, with Latin and Logic named as later components, aligning with classical liberal-arts aims. 
- Pedagogical notes highlighting a classical-method progression (memorization → reasoning → eloquent expression) and a foundation of formation through memory, with some evidence of Socratic-style engagement in higher grades being implied but not explicitly stated on every page. 
- A living, mentorship-centered faculty narrative (full-time teachers, intimate community, small classes) and a stated aim to partner with families in formation.  
- Parents and community engagement are central in the rhetoric of partnership, parent formation, and openness to visits and involvement.

Rationale
- Across five of the six categories, the school's public-facing materials align robustly with classical criteria, especially in religious grounding, formation-focused aims, curriculum anchored in Literature/Bible/History plus Latin/Logic, and a living-faculty/parent-partnership model. Pedagogy presents clear classical signals (memory-then-reasoning) but lacks explicit, consistently described higher-grade Socratic/seminar practices in the accessible content, hence its positioning as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in that category. The combined signals lead to an overall CLASSICAL rating.

What Would Move the Needle
- For Category 4 (Pedagogy): A publicly articulated, consistent, high-school and K-8 pedagogy plan that explicitly describes regular Socratic seminars, Paideia-style discussions, and structured rhetoric/seminar experiences across grade bands would push Category 4 toward CLASSICAL more decisively.
- For all categories, explicitly naming and integrating Great Books in the curriculum and clearly signaling the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) through public-facing statements would further strengthen the classical alignment.
- Additional explicit mentions of memorization/recitation, singing/chanting in lower grades, and a formal "Scholé" rhythm (restful learning) would reinforce Pedagogy (Category 4) and reflect broader classical ideals.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If any category were judged SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, concrete changes would include:
  - Category 4 (Pedagogy): Publish a formal Pedagogy Blueprint detailing regular Socratic seminars and Paideia-style seminars for upper grades, with explicit rubrics and examples to illustrate how logic, rhetoric, and dialectic are taught in practice.
  - Category 3 (Curriculum): Add explicit language tying curriculum to Great Books or Great Conversation and clearly map the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) into course sequences, even if they appear implicitly through Literature/History/Latin/Logic.
  - Category 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Strengthen explicit references to liturgical/corporate worship practices (e.g., daily chapel, liturgical calendars, seasonal feasts) to align with Liturgical & Communal Practices in the Six Essentials document.
  - Category 5 (Faculty): Publish a formal “Portrait of a Teacher” and “Living Curriculum” statement with concrete examples and expectations for mentor roles, ongoing professional formation, and teacher-student relationships.
