## Executive Summary

The Geneva School of Casselberry, FL (ZIP 32707) presents a clearly Christian classical education model. Public-facing materials portray a school that centers on formation of character within a Christian framework, organized around a traditional Trivium structure (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) across three divisions (grammar K4–6, dialectic 7–8, rhetoric 9–12). Public statements emphasize a biblical worldview, partnership with families, and a mission framed as “Inspiring students to love beauty, think deeply, and pursue Christ’s calling.” The organization is independent and not formally denominational, but it grounds its curriculum, pedagogy, and community life in Christian formation and classical educational aims. Evidence from the school’s materials supports a consistent classical education orientation across the six classical elements rubric (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community). The site also explicitly references liturgical/communal sensibilities via chaplaincy and prayerful partnership with families.

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

Key supporting observations:

- Clear religious-metaphysical orientation (historic Christian faith; biblical worldview; chaplaincy) and a liturgical/community sensibility suggested by daily life and governance.
- Purpose/goals oriented to formation, virtue, and life-long discernment within a Christian classical frame.
- Curriculum explicitly built around the Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) and the three-tier division (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) across grade bands.
- Pedagogy consistent with classical methods (Socratic/seminar-style dialogue is implied by the dialectic/rhetoric structure; lower grades feature a rhythm of formation, memory, and communal practices).
- Faculty described in terms that align with virtue-formation and mentorship; leadership roles (principal, chaplain, etc.) are named, indicating a culture of guided learning rather than a purely technocratic model.
- Parents & Community are framed as partners in formation, with explicit language about prayerful partnership and parental engagement.

Citations and textual anchors are drawn from the school’s public-facing materials and the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) framework.

- Foundational school identity and Christian classical framing: The Geneva School presents itself as a Christian Classical School and emphasizes biblical worldview in its mission and narrative.
- Independent status and denominational position: The school describes itself as independent and not affiliated with any particular church or denomination, while still upholding Christian formation.
- Liturgical/communal life and governance signals: A chaplain is listed in Fast Facts, signaling liturgical/community practices.
- Grade structure and Trivium-based curriculum: Grammar (K4–6), Dialectic (7–8), Rhetoric (9–12) are publicly identified, signaling a classical curriculum structure.
- Public emphasis on formation and life-long learning: “A Geneva education is an education for life!... foundations built in a child’s formative years” supports formation-focused aims.
- Parental partnership and community: The school foregrounds partnering with families in the educational process.
