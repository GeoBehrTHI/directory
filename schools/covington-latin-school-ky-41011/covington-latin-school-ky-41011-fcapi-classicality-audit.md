# Classicality Audit: Austin Classical School (Austin, TX)

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Why: The school explicitly centers its mission on glorifying Jesus Christ; grounds its aims in cultivating wisdom and virtue through truth, goodness, and beauty; implements a Trivium-centered, Great Books/Western tradition curriculum with Latin, logic, and rhetoric; and practices classical pedagogy (memory work, debate/discussion, thesis/rhetoric). Its collaborative “co‑teacher parent” model strongly supports the classical partnership with families. The only thinner area is faculty language (less explicit emphasis on “teachers as mentors/living curriculum”), which modestly lowers that single category to Somewhat Classical.
- Website used: https://austinclassical.org/ 

How to read this report
- For each of the six elements, you will see: (1) keywords found on the school’s site; (2) comparison to the “Six Essential Elements” positive/negative keyword lists; (3) a quantitative positive vs. negative estimate; (4) a rating for that category.
- Keyword lists and criteria are drawn from Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and companion lexicon materials. Examples of positive indicators include truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, Trivium, Great Books, Latin, recitation/chanting, debate/Socratic, scholé/restful learning, and wonder; negative indicators include values-neutral, career readiness emphasis, project-based-only/experiential-only, social studies replacing history, etc.

Overall Assessment
- Aggregate classification: CLASSICAL
- Five categories (1, 2, 3, 4, 6) are solidly classical using the Six Elements keyword framework; Faculty (Category 5) is “Somewhat Classical” due to lighter explicit language about teachers as mentors/living curriculum. The school’s mission, aims, curriculum, and pedagogy repeatedly use core classical keywords from the Six Elements lists—truth, goodness, beauty; wisdom/virtue; Trivium; Latin/logic/rhetoric; memory/recitation; debate/discussion; Great Books/Great Conversation—while avoiding negative indicators like values‑neutral education, social‑studies‑over‑history, or project‑based‑only pedagogy.  

What would move the needle (Faculty) from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL
- Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” (virtues, habits, intellectual life) and/or “Portrait of a Graduate” that frames faculty as mentors and the “living curriculum,” echoing the Six Elements’ teacher ideals. 
- Describe ongoing faculty formation: great books seminars for teachers, colloquia on pedagogy, catechesis/classical training expectations. 
- Name the mentoring model explicitly (e.g., “master‑teacher,” “apprenticeship,” “faculty of friends/collegial culture”) to align with positive faculty keywords.
