## Executive Summary

St Benedict Classical Catholic School in 47404 presents a clearly Catholic, liturgically oriented educational identity, anchored in a motto that foregrounds wisdom, faith, and virtue (Sapientia ~ Fides ~ Virtus) and in a stated aim to partner with parents to foster intellect and faith. Public materials consistently emphasize faith integration, liturgical life (Mass), Latin prayer practice, and Catholic affiliation (Archdiocese). These elements signal strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and robust Parents & Community engagement.

However, the publicly visible curriculum and pedagogy materials do not demonstrate the core liberal-arts content or classical teaching methods that define a fully classical program (e.g., grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books and Great Conversation; explicit Socratic or seminar-based pedagogy). In aggregate, the school’s public materials align more with a religious, faith-formation orientation that incorporates classical flavor (Latin prayers, liturgical life, parental partnership) than with a comprehensive Classical School model as defined by the Six Essential Elements. As a result, the overall classification is NOT CLASSICAL, with Element 2 showing some classical alignment (formation-oriented aims) but Categories 3–5 lacking evidence of classical curriculum and pedagogy, and Element 6 showing meaningful parental partnership. The single strongest classical-aligned thread appears in the religious-community dimension, but it does not extend comprehensively across the full classical rubric.

Key takeaways by category:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL (liturgical life, theology-inflected education, Catholic identity; Sapientia Fides Virtus; Mass; Archdiocese affiliation).
- School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (formation-oriented language around wisdom/virtue; faith-intellect integration; but lacking explicit classical telos statements beyond general formation).
- Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL (no explicit Great Books, liberal-arts Trinity, grammar/logic/rhetoric, or Great Books in public materials).
- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL (no explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in the public materials; liturgical/recitation cues exist but not a classical pedagogical framework).
- Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL (no clear description of teachers as “living curriculum” or mentor-models in the public materials).
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL (clear emphasis on partnering with parents; hospitality and family engagement are present as a core theme).

    - It mentions students learning Latin prayers in Pre-K and includes Mass imagery, signaling liturgical and communal practices.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present (from the Six Elements lexicon): theology, liturgy, Mass, faith, virtue, wisdom, sacred, reverence, holy, sacred music, liturgical life. The site directly artifacts Mass, Latin prayers, and Catholic identity, aligning with liturgical and theological markers.
    - Negative keywords are not evident in public materials.

- Why this rating
    - The public materials clearly embed theological formation, liturgical life, and Catholic identity, consistent with a Religi ous and Metaphysical Foundations orientation. The explicit Catholic framing and liturgical elements (Mass, Latin prayers) support a CLASSICAL alignment on these foundations, even though the overall program is not fully developed as a classical school in all six elements.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
    - The motto Sapientia ~ Fides ~ Virtus and the phrase “Partnering with parents to foster intellect and faith” point to a formation-centered purpose that aims at wisdom and virtue in harmony with faith.
    - The public materials center on formation and faith integration rather than a job- or skill-first framing. This aligns with the classical aim of formation over mere information, albeit not spelled out with the full classical telos language (true/good/beautiful).

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
    - Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, fidelity/faith, intellect (as part of “intellect and faith”), great conversation or classical heritage language is not explicitly present, but the foundational terms imply classical formation concepts.
    - Negative keywords: not present in the public materials.

- Why this rating
    - The school’s stated goal of forming virtue and wisdom within a Catholic framework suggests a classical formation ethos, but the absence of explicit classical telos language (the true, the good, the beautiful) and explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references keeps the alignment from reaching full CLASSICAL. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: Curriculum — NOT CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle

- If the school sought to shift toward a full CLASSICAL model, concrete steps could include:
    - Curriculum: Adopt and publicly publish a classical core curriculum that includes the grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence, Great Books or canonical primary texts, and explicit history as Western civilization framing.
    - Pedagogy: Implement explicit classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, memorization via memory work, narration/recitation, and opportunities for formal rhetoric) and communicate these approaches on the site.
    - Faculty: Articulate a living-curriculum model for teachers, including professional development in classical pedagogy and a visible “portrait” of teacher ideals aligned with classical formation.
    - History and Great Conversation: Integrate Great Books and a “Great Conversation” framing throughout the curriculum, ensuring cross-subject connections and interdisciplinary coherence.
    - Community formation: Expand explicit parent formation programs (book studies, lectures) that align with classical-philosophical aims and describe how parents co-educate in partnership with faculty.
    - Liturgical and metaphysical integration: Maintain Catholic identity while clearly tying liturgical rhythm to academic formation, with structured opportunities to articulate the transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty) in classroom practice.

Note: These needle-moving steps would move the school toward CLASSICAL status on multiple categories (Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and perhaps even Element 2) while preserving and clarifying its strong religious-communal identity (Element 1 and 6).
