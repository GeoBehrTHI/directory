# Classicality Audit: Bishop Ryan Catholic School

Executive Summary

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Why: Bishop Ryan’s public materials strongly emphasize Catholic worship, theology, and communal liturgical life (daily prayer, daily Mass, liturgical year), which aligns with the Religious & Metaphysical foundations of classical education. However, the site provides little-to-no evidence of a classical curriculum (Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Latin) or pedagogy (chanting/memorization, Socratic seminars, rhetoric training). Purpose/Goal language leans toward formation (virtue, character, discipleship) but lacks explicit “truth, goodness, and beauty” articulation. Parent partnership is affirmed. Net: strong Catholic formation, weak public evidence of distinctively classical curriculum/pedagogy.
- School website reviewed: https://bishopryan.com

How this report was scored: All six categories below include a mandatory keyword analysis against the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” positive/negative keyword indicators and supporting lexicon, then a category rating (Classical / Somewhat Classical / Not Classical) with quantitative estimates. Keyword lists and guidance were drawn from the Six Essential Elements document and lexicon across multiple sections .

Aggregate Classification

- Category ratings: C1 Classical; C2 Somewhat Classical; C3 Not Classical; C4 Not Classical; C5 Not Classical; C6 Somewhat Classical.
- Overall assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
    - Strength: Strong Catholic liturgical/theological foundation (daily prayer, Mass, liturgical year) , which accords with classical religious-metaphysical elements .
    - Gaps: Public-facing materials do not show a classical curriculum (Trivium, Great Books, Latin) or pedagogy (chanting/memorization, Socratic seminars, rhetoric) .

What would move the needle (from Somewhat Classical to Classical)

- State the telos explicitly: add “truth, goodness, and beauty,” “wisdom,” “human flourishing,” and “formation (not mere information)” to mission/purpose pages and program descriptions .
- Publish a classical curriculum map: show Trivium/Quadrivium language, list Great Books/primary sources, and name classical languages (Latin at minimum) with rationale; avoid “social studies” in favor of “history” to signal continuity with classical tradition .
- Describe pedagogy: highlight K–8 chanting/memorization and recitation; for upper grades, publish Socratic seminar/disputation practices and rhetoric deliverables (speeches, orations, theses) and any scholé/restful learning commitments .
- Faculty formation: add a “Portrait of a Teacher,” note faculty classical training/collegium and mentorship language .
- Parent formation: advertise parent reading groups, “What is classical education?” nights, or book clubs to align home and school loves (affections) .
- Calibrate outcomes language: keep AP as a tool, but ensure public framing emphasizes virtue/wisdom over metrics and college-prep signals .
