## Executive Summary

Kingdom Collegiate Academy (KCA) presents a clearly religiously rooted, college-preparatory program that bills itself as incorporating classical education with a Kingdom worldview. Public materials emphasize biblical instruction, weekly chapels, and a Kingdom Agenda philosophy, alongside modern, 21st-century learning tools and college-preparatory aims. The school publicly frames its mission around formation within a Christian milieu, leadership development, and high academic expectations, while also highlighting accreditation and partnerships with faith-based organizations.

Overall classification (six-category rubric): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale: The school elevates classical education as a core frame (explicitly described as “classical education” in its materials) and demonstrates elements of the classical structure (e.g., disciplined curriculum, emphasis on formation, and some dialogic/critical-thinking aspects in middle/high school). However, explicit classical pillars such as Great Books, routine Socratic seminars, emphasis on grammar-logic-rhetoric as a formal liberal-arts sequence, and the full Great Conversation are not consistently evidenced across all grade bands in the public materials. The religious-metaphysical foundations are robust and align with classical aims, which strengthens the classical profile, but the degree to which the curriculum and pedagogy fully embody the historic liberal-arts trivium as described in the classical rubric is partial rather than comprehensive. See category-by-category analysis for details and keyword evidence.

What would move the needle: 
- Add explicit Great Books/Great Conversation components and visible integration of grammar-logic-rhetoric as distinct stages within the curriculum.
- Tie higher-level discourse and Socratic-style seminars more clearly to upper grades, with documented opportunities for student-led rhetoric and public speaking.
- Provide concrete alignment of “formation” with explicit virtues and linking activities (e.g., memory work, recitation, and liturgical or contemplative practices) in lower grades where appropriate.
- Expand and document parents-as-co-teachers initiatives and more formal “community-learning” moments (book studies, parent seminars) to meet the full range of classical-school family-partnership expectations.

Citations: The analysis draws on KCA materials indicating biblical worldview, Kingdom worldview, chapels, and faith-based mission ([2], [3], [11], [12], [18]), as well as curriculum details (core subjects, literacy, math, science, social studies, and chapel-based formation) ([13], [3], [14], [2]). Evidence of accreditation and partnerships is noted ([0], [3], [11]). See inline citations throughout the category sections for specific evidence.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords: Kingdom worldview, nurturing Christian environment, formation/leadership/ College-preparatory framing (3–4 positive concepts). 
- Negative keywords: None clearly evident in the public materials (no explicit vocational-work-preparation emphasis that would conflict with formation focus).

Why this rating
- The school emphasizes formation, virtue-oriented aims, and the integration of faith with education, which align with the classical concept of education as formation of character and wisdom. However, explicit language about virtue-and-wisdom as the sole/primary aims, separate from college-prep or Kingdom-identity, is less overt than the strongest classical formulations. Therefore, this category is judged SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

Abstract citations: Mission and Kingdom worldview language ([11]); formation and worldview emphasis ([2]).

- Provide a formal articulation of the “great conversation” and how it informs purpose and goals beyond college prep.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — [SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL]

Evidence
- The middle school notes higher-level thinking and discussions about academic and professional careers, indicating dialogic pedagogy in the upper grades. 
- The program highlights that lower grades may involve singing and memory work, pointing to some contemplative/experiential elements in early-childhood pedagogy, alongside recitation and memory-friendly practices. 
- Upper school emphasis on discussion, seminars, and debates is implied (Socratic/argumentation skills are a hallmark of classical pedagogy), though explicit references to Socratic seminars or rhetoric instruction are not strongly documented in the public materials. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: higher-level thinking, discussions, seminars/debates (implied), discourse-based learning (3–4 positives). 
- Negative keywords present: project-based approach is visible in curriculum language, which classical pedagogy sometimes views with caution (e.g., “project learning” could be a negative indicator in strict classical rubrics). 

Why this rating
- The pedagogy shows a mix of dialogic, inquiry-oriented elements (discussions, higher-level thinking) and contemporary, technology-enhanced approaches (21st-century learning, active environments). The absence of consistently explicit, classroom-wide Socratic seminars and rhetoric-focused training across all high-school grades dampens a full CLASSICAL Pedagogy rating. Hence, SOMeWHAT CLASSICAL is appropriate.

Abstract citations: Middle school discussions/higher-level thinking ([3]); lower/upper-grade pedagogy language including dialogue and seminars is implied in the content ([3]).

What would move the needle (for category 4)
- Document concrete Socratic seminars and seminars/debates as a core pedagogy across grades, with rubrics for rhetoric and public speaking.
- Publish detailed syllabi showing explicit grammar/logic/rhetoric integration and Great Books-style discussion prompts tied to core subjects.
- Showcase explicit evidence of contemplative and restorative practice (scholé-like idle time or reflective learning) in the lower grades.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly document and publish a formal Great Books/Great Conversation program and show how grammar-logic-rhetoric are sequenced across grades.
- Add clear, public evidence of Socratic seminars, textual study groups, and explicit rhetoric training in upper grades (with rubrics and sample transcripts or outcomes).
- Augment the “formation” narrative with concrete virtue-outcome metrics and real-world examples of character formation integrated into daily classroom practice.
- Strengthen parent-facing materials that describe parents as co-teachers and outline structured parent learning opportunities (book studies, workshops) with schedules and outcomes.
- Increase transparency about the balance between classical forms and 21st-century tools to demonstrate how both converge in daily pedagogy across all grade levels.
