## ABSTRACT

Holy Family Academy (Manchester, NH 03102) demonstrates a strong CLASSICAL orientation in Religion and Curriculum, with clear Catholic, theological framing and a classical liberal arts curriculum built around the trivium/quadrivium and original texts. The school presents formation—intellectual, moral, and spiritual—as its telos, aligning with classical aims to cultivate virtue and wisdom within a Catholic framework. Evidence from public materials shows Catholic identity and theology as foundational, including a bedrock theological framing for the curriculum and parish-linked community life. The curricular formulation emphasizes classical disciplines, a liberal-arts core, and a capstone thesis culture, indicating a robust classical structure in the upper grades.

However, the Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community dimensions show consistency with classical aims but lack explicit public articulation to the same degree as the curricular and religious foundations. Pedagogical methods are suggested by capstone projects and a classical framing, but explicit statements about Socratic seminars, scholé, or chant/memory pedagogy are not strongly foregrounded in the site content. Faculty signals exist (Head of School, Dean of Academics, named teachers), yet the public-facing culture around the “living curriculum” or mentor-model faculty is not consistently described. Parent/Community signals are present (Catholic community, parish ties, events), but explicit parent-as-co-teacher language or formal parent-education programs are not strongly evident in the public materials.

Overall, Holy Family Academy presents a CLASSICAL core in Religion and Curriculum, with SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals across Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents/Community. The aggregate positioning is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
