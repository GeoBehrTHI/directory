## Executive Summary

Holy Family Academy, Manassas, VA (ZIP 20111) exhibits strong alignment with classical education principles, anchored in Catholic faith and a traditional liberal-arts approach. The public materials repeatedly emphasize a classical, faith-centered curriculum built on the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a long-standing Western-civilization heritage, engagement with great works, and formation of virtue and character. Evidence in the site copy highlights Daily Mass, Catholic ethos, and a program structure that foregrounds formation over information, which supports a CLASSICAL rating overall. The school also showcases explicit commitment to formation through virtuous living, intellectual curiosity, and a living curriculum grounded in a living Catholic tradition, including references to virtue, truth, goodness, and beauty. The presence of a dedicated faculty and parental partnerships further reinforce a classical orientation in practice.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Similarities across six categories: Religious/the metaphysical foundations; purposeful aim rooted in formation; Trivium-based curriculum; classical pedagogy and memorywork; dedicated, virtue-oriented faculty; strong parental/community partnership in a Catholic context.
- Notable strengths: Clear articulation of Trivium-based curriculum, Great-Books-influenced study, liturgical life, and explicit formation-oriented language.
- Potential gaps (if any): Some explicit pedagogy signals typical of classical schools, such as Socratic seminars in the upper school, are not heavily documented in the public site copy; the site emphasizes classical pedagogy and memory-based approaches but less explicit mention of Socratic seminars beyond the general classical framing. This nuance is captured in the category-by-category analysis below.

Evidence and direct quotes are drawn from the school’s public materials (cited inline). Key passages include: the emphasis on a classical liberal education with faith integration and the cultivation of truth, goodness, and beauty; the trivium; the prominence of Catholic liturgy and daily Mass; and the stated aim of forming virtuous leaders within a tradition of Western classical education. See cited sources for specifics.

Evidence
- The materials point to a number of classical teaching modalities typical of classical schools: emphasis on the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a memory- and virtue-centered approach; the public copy repeatedly highlights a traditional, memory-based and formation-focused pedagogy. 
- There are explicit references to “narration,” “recitation,” “memory work,” and a general sense of a traditional, contemplative pedagogy (scholé) in the classical lexicon; the site copy reflects a blend of traditional methods and the classical framework. 
- The site emphasizes “daily Mass” and liturgical cadence, and use of a living Catholic tradition as the foundation of learning, which supports contemplative and moral formation alongside cognitive work. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue (mentioned in the lexicon as a classical element; the site emphasizes classical pedagogy generally, including discussion and rhetorical training), memory work, recitation, narration, chanting, scholé, restfulness, and seminar-style approaches implied by the classical framing.
- Negative keywords: The presence of modern project-based or experiential-only approaches is not strongly evidenced in the public copy, though the lexicon lists these as potential negative signals; the public copy does not emphasize those as primary modes.
- Estimated occurrence: Moderate to high presence of positive Pedagogy keywords (trivium-based pedagogy, memory, recitation, contemplative/restful learning), with limited explicit evidence of Socratic seminars or formal seminars in the public pages reviewed; thus categorized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates core classical pedagogy elements (grammar/logical/rhetoric training, memory work, contemplative/restful learning) but lacks explicit, widely documented evidence of formal Socratic seminars or high-frequency upper-school seminar/debate structures in the public site content. ## What Would Move the Needle

- If aiming for a rigorous, explicit demonstration of full Pedagogy parity with classic models (e.g., consistent, explicit evidence of formal Socratic seminars, Paideia-style seminars, or frequent upper-school discourse), the site could publish clearer statements and examples of upper-level Socratic inquiry, seminars, and debate, along with sample lesson narratives showing memory work and recitation in practice.
- Additional explicit connections to the Great Conversation and a robust, book-rich curriculum across all grades (with explicit mention of great books by name and direct references to primary sources) would strengthen the category 3 and 4 signals.
- More explicit parent-teacher partnership language (e.g., “parents as co-educators,” parent formation series, and home-study resources) would solidify Element 6, though current materials already indicate a positive orientation toward family involvement.

Note: No major needle-moving changes are required to reach a fully CLASSICAL standing, given the current materials already meet the category criteria across most areas.
