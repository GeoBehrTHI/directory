## Executive Summary

Second Baptist School University-Model North Texas (SBSUM North) presents a robust classical-Christian program anchored in a University-Model framework. Across public-facing materials, the school foregrounds Christ-centered, biblical worldview formation, a strong classical curriculum (grammar–logic–rhetoric), and an explicit emphasis on parent partnership and community life. Evidence from SBSUM North’s site shows clear commitments to theology as the governing framework for learning, with statements about Christ-centered education, biblical worldview, discipleship, and a gospel-centered community. The curriculum is described as classical, featuring grammar–logic–rhetoric as well as access to book lists and “Great Books”–style study through booklists and rhetoric training. Pedagogy emphasizes Socratic/seminar-style discussion in upper grades and classical memory and recitation in earlier years, including explicit references to Harkness-style discussions and opportunities for public speaking. Faculty are portrayed as living mentors, and there is a strong emphasis on faculty who love God, teach well, and model virtue. Parents are explicitly framed as co-educators and partners in education, with ongoing opportunities for involvement and campus visits.

Overall, SBSUM North scores as CLASSICAL across the six categories. The materials consistently align with classical aims (truth, goodness, beauty; formation of virtue; liberal arts-informed curriculum; Socratic/seminar pedagogy; living-teacher-model; robust parent partnership). There are no evident signals of secularism, relativism, or a incompatible “workforce-preparation” emphasis that would push the school away from classical aims.

Key supports:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations anchored in Biblical/theological commitments and Christ-centered language.
- Purpose/goal oriented toward formation, virtue, and worldview formation rather than mere information or vocational training.
- Curriculum explicitly classical (grammar–logic–rhetoric) with references to Great Books/standard classical training and booklists.
- Pedagogy highlighting Socratic/Harkness-style discussion, rhetoric training, and public-speaking opportunities.
- Faculty described as living curriculum and mentors who love God and teach well.
- Parents as co-teachers and engaged partners with clear pathways for formation and campus involvement.

### Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The curriculum is described as classical and modeled on grammar–logic–rhetoric (the trivium). The site explicitly states: “The curriculum is fashioned after classical education, which is a methodology involving three stages (grammar, logic, and rhetoric), also known as the trivium.”
- The university-model program asserts a classical framework with a strong emphasis on thinking and reading (rigor across subjects). The “University-Model” materials emphasize the same classical base and the integration of disciplines.
- Booklists for Logic and Rhetoric are provided, signaling explicit engagement with classical texts and Great Books-style study.
- The Arts materials describe classical fine arts education and ties to the Seven Liberal Arts (music as part of the quadrivium, theater as part of rhetoric, etc.).

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric, trivium, seven liberal arts, Great Books, primary sources, classical education, Harkness/seminal dialogues (Socratic dialogue), booklists (Logic/Rhetoric), universality of classical education.
- Negative keywords: None observed (no substitution of history with “social studies,” nor heavy emphasis on project-based, purely experiential learning replacing classical texts).

Why this rating

- The explicit articulation of the trivium, associated related liberal arts, and the presence of booklists and classical pedagogical references align strongly with a classical curriculum orientation.

Score: CLASSICAL.

Citations: Grammar–logic–rhetoric; Great Books/primary sources; Logic/Rhetoric Booklists; classical arts framing.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Upper-level pedagogy includes Socratic discussion and Harkness-table style discussions for rigorous thinking and dialectic, with explicit references to classroom dialogue as a core method. “Rhetoric students learn to speak and write across all subject areas with eloquence… in oval Harkness table discussions.”
- The pedagogy includes training in rhetoric and opportunities to present oration, consistent with classical pedagogy.
- Lower grades emphasize memory work and chant as a foundation of learning and memorization (a traditional classical technique). (Direct language about lower-school chanting/memory is embedded in the broader classical pedagogy narrative across SBSUM materials.)
- The program includes on-campus and satellite-day structures, with on-campus classes two to three days a week, and satellite learning, which aligns with the University-Model approach but retains classical formation through schedule and teacher-led instruction.

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis

- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue, Harkness/seminar, rhetoric, recitation, memory work, classical pedagogy, formal oratory, seminar-style discussions.
- Negative keywords: The presence of “experiential learning” or purely project-based learning dominating pedagogy would be a potential negative signal, but SBSUM North’s materials frame classical pedagogy as integrated rather than exclusively project-based; there is no evidence of a wholesale shift to project-based learning as the sole approach.

Why this rating

- The explicit use of Socratic/Harkness discussions, memory-based learning, and rhetoric training signals a strong commitment to classical pedagogy. While there are satellite days and some arts-integrated activities, the core methods align with classical methods rather than non-classical, constructivist-only strategies.

Score: CLASSICAL.

Citations: Harkness/seminar and rhetoric emphasis; oval Harkness table discussions; rhetoric booklists.
