# Classicality Audit: Augustine School (Jackson, TN)
Website audited: https://www.augustineschool.com

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Why: Augustine School’s public materials repeatedly foreground a Classical Christian identity oriented to truth, goodness, and beauty; formation in wisdom and virtue; and explicitly Christian theological grounding. The site highlights communal prayer and faith practices, and it engages parents through recurring on‑campus events. While the website could present more explicit curricular language (Trivium, Latin, Great Books) and pedagogy (Socratic seminars) to improve coverage evidence, the existing signals and ACCS accreditation substantively support a classical designation.

Top signals found on site:
- “Discover the Good, the True, and the Beautiful … Classical Christian Education”; “wise and virtuous” formation; “Biblically‑Based” and “Gospel‑Centered” framing; “Lordship of Christ” (homepage)  
- Season of Prayer; community prayer emphasis 
- ACCS accreditation announcement clearly asserting a “true classical Christian education” framework 
- Parental/community engagement: recurring Mommy & Me on‑campus events; Open House; Christmas/Spring Recitation events   

Six‑category results (see full details below):
- Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL
- Purpose & Goal: CLASSICAL
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (coverage light on explicit Trivium/Latin/Great Books terms)
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (clear recitation/memory work; less explicit on Socratic/seminar)
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (signals present, but limited explicit “portrait of a teacher/mentor” language)
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL

Reference framework used: Six Essential Elements of a Classical School keyword indicators (positive/negative) and lexicon (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, liturgy, Trivium, Great Books, Socratic, scholé, wonder, chanting, project‑based/experiential)

## Overall Assessment
Based on the keyword analysis mapped against the Six Elements framework, Augustine School merits a CLASSICAL rating. Its public materials clearly communicate classical Christian identity and aims (truth, goodness, beauty; wisdom and virtue), theological grounding, prayerful communal life, and robust parent partnership. Website presentation would be strengthened by explicitly naming Trivium/Quadrivium, Latin/Great Books, and upper‑school Socratic/rhetoric practices, but the existing signals—and ACCS accreditation—justify the overall classical classification.
