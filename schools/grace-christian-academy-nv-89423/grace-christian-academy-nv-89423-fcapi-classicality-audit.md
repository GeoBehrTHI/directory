## Executive Summary

Grace Christian Academy Nv 89423 presents as a Christ-centered, classical Christian school that actively situates faith at the center of its curriculum and culture. Public materials consistently frame education through a Trivium-based lens (grammar, logic, rhetoric), integrate biblical faith across disciplines, and emphasize parental involvement as a key hinge of the educational formation. Evidence across multiple pages shows explicit statements about a biblical worldview, Theology as foundational, and a belief that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable through a Christian lens. The school also foregrounds small class settings, character formation, and a partnership with families. Taken together, the school aligns strongly with classical aims in several domains, with one area showing room for more explicit classical pedagogy signals (notably, direct documentation of Socratic seminars or Great Books discussions in the public-facing materials). Overall, Grace Christian Academy Nv 89423 is CLASSICAL.

Key observations driving this assessment include:
- Clear religious/metaphysical foundations embedded in doctrine, biblical authority, and Christ-centered pedagogy (evidence of The Word of God, The Trinity, and daily prayer in public materials)  .
- A stated purpose/call to formation, virtue, wisdom, and a theology-infused view of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty as real and knowable .
- Curriculum framed around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with explicit mention of Latin and integrated study across disciplines; emphasis on the classical method and a biblical worldview .
- Pedagogy signals include the classical method and memory/recitation orientation in traditional classical education, with potential room for more explicit Socratic/seminar practices in the public narrative; evidence points to a strong foundation but less explicit documentation of upper-school Socratic seminars in the visible materials .
- Faculty presence evidenced via public biographies and staff references; the living-curriculum idea is suggested through faculty mentioning and parent/testimonial content  .
- Parents & Community engagement is a major pillar, with explicit language about parental involvement, partnership with families, and open communication, consistent with classical education models  .

Abbreviated verdict: CLASSICAL with notable strengths in religious foundations, formation-focused aims, and Trivium-based curriculum; a single area to strengthen would be the explicit, high-profile articulation of Socratic-style pedagogy and Great Books engagement in public materials to reach a fuller “Socratic dialogue / Great Conversation” signaling.

## What Would Move the Needle

- To move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL in Pedagogy (Category 4), Grace Christian Academy Nv 89423 could:
  - Publish explicit, consistent statements and examples of Socratic seminars, Paideia-style discussions, and structured Great Books conversations across upper grades, including sample rubrics and unit maps.
  - Feature direct public-facing documentation of interdisciplinary, cross-subject Great Books engagements and primary-source study to reinforce the Great Conversation signals.
  - Add explicit signage of memory/chant-based routines (Scholé days, memory work, chanting) in the lower grades, with sample daily/weekly cycles published on the site.
- To further strengthen Category 3 (Curriculum), the school could:
  - Publish a clear, publicly accessible curriculum map that explicitly shows the progression through Trivium stages by grade, including Latin and classical texts (Great Books) where applicable.
  - Include explicit references to Great Books or primary-source engagement in major subjects (e.g., history, literature) with concrete examples (e.g., sample texts, author lists).
- To deepen Category 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Category 2 (Purpose and Goal):
  - Add a short, explicit statement tying all subject areas to transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) as a daily, visible banner across the site, with examples of how chapels, liturgical year, or sacred music integrate with core courses.
  - Provide a clear articulation of how the school’s formation aims are measured or evidenced (e.g., virtue-based assessments, character rubrics) to demonstrate the practical realization of formation as a goal.
