## Executive Summary

St. Regis Academy Mo 64138 presents a strongly Catholic, classical-leaning program that explicitly centers on virtuous formation, truth, and beauty within a liberal-arts framework. Public-facing materials emphasize a Christ-centered, Catholic Classical Liberal Arts education, extensive Latin study, an integrated curriculum, and a commitment to virtue formation and parish life. Project-based STREAM experiences, early exposure to Latin, and liturgical elements further align the school with classical education norms, though certain elements (notably detailed references to the full Trivium/Quadrivium, explicit Great Books engagement, and explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades) are less evident in the site content itself. Overall, the school reads as CLASSICAL, with notable strengths in religious foundations, formation goals, integrated curriculum, and family/community partnership; some areas in curriculum pedagogy and explicit Great Books/Logic-Rhetoric emphasis show room for fuller alignment.

Key observations driving the assessment:

- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: Catholic identity, Christ-centered mission, formation in truth/goodness/beauty, liturgical life, and parish connections.
- Purpose and goals emphasize formation, virtue, spiritual growth, and lifelong discipleship, not solely job-readiness.
- Curriculum shows core liberal-arts elements (Latin, grammar, history, composition, integrated curriculum, STREAM) though explicit Great Books / Trivium-Rhetoric connections are not always foregrounded in the public materials.
- Pedagogy features project-based learning (STREAM, PBL), integrated subjects, and a rhythm of contemplative elements (Scholé-like pacing is implied by integration and liturgical life) but explicit high-school Socratic seminars or advanced rhetoric training are not repeatedly highlighted in the public texts.
- Faculty messaging emphasizes educator formation, mentorship, and a living curriculum in practice, with visible investment in professional roles and long-range planning.
- Parent and community partnerships are strong: parents as co-educators, engagement through site content, and institutional emphasis on family formation and parish life.
