## Executive Summary

American Preparatory Academy West Valley 2 (APA WV2) presents a classical-liberal-arts orientation rooted in virtue, character formation, and a stated aim to educate both minds and hearts. Public materials emphasize classical language such as “classical, liberal-arts education,” and highlight character formation, leadership, and college prep within a community-focused framework. Evidence of explicit theological or liturgical foundations is limited in the public-facing content, while evidence of family involvement and community engagement is more pronounced (e.g., after-school programs and family-involvement language). Overall, APA WV2 demonstrates a Classical orientation in several dimensions (notably in curriculum framing and community-partnering) but falls short on explicit, consistent articulation of some core classical elements (notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis and explicitly liturgical/theological grounding). This yields a final assessment of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with notable strengths in community and character formation and clear classical framing, and identifiable opportunities to strengthen explicit classical pedagogy, curriculum specifics, and liturgical/theological integration.

- Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Key themes: classical-liberal arts framing; character and virtue formation; college preparation alongside moral formation; family/community involvement; explicit faculty dedication signals.

- Most actionable needle-moving opportunities (see “What Would Move the Needle”): foreground explicit Great Books/Great Conversation references in the curriculum; document and publish Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy and scholé/restful learning practices; increase explicit articulation of liturgical/theological foundations (if aligned with the school's mission) and a clearly defined “living curriculum” mapping for teachers; strengthen transparent parental co-educator messaging and open-campus opportunities.

Evidence references are drawn from APA WV2 public materials (mission statements, newsletters, staff pages, and after-school program descriptions) and the classical-education keyword lexicon and Six Essential Elements framework in the knowledge base.

- Key textual signals: “classical, liberal-arts education,” “virtuous character development,” “college preparation,” “Ambassadors” program, “Collegium Hall,” and “family involvement” in after-school/community materials. These signals are cited below in category-by-category detail.

Citations:
- Mission framing: APA WV2 public materials framing APA as a “classical, liberal-arts education” focused on virtue/character (evidence excerpted from the WV3 Secondary Newsletter and mission pages). 
- Community and family involvement: After-school/community programs emphasize family involvement and a mission that students graduate with college readiness (evidence excerpted from the After School Programs page). 
- Public-facing description of teachers and staff: Staff pages describe dedicated teachers and a community of educators (evidence excerpted from the Secondary Staff page). 
- Great Books / Great Conversation and deeper classical texts framing: While explicit Great Books references are not consistently foregrounded in WV2 materials, the classical-liberal-arts framing is present; the broader classical education lexicon in the knowledge base provides the canonical indicators for this category. See the Six Essential Elements framework for the keyword lists (positive/negative indicators) used in category scoring.

## Executive Summary — Category-by-Category Snapshot

### Category 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials repeatedly frame APA WV2 as pursuing a classical liberal arts education with a focus on virtue and character formation. For example, mission-focused messaging describes educating both minds and hearts and developing “virtuous character” as part of APA’s aim (secondary materials). 
- The same communications emphasize a moral formation orientation rather than an explicit theological or liturgical program in the public-facing materials, with limited explicit theology, liturgy, or sacred-practice references visible in the supplied content. The integration of theology as a primary driver is not clearly documented in these sources. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: virtue, character, classical, liberal-arts.
- Negative keywords: theology, liturgy, liturgical practices are not evidenced in the public-facing materials.
- Ratio (positive vs. negative): Presence of 2–4 positive signals vs. minimal/no explicit theology-liturgy signals.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates virtue- and character-centered framing consistent with classical aims, but lacks explicit, publicly documented theological grounding or liturgical practices in the available content. This yields a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL for this category. The absence of explicit liturgical/theological signals lowers the score from CLASSICAL, even though moral realism (virtue/character) is present. 

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Mission framing emphasizes formation and virtue, with aims of “educating minds and hearts” and “virtuous character development,” signaling formation as a primary telos. The language also emphasizes college preparation and leadership with civic engagement, indicating a blend of formation with college-readiness elements. 
- Public materials note a goal of graduates achieving college readiness and careers, suggesting alignment with broader workforce outcomes sometimes emphasized in modern schooling. This is evidenced in the after-school program description stating the goal that students will graduate, attain a college diploma, and be successful in their future careers. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom (where used in context), classical mission; college preparation appears as a stated goal in a public program description.
- Negative keywords present: explicit “workforce preparation” signals are present through language about college readiness and career outcomes in the public materials, which can be read as a shift away from formation-for-its-own-sake.
- Ratio: Positive signals (formation/virtue; classical mission) appear along with explicit college prep language; negative indicators (workforce-readiness framing) appear as explicit language in the same materials.

Why this rating
- The public materials strongly emphasize formation and virtue, with clear recognition of college preparation as a goal. The coexistence of formation-focused language with explicit college/career readiness signals places the category in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL, due to a mixed telos.

- Ratio: Positive signals exist, but explicit Great Books/grammar-logic-rhetoric cues are not consistently evidenced in the site content, yielding a mixed signal.

Why this rating
- The curriculum is clearly framed in classical terms, but the explicit, canonical curriculum components (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; continuous history study) are not consistently evidenced in the public-facing site content. This supports a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Category 3.

Some classical cues exist in the broader literature and referenced materials in the knowledge-base, but the West Valley WV2 public content does not foreground the core examples (Socratic dialogue, chanting, memory work, or structured deliberation) that define classical pedagogy. The available sources emphasize a general classical framing and institutional programs rather than the explicit pedagogy. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: none explicitly in the public materials; the presence of “classical-liberal arts” framing is indirect, not pedagogy-specific.
- Negative keywords present: lack of explicit “Socratic,” “Scholé,” “recitation,” “memory work,” or “Socratic seminars” in public-facing content.
- Ratio: Minimal direct positive pedagogy signals vs. explicit negative signals (absence of classical-pedagogy terms). This tilts toward NOT CLASSICAL in the absence of explicit pedagogy declarations, though not because of explicit negative statements.

Why this rating
- Despite a classical framing, the public materials do not present the core pedagogical methods required by the classical rubric (Socratic discussion, scholé, chant/memory, etc.). Therefore Category 4 is NOT CLASSICAL based on the absence of explicit classical-pedagogy signals in the accessible public content.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Category 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Add explicit, publicly accessible articulation of theological grounding or liturgical practices (if aligned with the school’s mission) and clearly identify how transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) are integrated across core disciplines. This would strengthen the Grounded in Theology and Liturgical/Communal Practices signals.
- Category 2 (Purpose and Goal): Balance formation with explicit, clearly stated non-workforce, non-career oriented telos where formation and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty are foregrounded, while still signaling college readiness as a pathway rather than an end in itself. Create a published telos map that emphasizes formation as the primary aim, with college readiness as a subsidiary outcome.
- Category 3 (Curriculum): Publicly publish a concrete curriculum map that names grammar, logic, and rhetoric as core components, and explicitly references Great Books/Great Conversation. Show how history is treated across the curriculum (not as social studies), and demonstrate how wonder and integrated inquiry are cultivated across grades.
- Category 4 (Pedagogy): Introduce explicit references to classical teaching methods (Socratic seminars, memory work, narration/recitation, etc.) with examples from current practice. If possible, pilot a scholé-friendly, contemplative component (restful-learning days) and document outcomes. Provide a public-facing descriptor of how upper-grade seminars function and how students perform rhetoric and debate.
- Category 5 (Faculty): Strengthen the public-facing articulation of the faculty model (mentor-teacher, living curriculum) with explicit faculty portrait statements and a formal "teacher as exemplar" profile. Show teacher development rooted in classical pedagogy (seminars, tutoring, master-teacher model) and publish a teacher-profile framework.
- Category 6 (Parents & Community): deepen and publicize parent-formation activities (book studies, parent seminars, family catechesis) and maintain transparent “parents as co-teachers” messaging with scheduled opportunities for visits and campus involvement.
