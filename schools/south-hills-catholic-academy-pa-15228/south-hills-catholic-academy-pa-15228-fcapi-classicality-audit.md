## Executive Summary

South Hills Catholic Academy (SHCA) presents a strong alignment with classical education principles as articulated in the classical-school rubric. Its public-facing materials emphasize a Traditional Catholic curriculum, formation of the whole person (body, mind, and soul), and a historically rooted liberal-arts approach (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; Christian-history framing). The school explicitly foregrounds Catholic theology, liturgical practice, sacramental life, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative elements with Socratic-discussion-like inquiry in upper grades and ritual/singing in lower grades. Taken together, these signals place SHCA solidly in the CLASSICAL category across all six rubric domains, with especially strong footing in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and Pedagogy, and clear commitments in Faculty and Parents & Community as well.

Key evidentiary threads include: the mission to form the whole person through a Catholic, sacred framework; the explicit use of a Traditional Catholic curriculum and a three-stage liberal-arts pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with Latin; a pedagogical mix of communal liturgy, devotional life, and choir/music programs; a head-of-school emphasis on forming minds to see truth, goodness, and beauty; and explicit parent partnership via a robust PVG (Parent Volunteer Group) structure and ongoing family-engagement initiatives. Citations drawn from SHCA’s public materials show the Catholic, liturgical, and formation-focused stance, as well as the classical-education scaffolding (grammar-logic-rhetoric, Latin, Great Books/Great Conversation language appears in the broader classical language of the movement, with explicit three-stage trivium references in SHCA’s curriculum pages). See evidentiary anchors from SHCA pages (mission, faith formation, liturgy, curriculum, and faculty-parent engagement) and the accompanying knowledge-set guidance on classical education fundamentals.

# ABSTRACT

South Hills Catholic Academy (SHCA) demonstrates a robust CLASSICAL alignment across all six categories of the classical-school rubric. The school presents a Catholic, formation-first purpose with a Traditional Catholic curriculum built on a liberal-arts framework (grammar-logic-rhetoric; Latin; historical/theological framing). Pedagogy blends contemplative and liturgical elements in lower grades with Socratic, seminar-style discourse, and rhetorical practice in upper grades. The faculty is portrayed as virtuous mentors within a living-curriculum aesthetic, and parents/community engagement is institutionalized through a strong PVG and a partner-with-families ethos. Public materials consistently reference the transcendental aims of truth, goodness, and beauty, the theological grounding of Catholic education, and the classical formation of students’ intellects and affections. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL, with no explicit signals in the public-facing materials that would prompt a downgrade to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL or NOT CLASSICAL.
