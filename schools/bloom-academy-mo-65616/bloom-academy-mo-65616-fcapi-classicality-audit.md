## Executive Summary

Bloom Academy (Branson, MO, 65616) presents a visible religious orientation in its publicly available materials, emphasizing formation of students through a Christian framework, worship-related practices, and ministry-focused degree offerings. However, the public materials available in the crawl show limited evidence of core classical elements such as a liberal arts curriculum centered on the trivium/quadrivium, Great Books, or explicit Socratic pedagogy. On balance, the school demonstrates strength in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations and Purpose/Goal, but shows limited alignment with the conventional Classical Curriculum and Classical Pedagogy hallmarks. The aggregate classification, based on the six-category rubric, is NOT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong, with explicit God-centered language, ministry emphasis, and liturgical practice signals (evidence: program descriptions and faith-focused language). Supporting details about worship-oriented practices appear in the public materials (e.g., chapel/worship references).
- Purpose and Goal: Formation and vocational calling framed through a Christian worldview (evidence: “Bloom into who God created them to be,” and ministry-focused aims).
- Curriculum: Not yet aligned with classical liberal-arts structure or Great Books emphasis; current offerings center on ministerial leadership degrees rather than Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, or classical canon (evidence: degree programs and partnerships rather than classical curriculum signals).
- Pedagogy: Public materials emphasize liturgical/community aspects (chapel, worship) and ministry training rather than explicit Socratic seminars, scholé, or memory-based classical methods (evidence: weekly chapel/prayer, ministry training).
- Faculty: Public materials show faculty shaping discipleship and ministry preparation, but explicit statements about teacher as “living curriculum” or mentor-mentee culture are not clearly articulated in the site text (evidence: About Us material).
- Parents & Community: Public materials do not prominently present a formal partnership with families in the classical sense (co-teaching, family formation, or parent education programs) (evidence: minimal explicit parent-education or family-partnership language).
