## Executive Summary

Coppell Classical Academy (TX 75019) presents a clearly classical-inflected orientation in its public materials, with strong emphasis on virtue, formation, and a classical curriculum. The public-facing content signals a mission rooted in forming virtuous citizens and a life-long love of learning, and it references a "classical curriculum" consistent with classical education aims. However, explicit, widely-branded references to core classical elements such as Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, explicit Socratic pedagogy, or the explicit living-curriculum faculty model are not consistently evident on the site. Parent involvement and community partnership are prominently featured, including resources aimed at parents as primary educators. Taken together, Coppell Classical Academy shows a definite classical orientation, but with substantial room for explicit articulation of several foundational classical practices in public materials. Category-by-category, the school earns a mix of CLASSICAL, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, and NOT CLASSICAL signals, with an overall leaning toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Strong CLASSICAL signals in School Purpose/Goal and in Parents & Community, particularly around formation, virtue, and parent-education resources. Evidence citations show explicit statements about forming virtuous citizens and families engaging in classical education together.  
- Moderate CLASSICAL signals in Curriculum and in broader mission; explicit Great Books or traditional Trivium/Rhetoric language is not consistently surfaced in public pages. 
- Mixed signals for Pedagogy and Faculty; explicit Socratic/seminar methods or “living curriculum” statements are not clearly present in the public pages, though faculty roles and school leadership are showcased.

- Positive/Negative keyword analysis

  - Negative keywords observed: none in the public materials accessible here (no direct relativism, secular-neutral language, etc., detected in these pages).
- Why this rating

The presence of virtue and formation pushes toward classical orientation, but the absence of explicit theological-liturgical framing places it in the “somewhat” range rather than fully classical in this category.

The public materials lean toward mission and curriculum framing rather than explicit pedagogy. This supports a NOT CLASSICAL rating for this category in the absence of explicit evidence.

- Make classical pedagogy explicit
  - Publicly articulate the intended pedagogy (e.g., formal Socratic seminars in upper grades, memory work/recitation, and scholé-centered rest in appropriate grades). Include concrete examples and grade-level plans on the website. This would move Element 4 from NOT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.
- Explicitly name the canonical curriculum components
  - Publicly enumerate the core curriculum elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric; history through a Western-civilization lens; Great Books or primary sources; Latin/Greek where applicable). Include a through-line showing how Great Books or their analogs connect to the Great Conversation. This would move Element 3 toward CLASSICAL.
- Strengthen the “living curriculum” framing for Faculty
  - Add public statements about the faculty as the living curriculum, teacher professional-development tracks in classical pedagogy, and portraits of what a teacher and a student should be. This would strengthen Element 5 toward CLASSICAL.
- Expand liturgical/communal elements (if aligned with mission)
  - If appropriate to the school’s mission, introduce liturgical or communal practices (e.g., morning reflection, shared traditions, or liturgical calendar integration) and describe them publicly. This could elevate Element 1 toward CLASSICAL.
- Deepen parental formation and co-teaching language
  - Expand the Parent & Community section to explicitly frame parents as co-teachers with structured family-learning programs, regular book studies, and facilitated gatherings. This would further strengthen Element 6 to CLASSICAL and support an overall shift toward CLASSICAL.
