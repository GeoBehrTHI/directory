## Executive Summary

King's Classical School (Greenville, SC, 29605) presents a robust classical Christian educational model. Public-facing materials repeatedly frame learning through a Christ-centered, classical lens, foregrounding the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), engagement with classic Western literature, and a conviction that education should form character and wisdom in addition to intellect. Students are described as moving through Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric stages, with emphasis on memorization, recitation, singing, discussion, and disciplined rhetorical development. The school stresses virtue, wisdom, and a Christian worldview as the telos of education, and it frames community life as formative through worship, Scripture, prayer, and shared religious practice. Based on the public materials, the school scores CLASSICAL overall, with especially strong alignment in Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, and Pedagogy. Evidence of dedicated faculty culture and parent/community partnership is present but less explicit in some public materials, which leads to a modest deduction in those two categories.

Key evidentiary notes drawn from the school’s materials:
- Core identity and purpose are explicitly Christian and classical: “Christ-centered, classical education,” “To educate students with excellence and virtue through a distinctly Christian worldview,” and an integrated biblical framework across Bible, history, literature, philosophy, mathematics, and the sciences. These statements anchor the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and the School Purpose & Goal categories. 
- Curriculum is clearly Trivium-focused with explicit Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric stages, plus Latin, and engagement with Great Books (the “Great Conversation”) alongside Western literature; there is a strong emphasis on integration of the liberal arts. 
- Pedagogy features singing/chanting in early grades, contemplative/restful elements, and Socratic-style discussion and rhetoric in higher grades; this aligns with classical pedagogy expectations in lower and upper grades. 
- Faculty and leadership language emphasizes community, virtue, and mentorship; there is visible presence of faculty and board pages, with statements about shaping students and forming a community. 
- Community and openness to families is signaled through “Join Our Community,” open-house opportunities, and a sense of partnership through leadership statements, though explicit language about parents as co-educators is less visible than the strongest classical signals. 

Overall classification by category: CLASSICAL.

Note: Where the rubric calls for explicit “positive/negative keyword indicators,” the school’s public materials show clear positive indicators (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; wisdom; Great Conversation; Trivium/Quadrivium; Great Books; liturgical/prayer life) and few, if any, explicit negative signals in these materials. The resulting aggregate ranking is CLASSICAL.

Abstract of the evaluation follows the category-by-category analysis.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower grades: classrooms described as alive with singing, chants, recitation, and memory work; a contemplative/restful element is evident in the pedagogy. 
- Upper grades: discussions, debates, and reasoned exploration guided by teachers; emphasis on identifying sound reasoning and mastering rhetoric. 
- Rhetoric stage emphasizes clear, persuasive, and gracious communication; students write and speak with clarity and conviction. 
- The public materials reference the “great conversation” in literature and philosophy and engage with primary sources, aligning with Socratic-style inquiry and dialectic in higher grades. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present (sample): singing/chanting, narration/recitation, memory work, scholé/restful learning, Socratic discussion, seminars, rhetoric, oratory, debates, great books dialogue, memory-based repetition, chanting. (From the school’s pedagogy language and the Six Elements’ positive signals for pedagogy.)
- Negative keywords present: The materials do not emphasize purely experiential, project-based, or student-led constructivist methods; no explicit heavy use of those terms is observed in the public materials, which aligns with classical methods rather than non-classical project-based approaches.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates a multi-stage pedagogy combining restfulness and memory work in the early years with Socratic/seminal discussion and advanced rhetoric in later years, which fits the classical pedagogy framework well.

Citations: Lower/Grammar stage music and memory; logic stage discussion; rhetoric stage; Great Conversation.

Why this rating

Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is warranted for this category.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school increases explicit, explicit articulation of parents as co-educators (e.g., a formal statement about parental partnership, home catechesis, parent study groups, and family formation efforts) with concrete programs and timelines, Element 6 would move toward CLASSICAL.

- If the school further foregrounds a documented “living curriculum” language about teachers as models of virtue and mentorship in public-facing materials (e.g., explicit portraits of teacher formation, mentorship structures, and professional formation), Element 5 would move toward CLASSICAL.

- If additional explicit language or artifacts show a deeper embrace of reverent liturgical practices and sacramental formation as a consistent curricular thread (e.g., regular liturgical scheduling, chapel calendars, and liturgical year integration in more pages), Element 1 would more fully saturate the religious-metaphysical foundation, reinforcing the CLASSICAL rating.
