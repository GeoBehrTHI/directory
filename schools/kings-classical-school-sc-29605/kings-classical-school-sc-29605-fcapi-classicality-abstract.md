## ABSTRACT

King's Classical School (Greenville, SC, 29605) demonstrates strong CLASSICAL alignment across categories 1–4, with Element 5 also aligning well and Element 6 aligning as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to explicit community signals but less explicit parent-educator co-teaching articulation in public materials. The school presents a Christ-centered, classical curriculum built on the Trivium and Quadrivium, integration of Great Books and classical canon, and pedagogy that blends memory-based learning with Socratic-style discussion and rhetorical training. The faculty emphasis on mentorship and a community of learners supports the Classical ideal, while parent-communication and community partnership are evident but not as strongly codified as the other elements.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

- Primary drivers: 
  - Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: Christ-centered, theological grounding, liturgical/worship integration, and the Great Conversation in the classical sense.   
  - School Purpose & Goal: Formation, wisdom, virtue, and a teleological view of truth/beauty.  
  - Curriculum: Trivium and Quadrivium; Great Books/Great Conversation; Latin; integrated history/literature/philosophy.  
  - Pedagogy: Singing/chanting in early years; memory work; Socratic-style discussion and rhetoric in upper grades. 
  - Faculty: Leadership language about cultivating a known, thriving community; presence of faculty bios and a board. 
  - Parents & Community: Community openness and events; call to inquiry; open-house opportunities; though explicit “parents as co-teachers” language is less visible. 

Key sources used:
- School public materials describing Christian classical pedagogy, Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, and a liturgical life that weaves Scripture into daily learning. 
- Mission statements and “Our Philosophy” pages emphasizing a Christian foundation and a teleological formation of intellect and virtue. 
- Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric stage descriptions and “Join Our Community” language highlighting ritual, worship, and public-facing community life. 
- Leadership statements about forming a community where every student is known and valued, and the existence of faculty and board profiles. 

End of ABSTRACT.
