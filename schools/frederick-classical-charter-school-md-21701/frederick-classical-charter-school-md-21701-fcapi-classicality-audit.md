## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — ABSTRACT

Frederick Classical Charter School Md 21701 demonstrates a predominantly classical orientation in curriculum and pedagogy, with clear alignment to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and memory-based, Socratic teaching strategies. The school emphasizes liberal arts content and a public, classical education ethos, and it fosters strong parent/community involvement.

- The strongest classical aspects are:
  - Curriculum (Element 3): explicit classical framing with grammar, logic, rhetoric; liberal arts emphasis; theoretical alignment with Great Books via classical tradition (supported by public statements about “classical methods”).  
  - Pedagogy (Element 4): use of Socratic and memory/recitation-based approaches corresponding to classical pedagogy (as described in the canonical classical education framework). 
  - Parents & Community (Element 6): strong parent partnership and family involvement, with explicit emphasis on parent engagement and volunteer programs. 

- Areas for growth:
  - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations (Element 1): no explicit religious/metaphysical grounding is evident in public materials; a more explicit metaphysical framing would strengthen classical alignment.
  - Faculty (Element 5): there is no explicit articulation of teachers as the living curriculum or a portrait of teacher/student role models in FCCS public content; developing a “faculty as living curriculum” narrative would strengthen this dimension.
  - Some aspects of the School Purpose/Goal (Element 2): while the school strongly emphasizes classical liberal arts education, the explicit emphasis on college-focused preparation (a modern workforce/college-readiness emphasis) introduces tension with classical telos; addressing this balance could move the school toward stronger classical alignment.

- General assessment: The school is CLASSICAL overall, with particular strength in Curriculum and Pedagogy and strong family/community engagement. The notable gaps in metaphysical foundations and explicit faculty formation temper the overall classical alignment but do not negate the substantial classical footprint.

Abstraction and Big Picture Note:

- The FCCS materials reveal a classical core (Trivium-based curriculum, memory/recitation pedagogy, liberal arts emphasis, and family involvement) but with secular public-school framing that omits explicit metaphysical directions and a fully articulated “living curriculum” faculty model. The aggregate shows a CLASSICAL orientation with growth opportunities, especially in the metaphysical/founding language and explicit faculty formation.
