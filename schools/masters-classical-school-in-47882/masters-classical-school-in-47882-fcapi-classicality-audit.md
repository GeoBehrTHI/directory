Category-by-category, the school largely meets Classical criteria across Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The Pedagogy category shows strong classical tendencies (teacher-led, structured classical program) but lacks explicit public mention of some hallmark Socratic/seminar practices andScholé-style contemplative routines in the site’s public materials, which places Element 4 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious foundation is explicit and central, with Christ-centric mission, Bible-based truth, and weekly chapel routines cited on-site. This strongly supports category 1.
- The school presents formation and virtue as primary goals, with references to character, beauty, truth, and the classical telos of human flourishing, supporting category 2.
- Curriculum is clearly classical (Memoria Press, Latin, Greco-Roman emphasis, regular use of classical texts and authors), supporting category 3.
- Pedagogy emphasizes a teacher-led, ordered classical environment with a strong academic framework, though explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy and Scholé/restful-learning language are not directly itemized in public content; this yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for category 4.
- Faculty are framed as exemplars and the living curriculum; the site presents a defined leadership/ Headmaster presence aligned with classical ideals, supporting category 5.
- Parents & Community are positioned as co-teachers and partners, with admissions info inviting visits and community involvement, supporting category 6.

What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases):

- Strengthen and publicize a precise “living curriculum” narrative with explicit faculty portraits and teacher formation programs, if not already present, to solidify category 5.
