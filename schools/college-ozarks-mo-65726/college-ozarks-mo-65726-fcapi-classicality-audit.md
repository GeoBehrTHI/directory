## Executive Summary

School Ozarks Mo 65726 (the College of the Ozarks’ School of the Ozarks lineage) presents a clearly Christian, liberal-arts oriented profile with visible classical elements, particularly in its stated mission, pillars, and history as a “classical laboratory high school” that later expanded to K–12 within the College’s ecosystem. Public materials emphasize Christian formation, virtue, and character, and highlight a living-curriculum approach led by faculty who are described as moral exemplars. The school also communicates a strong partnership stance with families, open campus opportunities, and parent covenant ideas. Taken together, the materials display a Classical orientation in several core elements (Religious Foundations, Faculty, and certain aspects of Curriculum and Community), but with notable gaps in explicit classical pedagogy (e.g., lack of clearly named Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric sequencing, or overt Socratic pedagogy across grades). Overall, the evaluation ranks the school as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL leaning, with a stronger leaning toward CLASSICAL in the Religious/Foundational and Faculty areas and more limited explicit classical pedagogical signaling in Pedagogy and Curriculum details.

- Executive snapshot: The strongest classical signals exist in Religious/Metaphysical Foundations and Faculty, with clear Christian formation and living-teacher emphasis. The Curriculum signals show liberal arts grounding and history, but fewer explicit Great Books or classic-trivium signals. Pedagogy signals are weaker (no explicit Socratic/seminar practice documented in public materials). Parent/community signals show openness to families and partnerships, but explicit “parents as co-teachers” language is less pervasive than the strongest classical exemplars. Aggregate impression: CLASSICAL in core ethos, with meaningful Classical signals in some domains and partial signals in others.

- What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases only): If the school aimed to strengthen to a full CLASSICAL profile, it could explicitly foreground Great Books and a formal grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, expand or institutionalize Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, and codify explicit parent-teacher partnership practices as core policy beyond open-campus opportunities.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If this school seeks to move from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL toward FULL CLASSICAL, consider:
 - Explicitly codifying and publicly communicating a Great Books orientation within the Curriculum category (e.g., a published Great Books list, primary sources, and a formal “Great Conversation” framework).
 - Establishing a clear, documented grammar–logic–rhetoric progression, including Latin/Greek options, and explicit signs of Socratic pedagogy and seminars at high school levels (or a stated path from K–8 to 12 with Socratic elements starting in 9–12).
 - Making Pedagogy signals explicit on the website: documented use of Socratic seminars, narration/recitation pathways, and memory-work structures as a formalized practice across grade bands.
 - Strengthening Parent and Community signals with formal “co-teaching” language, parent study groups, and published policies that describe parents as co-educators with specific roles, not just open-campus access.
 - Increasing emphasis on universal “permanent things” language and the triad of truth, goodness, and beauty in mission statements and curriculum maps (to echo transcendental realisms and the telos of classical education).

These changes would align more completely with the positive keyword indicators and address the gaps between a robust Christian liberal-arts program and a fully realized classical pedagogy.
