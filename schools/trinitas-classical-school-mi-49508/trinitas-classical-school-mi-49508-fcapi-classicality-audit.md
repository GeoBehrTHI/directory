## Executive Summary

Trinitas Classical School (Mi 49508) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning model that centers virtue, truth, and beauty within a Reformed/Christian-theological framework. Public-facing materials emphasize formation over mere information, a Trivium-oriented curriculum, active parental partnership, and a mentorship ethos embodied by a “classical education” mission. The presence of Philippians 4:8 as a guiding verse, explicit language about virtue and formation, and a Chapel-focused culture all signal strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and a mission oriented toward formation rather than job-prep. Public materials also articulate a commitment to a screen-free community and parental partnership, reinforcing a robust Parents & Community axis.

However, while Trinitas clearly communicates classical aims (notably the Trivium and a Christian formation focus), some elements of the full classical-education rubric—such as explicit, sustained Great Books engagement, a fully articulated Socratic pedagogy beyond occasional reference, and a fully explicit “living curriculum” in faculty practice—are less overt in the available public-facing content. As a result, the school leans solidly CLASSICAL overall, with specific areas that appear MOSTLY CLASSICAL or SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully explicit in every dimension.

Key takeaway: Trinitas is CLASSICAL overall, with strongest signals in religious/metaphysical foundations, formation as purpose, and parental/community partnership. The strongest growth opportunities relate to deeper articulation of curriculum (Great Books / Great Conversation), explicit pedagogical methods (Socratic/Scholé practices), and a more explicit living-faculty narrative.
