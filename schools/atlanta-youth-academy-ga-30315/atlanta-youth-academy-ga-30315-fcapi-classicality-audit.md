## Executive Summary

Atlanta Youth Academy (AYA) presents a clearly Christian, Christ-centered approach to schooling with explicit theological framing, daily prayer, and a mission oriented toward Christian formation and service. Public-facing materials emphasize a biblically-based worldview, prayerful culture, and small-class attention, alongside a mission to prepare students for high-school and college within a faith-centered context. However, the site does not clearly articulate foundational elements that classical education definitions expect in curriculum structure (notably the Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books/Great Conversation, or explicit Trivium-based progression), and evidence of Socratic pedagogy or a traditional "living curriculum" is indirect at best. Taken together, the school demonstrates a strong classical-leaning orientation in spirit and religious foundations, but the explicit classical curricular-pedagogical infrastructure (as defined by the Six Essential Elements) is not fully present on public materials. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear potential to move toward CLASSICAL through explicit incorporation of Great Books, Trivium-based curriculum, Socratic pedagogy, and a stated “Great Conversation” approach.

- Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot highlights:
    - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL (strong explicit theology, prayer, Christian worldview).
    - School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (formation/virtue emphasis present, but explicit classical telos and liberal-arts orientation are less explicit).
    - Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL (curriculum described is standard/private-school with Bible-focused content; no explicit Great Books or Seven Liberal Arts stated).
    - Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (some contemplative/restful elements implied; no explicit Socratic/seminar emphasis documented).
    - Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (faculty as mentors implied; living curriculum not explicitly articulated).
    - Parents & Community: CLASSICAL (explicit partnership with parents, family involvement, and public-facing openness).

Key evidentiary anchors include: explicit Christian/theological framing and daily prayer; Christ-centered mission; Bible-based curriculum elements; a strong emphasis on character formation, virtue, and servant-leadership; the Beck Library and reading initiatives; and parent/community engagement through events and open processes. Citations to the school’s public materials and the classical-education lexicon are provided inline with the relevant claims.
