## Executive Summary

Veritas Academy of Tucson presents a robust Classical Christian program built on a University-Model Schooling framework. Public-facing materials consistently emphasize Christian, classical education, a Trivium-based curriculum, strong parental involvement, and a biblically grounded worldview. The school explicitly identifies as Christian, classical, and university-model, with stated aims to form virtuous learners who engage with great books and a historically rooted curriculum. These characteristics align strongly with classical education norms, and the school’s own phrasing and curricular choices support a CLASSICAL designation across multiple rubrics. Evidence from the site highlights: a Christian worldview anchored in Scripture and theology (Bible, Christ, God) and classical elements (Trivium, Great Books, Latin, Western history), plus a public-facing model that foregrounds parental partnership and a college-preparatory cadence within the UMS framework. Some elements that could further strengthen the classical profile (and thus push categories toward a fuller, more explicit “classical” signal in every dimension) include more explicit liturgical/community practices within formal instruction and clearer articulation of the “living curriculum” language in public copy. Overall, Veritas Academy Tucson is best characterized as CLASSICAL, with strong fidelity to core classical norms and a clear, coherent pathway for growth toward deeper classical-school embodiment.

What would move the needle

  - Amplify explicit liturgical/community practices in public pages to more clearly signal liturgy and communal worship as formal educational components (beyond general Christian formation). This would strengthen Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and link more directly to liturgical-communal practices in the lexicon.
  - Make the “living curriculum” language explicit in public materials (e.g., teacher-mentorship language and faculty-as-living-curriculum statements) to further solidify Element 5 (Faculty) signals.
  - Increase explicit documentation of Socratic seminars, dialectic discussions, and upper-level rhetorical projects as formal, visible pedagogy in public content to reinforce Element 4 (Pedagogy) with sharper public signals of Socratic discourse and rhetoric as a continuous thread through K-12.
  - While the Great Books and Trivium elements are present, a public-facing, recurring articulation of a formal “Great Conversation” cadence across grades (e.g., scheduled Great Books units, primary-source emphasis) would push Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) further toward a fully explicit classical profile.
