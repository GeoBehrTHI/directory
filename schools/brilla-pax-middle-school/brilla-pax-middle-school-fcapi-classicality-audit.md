## Executive Summary

Brilla Pax Middle School shows a partial alignment with classical education principles. Public materials from the Brilla Pax site emphasize a "virtue-centered" and "classical approach" to education, with references to rigorous curricula, authentic texts, and a blended-learning/co-teaching model. These signals indicate a classical orientation in several domains (notably through formation of character and adoption of classical framing). However, explicit classical elements that define a fully classical program—such as a stated seven liberal arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; trivium; quadrivium), a rigorous Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis, explicit Socratic pedagogy, or a “living curriculum” as the core of faculty and parent partnerships—are not robustly documented in the public materials reviewed. The school notes a transition from 5th grade in 2025 to grades 5–8, and it frames its mission around virtue-centered education and college readiness, rather than an exclusively formation-first or Great Books-centric model.

Overall, the school lands as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL across category criteria, with stronger signals in categories tied to character formation and classical framing, but weaker signals in areas that require explicit Great Books/Trivium/Scholé pedagogy, liturgical or theological grounding, and expansive parent/community classical practices. This yields an aggregate rating of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Executive insights per category (category-by-category scoring is explained in detail below). The Brilla Pax materials cited include: the public Brilla Pax Middle School description and related Brilla Pax Elementary/Middle School content, which describe a virtue-centered, academically rigorous, classically oriented program and a blended/co-teaching model. See the Brilla Pax materials for evidence of the site’s stated aims and pedagogy.  

What would move the needle for Brilla Pax toward a fuller CLASSICAL classification is explicit, public articulation of: a Seven Liberal Arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Trivium; Quadrivium), robust Great Books/Great Conversation commitments, explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, a stated liturgical/communal or theological framework, a clear “living curriculum” model for faculty, and a formal, public commitment to parent-as-co-educator partnerships. These steps would strengthen alignment with the Six Essential Elements framework and its keyword indicators.

- What would move the needle (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL → CLASSICAL)
  - Publicly articulate and implement a Seven Liberal Arts-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; with Quadrivium components) and a formal Great Books program.
  - Publish evidence of Socratic/dialogic pedagogy in upper grades and memory/recitation components in lower grades.
  - Establish a liturgical or theological grounding (or clearly articulate how theology informs other disciplines) and reference liturgical/community practices in school life.
  - Feature explicit “living curriculum” statements about the faculty as mentors and living exemplars, with parent-partnership language (parents as co-teachers) and predictable community engagement.
  - Increase parent-facing programming that educates parents about classical education (book studies, lecture series, “family formation” activities), and welcome open campus visits as a regular practice.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly publish and implement a Seven Liberal Arts-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; with Quadrivium considerations) and a robust Great Books/Great Conversation program, with public-facing syllabi and sample year-by-year sequences.
- Publicly document and operationalize a Socratic pedagogy framework in upper grades (Socratic seminars, dialectic discussions) and memory/recitation practices (Scholé-inspired routines) as core teaching methods, with classroom examples and rubrics.
- Establish explicit liturgical/theological grounding (even if broadly Christian) and reference liturgical/community practices (chapel, prayer, liturgical calendar) as part of daily life and curriculum—clearly linking theology to other disciplines as a Mistress Science.
- Build a formal “living curriculum” model for faculty, including mentorship, teacher-formation programs, faculty seminars, and living-virtue exemplars, with public documentation of teacher profiles that highlight virtue and mastery.
- Develop and publicize a robust Parent & Community program (parent formation, classical book clubs, parent visitation policies, and co-teaching partnerships) with explicit language about parents as co-educators and school-community partnerships.
- Enhance transparency around “College and Career Readiness” language to align with classical aims: reframe as “human flourishing through liberal education” rather than primarily job-readiness, or clearly articulate a classical telos that integrates both formation and career preparation harmoniously.
- Expand language on the Great Conversation, Great Books, and classical canon in public-facing pages to signal a fuller classical curriculum to families and accrediting bodies.
