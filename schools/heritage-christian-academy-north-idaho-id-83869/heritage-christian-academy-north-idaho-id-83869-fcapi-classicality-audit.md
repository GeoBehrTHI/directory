## Executive Summary

Heritage Christian Academy (North Idaho, 83869) presents itself as a Christ-centered private school grounded in biblical worldview and founded on a community-centric, formation-focused approach. Public-facing materials consistently emphasize faith integration across subjects, a family-partnered model, and a distinctive four-day-week schedule designed to support family life, spiritual growth, and disciplined academics. The school highlights small class sizes, devoted faculty, and a mission to form students’ character in alignment with Christian virtue and truth. Based on the public materials in the school’s crawl and the classical-education rubric in the knowledge base, Heritage Christian Academy shows strong alignment with core religious and community aspects, and with formation-oriented aims, while exhibiting more modest alignment with classical liberal-arts methods (e.g., absence (so far) of explicit Great Books / Trivium-based curriculum in public materials). Overall, the school is ranked CLASSICAL, with notable strength in religious foundations, formation-focused aims, and community/family partnership, and partial (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) alignment in curriculum specifics. The strongest needle-moving opportunities would be to explicitly articulate a liberal-arts scope (grammar, logic, rhetoric), engage with Great Books or Great Conversation concepts, and document any Socratic pedagogy for upper grades to deepen alignment with the classical model.

Key evidence anchors:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: explicit Christ-centeredness, biblical worldview in every subject, daily scripture/prayer emphasis, and a mission to honor God in learning.   
- Purpose and Goal: formation and virtue orientation, preparation to defend Christian faith, and a life-formation emphasis rather than mere job-prep.  
- Curriculum: biblically-grounded curriculum; use of church-adjacent curriculum providers (e.g., BJU Press) and emphasis on Type to Learn for digital literacy; no explicit Great Books / Trivium mention in the public materials reviewed.  
- Pedagogy: four-day-week with longer days cited as a structural pedagogy choice; emphasis on focused academics and spiritual growth; limited public evidence of Socratic seminars or classical dialogues in the public materials. 
- Faculty: teachers as committed, Christ-centered professionals; small classes; living-contact emphasis; close-knit community. 
- Parents & Community: explicit partnership with families; welcoming parents for visits; community-focused and parent-engaged approach. 

Note: The analysis relies on the school’s public-facing crawl results and the classical rubric’s keyword indicators from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) knowledge base.

- Negative keywords present: none detected in public materials (no secular or nonsectarian terminology observed). The Six Elements document lists secular/neutral terms as Negative Keywords, which do not appear in Heritage materials reviewed here. 

Why this rating
- The school’s materials consistently foreground theology and liturgical/community practices aligned with a religious-metaphysical foundation. The prevalence of biblical worldview and Christ-centered language, supported by mission statements and daily prayer/scripture emphasis, strongly supports CLASSICAL in this category.

Hence SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL best captures the category: formation-focused and truth-centered, but not fully aligned with classical curriculum articulation in public materials.

The public materials emphasize biblical integration and Christian-centered curricula rather than the classical canon and liberal-arts framework. 
- Negative keywords present: absence of Great Books, Great Conversation, Seven Liberal Arts, and explicit Trivium/Quadrivium language in public materials; these would be Negative Keywords in the Classical rubric if present, and their absence lowers alignment. 

Why this rating

The school’s public materials point to a biblically-focused program and external curriculum provider rather than a classical liberal-arts core. Therefore, NOT CLASSICAL is warranted for Element 3.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The most explicit pedagogical feature publicly described is the four-day school week with longer daily hours, aimed at focused academics and family balance, rather than a stated set of classical pedagogical methods (e.g., Socratic seminars, debates, memory-focused recitation, or rhetoric training in upper grades). 
- There is some emphasis on memory and singing in lower grades in the classical rubric, but public Heritage materials do not cite singing/chanting or Socratic discussion in lower/upper grades; rather they emphasize faith integration, smaller classes, and teacher mentorship. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: contemplation or restfulness in education is a listed possibility in classical pedagogy; but the Heritage site does not explicitly promote contemplative pedagogy as a hallmark—rather it emphasizes structure (four-day week), focused academics, and spiritual growth. The direct classical terms (Socratic seminars, rhetoric training in upper grades, Great Conversation) are not present in public materials. 
- Negative keywords present: absence of explicit Socratic discourse, seminars, or rhetoric-based upper-level training; lack of explicit commitment to a traditional classical pedagogy signals weaker alignment. 

Why this rating
- The public-facing pedagogical language centers on structure, faith integration, and community orientation rather than explicit classical pedagogical methods (Socratic seminars, rigorous rhetoric training, or a pattern of memory-based pedagogy). Given this absence of the canonical classical pedagogy signals, Element 4 is NOT CLASSICAL.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 (Purpose and Goal): Introduce explicit language and programming around liberal-arts formation, including explicit articulation of a goal structure centered on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), and connect this to Great Books or classic texts wherever possible. This would strengthen the formation-centered aim with explicit classical curriculum rails.
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly articulate a liberal-arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and, where appropriate, Great Books or Great Conversation components; show alignment with interdisciplinary integration and wonder through explicit program descriptions and sample syllabi. This would elevate the curriculum to a more robust CLASSICAL alignment.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Incorporate explicit classical pedagogical modalities (Socratic discussion, seminars, rhetoric instruction for upper grades) and evidence of contemplative, memory-theory, or chant-based practices in appropriate grade bands to align more with classical pedagogy signals.

Note: Since the school already demonstrates strong religious foundations, formation focus, and family/community engagement, these needle-moving items would align the public materials even more closely with classical education norms.
