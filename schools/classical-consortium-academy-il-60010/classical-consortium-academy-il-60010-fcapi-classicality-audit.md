## Executive Summary

Classical Consortium Academy (CCA) in Illinois demonstrates strong alignment with the core tenets of classical Christian education as defined by the six essential elements rubric. Public-facing materials show explicit commitments to a Christian worldview, formation of virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The curriculum centers on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and, in upper levels, exposes students to the Quadrivium and Great Books through Omnibus-style courses. Pedagogy combines contemplative, memory-based practices (circle time, memory work, liturgical rhythm) with Socratic discussion and rhetoric in the upper grades. The faculty are presented as virtuous, mentoring teachers who form the living curriculum, and parents are positioned as partners in the education process. The school operates on a hybrid model across two campuses (Barrington and Glenview), with a strong emphasis on community, liturgy, and theological formation.

Overall classification by category: CLASSICAL in all six categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community). This assessment draws on explicit statements of faith, formation-focused aims, the integrated classical curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin; Omnibus/Great-Books approach), Socratic and memory-based pedagogy, a culture of virtuous living modeled by faculty, and active partnership with families.

- Evidence for the overall CLASSICAL classification is drawn from the school’s Vision & Values, Distinctives, and Academics materials, including explicit statements about Truth/God/Beauty, formation and virtue, Great Books/Omnibus across grades, and a teacher-led living curriculum. Examples include: “God is at the center of all learning and our ultimate source of Truth… see the Good, the True, and the Beautiful” and the own-faith commitments in the Statement of Faith; a two-campus setting with a structured four-level trivium progression; Omnibus History/Literature and Latin offerings; and explicit mention of Socratic discussion and rhetoric in upper grades.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- A blend of contemplative/restful learning (Scholé) and rigorous inquiry is described; lower grades emphasize singing, memorization, and circle-time prayer, while upper grades engage in Socratic discussion, seminars, debates, and rhetoric.   
- The pedagogy explicitly includes Socratic dialogue/seminars in higher levels; rhetoric training and public speaking are part of the upper grades (Rhetoric Level) with opportunities for speeches and theses. 
- The program emphasizes memorization, chreia, progymnasmata (early rhetorical exercises), and Latin chants as a memory/discipline foundation. 
- The Omnibus/Integrated approach creates cross-disciplinary discussions, primary texts, and the “great conversation” around foundational texts. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue, seminars, debates, rhetoric, memorization/recitation, chanting, scholé, contemplative learning, liturgical rhythm, master-teacher modeling, living curriculum, circles/prayer, “great conversation,” and integration of faith with study.    
- Negative keywords observed: none of the documented material shows a predominant reliance on purely project-based or constructivist-only pedagogy; rather, there is a balanced mix with direct memory work and Socratic inquiry. The public materials do not emphasize a solitary, purely student-directed approach.

Why this rating
- The school employs a classical pedagogy that harmonizes contemplative/restful learning with rigorous analytical discussion and public-speaking training. The explicit presence of the trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic seminars, and memorization-based instruction supports a CLASSICAL rating.
