## Executive Summary

Logos Christian Academy NV 89406 (Fallon, NV) presents a robust Christian classical orientation, anchored in explicit theology, formation aims, and a developing classical pedagogy. Public materials emphasize a Christ-centered epistemology (the Logos as source of true knowledge), a liturgical cadence (chapel and worship-oriented culture), and a commitment to Latin study and a traditional, teacher-centered approach. While these elements align with several core features of classical education, the public materials show more explicit emphasis on theology, formation, and specific pedagogical steps (e.g., pre-logic to logic and rhetoric) than on a fully articulated liberal-arts curriculum or a complete Great Books-based program. As a result, Logos Fallon sits toward the classical spectrum but stops short of full, explicit implementation of all six elements in the classical rubric. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with strong CLASSICAL elements in religious foundation, pedagogy, and faculty, and partial alignment in curriculum breadth and parent/community articulation.

Positive indicators exist (formation, virtue, human flourishing, telos in a classical sense), but the Great-Conversation-like framing or explicit articulation of truth-goodness-beauty as guiding ideals is not consistently spelled out across the site copy.

Overall aggregate assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The school demonstrates strong CLASSICAL features in religious/metaphysical foundations, pedagogy, and faculty, and robust formation-oriented goals, with visible elements of Latin study and liturgical life. However, the curriculum breadth (full liberal-arts framework and Great Books/Great Conversation in explicit terms) and the depth of parent/community formation are less clearly articulated in the public materials. Taken together, Logos Fallon sits in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL category overall.

What Would Move the Needle

- Make Great Books and Great Conversation explicit: Publish a clearly stated curriculum map that includes the Great Books/Great Conversation framework, with specific texts, reading schedules, and discussion formats across grades K–12.
- Expand explicit liberal-arts articulation: Include a public, explicit statement of the grammar, logic, rhetoric sequence (and how it relates to the quadrivium/trivium) and show how history is woven through the curriculum as a liberal-arts continuum rather than social-studies framing.
- Strengthen parental formation and co-teaching narratives: Publicly articulate a robust Parents & Community section that describes co-teaching partnerships, parent book clubs, family liturgical practices, and open-campus visitation in line with classical expectations.
- Clarify the Great-Conversation language across materials: Use explicit phrases such as “great books,” “great conversation,” and “permanent questions” to reflect a thorough classical formation ethos.
- Bolster liturgical and communal practices as an integrated pedagogy: Provide concrete examples of liturgical calendar integration, sacred-m music/narration in lower grades, and how chant or hymns reinforce memory and virtue in daily practice.
