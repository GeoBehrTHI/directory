## Executive Summary

Valiant Cross Academy (Montgomery, AL 36104) presents a hybrid profile of classical-leaning formation with strong religious/community emphasis, but with several modern, non-classical elements (notably project-based pedagogy and CCRS-aligned curriculum). Taken as a whole, the public materials demonstrate clear religious foundations, character formation aims, and active parent/community engagement, but they also reveal a curriculum and pedagogy that diverge from a fully classical model (e.g., limited explicit Great Books emphasis, explicit Trivium/Quadrivium framing, and an intentional integration of vocational/workforce orientation). The aggregate assessment is MOSTLY CLASSICAL with notable gaps, i.e., SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with strong elements in Religous Foundations, Formation/Community orientation, and Living Mentorship, but weaker evidence for a fully classical curriculum and pedagogy.

Key supporting signals:
- Religious foundations are explicit: private Christian school with weekly chapel and theology-inflected language in admissions and mission statements. This supports Element 1 (CLASSICAL). See About/About-page-style language and weekly chapel reference.  
- Formation and virtue-language is present (character formation, leadership, community service, “rise above,” etc.), supporting Element 2 (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL depends on depth). Evidence includes mission/vision statements and statements about character leadership.  
- Curriculum signals modern standards alignment and project-based approaches, with explicit CCRS alignment and emphasis on differentiated instruction and project-based learning. This shows Element 3 (NOT CLASSICAL) tendencies, given the paucity of explicit Great Books/Trivium framing in public materials. 
- Pedagogy shows some classical indicators (memory/recitation language appears in the classical lexicon) but the site emphasizes experiential learning, arts, and community engagement rather than a fully Scholé/Socratic rhythm. Element 4 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty signals include listed teachers, administrators, and leaders, with a strong “living curriculum” feel in materials about leadership, faculty, and administration. Element 5 is CLASSICAL.
- Parent/community signals are robust (open houses, admissions, parent coordination, community events, and fundraising), aligning Element 6 with CLASSICAL parent-partnership expectations. 

Overall ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with several CLASSICAL sub-panels (Religious Foundations, Faculty, Parents/Community) and at least one NOT CLASSICAL indicator (Curriculum) pulling the aggregate toward a mixed-but-positive classical orientation.
