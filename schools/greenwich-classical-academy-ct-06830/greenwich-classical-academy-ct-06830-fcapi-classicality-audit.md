## Executive Summary

Greenwich Academy (ZIP 06830) publicly emphasizes character formation, leadership development, and a robust, interdisciplinary private-school program with strong language study (including Latin) and extensive co-curricular offerings. While Latin and some cross-disciplinary practices appear, the materials do not consistently reveal the core Great Books/Trivium-based curriculum, explicit Socratic pedagogy, or scholé (restful) teaching as foundational methods. Based on the presence/absence of the Six Essential Elements indicators and the school’s publicly stated priorities, this evaluation classifies Greenwich Academy as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall. The school shows several classical-leaning signals (notably Latin and leadership-focused formation, some integration of curricula and public-purpose activities), but it lacks pervasive evidence of a fully classical liturgical-theological base, deep Great Books engagement, and sustained Socratic/seminar pedagogy across grades.

Key drivers for the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating:
- Positive classical indicators present: Latin study, some cross-disciplinary programs, emphasis on virtue/character through leadership-oriented initiatives, public purpose, and a living-faculty/mentor-type ethos in advisory structures. Evidence includes explicit Latin coursework in the language program and a characterization of character and leadership as central to GA’s mission and programs.  
- Gaps relative to a full classical model: Limited explicit references to Great Books, a traditional Trivium/Quadrivium framework, or sustained Socratic pedagogy; scarcity of liturgical/theological foundations in public materials; pedagogy evidence is more contemporary and leadership- and wellness-oriented than strictly scholé or Socratic. These gaps anchor the assessment away from CLASSICAL and toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle

- Explicit Great Books / Great Conversation integration: Publicly enumerate a Great Books program and cross-disciplinary Great Conversation elements (e.g., assign canonical texts and structured discussions across divisions). 
- Codify and publish a Scholé-based pedagogy: Include restful learning cycles, memorization/recitation as appropriate, and a commitment to slower, contemplative pedagogy across key grades.
- Strengthen Socratic / seminar-based pedagogy in upper grades: Introduce regular Socratic seminars (Harkness-style discussions) for core humanities and integrate rhetoric training to culminate in meaningful student orations and public speaking.
- Expand and publicly articulate the Trivium/Quadrivium framework: Anchor the curriculum in grammar, logic, rhetoric as a scaffold with math/science/arts aligned to a Quadrivium progression, making the classical structure explicit on the website.
- Deepen liturgical/theological grounding (where appropriate for the school’s mission): If the school intends to maintain a classical, transcendent framework, consider a defined religious/metaphysical strand or clearly explained compatible rituals, rituals of learning, or communal practices that align with the classical tradition.
- Elevate the "faculty living curriculum" language: Publish a formal portrait of a teacher and explicit statements about faculty-as-living-curriculum, mentorship roles, and teacher formation in classical pedagogy.
- Strengthen parent formation and co-teaching language: Provide more explicit opportunities for parental formation (book studies, classical education seminars) and confirm an open-campus approach with defined co-teaching expectations.
