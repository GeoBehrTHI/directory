## ABSTRACT

Greenwich Academy’s public-facing materials exhibit a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL orientation. The school demonstrates classical-leaning signals such as a Latin language requirement and a governance/formation emphasis centered on character and leadership, as well as a strong emphasis on community engagement and parental involvement. However, explicit classical infrastructure—such as a formal Great Books program, a clearly defined Trivium/Quadrivium curriculum, and established Socratic/seminar pedagogy across the upper grades—is not consistently evident in the accessible public materials. Consequently, while the school aligns with several classical education values, it does not present a fully classical program according to the Six Essential Elements framework. The most pronounced classical signals appear under Curriculum (Latin) and School Purpose/Goal (formation through leadership and public purpose), with secondary signals in Faculty and Parents/Community through advisory structures and community engagement initiatives. The Pedagogy category lacks explicit scholé/Socratic foundations in the public materials, and the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations category shows minimal explicit theological or liturgical grounding.
