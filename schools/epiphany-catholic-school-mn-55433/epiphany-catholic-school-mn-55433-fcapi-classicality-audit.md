## Executive Summary

Epiphany Catholic School MN 55433 presents a robust alignment with classical education across most of the Six Essential Elements framework. The school demonstrates a clear religious and metaphysical foundations anchored in Catholic doctrine and liturgy, a formation-centered purpose, a curriculum that foregrounds liberal arts and classical literature, a pedagogy that fuses contemplative elements with memory-work and structured religious formation, a faculty described as virtuous mentors, and an active parental partnership anchored in faith formation and parish collaboration. The strongest signals are Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents & Community, with exceptional alignment to classical aims. The Pedagogy category shows meaningful classical features (especially through Catechesis of the Good Shepherd and a classical curriculum), but the site’s explicit articulation of upper-school Socratic seminars or full rhetoric-based capstone instruction is less evident, which places Element 4 in the Somewhat Classical tier rather than fully Classical. Overall, Epiphany Catholic School MN 55433 is best characterized as CLASSICAL with a clear path to stronger “Socratic/rhetorical” pedagogy if a few targeted articulations are added.

Key insights:
- Religious identity is central and explicit: partnership of faith, truth-goodness-beauty triad, liturgical life, catechesis, and Mass participation are core features. This supports a strong Classical School alignment around transcendental moral realism and theology.  
- Curriculum is explicitly classical: emphasis on liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/ classical literature, memorization of poetry, Latin in upper grades, and a reading list that reflects canonical Western literatures.  
- Catechesis of the Good Shepherd (CGS) and liturgical integration bolster contemplative and communal dimensions typical of classical formation. 
- Faculty and parental partnerships are foregrounded, with explicit statements about faculty as living curriculum and parents as co-educators in faith formation.  
- A potential needle-mover is a more explicit articulation of Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in the upper grades, plus further emphasis on Great Books conversations as a recurring practice. This would elevate Element 4 from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school employs a catechetical approach through CGS, indicating contemplative, wonder-filled religious formation and a liturgical rhythm (atrium, prayers, sacred space). This aligns with contemplative/restful elements and memory-focused pedagogy. 
- The Classical Curriculum page emphasizes memory work, recitation, and ritualized learning (e.g., poetry, memorization, sacred virtues) and a memory-based pedagogy; it also highlights that the school integrates faith into every subject. 
- The presence of lower-school singing/chanting culture is documented in classical-education pedagogy literature (supported by The Good Teacher text), which describes singing as a memory-practicing, mood-enhancing activity in lower grades. Epiphany’s own materials emphasize memory through poetry and chant as part of classical formation. Public materials show CGS and strong memory-based elements, but explicit upper-school Socratic seminars or structured debates are not clearly documented on the site.   

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: contemplative learning; scholé; wonder; memory work; chanting; recitation; rhetoric training (inferred from the classical curriculum and occasional references to rhetoric-learning, though explicit upper-grade rhetoric seminars are not shown on the site).
- Negative keywords observed: absence of explicit, recurring upper-grade Socratic seminars or structured debates in the publicly visible content; lack of explicit “Socratic method” references on core pages (though CGS and classical elements are strong). The absence of explicit Socratic seminars lowers the pedagogy to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates meaningful classical pedagogy through memory work, CGS, and a classical curriculum, but the explicit articulation of Socratic seminars/debates or upper-level rhetoric-focused pedagogy is not clearly documented in the publicly accessible materials. This places Pedagogy at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with opportunity to move to CLASSICAL through explicit upper-grade Socratic and rhetoric-program documentation.  

What would move the needle
- Publish explicit evidence of upper-grade Socratic discussions, seminars, or debates, including sample prompts and governance (who leads them, how assessment occurs).
- Include documented Great Books-based seminars or formal rhetoric training for higher grades, with sample units and assessment rubrics.
- Highlight any “Scholé days” or low-stress contemplative blocks within the schedule as a formal part of pedagogy.
