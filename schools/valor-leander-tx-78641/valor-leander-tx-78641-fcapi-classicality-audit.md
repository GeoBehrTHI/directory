## Executive Summary

Valor Leander (Valor Education, Leander TX 78641) displays a substantial classical orientation across multiple dimensions, anchored by a clearly stated mission to provide a classical K–12 education with emphasis on virtue, wisdom, and knowledge-based formation. Public materials emphasize the value of great literature, engagement with time-tested thinkers, and a culture of community that includes family involvement. Notable classical elements include a declared classical curriculum, involvement with Latin and humanities, and a leadership emphasis on wisdom and virtue. Evidence includes the Headmaster’s address to families that foregrounds truth, goodness, beauty, wisdom, and virtue; explicit references to great literature and great thinkers; and indications of a structured K–12 path (Kindergarten–2nd at one campus, 3rd–10th at another) with dedicated faculty leadership and a Family Handbook. The school also highlights a robust parent/family partnership through a Parent Service Organization (PSO), and practical classical practices such as a focus on a structured calendar, location details, and a classroom-based lower/upper school structure. See cited materials for specific statements, including the From the Headmaster letter and campus location details.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Why some aspects are clearly classical: explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty; formation and virtue; engagement with great literature and the tradition; presence of Latin and humanities in the upper grades; a stated classical-curriculum orientation and a family/community framework that aligns with traditional classical education values.
- Why some elements are not fully classical (hence not CLASSICAL): while there is strong classical framing, explicit mention of foundational theological grounding, liturgical practices, and a fully articulated “Socratic/Scholé” pedagogy is less evident in public site materials. The materials foreground literary engagement and virtue formation, but do not consistently document liturgical worship, formal theology integration, or an explicit high-school-style Socratic/seminar program on the public pages provided.

# ABSTRACT

Valor Leander presents a public-facing classical-education orientation with a strong emphasis on formation, virtue, wisdom, and engagement with canonical literary and historical tradition. The school demonstrates CLASSICAL alignment in Curriculum, and in a strong Parents & Community orientation. It shows substantial, but not fully explicit, indicators of Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Pedagogy, and moderate indicators in Faculty. The aggregate assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reflecting robust classical elements but missing complete, explicit documentation of theology integration, liturgical practice, and high-fidelity Socratic/seminar pedagogy across grades in publicly accessible materials. The school’s leadership messaging and family engagement strongly support classical aims, while opportunities remain to publicly codify and communicate deeper theological integration, formalized pedagogical methodologies (Socratic/seminar pedagogy across grades), and a more explicit “living curriculum” portrait of the faculty.

- Element 1: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Evidence of transcendental aims and virtue formation; limited theological/liturgical documentation on public pages.
- Element 2: CLASSICAL — Formation/virtue/wisdom over information; teleology toward true/good/beautiful.
- Element 3: CLASSICAL — Classical curriculum framing, Latin/humanities, Great Books-oriented language, and grade-structure aligned with classical education.
- Element 4: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Restful/contemplative pedagogy and lower-school singing; limited explicit documentation of Socratic/seminar pedagogy.
- Element 5: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — Leadership presence and community ethos; lack of explicit “living curriculum” language in public materials.
- Element 6: CLASSICAL — Strong parent partnership, PSO, family handbook, and open-campus approach.
