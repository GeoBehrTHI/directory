## ABSTRACT

Founders Classical Academy West Little Rock presents a classical framing with explicit formation aims and a public emphasis on virtue and citizenship. However, the site does not robustly demonstrate the full classical core across all six categories (pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement are not clearly articulated in a way that meets classical benchmarks; curriculum evidence is present but not fully developed around Great Books and liberal-arts structure; religious/metaphysical grounding and liturgical practices are not publicly evidenced). The overall assessment is NOT CLASSICAL, with Category 2 (School Purpose and Goal) scoring as Classical, Category 1 and Category 3 as Somewhat Classical, and Categories 4–6 as Not Classical.

- Primary signals from the knowledge base (Six Essential Elements) underpinning the rubric emphasize Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts, scholé, Socratic dialogue, the living curriculum, and partner-parent communities as core features; these signals are not publicly demonstrated in the FCA West Little Rock website content examined here. 

- Key category acknowledgments:
  - Religious & Metaphysical Foundations: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (virtue and formation present; theology and liturgy not public).
  - School Purpose & Goal: CLASSICAL (formation of virtue and citizenship; classical framing).
  - Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (public mention of classical courses; lack of explicit Great Books/Seven Liberal Arts).
  - Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL (no explicit pedogogical methods documented publicly).
  - Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL (no living-curriculum/faculty-portrait published publicly).
  - Parents & Community: NOT CLASSICAL (no robust parent-co-teacher program publicly evidenced).

Notes on methodology and sources:
- Public-site signals were drawn from FCA West Little Rock public content (news, events, educational imperative, FAQs) to identify explicit language about virtue formation, classical framing, and curriculum. For example, the Educational Imperative page emphasizes forming virtuous citizens and civic outcomes. 
- The six-element rubric and associated keyword lists were drawn from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School (1) documents, which provide the Positive/Negative Keyword indicators and the canonical elements (great books, trivium/quadrivium, Socratic pedagogy, living curriculum, parent partnership, etc.). See the rubric and lexicon for Signifiers, including paths for Pedagogy (Socratic, scholé, recitation) and the Parent/Community signals.  
- The school’s public-curriculum language includes “traditional classical high school courses” and a “Collegiate pathway,” which signals classical curriculum orientation but does not fully verify Great Books/Seven Liberal Arts or a full canonic Great Conversation demonstration on public pages. 

This evaluation provides a single integrated assessment using both the public materials from the school’s crawl and the classical-education rubric from the knowledge base.
