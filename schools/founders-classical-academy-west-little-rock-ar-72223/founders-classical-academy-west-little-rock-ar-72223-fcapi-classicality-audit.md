## Executive Summary

Founders Classical Academy West Little Rock (AR 72223) demonstrates a classical orientation and a stated aim to form virtuous citizens through a classical framework. The public materials emphasize formation, virtue, and a classical vocabulary (e.g., “classical schools,” “formation,” and “virtue”). However, there is limited explicit evidence of several core classical elements in the school’s public materials, such as a grounded theology or liturgical/community practices, robust Great Books engagement, and explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy, as described in the classical rubric. On balance, the school shows notable classical instincts (virtue formation, traditional/classical framing, and a pathway labeled “classical”), but it does not consistently demonstrate the full six-element classical program across all six categories. Overall classification: NOT CLASSICAL.

Key takeaways:
- Strengths align with Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal) and Element 1 to a lesser degree (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) due to explicit emphasis on virtue and formation. Evidence: “Educational Imperative” describes forming virtuous citizens and moral conduct as educational ends; and the site talks about classical aims and formation. 
- Element 3 (Curriculum) shows some classical orientation (references to “traditional classical high school courses”) but lacks explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and the full Seven Liberal Arts framing on public pages. Evidence: “New High School Course Pathway” mentions classical high school courses; pathway toward a collegiate degree is noted. 
- Categories 4 (Pedagogy), 5 (Faculty), and 6 (Parents & Community) lack clear, explicit evidence in public-facing materials for the full classical pedagogy (Socratic dialogue, scholé, living curriculum), a “faculty living curriculum,” and active parent partnerships as co-educators. The knowledge-base rubric (Six Essential Elements) presents these as strong markers, but the FCA West Little Rock site does not consistently display these elements in public content. See the classical rubric for the pedagogical and community expectations.

Aggregate Assessment

- Aggregate ranking: NOT CLASSICAL

- Rationale
  - While the school exhibits classical language and an orientation toward formation and virtue (Element 2 strongly; Element 1 partially), the six-category framework shows meaningful gaps in Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community, and only partial alignment in Curriculum. The absence or weak public articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation, the Seven Liberal Arts framing, Socratic pedagogy, liturgical/community practices, and robust faculty/parent partnerships prevents a CLASSICAL categorization overall. The rubric’s requirement that the majority of categories be “CLASSICAL” is not met; therefore, the aggregate evaluation is NOT CLASSICAL.

- Evidence highlights across categories
  - Element 1: Virtue and formation appear, but theology, liturgy, and liturgical practices are not evidenced publicly. 
  - Element 2: Clear formation/virtue orientation aligns with classical aims. 
  - Element 3: Curriculum mentions “classical high school courses,” but lacks explicit Great Books/Seven Liberal Arts framing. 
  - Element 4: Public pedagogy language is not publicly documented in classical signals (Socratic, scholé, memorization, etc.). 
  - Element 5: No explicit living-curriculum or teacher-virtue modeling public articulation. 
  - Element 6: Parental partnership and community formation are not robustly demonstrated in public content. 

What Would Move the Needle

- If the school sought to become fully CLASSICAL, concrete steps could include:
  - Public articulation and adoption of overt Pedagogy signals: introduce and describe Socratic seminars, scholé days, memorization/recitation practices, and formation-through-imitation in classroom procedures (and publish these on the website). This would align Element 4 with the classical rubric. 
  - Expand Curriculum to explicit Great Books and Seven Liberal Arts framing: publish a clear curriculum map that includes Trivium/Quadrivium components, Great Books/primary-sources, and integration with history, philosophy, and theology. This would strengthen Element 3.
  - Develop a visible Living Curriculum and Faculty Portrait: publish a “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and a “Living Curriculum” narrative showing how faculty model virtue and serve as mentors, addressing Element 5.
  - Strengthen Parent and Community Partnerships: establish and publicize formal parent-education offerings (book studies, lectures, school visits, and parent-co-teacher initiatives) to demonstrate Element 6 alignment.
  - Consider theological grounding and liturgical/community practices if the school intends a stronger Religious/Metaphysical Foundation: provide publicly stated theological framing or liturgical activities that connect with classical education’s traditional Catholic/Protestant/orthodox channels, depending on the school’s chosen orientation.

- These concrete steps would push the school toward a CLASSICAL classification across more categories and improve the aggregate score.
