## Executive Summary

Cedar Classical Academy (Lansing, MI) presents a robust classical Christian model that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements framework. Across all six categories, the school demonstrates characteristics typical of CLASSICAL education: a coherent religious/metaphysical foundation, a formation-focused purpose, a curriculum anchored in the liberal arts (Grammar–Logic–Rhetoric, Great Books, and Hillsdale-K-12 content), a pedagogy that blends contemplative/restful practices with Socratic or rhetoric-oriented instruction, a faculty modeled as virtuous mentors, and an active, partnership-oriented relationship with parents and the broader learning community. Public materials consistently emphasize truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, liturgy, and formation of character alongside rigorous study, and they foreground parental involvement and community life as core elements of the educational project. This combination supports a general classification of CLASSICAL, with particularly strong alignment in Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community; Religious foundations and School Purpose are clearly oriented toward formation and transcendentals; and the public-facing evidence of Great Books/Great Conversation-style aims is present, though some explicit phrases (e.g., “Great Books” or “Great Conversation” by name) are less frequent than other classical schools that foreground them. Overall, Cedar Classical Academy scores CLASSICAL across categories, yielding an aggregate CLASSICAL designation.

Key takeaways
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are explicit: the school centers a Christian worldview, theology in practice, liturgical rhythm, and communal worship as part of education. Evidence includes explicit statements about cultivating virtue in relation to God and a theology-grounded curriculum.  
- Purpose and goal are formation-first: the mission emphasizes formation of wisdom and virtue rather than information alone; Vision/Graduate outcomes articulate a virtuous, well-educated citizenry formed in faith.  
- Curriculum shows Trivium and a Hillsdale-based K-12 program, with evidence of grammar/logic/rhetoric, language study, and integration of liberal arts through a structured curriculum map and reading program.  
- Pedagogy blends rest (Scholé-like rhythm) with active discourse and rhetoric; lower grades incorporate song/memory, while upper grades show debate/discussion and rhetoric-focused opportunities. Evidence appears in details about Opening Ceremony, memory/chant practices, and upper-school rhetoric activities.  
- Faculty are described as living curriculum and mentors; staff bios and the Head of School’s role illustrate a model-teacher ethos. 
- Parents & Community are explicitly engaged as co-educators and partners; there are regular events, parent participation opportunities, and visible emphasis on open access to visits and involvement.
