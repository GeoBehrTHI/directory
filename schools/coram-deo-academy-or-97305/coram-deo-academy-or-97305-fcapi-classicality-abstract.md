## Abstract

Coram Deo Academy (Or 97385) is a public-facing Classical Christian school that frames its mission and practice around discipleship, virtue formation, and biblical-theological grounding, with a long-range plan to become a K–12 classical program. The school leans strongly into the Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose & Goal categories, presenting a coherent CHRIST-centered formation model that emphasizes wisdom, virtue, and the true/beautiful/good. The curriculum and pedagogy categories show a partial to moderate alignment with classical principles, with explicit grade-level curriculum pages and a clear classical framing, but less explicit, public explication of core classical curricular components (e.g., full Great Books program, Latin/Greek, and explicit Trivium pedagogy) in the accessible content, leading to a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Curriculum and Pedagogy. Faculty and Parents & Community are robustly classical in tone: teachers are described as living curriculum and mentors, and parents are positioned as co-educators and partners in formation.

Overall, the aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL. The school’s consistent emphasis on virtue, wisdom, formation, and a covenantal, Christ-centered community, together with a strong parental-partnership orientation and a living-curriculum approach among faculty, anchor Coram Deo Academy firmly in the classical tradition. The presence of a long-term K–12 classical vision further reinforces this classification, while the current K–6 scope represents a partial but coherent realization of that classical program.

Key insights
- Religious foundations and a Christ-centered mission are foundational and strongly articulated, aligning with the classical lexicon’s spiritual and theological signals.
- The school explicitly frames its mission as formation over information, underscoring the classical aim of nurturing character and intellect in a virtuous, wisdom-centered culture.
- Curriculum and pedagogy show clear classical orientation but require more explicit public documentation of classical components (e.g., Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric emphasis) to be fully demonstrable in all public materials.
- Faculty and parent-community relationships are particularly strong in the classical sense, with teachers as mentors and parents as partners in formation.

What would move the needle (only applicable if SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)
- Publicly articulate and publish explicit Great Books selections, Latin/Greek offerings (as appropriate for levels), and direct references to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across grade bands.
- Provide explicit descriptions of classroom pedagogy that map directly to classical methods (Socratic seminars in upper grades, structured memory work and recitation in lower grades, and formal rhetorical opportunities in middle-to-upper grades).
- Expand parent formation programming with clear, outcome-focused opportunities (reading groups, seminars on classical education philosophy, and structured involvement in the Great Conversation).

## ABSTRACTION OVERVIEW

- The school’s explicit Christian formation and classical framing align with the "Religious and Metaphysical Foundations" and "School Purpose and Goal" elements of the classical rubric. 
- Curriculum and pedagogy show partial alignment; there is a strong classical frame, but explicit, public documentation of foundational classical components (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, Latin/Greek) is not consistently surfaced in the accessible site text. 
- Faculty and parental/community integration strongly reflect classical norms: living curriculum, mentorship, and parent partnership.
- Aggregate conclusion: CLASSICAL, with opportunities to strengthen explicit coverage of core classical curricular and pedagogical elements to move toward fuller CLASSICAL alignment.
