## Executive Summary

Kings Academy Christian School (Tyler, TX) presents a strongly Christian, parent-partnered University-Model approach. The public materials emphasize Christ-centered formation, discipleship, prayer, worship, Bible study, and close parental involvement in education. While these features anchor Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 6 (Parents & Community) as CLASSICAL, other core elements of the classical rubric—most notably Curriculum and Pedagogy rooted in liberal arts, Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric, and Socratic/Scholé traditions—are not explicitly documented in the publicly available materials. The result is a classification that is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with clear strengths in religious foundations and parent-community partnership but notable gaps in explicitly classical curriculum and pedagogy design.

Key strengths
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are clearly articulated: biblically-based, Christ-centered life, theology-infused practice, prayer, Bible study, and discipleship. This is reinforced by explicit statements about “discipleship-based education” and a Christ-centered formation of students.  
- Strong parents-and-community emphasis: explicit partnership with families, parent formation, and training; parental involvement touted as a central factor in student success.  
- The school publicly frames itself as a K–12 University-Model school actively partnering with families to educate Christ-centered students, with two campuses and intentional family-teacher collaboration. 

Key gaps
- Curriculum and pedagogy are not explicitly aligned with classical liberal arts criteria (grammar, logic, rhetoric; great books; classical history scope; memory work; chanting; scholé; etc.). Public materials emphasize the University-Model structure and parental involvement rather than a doctrinally explicit classical curriculum. This reduces the strength of Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) below CLASSICAL levels. 
- Little explicit reference to Great Books, liberal arts canon, or explicit Socratic/seminar-based upper-school pedagogy in the public materials. This further weakens Element 3 and Element 4 relative to a full classical rubric. 

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

What would move the needle
- Explicitly adopt and publish a classical-core curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) aligned with liberal arts canon (Great Books selections or a robust Great Conversation program).
- Introduce and document explicit classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars in more grades, memory work, recitation, and Paideia-style seminars at appropriate levels).
- Add classical-language components (Latin or Greek sequence) and historically grounded content (Western civilization/history through a classical lens) to strengthen the Curriculum category.
- Enhance the internal living-curriculum narrative with a formal teacher-profiles-and-competencies framework that mirrors the “living curriculum” ideal in classical education.
- Extend parent formation with structured classical literature/book-group offerings, and clearly articulate how parent-teacher collaboration advances a classical telos.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

- There is no explicit public documentation of Socratic seminars, rigorous rhetoric training, or upper-school seminars/debates; rather, the high-school pedagogy appears to be traditional teacher-directed with student-home integration.

The University-Model model’s emphasis on parental involvement and a hybrid schedule is not aligned with the classical pedagogy described in the Six Essential Elements. Citations illustrate the University-Model and home-day structure.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If you want to elevate Kings Academy to FULLY CLASSICAL, publicly and clearly integrate and publish: 
  - A classical core curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with explicit Great Books selections or a robust Great Conversation program.
  - A documented classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars across more grade levels, Paideia-style discussions, memory work, recitation).

  - A public-facing articulation of history content with a classical lens (chronology, Western civilization, moral philosophy) integrated across grades.
