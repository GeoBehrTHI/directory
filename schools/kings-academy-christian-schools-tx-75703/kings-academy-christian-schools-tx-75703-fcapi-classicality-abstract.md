## ABSTRACT

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Category-by-category snapshot:
  - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Strong explicit Christian-theology framing, discipleship, prayer, worship, and Christ-centered formation anchor this category. Public phrases emphasize biblically-based, Christ-centered education and discipleship as core.  
  - School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Formation and discipleship are emphasized as primary educational aims, with a Christ-centered telos and partnership with families; however, explicit classical telos language (true/good/beautiful) and Great Conversation notions are not clearly documented in the public materials. 
  - Curriculum: NOT CLASSICAL. The publicly available curriculum materials emphasize the University-Model and faith-based structure rather than a classical core (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books; liberal arts) as the central axis. 
  - Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL. Pedagogy described centers on the University-Model, home-school days, and parental involvement, not explicitly classical pedagogy like Socratic seminars or scholé. 
  - Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL. Descriptions focus on administrators and teachers within a faith-based environment; explicit “living curriculum” faculty modeling or classical-formation language is not evident in public materials. 
  - Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong, explicit parent-partnership orientation, parental formation, and recognition of parental involvement as central to student success. Public materials cite joint-educational responsibility and involvement as foundational.  

- Needle-mover recommendations (concrete steps)
  - Adopt and publish a classical core: grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books or a robust “Great Conversation” program with concrete book lists.
  - Implement explicit classical pedagogy: Socratic seminars, Paideia-style training, memory work/recitation, and a scholé-friendly schedule.
  - Introduce classical-language study (Latin/Greek) and a historically grounded Western civ sequence.
  - Build a formal “living curriculum” framework for faculty development and a teacher-portrait narrative to foreground classical formation.
  - Expand parent formation offerings (book clubs, seminars) tied to classical pedagogy and curriculum so parents understand and participate in the classical transformation of learning.

Cited sources and notes
- The King’s Academy public materials consistently describe a Christ-centered, biblically-based education with discipleship and prayer/bible study, highlighting religious foundations and family partnership. See explicitly: “biblically-based, parentally-guided, Christian faith and a sound, teacher-directed, academic environment”; and “The University-Model School concept… molds them into one model.”  
- Public statements on partnership with families and parental involvement emphasize the central role of engaged parents in student success. 
- Public materials about the two-campus structure and the University-Model framework demonstrate a strong non-classical pedagogy foundation rather than a classical liberal-arts core. 
- The curricular framing in public pages emphasizes structure, admissions, and faith integration rather than explicit classical-liberal-arts curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books).
