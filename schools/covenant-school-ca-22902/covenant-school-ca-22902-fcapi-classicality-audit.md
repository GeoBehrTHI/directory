## Executive Summary

Covenant School Charlottesville (The Covenant School) presents a robust alignment with several classical-education hallmarks, especially in its religious/metaphysical framing and formation-focused aims. The school publicly identifies as a Christian, private day school and embeds theology, liturgical tradition, and virtue formation into its culture and communications. Evidence from the crawl points to explicit uses of Bible, formation and virtue, and liturgical/traditional practices (e.g., Bethlehem traditions), demonstrating a substantive religious foundation and communal practices. The institution also emphasizes a Christian liberal-arts orientation that seeks to form students toward truth, goodness, and beauty, and notes a mission that centers on character and virtue in addition to academics. Yet certain key elements typically associated with a fully classical program—notably explicit Great Books/Great Conversation engagement, grammar-logic-rhetoric curricula, and explicit Socratic pedagogy—are less clearly documented in the public materials. Taken together, Covenant School exhibits strong classical-leaning tendencies in several domains but also includes non-classical features in others. Overall, the school is best described as CLASSICAL, with some elements that are clearly classical and others that are only partially classical.

- Aggregate ranking: CLASSICAL

- Key insights:
  - Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are well-supported via explicit Christian identity, Bible usage, liturgical/traditional practices, and a theological frame for education.
  - School Purpose and Goal emphasizes formation, virtue, and pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, aligned with classical aims.
  - Curriculum shows liberal-arts orientation but lacks explicit, documented Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis or classical-trivium focus in public materials.
  - Pedagogy remains less clearly documented in traditional classical-education terms (Socratic seminars, scholé, memorization, etc.) in public-facing content.
  - Faculty materials show standard leadership roles; while not explicitly described as “living curriculum,” there is evidence of active faculty leadership and a structured institutional culture.
  - Parents & Community emphasis is strong, including partnership with families and opportunities for parent formation and campus visits.

- What would move the needle (if aiming to be more fully CLASSICAL):
  - More explicit articulation of grammar-logic-rhetoric progression in K–12, with documented pedagogy (Socratic dialogue, memorization/recitation, and master-teacher modeling) on the website.
  - Clear references to Great Books and Great Conversation, including a defined cross-curricular program and a visible library/readings plan aligned with classical canon.
  - Documentation of a formal “living curriculum” philosophy tied to classical content, including quotations, syllabi, and example lessons that connect Virtue, Truth, and Beauty across disciplines.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If the school sought to become more fully CLASSICAL across all categories, concrete steps could include:
  - Publicly articulate and publish a formal K–12 grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence, including sample lesson plans and milestones, to demonstrate a clear Trivium progression.
  - Explicitly describe a Great Books/Great Conversation program, including a defined reading list, primary sources, and how discussion is structured in upper grades.
  - Document a formal “living-curriculum” model in which the faculty acts as mentors, with exemplars and a detailed description of how the curriculum is embodied by the teachers.
  - Expand public materials to include classic pedagogy (scholé days, memorization/recitation routines, and Socratic seminars) with sample classroom activities and student outcomes.
  - Provide a canonical list of Great Works across history, with cross-disciplinary connections to history, literature, philosophy, and the sciences, demonstrating unity of truth, beauty, and goodness.

Note: Given Covenant School’s public-grade CLASSICAL orientation (categories 1, 2, and 6 strongly CLASSICAL), these needle-moving steps would primarily target strengthening Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy) to move from NOT CLASSICAL toward CLASSICAL.
