## Executive Summary

Hastings Christian School (HCS) presents a clearly Christian, classical-leaning orientation in its public materials. The school emphasizes a Christian liberal arts framework, a Trivium/Quadrivium-informed curriculum, and a distinctive partnership model with families. Public statements stress seeking truth, goodness, and beauty within a formation-focused aims rather than job-market preparation. Overall, the school aligns with several core classical indicators but exhibits some gaps in explicit articulation of certain classical pedagogy and faculty/community constructs.

- Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Key strengths:
  - Explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable (Foundations and Purpose) and a clear formation orientation toward virtue and discernment (Purpose/Goal).
  - Curriculum signals classical structure (Trivium and Quadrivium) and a liberal-arts orientation.
  - Explicitly Christian theological framing (Our mission to disciple image bearers to love God) and a stated family-partnership approach to education.

- Notable gaps for deeper classical alignment:
  - Limited explicit public articulation of certain classical pedagogy staples (e.g., Socratic seminars across grade bands; detailed evidence of a living curriculum or faculty-as-core-piece language).
  - Less explicit language around Great Books/Great Conversation or formal catechesis structures in the public materials reviewed.
  - The Faculty and Parents & Community categories have partial signals but not uniformly rich statements that fully match classical templates.

- What would move the needle: A more explicit articulation of Great Books/Great Conversation, stronger statements about Socratic pedagogy in upper grades, clearer “living curriculum” language for faculty, and stronger, public-facing evidence of deeper parent formation programs and community liturgical or shared worship practices.

Evidence is drawn from Hastings Christian School public materials (as captured by the crawl) and aligned with the Positive/Negative keyword indicators from the classical lexicon. See category-by-category sections for the quantitative keyword analysis and rationale. Key passages cited include mission statements, distinctive program descriptions, and explicit references to Trivium/Quadrivium and the aim to cultivate discernment and virtue.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly reference and implement Great Books/Great Conversation in curriculum statements, with direct public articulation of a Great Books list or core texts and a public commitment to the Great Conversation as a daily or yearly framework.
- Strengthen Pedagogy language by publicly detailing Socratic seminars across grades, more explicit memory/recitation routines, and a formal “living curriculum” articulation for faculty—position the faculty as the core vehicle of classical formation.
- Expand Visible liturgy/communal practices: articulate morning or weekly liturgical routines, shared prayer, and calendar-based liturgical events to satisfy the Liturgical & Communal Practices criteria.
- Expand Parent Formation: publish concrete families-facing programs (e.g., classical book clubs, teacher-led parent seminars, covenant-style family learning plans) and open sessions to visits and involvement beyond the current partnership framing.
- Make explicit the role of Great Books and primary sources within the curriculum and strengthen the history/Western civ scaffolding as a clearly named pillar.
- Elevate the explicit articulation of the “living curriculum” as a formal concept tied to teacher professional development and student outcomes.
