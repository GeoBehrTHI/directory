## Executive Summary

Second Baptist University Model Schools TX 77024 (SBSUM) presents a distinctly Christian, University-Model approach with clear classical leanings. Public materials emphasize the partnership between parents and the school, a biblically-centered worldview, and a mission-oriented education that seeks to form character, think critically, and lead courageously within a Christ-centered framework. The school markets itself as “distinctively classical” within a university-model structure and highlights truth, beauty, and excellence as guiding ideals, while also underscoring the parental role in shaping learning. Across the six classical-element categories, SBSUM shows strong alignment in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and Parents & Community, partial alignment in Pedagogy and Faculty, modest alignment in Curriculum and School Purpose/Goal, and mixed signals in Curriculum. The overall classification is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with concrete steps that would move it toward full classical alignment outlined in the “What Would Move the Needle” section.

- Strengths: Explicit Christian/theological grounding; explicit emphasis on truth, beauty, and excellence; strong parent-school partnership; visible commitment to forming students in a biblical worldview; explicit “classical methodologies” phrasing and explicit on-campus/at-home balance that preserves a traditional character.
- Gaps: Limited explicit references to canonical classical elements (Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric, formal Socratic pedagogy, and a historically grounded Great Conversation) within core curriculum disclosures; pedagogy described primarily as a university-model structure rather than a full Paideia/Socratic program; lack of explicit liturgical/communal practices in the site copy; faculty descriptions are positive but not clearly articulated as “living curriculum” in the materials provided.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Make explicit the canonical classical curriculum components (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Latin/Greek; primary sources; history-as-substance rather than “social studies”) within public-facing materials. Adoption of a clear Great Books list and a visible Trivium progression would move this category toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Adopt explicit Socratic/Paideia-style practices (e.g., regular Socratic seminars in upper grades, formal narration/recitation, memory-work routines, and restful scholé days) and document these methods on the site to advance toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Emphasize and publicly articulate the living-curriculum approach in faculty profiles (e.g., portraits of teachers as mentors, living curriculum, and ongoing classical pedagogy training). This would strengthen the CLASSICAL reading for the faculty category.
- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): Include explicit language about formation of virtue and human flourishing as telos in the public materials and tie this more clearly to a classical paideia model (including references to virtue, telos, and paideia in curriculum goals). This would tilt Element 2 toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations): Consider more explicit articulation of liturgical or communal practices (even if modest) and a clearer alignment with a liturgical calendar or common prayer in school routines (to align more fully with liturgical-centered foundations). This would push Element 1 toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 6 (Parents and Community): Maintain and deepen the active parent-formation programming (e.g., more parent reading groups and formal “co-teacher” training sessions) to reinforce the classical model and move toward a stronger CLASSICAL standing.

Abstract of concrete steps
- Publish a publicly accessible curriculum map that enumerates grammar, logic, rhetoric requirements, Great Books selections, and primary sources by grade level.
- Introduce regular Socratic seminars in upper grades and document in website copy with examples of student discourse, prompts, and assessment rubrics.
- Expand faculty profiles to emphasize “living curriculum” status and teacher-mentors with a clear professional-development path in classical pedagogy.
- Add explicit statements about formation of virtue, telos, and classical aims (in addition to biblical worldview) in the School Purpose and Goal section.
- Expand liturgical or communal practices language (even if modest) to reflect liturgical rhythm within the school day.
