## Executive Summary

La Luz Christian Academy NM 87120 presents a public-facing profile that signals classical Christian orientation rather than a fully explicit classical curriculum. Public materials emphasize a Christian theological foundation, character formation, and a mission to partner with families, with statements that highlight virtue, wisdom, truth, and a deep faith-based formation. Evidence such as the site tagline Classical Christian School and mission statements stressing mind–heart formation and a Christian foundation support this reading. However, explicit Classical elements most often associated with a robust classical program (e.g., Great Books, the Trivium/Quadrivium, Socratic seminars, Scholé restfulness, and a clearly articulated “living curriculum” modeled by faculty) are not clearly enumerated in the visible public materials. The result is a classification of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall. The school shows strong religious foundations and formation aims, and it partners with parents, but it does not consistently demonstrate the full spectrum of the six classical elements in its public-facing content. If the school wishes to move toward a full CLASSICAL designation, it should make explicit in accessible materials its engagement with Great Books and Great Conversation, the liberal arts structure (grammar–logic–rhetoric; Trivium/Quadrivium), contemplative Scholé practices, explicit “living curriculum” faculty modeling, and a more explicit articulation of parent-as-co-teachers in daily practice.

- Overall aggregate rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Key drivers of this assessment:
  - Strong religious/metaphysical orientation and explicit Christian formation (evidence of theology and Christian worldview; Aristotle quote and mission language reinforce formation and virtue aims)  
  - Explicit classical vocabulary and aims (the school identifies itself publicly as a Classical Christian School) but does not consistently foreground core classical elements like Great Books, Great Conversation, or the Trivium/Quadrivium in the accessible public materials. 
  - Active parent partnership is stated, and there is a visible emphasis on forming leaders within a Christian framework, aligning with the “Parents & Community” element, though explicit co-teaching language is not consistently demonstrated in public text. 

- What would move the needle: To reach a full CLASSICAL designation, La Luz Christian Academy could publicly codify and highlight:
  - A Great Books–based core and a Great Conversation framework.
  - A clear Trivium/Quadrivium structure (grammar, logic, rhetoric; arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) within the curriculum, with mapping to grade levels.
  - Explicit mention of Scholé/restful learning, contemplative time, and disciplined memory work (chanting, recitation) in pedagogy.
  - A defined living-curriculum model with faculty as mentors and living exemplars, including formal statements about faculty development in classical pedagogy.
  - A clearly stated model of parents as co-educators with concrete opportunities for parental formation and involvement as part of daily practice.

Citations anchor points:
- Classical Christian identity and mission language: La Luz public materials describing the school as a Classical Christian School and mission that emphasizes mind–heart formation and Christian faith. 
- Formation emphasis and virtue/theology framing: Direct references to forming leaders with virtue and a Christian foundation; supportive language around truth and goodness in mission-related text. 
- Aristotelian/formation motto: The well-known line “Educating the mind without educating the heart is no education at all,” illustrating the formation emphasis that is central to classical education ethos.
