## ABSTRACT

Northern Colorado Christian Academy (NCCA) presents a robust classical Christian profile anchored in biblical foundations, virtue formation, and liberal-arts pedagogy. The school’s public materials consistently foreground truth, goodness, beauty, and a biblically grounded worldview, while its curriculum and pedagogy align with the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and include Latin study and history integrated with Western civilization. Parental partnership is a deliberate and central theme, with explicit statements about parents as co-educators and baptized in a framework of biblical responsibility. The faculty category is the only one with partial alignment (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) due to a comparatively less explicit articulation of a formal living-curriculum narrative for teachers, though there are clear signs of teacher formation within a Christian framework. The overall assessment is CLASSICAL, with opportunities to strengthen explicit faculty development narratives and signaling around Great Books/Great Conversation in the curriculum, which would elevate Element 5 to Classical status and further reinforce the school’s classical identity.

Key category notes:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL (biblical framework, worship, theocentric aims).
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL (formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal-arts orientation).
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin; history; Western civilization; integration with biblical worldview).
- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL (Socratic/dialogic upper-school practice; memorization and chant in lower grades; contemplative/restful elements).
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (explicit teacher-god-call language, governance; needs clearer living-curriculum articulation & faculty-development narrative).
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL (parity with parental partnership, formation, openness to involvement).

Cited sources draw from NCCA public materials describing vision, mission, academic philosophy, curriculum, pedagogy, and family engagement, as well as the classical-education lexicon and framework that underpins the evaluation. Representative citations include explicit statements about biblical framework and parental partnership, the Trivium-based curriculum, and evidence of liturgical practice.
