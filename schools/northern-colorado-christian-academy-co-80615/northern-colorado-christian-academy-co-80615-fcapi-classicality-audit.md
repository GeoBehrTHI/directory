## Executive Summary

Northern Colorado Christian Academy (NCCA) presents a clearly aligned classical Christian program. Public-facing materials consistently anchor education in biblical faith, virtue, and a liberal-arts-centered Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) approach with explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty. The school’s mission, vision, and curricular structure foreground a biblically informed worldview, parental partnership, and a teachers-as-virtue-model stance. The overall assessment across the six classical criteria is CLASSICAL, with Element 5 (Faculty) showing only partial alignment (SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL) due to comparatively less explicit articulation of the living-curriculum and faculty formation details. The remaining categories demonstrate strong alignment to classical ideals: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Parents & Community all show robust evidence of classical orientation. The aggregate reading suggests a fully classical orientation with a single category requiring targeted enhancement to reach full classical fidelity.

Key evidence highlights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are pervasive: explicit Bible-based framing, God-centered aims, and liturgical dimensions (Chapel, worship) embedded in daily life and coursework. These are reinforced by mission/vision statements and doctrinal commitments.   
- Purpose and goals foreground formation of virtue, wisdom, and human flourishing under a liberal arts umbrella, with direct statements about virtue and truth anchored in a Christian worldview. 
- Curriculum centers on grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin is taught; history and Western civilization are integrated; Great Books/Paideia-related reading is encouraged via recommended texts.  
- Pedagogy reflects the classical Trivium in practice: chanting/recitation during the Grammar stage, and Socratic seminars, debates, and rhetoric in the upper grades; contemplative/restful elements ( scholé ) are noted as part of the philosophy. 
- Faculty and leadership are depicted as stewards of virtue and curriculum, with explicit recognition of teachers as gifted by God and forming part of the living curriculum; formal governance structures exist (Organizing Board).  
- Parents and community are positioned as partners in education, with explicit calls for parent involvement and formation, and open-campus/visitation norms.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 5 (Faculty): Add explicit, public articulation of a “living curriculum” faculty framework and ongoing classical-pedagogy professional development. Publish a formal statement detailing how faculty engage in great-books seminars, faculty collaboration, mentoring, and teacher formation within a classical-Christian framework.
- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Strengthen explicit language about “Great Books” and “Great Conversation” as a measurable curricular component, with a published list of core Great Books and a plan for how those texts are integrated across grades. This would more clearly align with the lexicon’s Great Books/Paideia signals.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Continue to document and publish specifics about scholé (restful learning) practices, contemplative days, and structured opportunities for student-led inquiry within the grammar/logic/rhetoric progression, with examples of student work and assessment aligned to these practices.
- For Element 1/Element 2 (Foundations and Goals): Maintain and deepen explicit references to transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) and the theological framing of learning, ensuring that all disciplines articulate how these enduring realities shape curriculum decisions, assessment, and student formation.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand on parent-formation programming (book studies, lectures, family-scholarly events) with a published calendar and outcomes metrics to demonstrate tangible parent-partner impact.

These needle-movers would consolidate Element 5 and push the overall profile toward a fully CLASSICAL classification.
