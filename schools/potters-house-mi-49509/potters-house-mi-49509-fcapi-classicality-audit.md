## Executive Summary

Potter’s House (MI 49509) presents as a faith-centered school emphasizing Christ-centered education, discipleship, prayer, and worship within an urban setting. Public-facing materials consistently frame the school around theological formation, character values, and a Christ-centered worldview, with explicit statements about high academic standards and nurturing a diverse community. However, the publicly showcased curricular and pedagogical practices do not align with core classical elements such as the Seven Liberal Arts (grammar/logic/rhetoric), Great Books and the Great Conversation, or explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy. Taken together, the school demonstrates clear Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and strong formation aims, with partial alignment in formation-oriented goals, but limited alignment in traditional classical curricula and pedagogy. On balance, the school is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in some domains (notably in formation and religious foundations) but not fully Classical according to the Six Essential Elements rubric.

Overall aggregate: NOT CLASSICAL (with notable SOMETIMES-CLASSICAL cues in formation, theology, and parental/community engagement)

What would move the needle: If Potter’s House publicly articulates and demonstrates explicit classical-curriculum elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Great Books, history-as-history rather than “social studies” framing, and Socratic/seminar pedagogy from upper grades), and if it broadens documentation of the faculty living the curriculum and deeper parental partnership as co-educators, it would move toward CLASSICAL. Concrete steps include: (1) adopting a formal Seven Liberal Arts framework (grammar/logic/rhetoric in lower/upper grades) and integrating Great Books into a “Great Conversation” spine; (2) expanding to a clearly articulated classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, memory work, recitation) across appropriate grade bands; (3) publicly detailing a "living curriculum" model (faculty as mentors and exemplar models) and a robust parent formation program with ongoing family catechesis; (4) explicitly naming and mapping a cross-disciplinary historical-cultural curriculum that frames history as Western/Christian civilization rather than a stand-alone social-studies track.

ABSTRACT

Potter’s House Mi 49509 demonstrates a strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundation through explicit Christ-centered theology, prayer, and worship, and shows a formation-oriented aim for virtue and character. However, the school’s public materials do not articulate a full Classical Curriculum (Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books, or a formal Trivium/Quadrivium framework), nor do they detail canonical classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, scholé, memorization) in a public-facing way. The Faculty and Parents/Community cues are present (highly-acknowledged teachers, partner programs), but not richly developed into a living curriculum or parent-formation ecosystem. Across categories, the school leans toward CLASSICAL in some areas (Religious foundations; Formation goals) and NOT CLASSICAL in others (Curriculum; Pedagogy), with several SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signals in Faculty and Parents/Community. The aggregate classification is NOT CLASSICAL, with meaningful caveats for potential growth toward classical education through explicit curriculum, pedagogy, and family-partnership practices.
