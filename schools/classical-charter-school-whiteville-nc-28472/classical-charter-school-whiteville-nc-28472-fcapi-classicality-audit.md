## Method: Keyword‑Based Classicality Audit

I used the **Six Essential Elements of a Classical School** keyword framework (positive and negative indicators) across the six categories: Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community.    

For each category I:

1. **Extracted phrases** from the crawl of ccswhiteville.net. –  
2. **Matched** them against the positive/negative keyword lists (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, scholé, wonder, chanting, Socratic vs. project‑based, experiential learning, data‑driven, college‑and‑career ready, etc.).    
3. **Counted hits** and estimated the ratio of positive to negative indicators.
4. Assigned a rating: **CLASSICAL / SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL / NOT CLASSICAL**.

## What Would Move the Needle (If They Wanted to Become Classical)

Even though the overall rating is NOT CLASSICAL, here are concrete steps that would measurably shift the keyword profile and substance:

1. **Religious & Metaphysical Foundations**
   - Explicitly articulate a **transcendental moral realist** vision: use and explain “truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, wisdom, human flourishing, natural law, telos, permanent things.”    
   - If appropriate to their charter, ground the school in a **theological framework** (even if broadly Christian) and integrate language like “theology, faith and reason, sacred scripture, Christ‑centered education, liturgy, worship, reverence.”   

2. **Purpose & Goal**
   - Reframe the mission around **formation, not information**: “cultivation of wisdom and virtue,” “formation of the soul,” “education ordered to the true, the good, and the beautiful,” “human excellence,” “liberal education.”   
   - De‑emphasize test scores and accountability language in mission/vision pages; keep those in compliance documents, not as the school’s stated telos.

3. **Curriculum**
   - Publicly commit to the **seven liberal arts**: name “grammar, logic, rhetoric, arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy” and show where they live in the program.   
   - Introduce and advertise **Latin** (and possibly Greek) and a **Great Books / primary‑source humanities sequence**; use terms like “Great Books, Great Conversation, Western civilization, classical canon.”    
   - Replace “social studies” framing with **chronological history** and “Western civilization” plus world history.

4. **Pedagogy**
   - Describe and implement **Socratic seminars, guided discussions, dialectic**, especially in upper grades; use those terms on the website.   
   - In lower grades, highlight **chanting, singing, recitation, memory work, copywork, imitation**; use those words explicitly.    
   - Introduce the language and practice of **scholé** and “restful learning,” emphasizing contemplation and wonder, not just rigor and test prep.   

5. **Faculty**
   - Publish a **“Portrait of a Classical Teacher”** that uses terms like “mentor, model of virtue, living curriculum, faculty of friends, lifelong learner, lover of wisdom.”   
   - Describe ongoing **classical formation** for faculty (Great Books seminars, training in classical pedagogy).

6. **Parents & Community**
   - Move beyond information nights to **parent formation**: classical book clubs, talks on classical education, and explicit language about parents as **co‑educators** and the school as an **extension of the home**.   

If future crawls show these kinds of changes—especially explicit use of the classical lexicon and corresponding structural changes in curriculum and pedagogy—the school’s rating could move toward **SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL** and eventually **CLASSICAL**.
