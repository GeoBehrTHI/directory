## ABSTRACT

This audit evaluates **Classical Charter Schools of Whiteville** using the **Six Essential Elements of a Classical School** keyword framework. The crawl data reveals a school deeply shaped by **state accountability, standardized testing, data‑driven instruction, MTSS, and mental‑health/SEL initiatives**, with only generic references to “traditional values” and “love for learning and discovery.”    

Across the six categories:

1. **Religious & Metaphysical Foundations:** No evidence of transcendental moral realism, theology, or liturgical life; language is secular‑therapeutic. **NOT CLASSICAL.**    
2. **School Purpose & Goal:** Emphasis on academic success, proficiency, and accountability metrics rather than wisdom and virtue ordered to truth, goodness, and beauty. **NOT CLASSICAL.**    
3. **Curriculum:** No mention of Trivium/Quadrivium, Latin, Great Books, or Great Conversation; curriculum is test‑ and standards‑driven. **NOT CLASSICAL.**    
4. **Pedagogy:** Direct instruction, interventions, and data‑driven practices dominate; no Socratic, scholé, chanting, or mimetic pedagogy. **NOT CLASSICAL.**     
5. **Faculty:** Faculty are framed as staff within a bureaucratic system, not as a “faculty of friends” or living curriculum. **NOT CLASSICAL.**    
6. **Parents & Community:** Some partnership language (communication, curriculum nights) but no classical parent formation or co‑educator ethos. **SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (weak).**    

Overall, the **classical keyword coverage is extremely low**, and negative/modernist indicators are pervasive. The aggregate rating is therefore **NOT CLASSICAL**. The report concludes with specific recommendations—adding explicit transcendental and theological language, adopting a liberal‑arts/Great Books curriculum, implementing Socratic and scholé‑based pedagogy, forming a faculty of friends, and deepening parent formation—that could, if implemented and reflected in public materials, move the school toward genuine classicality.
