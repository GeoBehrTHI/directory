## Executive Summary

Veritas School (Richmond, VA, 23227) presents a robust, explicitly classical Christian program that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric. Public-facing materials emphasize formation, a Christ-centered framework, the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Conversations via Humane Letters, robust Latin/logic training, and a Portrait of a Graduate that foregrounds virtue, wisdom, and service. Across categories, evidence shows consistent commitment to formation over mere information, integration of theology, memory-work and chant-like elements in pedagogy, and a deep partnership with parents. The school earns a CLASSICAL rating across all six categories.

Key evidentiary threads include: a mission anchored in glorifying God by cultivating wisdom and virtue through classical, Christ-centered education; explicit references to formation, the liberal arts, Great Conversations, and Great Books; explicit incorporation of Latin, Logic, and Rhetoric in the upper programs; lower- and middle-school pedagogical practices that emphasize memory, singing, and recitation in service of formation; a living faculty and a Fellows program; and active parent partnership and community-building. These signals appear repeatedly in Veritas’s mission pages, distinctives, curriculum descriptions, and student-facing materials.

What Would Move the Needle

- If you sought further needle-moving refinements toward a more explicit “Classic” labeling across every category, Veritas could further foreground:
 - Explicit, named references to Great Books and Great Conversation in grade-level objectives and assessment rubrics, with sample readings and dialectical prompts clearly mapped to the Great Conversation framework.
 - More overt articulation of liturgical or sacramental practices as routine dimensions of daily life (e.g., liturgical music, corporate worship, or communal prayer rhythms) with documented policies or schedules.
 - A formal, published description of Socratic seminars and seminars in upper grades paired with sample prompts and evaluation rubrics to demonstrate the extent and uniformity of Socratic pedagogy.
 - Additional public-facing evidence of “memory work/recitation” programs (e.g., specific chants, poems, or memory-line curricula) and how they connect to the five classical arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric, arithmetic, music, geometry).
