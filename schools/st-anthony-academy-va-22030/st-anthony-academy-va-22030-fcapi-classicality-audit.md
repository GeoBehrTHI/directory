## Executive Summary

St Anthony Academy Fairfax (Fairfax, VA 22030) presents a robust classical-Catholic program that aligns across the core classical criteria. Public materials repeatedly emphasize the Trivium (grammar, dialectic/logic, rhetoric), engagement with Great Books, Latin studies, memorization and chant, and a Catholic community formation including liturgy and daily prayer. The school openly situates itself within the Classical Latin School Association and foregrounds Catholic doctrine, liturgy, and sacred tradition as foundational to its pedagogy and culture. Taken together, these elements satisfy the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education and indicate a CLASSICAL overall classification across all six categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose & Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community). This assessment relies on multiple public-facing sources in the school’s crawl, including explicit statements about Trivium-based curriculum, Great Books, Latin, liturgical practice, and a Catholic formation emphasis, as well as corroborating references to the school’s staff, mission, and parent/community engagement. See category-by-category snapshots below for the keyword-level analysis and justification, with citations to the source materials.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Catholic identity, liturgical practice, devotion, and theology integrated into formation; daily prayer and the Traditional Latin Mass are described; explicit Catholic formation and sacred tradition are foregrounded.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include Catholic/Catholic Tradition, liturgy, Mass, rosary, catechesis, theology, liturgical practices, and the concept of the True/Good/Beautiful; no explicit negative signals dominate the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The materials consistently anchor formation in transcendentals and sacred tradition, with communal liturgy and explicit Catholic theology, fulfilling the transcendental and liturgical signals of classical foundations.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Formation of virtue and wisdom; education framed as formation of character in a Catholic, classical mold; references to the True, Good, Beautiful and a telos-oriented aim.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive terms include formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education, classical mission, great conversation; negative signals (e.g., workforce prep) are not primary drivers in the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The aim centers on human flourishing and moral formation rather than solely a job- or credential-centered outcome, aligning with classical telos.

- Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric); study of Great Books (Homer, Virgil, Dante, Shakespeare); emphasis on Latin; history as a central through-line; memorization and cultivation of wonder.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include Trivium, Great Books, primary sources, Latin, history, rhetoric, dialectic, memorization, wonder; negative signals (e.g., “social studies” framing replacing history or STEM-first emphasis) are not predominant in the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The explicit articulation of the Trivium, Great Books, Latin, and a history-centered, interconnected curriculum fits CLASSICAL expectations.

- Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Lower-level singing/chanting and memory-work; upper-level Socratic discussion/seminars or debates; rhetoric training and public speaking opportunities are described; engagement with wonder and contemplative elements in the lower grades; classic pedagogy elements are visible in multiple grade bands.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include singing/chanting, memory work, recitation, Socratic seminar, dialectic, rhetoric, and seminars; contemplative/restful learning and scholé appear as supportive cues; no predominant shift to non-classical experiential/project-based-only instruction is evident in the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The pedagogy embraces both memory-based, contemplative elements (scholé-like practices) and higher-level Socratic/rhetoric work, consistent with classical teaching methods.

- Element 5: Faculty — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: The faculty are framed as mentors and living curriculum; staff/stewardship described in terms of virtue, character formation, and collaborative culture; the school emphasizes a staff with deep content knowledge and faith-based formation.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive signals include model teachers, living curriculum, faculty of friends, collegial culture, and teacher formation; negative signals are not prominent.
    - Why this rating: The public materials present faculty as exemplary educators who embody and transmit classical pedagogy and Catholic formation, meeting the core faculty criteria for classical schools.

- Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
    - Evidence: Strong partner-teacher model; parent formation and ongoing engagement through publications and gatherings; school opens visits and maintains active parent involvement; open campus and parent participation is encouraged.
    - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive keywords include partnership with families, co-teachers, parent formation, seminars, classical book clubs, and open campus; any negative signals are not evident in the public materials.
    - Why this rating: The school presents a clear, ongoing partnership with parents in forming a classical, Catholic education, consistent with classical-parent community signals.

Aggregate assessment: CLASSICAL
