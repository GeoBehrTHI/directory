## Executive Summary

Kawartha Classical Christian School (KCCS) presents a clearly Christian, formation-oriented approach that aligns with several core elements of classical education, but with notable gaps in explicit articulation of core classical curriculum and pedagogy. Public materials emphasize a Christ-centered aims, formation of virtue, and an emphasis on truth, beauty, and goodness, along with a strong faculty ethos and open parental engagement. Evidence of explicit classical curriculum components such as the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books, and the Great Conversation is not clearly stated in the public materials available here. As a result, the school demonstrates meaningful classical alignment in certain areas (notably religious/theological foundations and a formation-focused purpose with a robust faculty posture) but falls short of full classical articulation in others (especially explicit curriculum and pedagogy). The overall assessment is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key evidentiary notes:
- Christ-centered mission and explicit theology are central to materials (e.g., “Tuning Hearts. Training Minds. To the Glory of Christ,” and a stated Statement of Faith). This supports Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose.  
- The faculty is described in warmly favorable terms, with emphasis on godliness and care, signaling a living-teacher/mentors model aligned with classical expectations about the faculty. 
- Parental engagement and transparent admissions processes hint at a community orientation consistent with classical expectations for parent partnership, though explicit “co-teacher” programming or formal parent-formation activities are not deeply described in the visible material.  

Note: This evaluation uses the six-element rubric and the associated positive/negative keyword indicators from the classical-education reference documents (the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and related lexicon) to ground category-by-category scoring. See the cited sources for the keyword framework (e.g., truth, goodness, beauty; theology; liturgy; Great Books; Socratic pedagogy; etc.).

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly document and communicate the liberal arts framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Quadrivium), Great Books, and the Great Conversation on the website or in admissions materials. Include references to primary texts, memory work, and classic authors where applicable. This would elevate Curriculum to CLASSICAL.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide a clear statement of pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminars, scholarly dialogues, and reformulated memory/recitation practices) and how upper- and lower-school practices align with scholé/restful learning versus rigid rigor. Incorporating explicit signals of classical pedagogy would move Pedagogy toward CLASSICAL.
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): Outline formal parent-formation initiatives (reading groups, book studies, liturgical/calendar alignment with family life, home-parent learning supports) beyond admissions interactions. A named, ongoing program would push Parents & Community toward CLASSICAL.
- Across the board, a concise, public articulation of how Great Books, Great Conversation, and the Trivium are integrated into daily teaching would create stronger Element 1–3 alignment and elevate the overall classical profile.

Citations: rationale aligns with the gap analysis between observed materials and the Six Essential Elements framework.
