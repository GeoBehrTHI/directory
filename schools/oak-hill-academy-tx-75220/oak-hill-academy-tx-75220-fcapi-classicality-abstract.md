## ABSTRACT

Oak Hill Academy’s public materials depict a formation-oriented, student-centered program with small class sizes, a strong emphasis on social-emotional development, and a community-driven ethos. While it demonstrates several foundational classical elements—such as a tiered curriculum, focus on character and formation, and a faculty that acts as a living curriculum—it stops short of a fully classical program. Notable strengths include a nurturing faculty, a robust emphasis on the “whole-child” and social skills, and clear parent/community engagement. Gaps include explicit Great Books/grammar–logic–rhetoric curriculum, explicit Socratic pedagogy, and overt religious/metaphysical foundations. The aggregate classification across the six categories is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: NOT CLASSICAL. The school lacks explicit theological or liturgical grounding in public statements.
- School Purpose and Goal: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Formation and the whole-child focus align with classical aims, but explicit telos language is not consistently foregrounded, and there is explicit college/career emphasis in some materials.
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. There is grammar/history/literature in the curriculum; however, there is a strong project-based emphasis and no explicit Great Books canon in public materials.
- Pedagogy: NOT CLASSICAL. Project-based, experiential approaches predominate publicly; lack of explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy in the public materials.
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. The faculty is described as living curriculum and mentors; there is a strong emphasis on teacher excellence and collaborative culture.
- Parents & Community: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. There is meaningful parent partnership and community engagement, but formalized classical parent formation and family liturgy practices are not explicitly documented.

Overall, Oak Hill Academy sits in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL territory, with the strongest alignment in Category 5 (Faculty) and moderate alignment in Category 2, Category 3, and Category 6. The school would move more decisively into CLASSICAL territory with targeted, public-facing expansions into Great Books/Scholé-inspired pedagogy, explicit classical canonical readings, and a deeper articulation of religious/metaphysical foundations where appropriate.

- Abstracted evidence sources include the Mission & Services pages (public statements on whole-child education and equity) and Head of School materials (faculty as living curriculum), as well as curriculum and admission materials (grading/learning structure and history/grammar content).
