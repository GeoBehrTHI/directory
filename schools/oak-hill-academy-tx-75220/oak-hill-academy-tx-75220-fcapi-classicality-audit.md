## Executive Summary

Oak Hill Academy (Dallas, TX 75220) presents a mixed profile in relation to classical education. It demonstrates several formation-oriented and student-centered elements (e.g., a whole-child approach, small student-teacher ratios, emphasis on social-emotional growth, and a tiered curriculum) that align with classic aims of education as formation and virtue. However, explicit classical markers—such as sustained attention to Great Books, a grammar–logic–rhetoric trinity, deliberate Socratic pedagogy, liturgical or theological foundations, and a public-facing emphasis on traditional liberal arts canon—are not consistently foregrounded in public materials. The school does articulate a strong, serviceable pedagogy and a community-centric ethos, but its public materials show significant emphasis on individual learning needs, therapies, and college/career preparation, rather than a wholesale embrace of classical pedagogy.

Overall classification (based on the six-category rubric and the accompanying keyword framework): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Why this matters: Element 5 (Faculty) and the presence of formation-oriented language tilt toward classical sensibilities, but Element 1 (Religious/Metaphysical Foundations), Element 3 (Curriculum) cross-cut with a heavy project-based emphasis, and Element 4 (Pedagogy) reveal notable deviations from a full classical model. The aggregate pattern yields a profile that is leaning toward classical ideals in some respects but not fully, consistently classical across all six areas.

- Most consequential insights:
  - Strengths: Faculty as living curriculum; strong emphasis on formation, social skills, and a nurturing teaching culture; high degree of family/community engagement; small classes and individualized support.
  - Gaps: Absence of explicit Great Books or canonical liberal arts framing; limited public articulation of Socratic/seminar pedagogy; explicit religious/metaphysical foundations and liturgical/community practices are not evident in core materials.
  - Needle-moving opportunities: explicit articulation of classical aims (telos, truth/good/beauty language), integration of Great Books or classical readings, and the adoption of explicit Socratic or scholé-inspired pedagogy in higher grades; enhanced parent formation activities aligned with classical education principles.

Citations and evidence drawn from Oak Hill Academy’s public materials (school site content) and the classical lexicon are embedded within each category below.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 2 (School Purpose and Goal): 
  - Strengthen explicit classical telos language in public-facing materials (e.g., clearly articulate a classical mission that centers on truth, goodness, beauty and human flourishing).
  - De-emphasize workforce-readiness language in core mission statements; elevate formation and liberal-arts aims.
  - Introduce a stated commitment to Great Books or canonical texts as a core component of at least certain grade levels, with a public-facing rationale.

- Element 3 (Curriculum): 
  - Introduce explicit Great Books/primary-sources components and a more overt liberal-arts framing (grammar, logic, rhetoric) within core curriculum descriptions.
  - Add a publicly stated history of Western civilization or classical-era history as foundational content across grades (beyond social studies history).
  - Minimize or balance project-based learning with classical pedagogical practices (recitation, narration, memory work, and occasional Socratic-style inquiry) to align more clearly with a classical approach.

- Element 6 (Parents & Community):
  - Formalize parent formation and classical book clubs and lay out a structured “parents as co-teachers” program with measurable activities (reading groups, family liturgies, study guides) that connect with classical aims.
  - Expand family-centered offerings into a formal, public-facing pathway that mirrors the school’s classical mission (e.g., family sessions on classical education philosophy, household liturgy alignment, or liturgical calendar events).

- General needle-moving items:
  - More explicit articulation of religious/metaphysical foundations if the school intends to align with a classical framework whose core signals include transcendental truth, virtue, and beauty.
  - Publicly publish a pedagogical plan that includes elements of scholé, contemplation, and structured rhetoric (with grade-appropriate examples).
