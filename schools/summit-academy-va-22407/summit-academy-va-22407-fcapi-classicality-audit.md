## Executive Summary

The Summit Academy in Fredericksburg, VA (zip 22407) presents a robust classical-eduction profile grounded in Catholic tradition, with explicit emphasis on liberal arts, Great Books, and Socratic pedagogy. Public materials on the school’s site consistently frame education as formation of character and virtue, anchored in truth, goodness, and beauty, and pursued through a liberal-arts foundation. The school also foregrounds parental partnership and community engagement, and maintains a faculty roster whose biographies present teachers as committed mentors and living exemplars of the school’s ideals. The predominant signal across categories is CLASSICAL, with Element 3 (Curriculum) meeting many classical markers but not always explicit about every granular element (e.g., explicit reference to Trivium/Quadrivium grammar-logic-rhetoric in published statements). Overall, the aggregate assessment leans CLASSICAL, with a note that Curriculum shows some room for fuller alignment with all six “essential elements” in practice.

Key evidence anchors from the public materials include:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations: explicit rooting in Catholic tradition and Christ-centered aims; formation in Christian virtue; shared commitments between faith and learning. See statements about being “Rooted in the Catholic Tradition” and mission-driven formation of virtue.  
- Purpose and goal: education framed as formation toward wisdom and virtue, not merely job-prep; explicit reference to truth/goodness/beauty as real, knowable realities.  
- Curriculum: emphasis on Liberal Arts and Great Books tradition; explicit mention of Great Books and the Liberal Arts as organizing framework for study. 
- Pedagogy: clear use of Socratic seminars and a rhetoric-informed approach; emphasis on memory, wonder, and active student participation in upper grades. 
- Faculty: biographies describing teachers as mentors living the curriculum and modeling virtue; multiple faculty profiles illustrate ongoing professional formation and practical commitment. 
- Parents & Community: strong emphasis on partnering with parents, open houses, visits, and opportunities to engage in education (e.g., information sessions, tours). 

Aggregate ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Reasoning: The Summit Academy strongly aligns with classical education through Catholic formation, Great Books, Liberal Arts orientation, and Socratic pedagogy, and it actively involves parents and the wider community. The Curriculum category shows substantial alignment but does not consistently publish explicit references to every traditional curricular element (e.g., explicit statement that grammar, logic, and rhetoric are central components of the curriculum). Thus, while overall classical in approach and execution, a single category sits at a level of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, pulling the aggregate rating to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle (for a fully Classical rating)
- Make explicit the Trivium framework in published curricular statements (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and show concrete alignment of student work with those stages across grades.
- Deepen articulation of liturgical or communal practices tied to the Catholic identity (e.g., explicit references to liturgical rhythm, worship, music in daily life) to strengthen Religious and Metaphysical Foundations.
- Expand on the Great Conversation through published syllabi or program descriptions that explicitly connect Great Books selections to disciplinary work (history, philosophy, literature) across grades.
- More explicit statements about the integration of faith and reason in daily classroom practice could further bolster Formation and Integration.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curricular Explicitness: Publish more explicit curricular mappings to the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and show cross-grade alignment with classic sequences. This would strengthen Element 3 to CLASSICAL by clarifying the grammar/logic/rhetoric core within a Great Books/Liberal Arts framework.
- Communal Liturgical Practices: Include more explicit references to liturgical practices or communal worship within the curriculum and schedule, to strengthen Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) with liturgical/communal evidence.
- Great Conversation Mapping: Provide published syllabi or program descriptions that demonstrate how Great Books selections connect to specific disciplinary outcomes, ensuring stronger Element 3 alignment with the Sixth Element and Great Conversation expectations.
- Narrative of Wonder: More explicit articulation of how wonder, awe, and poetry are cultivated across grade levels (beyond the outdoor program) could reinforce Element 3 and Element 4, linking contemplation with rigorous discourse.
