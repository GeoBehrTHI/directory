## Executive Summary

West Christian Prep (WCP) TX 77077 presents a clearly classical-inflected profile, anchored by a stated mission of classical Christian formation, an all-boys environment, a Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric curriculum approach, and explicit parental partnership. Public materials emphasize virtues such as wisdom, stature, and grace, a Christ-centered community, and a pedagogy that foregrounds structure, memorization-like practices, and rhetorical training. Across categories 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations), 2 (Purpose & Goal), 4 (Pedagogy), 5 (Faculty), and 6 (Parents & Community), the school demonstrates many hallmark classical indicators. Element 3 (Curriculum) shows strong foundational elements (grammar-logic-rhetoric) but is weaker on explicit Great Books / Great Conversation emphasis in public materials, making that category the sole candidate for a somewhat classical reading. Overall, the school qualifies as CLASSICAL.

Key takeaways
- Religious & metaphysical foundations are robust: Christ-centered, classical Christian framing, and liturgical cues in public materials, with explicit formation aims tied to wisdom and virtue. This aligns strongly with classical expectations in this domain. Evidence: “classical Christian education in a Christ-centered community,” “Luke 2:52,” and references to prayer and church in site content.  
- Purpose & Goal are clearly formation- and virtue-oriented: formation of virtue and wisdom, and human flourishing framed in a classical-Christian telos. Evidence: mission statements about forming boys in wisdom, stature, and grace; partnership with parents for formation. 
- Curriculum centers the Trivium (Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric) and classical framing; explicit Great Books/Great Conversation are not publicly advertised, which lowers the Curriculum score to Somewhat Classical rather than Classical. Evidence: explicit mention of Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric; limited (if any) Great Books references in public site copies. 
- Pedagogy leans classical with emphasis on rhetoric, memory-style elements, and Socratic-like engagement in upper levels implied by rhetoric work; lower-level singing/memory practices are less explicit in site materials but are supported by the classical lexicon. Evidence: “grammar-logic-rhetoric approach,” “rhetoric and have opportunities to give speeches,” and indications of a public-facing emphasis on classical pedagogy. 
- Faculty are presented as virtuous, male role models who mentor and form character; public materials emphasize an all-male faculty and mentorship. This aligns with classical expectations around the teacher as exemplar. Evidence: “ALL-MALE FACULTY,” “Mentorship & curriculum built for boys,” and “Male role models lead every classroom.” 
- Parents & Community are positioned as co-educators and partners in formation; families are welcomed and engaged through previews, visits, and joint formation efforts. Evidence: partnership language and parent-facing content, admissions/parent preview options. 

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Public materials consistently frame WCP as a classical Christian program: “classical Christian education in a Christ-centered community” and “Kingdom-centered boys,” with references to prayer, church, and scripture (Luke 2:52) on site.  
  - References to an all-male faculty and mentorship emphasize virtue-guided character formation rooted in a religious worldview. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Christ-centered, classical Christian, theology-related framing, prayer, church, Luke 2:52, virtue, wisdom, grace, God (as part of mission). 
  - Negative keywords: none evident in public content (no relativism, secular humanism, or value-neutral framing observed). 
- Why this rating
  - The school foregrounds metaphysical commitments (theology, virtue, a sacred/worship-oriented frame) in public materials, consistent with a Classical-Christian orientation. This aligns strongly with the Positive indicators for this category and shows minimal presence of negative indicators.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Mission emphasizes forming boys through a classical Christian education; language centers on virtue, wisdom, and love of God, with a telos toward human flourishing and character. 
  - Phrasing such as “Men of Wisdom,” “Men of Grace,” and a mission to form boys in wisdom, stature, and grace signals formation over mere information. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education (implied via classical framing), telos, great conversation (implied in classical mission language though not always explicit), human flourishing (implicit), love of God. 
  - Negative keywords: minimal to none detected (no explicit emphasis on workforce prep or 21st-century skills as primary telos). 
- Why this rating
  - The public materials strongly present formation-focused aims, virtue/wisdom, and a transcendent telos anchored in Christian faith, meeting the core criteria of the category.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Explicit mention of a Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric approach signals the Trivium-based curriculum characteristic of classical education. 
  - The program frames “classical” study, with attention to memory/recitation themes in the classical lexicon, and a historical framing consistent with classical curricula. 
  - Public materials do not consistently advertise Great Books / Great Conversation, nor explicit coverage of the Quadrivium or sustained Great Books reading lists in the site content. This absence is notable when measured against the classical rubric’s explicit Curriculum criteria. 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Seven Liberal Arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), classical, history (implied through historical framing in classical education), rhetoric, memory work (implied by classical pedagogy; not always explicit on site), possibly Latin/Greek (not explicit in content shown). 
  - Negative keywords present: lack of explicit Great Books / Great Conversation references; lack of explicit quadrivium/Latin/Greek across public site text. Negative indicators in the classical lexicon include “Lack of Great Books and Historical Engagement” and “Lack of an Interdisciplinary, Integrated Approach,” but the school does not publicly advertise those elements, which weighs toward a partial classical status. 
- Why this rating
  - The Curriculum strongly centers the Trivium and classical framing, but the absence of explicit Great Books, Great Conversation, and broader quadrivium/historical interdisciplinarity in public materials means it does not fully meet all six liberal-arts/Great-Conversation criteria in the public-facing materials. Therefore, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is warranted.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Pedagogical framing includes a Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric approach and rhetoric training, with upper-level emphasis on speeches and rhetoric exercises; this aligns with classical pedagogy expectations. 
  - Descriptions of boy-focused, structured pedagogy (mentorship, deliberate development of virtue through models) and a framework that implies Socratic-like discussion and formal recitation/rhetoric training in upper levels align with classical methods. 
  - Language around “formation through imitation and practice” and mentorship is present in the public materials (e.g., “Men of Wisdom,” “Men of Grace”). 
- Positive/Negative keyword analysis
  - Positive keywords present: Socratic dialogue (implied through rhetoric and discussion in upper levels), rational discourse, recitation/memory, rhetoric training, seminar-like engagement, training in public speaking. 
  - Negative keywords: explicit “experiential learning,” project-based learning dominance, or student-led constructivism are not foregrounded in site materials; the absence of these negative indicators supports/classical alignment. 
- Why this rating
  - The pedagogy signals classical method through explicit grammar-logic-rhetoric framing and public emphasis on rhetoric and speech, consistent with a classical pedagogy. Some classic elements (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars and memory-based practices) are implied or supported by the broader classical lexicon in the sources.

### Element 6: School Parents and Community — CLASSICAL

What Would Move the Needle
- Element 3 (Curriculum): Explicitly publish and highlight Great Books and Great Conversation elements, including a stated plan for integrating Great Books with a Western-civilizational curriculum and, where appropriate, aQuadrivium emphasis (Latin/Greek, mathematics, astronomy). Include explicit references to a cross-disciplinary approach that ties history, literature, philosophy, and sciences together. This would move Curriculum from Somewhat Classical to Classical.
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide explicit public details of Socratic seminar/adult-led dialectic practices in upper grades, and clearly indicate any structured contemplative (Scholé) days or rest-based learning to balance rigorous pedagogy. This would strengthen the Pedagogy category toward a fuller Classical profile.
- Element 3 and Element 5 (Faculty): If not already explicit, publish a public-facing “living curriculum” statement and teacher portraits that show how faculty embody classical virtues and exemplars, reinforcing the Faculty category.
- Element 2 (Purpose & Goal) and Element 6 (Parents & Community): Continue and deepen the public articulation of telos (truth, goodness, beauty) and formalize parent education opportunities (book studies, philosophy of education gatherings) to reinforce these categories.
