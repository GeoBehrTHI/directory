# ABSTRACT

Overall Classification: CLASSICAL

CLA’s public-facing materials present a coherent classical, Christian education model anchored in formation, virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty. The school foregrounds Biblical worldview, the Great Commission, and a parental partnership that forms the basis for the student’s education. The curriculum embraces classical elements (Trivium and Quadrivium) and integrates Great Books/Western civilization within a Christian framework, while the University-Model delivery emphasizes co-teaching with parents and at-home study. Pedagogical delivery is classical in content but employs a modern home-based co-teaching model, producing a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy, and NOT CLASSICAL for Faculty given explicit living-curriculum language is not strongly present in accessible materials. The Parents & Community category is CLASSICAL given the strong emphasis on parental partnership and formation.

Key insights by category:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. The school centers on Christian faith, Biblical Worldview, theology, and the Kingdom of God as foundational to all learning.
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. The formation of virtue and wisdom, not merely information or job-readiness, is present and explicit.
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Clear emphasis on Trivium/Quadrivium, classical canon, and integration with faith.
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The University-Model and home-education partnership align with classical aims but the explicit use of traditional Socratic/Scholé practice is not robustly evidenced in public messaging.
- Faculty: NOT CLASSICAL. While classical aims are evident, explicit faculty archetypes (living curriculum, portraits) are not strongly present in accessible CLA content.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong emphasis on parental partnership, training, and co-education responsibilities.

Important caveats:

- Some key classical practices (e.g., explicit Socratic seminars, scholé days, or sustained recitation programs) are not strongly evidenced in visible CLA content. The classical orientation is clear, but the delivery model (University-Model) introduces modern pedagogical dynamics not always captured in classical lexicons.
