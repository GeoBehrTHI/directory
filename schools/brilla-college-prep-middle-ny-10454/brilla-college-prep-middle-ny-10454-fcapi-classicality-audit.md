## Executive Summary

Brilla College Prep Middle School (Bronx, NY 10454) demonstrates a discernible classical orientation centered on virtue, character formation, and a rigorous academic culture, but it does not consistently articulate or implement the full spectrum of classical education’s core practices (e.g., Great Books, grammars of language, Socratic pedagogy, liturgical or religious scaffolding, and a living, mentor-driven faculty culture) in publicly surfaced materials. The public materials emphasize virtue-centered education, character formation, and college-oriented outcomes, with explicit notes about openness and access (e.g., free enrollment, no entrance exams). However, there is limited evidence of explicit liberal arts curriculum elements (grammar–logic–rhetoric), Great Books study, or the full spectrum of classical pedagogy (Socratic seminars, memory work, chanting, or a formal “living curriculum”). On balance, the school shows meaningful classical cues but does not consistently meet the full rubric across all six categories. Overall, Brilla College Prep Middle School is best characterized as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear opportunities to move toward CLASSICAL in multiple areas.

Key public signals observed:
- Emphasis on virtue and character in service of collegiate and life outcomes (e.g., “academically rigorous and virtue-centered education,” “developing the minds and hearts,” “character necessary to succeed in college and beyond”).
- Public commitment to accessibility and equity (free enrollment, no entrance exams) and a statement of joyful, relationship-based culture.
- Presence of a Humanities faculty role and K-8 orientation, but no explicit Great Books, grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence, or explicit Socratic pedagogy in the surfaced materials.
- The school’s branding as part of a network of eight Brilla schools emphasizes high performance and college-going expectations, with a continued focus on virtue-centered learning.

The following category-by-category analysis uses the Six Essential Elements’ Positive/Negative Keyword indicators as the scoring framework, triangulated with the Brilla materials cited above and the classical lexicon.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize virtue-centered education and character formation as foundational aims (e.g., “academically rigorous and virtue-centered education,” “developing the minds and hearts of our students…character”).
- The site does not present explicit references to theology, liturgy, or liturgical practice. There is no stated grounding in theology or liturgical worship in the surfaced content.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators present: virtue, character (Element 1 positives in the Classical Lexicon). The lexicon lists virtue and the virtuous life as core positive signals; “theology,” “liturgy,” and related liturgical practices are not surfaced. 
- Negative indicators: no explicit messaging around relativism, secular humanism, or morally neutral schooling; however, the absence of theology or liturgy weakens a full Classically religious/metaphysical grounding. The absence is informational rather than explicit negative signal.

Why this rating
- The school clearly foregrounds virtue and character formation, aligning with the Positive keywords for Element 1 (virtue, moral realism). The lack of explicit theological grounding, liturgical practice, or metaphysical theology reduces it from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The Brilla materials show moral formation as central, but do not present a comprehensive religious/metaphysical program as defined by the full Classical rubric. 

What would move the needle
- Articulate a grounded metaphysical or theological framework (even in a non-denominational form) and integrate liturgical or communal practices (e.g., a morning routine with reverence or sung/musical elements aligned with a shared calendar). This would elevate Element 1 toward CLASSICAL. See the lexicon’s emphasis on theology, liturgy, and sacred practices as key signals for a higher rating.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

### Element 3: School Curriculum — NOT CLASSICAL

This contrasts with the classical lexicon’s prescribed curricular signals (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; history as a liberal-arts core). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The lexicon identifies liberals-arts-forward cues as positives; Brilla’s surfaced content does not demonstrate these cues. Therefore, positive keyword presence is minimal to none in the surfaced public materials. 
- Negative indicators: the absence of standard classical curriculum signals (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Latin/Greek; primary sources) is a negative signal for Element 3 from a classical perspective.

Why this rating
- The absence of classical curriculum signals in the surfaced Brilla materials leads to a NOT CLASSICAL classification for Element 3. The Brilla materials emphasize virtue-centered, college-oriented outcomes rather than a liberal-arts curriculum with the core classical scaffolds. 

What would move the needle
- Explicitly adopt a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with a history canon built around Great Books or primary sources, and implement a Western civilizational survey integrated across grades. Add Latin/Greek or classical-language components and a systematic history sequence that aligns with “history throughout the curriculum” as a canonical element. These steps would move Element 3 toward CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The surfaced Brilla materials show a Humanities teacher and general K–8 structure, with no explicit mention of Socratic seminars, memory work, chanting, recitation, or other classical pedagogy signals. The content also does not reveal a dedicated Scholé rhythm (restful learning days, contemplative cycles) or an explicit upper-school rhetoric festival or debates program..
- The site highlights a joyful, virtue-centered culture and relationship-building but does not demonstrate the explicit, recurring use of contemplative or Socratic pedagogy that characterizes classical pedagogy as described in the lexicon..

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive indicators: within the classical pedagogy lexicon, signals include Socratic seminars, narration/recitation, memory work, chanting, and memory-driven pedagogy. These are not visibly present in the surfaced Brilla materials. The only proximate signal is the inclusion of a humanities-focused teacher role, which is insufficient for a strong positive signal. 
- Negative indicators: absence of explicit classical pedagogy signals (Socratic dialogue, Paideia seminars, memory work, recitation, liturgical or contemplative cycles) is a negative signal for Element 4.

Why this rating
- Without explicit, regular use of Socratic/Paideia-based pedagogy, chanting, or memory work, Element 4 remains NOT CLASSICAL. The presence of a humanities focus is not enough to meet the canonical pedagogy signals in the Classical Lexicon. 

What would move the needle
- Implement a structured classical pedagogy across grades: regular Socratic seminars, memory work, narration/recitation cycles, and a deliberate shift to a rest-focused Scholé rhythm with contemplative learning days. Develop students’ rhetoric through speeches and debates in upper grades. These changes would move Element 4 toward CLASSICAL.
