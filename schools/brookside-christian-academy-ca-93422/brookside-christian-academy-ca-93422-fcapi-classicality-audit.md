## Executive Summary

Brookside Christian Academy in Atascadero (CA 93422) presents itself publicly as a Christian, classical-leaning school, branding itself as “Christian, Classical Education in a Joyful & Relational Community.” Its stated mission centers on forming students through spiritual discipline and character, aiming to cultivate truth, beauty, and goodness in alignment with a Christian paideia. Evidence from the school’s public materials emphasizes pursuing Truth, recognizing beauty, and knowing good from evil, and it frames education as formation with a purpose driven by faith and reverence for God. These elements correspond with a classical orientation in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose and Goal, and they signal a classical sensibility toward parents as partners in formation. However, explicit, traditional classical-curriculum markers (e.g., explicit seven liberal arts pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric); Great Books/Great Conversation; widespread reclamation of scholé in classroom pedagogy) are not clearly enumerated in the public materials available on the site. Public-facing materials also do not clearly document a full Great Books–centric curriculum or Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades. Taken together, Brookside demonstrates foundational classical-Christian themes (especially in Religious Foundations and Purpose), but it does not consistently articulate all six elements of a prototypical classical program across all six categories.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Why SOMEWHAT: Clear classical-Christian framing, mission of formation and transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty), and explicit parental partnership align with several classical indicators. However, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation, as well as detailed, page-by-page signaling of a full liberal-arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; seven liberal arts; and sustained Socratic pedagogy) are not publicly documented in the content sampled from the school’s site. See category-by-category notes below for the evidence and keyword analyses that support this overall assessment. Evidence sources include Brookside’s public mission statements and site content describing the school’s classical-Christian framing. 

What would move the needle (if continuing toward a robust Classical profile):
- Explicitly articulate Great Books and the Great Conversation in Our Philosophy or Curriculum pages, with representative titles and a history of reading programs.
- Name and describe the use of the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium where appropriate, and show how these liberal-arts stages unfold across K–12.
- Document concrete, classroom-level pedagogy (e.g., Socratic seminar, disciplined memory work, and contemplative Scholé practices) and show alignment with the public-facing mission.
- Provide a clear description of a Great Books–led history/literature sequence and a cross-curricular integration plan that connects history, literature, and philosophy across grades.
- Elevate visible evidence of the faculty as mentors and living curriculum, with explicit teacher-practice statements that connect virtue formation and scholarly excellence to daily practice.
- Increase parental engagement resources that mirror classical practices (e.g., book studies, liturgical/seasonal celebrations, family catechesis plans) to reinforce the partnership with families.
