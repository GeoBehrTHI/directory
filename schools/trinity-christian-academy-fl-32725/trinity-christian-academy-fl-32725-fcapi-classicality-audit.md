## Executive Summary

Trinity Christian Academy (FL 32725) presents a clearly classical Christian orientation in its public-facing materials, centering biblical worldview, the Great Books conversation, and a Trivium-inspired approach to curriculum. The school emphasizes formation of character and virtue alongside intellectual development, and it explicitly positions itself within the classical tradition (including references to Great Books, the Trivium, and a biblical worldview). However, the publicly accessible materials provide less explicit, detailed description of certain pedagogical practices (e.g., formal Socratic seminars, explicit use of chanting/recitation, or liturgical practices) than the strongest classical models often cite. Based on the six-category classical rubric, Trinity Christian Academy’s materials align most strongly with CLASSICAL in Categories 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6, with Element 4 showing partial alignment and thus receiving a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating. The aggregate assessment places the school in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL range.

Key observations:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are evident: explicit biblical framing, a stated Statement of Faith, and a biblically integrated curriculum framework. Evidence includes Bible-first routines and a biblically grounded worldview.
- School Purpose and Goal emphasize formation, virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, rather than vocational justifications alone. This aligns with the classical aim of forming humans rather than just information transfer.
- Curriculum shows clear classical elements: reference to Great Books, the Great Conversation, Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and a biblically integrated liberal-arts framework. This is a strong classical signal.
- Pedagogy appears to include classical methods (grammar/logic/rhetoric, likely some forms of disciplined dialogue and memory work), but explicit, recurring descriptions of Socratic seminars or chant/memory routines in public pages are not robustly demonstrated in the accessible materials. This yields a partial, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, reading for Pedagogy.
- Faculty portrayal leans into virtue, mentorship, and a living-curriculum posture, consistent with classical models. The materials emphasize teachers as virtuous, God-fearing mentors and as central figures in the educational project.
- Parent and Community relations are foregrounded through partnership with families and access to parent resources, aligning with the classical emphasis on families as co-educators.

# ABSTRACT

: Based on the publicly available school content and the classical-elements rubric, Trinity Christian Academy’s materials lean strongly CLASSICAL in most categories, with Pedagogy showing somewhat less explicit classical implementation in the public materials. The overall classification is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The program centers Bible study and a biblical worldview as core framing for all subjects. Example: “The day starts with Bible and is followed by all core subjects taught from a Biblical worldview, with the addition of music, art, and physical education—an educational feast of God’s truth, beauty and goodness!”
- A formal Statement of Faith articulates Trinitarian theology (God the Father, Jesus the Son, the Holy Spirit) and orthodox Christian doctrine as foundational.
- Public materials frame the curriculum as integrated within a Christian worldview and biblical formation of the whole person.

Positive keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present on public materials: truth, goodness, beauty, virtue (implicit in “pursuit” of these transcendental goods), wisdom, sacred/worship language (Bible, theology, biblical worldview, faith).
- Direct textual signals include: truth, beauty, goodness; biblical worldview; Statement of Faith; Great Books framing within a Christian context.

Negative keyword analysis

- No explicit public-facing signals of secular relativism or value-neutral ethics; no clear presence of “secular ethics,” “values-neutral,” or “religion-free” language.

Why this rating

- The materials consistently foreground transcendental ideals (truth, goodness, beauty) anchored in theology and sacred texts, with explicit theological grounding (biblical worldview and a formal Statement of Faith). This aligns with Transcendental Moral Realism and Grounded Theology signals typical of CLASSICAL programs. The absence of secular-relativist language in the public content further supports classification as CLASSICAL.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- The school frames education as formation toward virtue and wisdom, not merely work-readiness, and centers truth/goodness/beauty as real and knowable. The system emphasizes formation through biblical/worldview-integrated study and Great Books language.
- Phrasings such as “pursuit of truth, beauty, and goodness” and “integration of subjects under a biblical worldview” reflect the classical telos and paideia aims rather than merely job preparation.
- The Great Conversation and Great Books framing supplies a classical sense of intellectual formation beyond information.

Positive keyword analysis

Negative keyword analysis

Why this rating

- The emphasis on formation, virtue, and the triad of truth/goodness/beauty, plus the explicit Great Books/canonical canon framing, aligns strongly with CLASSICAL purposes and goals. The absence of vocational language in public messaging reinforces CLASSICAL rather than NOT CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public-facing materials emphasize classical content (Trivium, Great Books) and formation, but explicit, recurring descriptions of timeless classical pedagogical practices such as Socratic seminars, quiet scholé days, memorization/recitation, or liturgical rhythm are not consistently documented in the visible pages. The presence of “Great Conversation” and Trivium signals classical methods, but detailed pedagogy is less visible.
- There is some indication of restfulness or contemplative aspects through the emphasis on formation and integrated study, but explicit statements about contemplative pedagogy or Scholé days are not clearly documented at the public surface level.
- Some lower-level signals about memory and recitation echo classical practices in the broader literature, but not strongly evidenced in the public site text (the Six Elements lexicon is the source of these signals rather than the school's own public pages).

Positive keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: Great Books, Great Conversation, Trivium (grammar/logic/rhetoric), liberal arts, formation, memory or recitation signals are not strongly evidenced in public text; cantons of recitation/memory are not explicit on the site.
- The strong signal remains: Trivium + Great Books content.

Negative keyword analysis

- Lack of explicit Socratic seminars, formal Socratic or dialectic pedagogy on visible pages; limited evidence for chanting/singing/recitation or liturgical pedagogy on public pages.
- Absence of clearly public statements about Scholé days or contemplative rest in classrooms.

Why this rating

- The presence of canonical classical elements (Trivium, Great Books) supports CLASSICAL. However, the absence of explicit, consistently described contemplative/scholé pedagogy and the lack of visible Socratic-seminar language in public content lead to a partial, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL classification in this category.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Pedagogy (Element 4): Provide and publicly highlight explicit descriptions of classical pedagogy in action (e.g., documented use of Socratic seminars, disciplined dialectic, memory work/recitation routines, and Scholé days) to elevate from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL to CLASSICAL. Public case studies or sample lesson plans showing Socratic dialogue and disciplined memory work would strengthen the signal.
- For Liturgical/Communal Practices (Element 1 sub-aspect): If not already explicit, publish a clear description of any liturgical rhythms, communal prayer, morning worship, or chapel practices to demonstrate liturgical and communal foundations in a more concrete way.
- For Curriculum (Element 3 detail): Provide public, explicit mapping of the Trivium components (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) across grades, with sample Great Books lists and a clear articulation of how Great Books are integrated into each discipline across the K–12 experience.
- For Faculty (Element 5): Include faculty profiles that articulate how teachers model virtue, their own ongoing formation, and how they participate in mentoring and collaborative growth (e.g., professional learning communities, mentorship programs). This would strengthen the perception of teachers as the living curriculum.
- For Parent & Community (Element 6): Expand parent-facing programming (e.g., book studies, seminars, parent evenings, and clear pathways for deeper parent formation) and publicly document these offerings to reinforce the partnership narrative.
