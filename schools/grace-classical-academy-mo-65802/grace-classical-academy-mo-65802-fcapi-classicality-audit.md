## Executive Summary

Grace Classical Academy Missouri (Springfield, 65802) presents a robust classical Christian orientation across its public materials and school narrative. Public materials consistently frame the school as Christ-centered and “classical,” with explicit integration of faith into all subjects, a mission to form character and virtue, and a curriculum and pedagogy aligned with classical education (grammar–logic–rhetoric, exposure to Latin, and engagement with historical and literary tradition). The school also foregrounds partnership with parents and broad community touchpoints (e.g., enrollment processes, parent journeys, and collaborations with Evangel University). Taken together with the classical criteria in the knowledge-base rubric, Grace Classical Academy demonstrates CLASSICAL alignment in all six categories (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community). The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key evidence evolves from the school’s own materials, including explicit statements about Christian-centered education, a Biblical worldview, and the integration of faith across disciplines, as well as a vision/mission that emphasizes truth, goodness, and beauty within a classical framework. For example, the school presents a vision and mission grounded in a Biblical worldview and the belief that education should cultivate truth, goodness, and beauty; it notes that “Christianity is more than a subject we study: it is the lens through which all knowledge is viewed” and that “education should cultivate a desire for truth, goodness, and beauty” (CLASSICAL orientation). It also emphasizes liturgical and communal aspects (Bible, chapel) and a Christ-centered educational project across all grades. The curriculum and pedagogy are described within a classical frame (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; Great Books/Western tradition; Socratic or seminaring elements appear in the broader classical lexicon; drama and memory work are used; and the school highlights the primacy of character and virtue formation). The school’s public materials emphasize parental partnership and community formation in alignment with classical aims.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Since all six categories are CLASSICAL, no major structural needle-moving changes are required for a full CLASSICAL rating.
- If desired, minor refinements could include:
  - Explicitly naming and highlighting Great Books/Great Conversation across more grade bands to strengthen the Curriculum category’s explicit tie to canonical texts.
  - Increasing explicit articulation of Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy in high school programs to align the Pedagogy category even more tightly with classical practice.
  - Broadening public-facing statements on the Great Conversation and Great Books to showcase a direct link between student work and canonical works in science, literature, and philosophy, beyond general references to “classical” traditions.
  - Amplifying explicit articulation of “Scholé” (restful learning) days in the school calendar to reinforce the Pedagogy dimension in day-to-day operations.

Note: These are minor refinements rather than fundamental changes, given the strong CLASSICAL alignment already demonstrated across all six categories.
