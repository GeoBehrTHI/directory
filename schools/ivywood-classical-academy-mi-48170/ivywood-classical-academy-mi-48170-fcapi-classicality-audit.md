## Executive Summary

Ivywood Classical Academy Michigan (ICA, Plymouth/Canton area, zip 48170) presents a robust Classical education profile with clear emphases on virtue formation, a content-rich liberal-arts curriculum, and teacher-led, Socratic pedagogy. Public materials consistently describe a virtues-based, Great Books–oriented program that foregrounds character formation, truth, goodness, and beauty, with explicit citations to Socratic discussion, memory through recitation, and a strong teacher-centred classroom. The school also highlights parent engagement and a community of learners, along with a public charter model.

Based on the six-category Classical rubric drawn from the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School, ICA demonstrates strong alignment with Classical education in most categories, with Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) showing the most room for explicit alignment (the public materials emphasize virtue and formation but are less explicit about theology, liturgical practice, or formal doctrinal grounding). Overall, ICA falls solidly in CLASSICAL territory, with Element 1 being the primary area where it sits at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL and the remaining categories meeting the Classical criteria.

Key insights: ICA’s public materials consistently articulate virtue formation, truth, goodness, beauty, and lifelong formation through Great Books and liberal-arts approaches. Socratic pedagogy and teacher-led classrooms are highlighted, and parent/community involvement is evident. The main potential needle-movement would be to strengthen explicit theological grounding and liturgical/community practices on public pages to meet Element 1’s theological and liturgical signals more clearly.

What Would Move the Needle

- Add explicit statements or pages tying the Classical curriculum to theological foundations or explicit metaphysical commitments (e.g., a public-facing theology statement or integration notes across disciplines).
- Publicly enumerate liturgical or communal practices (when they occur, what form they take, and their purpose in formation).
- Clarify how theology, worship, or sacred-tradition elements are integrated into the Great Books and history curriculum, if applicable, to strengthen Element 1 alignment without compromising nonsectarian public charter requirements.

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Public materials emphasize a virtues-based, character-formation approach within a classical education framework (e.g., “virtues-based, content-rich, classical education tradition” and “Character formation is at the heart of a classical education. … taught virtues such as wisdom, justice, and courage.”).
- The materials frequently reference truth, goodness, and beauty as formative aims (e.g., “truth, goodness, and beauty” referenced in the context of forming virtuous citizens).
- Staff bios and narratives mention religious life (e.g., serving in church, moral formation) but public-facing documentation does not explicitly state formal liturgical practices or a theological framework integrated across the curriculum.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords present: virtue, formation, truth, goodness, beauty, moral character, sacred tradition (contextually), contemplation. Examples drawn from ICA materials include “virtues-based,” “Character formation,” “truth, goodness, and beauty,” and “formation of the soul.”
- Negative keywords present: Not explicitly evidenced in public materials (no clear statements about the lack of theological grounding or liturgical neglect on public pages). Public content does not demonstrate explicit theology or liturgical practices.

Why this rating

- The school demonstrates a strong moral-formation and teleology (transcending mere info delivery) aligned with Classical education’s moral/metaphysical aims, but lacks explicit, public-facing theological grounding and liturgical/community practice language on core school materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation for Element 1 rather than CLASSICAL.

Cited elements

- “virtues-based, content-rich, classical education tradition” and “Character formation is at the heart of a classical education … virtues such as wisdom, justice, and courage.”
- “truth, goodness, and beauty” in the formation context.
- Personal faith-life signals (serving in church) in staff bios as context for religious life, not as formal programmatic liturgy.

Why this rating

Evidence quotes

Why this rating

Evidence quotes
