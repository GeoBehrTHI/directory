# Bishop Ryan Catholic School — Classicality Audit
Website used: https://bishopryan.com

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Why: Bishop Ryan publicly centers its mission on “education in virtue,” explicitly grounding education in theology, liturgy, and the formation of the whole person. The site highlights daily prayer, the opportunity to attend daily Mass, religion classes, retreats, service, and celebrations throughout the liturgical year, all of which align strongly with the “Religious & Metaphysical Foundations” and “Purpose & Goal” elements of classical education as defined in our Six Elements framework and lexicon (Mass, prayer, liturgy, virtue, formation)    . The school also states it adopted a “classically rooted educational plan” in 2018, signaling intent and orientation .

What’s missing: The public materials currently do not document the classical liberal arts curriculum (Trivium/Quadrivium), Great Books/primary texts, Latin requirements, or Socratic pedagogy. Instead, we see typical modern markers (e.g., “Advanced Placement opportunities”), which weakly signal a conventional college-prep posture in pedagogy rather than distinctly classical methods like Socratic seminars, chanting/memorization, or scholé/restful learning   . Faculty formation and a public portrait of a teacher are also not described.

Net: Strong theological and virtue-first identity with community and liturgical life; insufficient publicly visible evidence of classical curriculum and pedagogy.

## “What would move the needle” (to reach CLASSICAL)
Concrete, public-facing steps:
1) Publish a classical curriculum map:
   - Name the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) as organizing arts; show where each is taught and how it shapes instruction and assessment K–12  .
   - Publish Great Books/primary source reading lists by grade; emphasize the Great Conversation across humanities and sciences .
   - Clarify classical languages (Latin/Greek): scope/sequence, aims, and rationale .

2) Describe classical pedagogy in action:
   - Socratic/seminar in upper grades; narration/recitation/chanting in lower grades; progymnasmata & imitation in writing; rhetoric orations/capstone; debate/oratory strands .
   - Explain scholé/restful learning (depth over pace; multum non multa) and how AP (if retained) is subordinated to formation, not the driver .

3) Faculty formation and “portrait”:
   - Publish a Portrait of a Teacher and outline faculty formation in classical pedagogy; underscore teachers as mentors/models of virtue and the “living curriculum” .

4) Parent formation:
   - Publicize parent book clubs/seminars on classical education and household liturgical alignment; emphasize parents as co-educators .
