## ABSTRACT

Fortis Academy demonstrates a sustained CLASSICAL orientation across all six categories of the classical-education rubric.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Fortis presents a Christ-centered, biblical worldview with explicit theology and a focus on truth, beauty, and goodness as transcendentals. Public materials emphasize Christian formation and biblical worldview, with theology as a core, even while acknowledging its non-denominational stance.  
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. The mission emphasizes formation of virtue and wisdom, human flourishing, and the Great Conversation as ends of education. Public materials articulate a telos aligned with classical aims rather than mere job preparation. 
- Curriculum: CLASSICAL. Fortis foregrounds grammar–logic–rhetoric (Trivium), Great Books, classical languages, and a unified liberal-arts curriculum across K–12. The Great Conversation and Great Books appear as central to curriculum aims. 
- Pedagogy: CLASSICAL. The school describes Socratic discussions, memory work (recitation, chanting), and formation through imitation as core methods; Scholé-like elements are also presented in the pedagogy.  
- Faculty: CLASSICAL. Fortis highlights distinguished, virtuous, and mentor-like faculty forming the living curriculum; collaboration and collegial culture are emphasized. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. The University-Model approach integrates active parental involvement, parent co-teaching roles, and ongoing parent education/engagement. 

Overall, Fortis Academy presents a coherent CLASSICAL profile consistent with the Six Essential Elements’ criteria for a classical school. The public materials explicitly align with transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty), formation, Great Books, a Trivium-based curriculum, Socratic pedagogy, exemplary faculty, and robust parental partnership. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.
