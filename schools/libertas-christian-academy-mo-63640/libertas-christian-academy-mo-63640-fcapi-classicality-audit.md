## Executive Summary

Libertas Christian Academy (Farmington, MO) presents a robust Classical-Christian program that aligns closely with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School. The public materials consistently articulate a Christian foundation, a Trivium-centered curriculum (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), and explicit emphasis on the seven liberal arts, great books, and Western civilization. The school itself states a mission to partner with Christian parents in the education and spiritual development of their children, and it situates its program within a church-affiliated context (Parkland Chapel). These elements collectively signal a classical orientation, with a strong emphasis on formation of character, virtue, and assent to transcendent truths. The overall assessment across the six rubric categories is CLASSICAL, with only minor ambiguity in pedagogy granularity (some traditional/classical indicators are present, while explicit description of every classical pedagogy element (e.g., specific Socratic seminar practices) is less overt in the site copy).

Key evidence highlights include:
- Explicit identification as a Christian and Classical school, with the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) defined as foundational and the School of Grammar (K-6), School of Logic (7-9), and School of Rhetoric (10-12) described on the site. 
- Clear articulation of the Classical curriculum emphasis, including the seven liberal arts and the great books of Western civilization. 
- The Word of God is presented as Truth, with explicit references to scripture and the school’s theological grounding (Statement of Faith, Parkland Chapel affiliation).  
- Mission and outcomes frame education as formation (virtue, wisdom) in a classical framework, and there is explicit language about partnership with Christian parents. 
- Evidence of liturgical/congregational framing (Discipleship, Word of God page, coat-of-arms motto Veritas Vos Liberabit) that reinforces metaphysical foundations.

## Overall Assessment

Aggregate Ranking: CLASSICAL

- The Libertas Christian Academy materials demonstrate a coherent Classical-Christian identity across Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Faculty, and Parents/Community.
- Pedagogy, while clearly rooted in classical aims (Trivium, Great Books, formation), is not as exhaustively described in public copy for every traditional pedagogical practice (e.g., explicit long-form Socratic seminars or scholé protocols). This yields a rating of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Pedagogy, but does not override the strong overall Classical alignment in the other five categories.
- Taken together, the school’s public-facing materials align with CLASSICAL education as defined by the Six Essential Elements.

What would move the needle (only relevant if SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL)
- If Pedagogy were documented with explicit, recurring practices such as regular Socratic seminars, formal scholé days, memorization/recitation protocols, and explicit formation-through-imitation practices in the upper grades, the Pedagogy rating would shift to CLASSICAL.
