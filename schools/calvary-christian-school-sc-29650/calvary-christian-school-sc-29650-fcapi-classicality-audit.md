# Classicality Audit Report

Executive Summary
- School: Austin Classical School (ACS)
- Website used: https://austinclassical.org/
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Why: ACS explicitly orients its mission to Christ and to cultivating wisdom and virtue through truth, goodness, and beauty, structures its curriculum around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with Latin, logic, rhetoric, and Great Books/Great Conversation, uses memorization/recitation and Socratic methods, forms faculty to love and teach within a classical, Christian frame, and practices robust partnership with parents (co‑teacher model), including chapel as a communal/liturgical practice for the school community. The school’s own language closely matches the positive indicators in the “Six Essential Elements” lexicon for each category (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose and Goal; Curriculum; Pedagogy; Faculty; Parents & Community)  .

How to read this report
- For each of the six categories, you will see:
  1) Keywords or phrases pulled from ACS’s public materials, 
  2) Mapping to the “Six Essential Elements” positive/negative indicators,
  3) A quantitative estimate of positive vs. negative keyword hits and coverage,
  4) A category rating (Classical / Somewhat Classical / Not Classical).
- All keyword criteria and signals are drawn from “Six Essential Elements” and the Classicality Lexicon (truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, theology, Socratic, scholé, wonder, chanting, project‑based, experiential learning, etc.)  .

Aggregate Rating
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Rationale: ACS’s mission and language align almost verbatim with the positive keyword indicators across all six elements (e.g., truth/goodness/beauty; wisdom/virtue; Trivium; Latin/logic/rhetoric; Great Books/Great Conversation; Socratic discussion; memorization/recitation; chapel/prayer; faculty virtue; parent partnership/co‑teacher model) as described in “Six Essential Elements” and the Classicality Lexicon  . Evidence is abundant in program pages, the academic model, upper school course grids, and the head of school and philosophy statements (e.g., chapel and prayer; Christian Studies courses; rhetoric thesis; Latin; explicit Socratic method; co‑teacher training)   .

Additional Observations (signals)
- House System (heritage feature for culture/virtue formation) present in Upper School life and required for participation; includes retreats, service, leadership, and competitions  .
- Low‑tech posture: FAQ emphasizes “embodied learning,” reading/annotating books, “we don’t use technology for technology’s sake” (supports a classical learning environment) .
