## Executive Summary

Chesterton Academy Rochester, NY 14617 presents a robust, Catholic, classical high-school model that aligns strongly with core classical education criteria. Public-facing materials emphasize a Catholic, formation-focused mission; a curriculum centered on liberal arts, liturgical life, and a humanities-forward progression; a pedagogy that includes Socratic discussion and rhetoric; a faculty described as a living curriculum who take formal vows to uphold magisterial teaching; and an active partner relationship with families and the broader school community. Taken together, these elements yield an overall CLASSICAL assessment, with the most explicit strengths in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose and Goal, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The Curriculum category shows substantial classical substance but lacks explicit public statements about some canonical phrases (e.g., direct “Great Books” language) that would push it toward a firmer “CLASSICAL” label in every detail; as such Element 3 is judged SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, while the other categories meet or exceed classical expectations. The aggregate classification is CLASSICAL.

Key strengths include: explicit Catholic identity and liturgical practice (Mass daily, daily prayer, Liturgy of the Hours), a mission framed around formation and virtue, a history/history-philosophy-theology-languages-heavy curriculum with readings of classic authors, a Socratic method emphasis in upper-level humanities, and a faithful-faculty model including the Magisterium oath. The school also foregrounds parental partnership, open houses, shadow days, and annual events consistent with classical education’s emphasis on family involvement.

Evidence sources include direct statements from Chesterton Academy Rochester’s site (Our Mission and Model, Catholic Identity, Spiritual Life, course and humanities offerings, faculty, house system, liturgy, and parental engagement) as well as a publicly accessible summary of the classical-education lexicon from the Six Essential Elements materials (Positive Keywords and formation-focused indicators). See citations to the Chesterton Academy Rochester site and to the Six Essential Elements documents for keyword frameworks.
