## Executive Summary

Ozark Catholic Academy (OCA) presents a robust classical-leaning profile embedded in a Catholic, faith-centered framework. The public materials emphasize formation of the whole person—intellectual, spiritual, moral, and physical—through a archtypal classical pedagogy (Humane Letters, Socratic inquiry, and interdisciplinary study) anchored in Catholic theology and liturgical life. The school explicitly positions its teachers as mentors and the faith life as central to daily life, with Mass and sacraments integrated into the culture. Curriculum elements highlighted on the site include humane letters, interdisciplinary study, and Socratic teaching methods, complemented by Latin, philosophy, and a college-prep orientation. Parental partnership is referenced as part of the educational mission, aligning with classical education’s view of parents as co-educators. Overall, the evidence supports classically oriented aims across all six categories; the aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: Catholic identity, liturgy, sacraments, and theological grounding permeate the culture.  
- Purpose and goals center on formation, virtue, and wisdom rather than mere job preparation. 
- Curriculum emphasizes humane letters, the Socratic method, liberal arts, and interdisciplinary study (with explicit mention of Socratic teaching).  
- Pedagogy includes contemplative/restful elements and Socratic/rhetorical training; upper-level engagement with rhetoric and speaking appears in the program. 
- Faculty are framed as mentors and living curriculum; a strong teacher-student relationship and collegial culture are highlighted. 
- Parents and community are situated as partners in education and formation, with explicit reference to parental involvement and the primary educator role.
