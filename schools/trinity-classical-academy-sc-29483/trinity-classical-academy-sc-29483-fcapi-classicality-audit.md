## Executive Summary

Trinity Classical Academy Summerville (SC 29483) demonstrates a clear classical orientation with a Christian formation emphasis, underscored by public materials that frame education as a moral and intellectual formation within a biblical-Christian worldview. Public pages emphasize a classical Christian identity, partnership with parents, and a curriculum built on the Core Knowledge Sequence, with attention to independent reading, memory-friendly routines, and foundational knowledge across disciplines. Evidence from the school’s site includes explicit references to a Christian environment, the aim of forming character, and parental partnership, as well as a curriculum built on a standardized classical core (Core Knowledge) rather than a primary focus on Great Books or the Trivium/Quadrivium framework. The faculty pages and student/parent materials present a culture of leadership, integrity, and character formation, while ongoing family engagement is evident through admissions, parent workshops, and volunteer opportunities. Taken as a whole, the school operates with a classical-influenced curriculum and Christian formation, but it does not consistently foreground several hallmark elements of traditional classical education (e.g., explicit Great Books/Great Conversation discussions, extensive Socratic pedagogy, or a primary emphasis on the Trivium/Quadrivium sequence). Therefore, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with strong CLASSICAL elements in religious/mission-focused areas and in community/partnership aspects.

Key insights by category (summary view):

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL. Clear Christian formation, theology-inflected framing, and character formation are foregrounded in public materials. Evidence of theology-integrated orientation and Christ-centered character is visible in multiple pages.
- Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL. Emphasis on virtue, formation, and partnership with families aligns with classical aims beyond mere job prep.
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Core Knowledge Sequence drives the curriculum; explicit Great Books/Great Conversation and Trivium/Quadrivium signals are not foregrounded in public materials.
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Public materials describe a rigorous, knowledge-driven program with some traditional practices, but do not consistently showcase Socratic seminars or the full classical pedagogy stack.
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Faculty roles are shown (Head of Schools, etc.), and character-focused attributes appear in student/parent materials, but “living curriculum” or explicit mentorship framing is not strongly foregrounded.
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL. Strong emphasis on partnership with families and parental involvement, including workshops and admissions processes that stress family engagement.
