## Executive Summary

This yields a nuanced result: the school demonstrates partial alignment with classical education concepts (notably in its religious/theological orientation and a formation-focused purpose), but it lacks explicit Great Books, liberal arts infrastructure, Socratic pedagogy, and the other hallmarks described in the classical rubric.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Key insights:

Evidence: site identifies Judah Christian Academy as a Christian School and references a Statement of Faith.

Evidence: “formation” language and community emphasis are present, not explicit classical cueing.

Evidence: the public content is largely admissions, news, and general faith-based messaging, with no clear Great Books or Trivium signals.

The Judah site shows some religious signaling but lacks the explicit classical signals cited in the lexicon. This creates partial alignment rather than full classical alignment.

What would move the needle

To shift Judah Christian Academy toward a fully CLASSICAL profile, concrete, tangible steps aligned with the classical framework should be undertaken and publicly documented:

- Curriculum transformation
  - Adopt and publicly articulate a liberal-arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric) across appropriate grade bands, including a planned sequence of reading lists (Great Books), primary sources, and a Western-civilization/history emphasis that treats history as a continuous thread rather than “social studies.”
  - Introduce Great Books program and reference the Great Conversation in admissions and curriculum guides; include explicit language in the mission and program pages.
  - Consider classical languages (Latin or Greek) as an option for upper elementary or middle school with a clear rationale and progression.

- Pedagogy upgrades
  - Embed Socratic dialogue, seminars, and debate in upper grades; document the pedagogical approach and provide teacher training resources on classical methods.
  - Implement scholé-inspired restful learning days or cycles to balance rigorous inquiry with contemplation and wonder.
  - Incorporate memory work, recitation, and potentially liturgical music or chant in appropriate grade levels as a conscious method of fostering memory and reverence for truth, goodness, and beauty.

- Faculty development and portrait
  - Develop a “living curriculum” framework and a formal “portrait of a teacher” that positions faculty as mentors and guides in the classical sense (sages, exemplars, and co-learners with students).
  - Provide ongoing professional development in classical pedagogy, including opportunities to engage with classical education resources and fellow teachers.

- Parental engagement and community
  - Create formal parent-formation programs (book studies, lectures, and book clubs) focused on classical education, paideia, and the Great Conversation.
  - Position parents as co-teachers by inviting them into seminars, readings, and school events; open campus visits and curated parent nights to discuss classical education philosophy and practices.

- Explicit alignment and branding
  - Update mission and communications to explicitly reference classical education terms (truth, goodness, beauty; liberal arts; paideia; Great Books; scholé).
  - Develop and publish a short, public document outlining a classical education plan, with milestones for grades K–12 and a timeline for implementation.

These needle-moving steps align with the Positive Keyword framework and the Six Essential Elements’ emphasis on liberty, formation, and Great Books-centric pedagogy.
