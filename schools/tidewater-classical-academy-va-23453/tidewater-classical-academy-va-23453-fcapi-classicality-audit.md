## Executive Summary

Tidewater Classical Academy (Virginia Beach, VA, 23453) shows a strong religio-centric orientation in its publicly available materials, evidenced by clear biblical content, sermons, and church-adjacent vocabulary in the school’s crawl content (hosted on greenrunbaptist.com). The material strongly demonstrates Liturgical and Communal Practices and Grounded in Theology, but it does not provide explicit evidence of Great Books, liberal-arts curriculum, or the formal pedagogical rituals associated with classical education (grammar-logic-rhetoric progression, Socratic seminars, Great Conversations, etc.). On a category-by-category basis, Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) trends CLASSICAL due to explicit theological framing and liturgical practice; Categories 2–6 largely fail to meet the classical rubric as defined by the Six Essential Elements, resulting in an overall assessment of NOT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle: to elevate Tidewater Classical Academy into a robust CLASSICAL standing, the school would need to publicly articulate and implement a classical curriculum and pedagogy. This would include: (1) a stated Mission/Goal focused on formation as virtue and wisdom (not merely religious formation), (2) a liberal-arts curriculum centered on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium, (3) explicit Great Books study and the Great Conversation, (4) explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy for upper grades, (5) a clearly defined faculty profile aligned with classical ideals, and (6) a formal plan for parent/community partnership in the classical formation process.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Publicly articulate a CLASSICAL mission and goals that foreground formation toward truth, goodness, and beauty through the liberal arts, not solely religious formation.
- Implement a classical curriculum that explicitly includes:
 - The seven liberal arts, with Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) in the early grades and the Quadrivium in the upper grades.

 - A clear progression of pedagogy (Scholé/restful learning, Socratic questioning, seminars, and rhetoric training) across K–12.
