## Executive Summary

Midland Classical Academy (MCA) in Midland, TX appears to be a robust CLASSICAL school with a clearly Christian, classical formation. Public materials on the MCA site emphasize a Biblical Worldview, Christ-centered education, engagement with Great Books, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), and Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades, alongside a hybrid Grammar School model that involves families as co-educators. Parental partnership is a core feature, and there is concrete attention to classical content (Great Books, Latin/Koine Greek, logic, rhetoric) and to the Great Conversation. While the school presents itself as fully classical in its aims and methods, it also foregrounds a strong ecclesial orientation (pursuing Christ) and a hybrid model that integrates family involvement into the learning process. Taken together across the six classical criteria, MCA aligns strongly with Classical education, with evidence of some non-traditional strands (home-school hybrid model; some parent-led elements) that temper a pure canonical classroom model but do not undermine its overall classical orientation.

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The public materials explicitly describe Socratic discussion, seminars, and debates in upper grades, as well as training in rhetoric and opportunities to present speeches (classical pedagogy). The “What is Classical Education?” material within MCA references the Trivium and the Socratic Method as central to pedagogy. 
- Lower grades emphasize contemplative, memory-building elements (repeated review, rhythm, memorization) through language, song, and repetition (implied by the “Grammar School Culture” language and the hybrid schooling approach that fosters memorization and recall through routine). 
- Upper School emphasizes Socratic seminars, dialectic, and the development of logical reasoning and rhetoric, including opportunities for public speaking and debate. 
- There is clear articulation of upper-level discourse practices (Socratic method, seminars, debates) as well as lower-level memory-oriented routines (chanting/repetition as a memory aid, in line with scholé concepts). 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: Socratic Method, Socratic dialogue, great conversation, seminars, debate, rhetoric, logic, grammar (Trivium), scholé/restful learning (implied by contemplative/reflective pace), memory/recitation (implied by phraseology around memorization/history).
- Negative keywords present: None explicitly; no persistent emphasis on purely project-based or heavily modern, non-classical pedagogy. The hybrid grammar-school model is a logistical feature, not a pedagogy-non-classical signal.

Why this rating
- Public-facing pedagogy aligns with classical expectations (Socratic inquiry; rhetoric; public speaking; memory-based lower-division pedagogy; Great Books-driven inquiry). CLASSICAL.

Cited references: Socratic method, Great Conversation, rhetoric, public speaking; lower/upper-school pedagogy descriptions. 

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- MCA describes its staff as catalysts in “engaging minds,” drawing students out, and guiding inquiry, which positions teachers as mentors rather than mere transmitters of content. The phrasing “Our staff draws students out – encouraging them to share what they know, ask their questions, and seek real answers” signals a teacher as guide and mentor. 
- The school presents the faculty as the living curriculum through relational teaching and mentorship (implied by the emphasis on tutor-led inquiry and parental partnership). However, explicit language such as “living curriculum,” “portrait of a teacher,” or formal teacher-professional profiles is less evident in the public-facing materials.
- There is evidence of teacher collaboration and family partnerships (tutors and parents working together in the homeschool/heralded hybrid Grammar School model), which suggests a collegial, mentorship-oriented culture, though explicit “faculty as living curriculum” language is not strongly foregrounded in the public site.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: mentor/teacher as guide, formation through example, virtue-centered teaching (as implied by Christ-centered formation and character formation), shared inquiry, professional development (implied via ongoing school growth).
- Negative keywords present: The explicit, codified language of “living curriculum,” “faculty portrait,” or “faculty collegium” is not strongly evident in the public site; there is a lack of direct, formal articulation of faculty as a defined “living curriculum.”

Why this rating
- The school demonstrates several classical faculty traits (model virtue; teacher as guide; mentorship; collegiality implied through teacher-parent partnership), but it stops short of explicit, codified phrases commonly used in the classical rubric to describe the faculty as “the living curriculum.” Given that, the assessment lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Element 5.

Cited references: Evidence of teacher as guide/mentors and tutoring culture; absence of strongly explicit “living curriculum” language in public pages (e.g., “Our staff draws students out” phrasing cited). 

### Element 6: Parents & Community — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Parental involvement is a central, formal feature: MCA positions parents as primary educators in the Grammar School model, and requires service hours, reflecting a deep partnership between families and the school. The program is described as parents serving as co-educators in a hybrid model (home-school collaboration). 
- The school emphasizes partnerships with families to develop Christlike leaders, inviting parents to participate in schooling through service hours, tutoring, and regular communication; the “grammar school hybrid” model explicitly integrates parental involvement as essential to student success. 
- The Admissions/Parental Involvement materials frame the school as a family-centered community in which parents are active participants, co-planners, and co-learners, with regular communication between tutors and parents. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: partnership with families, co-educators, parental engagement, home-school hybrid, family involvement, parent formation, school-parent synergy, parental leadership, community of learners.
- Negative keywords present: None evident (no explicit statements of “parent disengagement” or “minimal parent involvement”).

Why this rating
- The explicit emphasis on parental partnership and family involvement places MCA firmly within the classical rubric’s Parents & Community category as CLASSICAL.

Cited references: Parental Involvement section detailing co-educator role, service hours, home-school hybrid model, and collaboration between parents and tutors. 

Aggregate assessment

- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
  - The school earns CLASSICAL across Categories 1, 3, 4, and 6, with Element 2 also aligning well toward formation-oriented purpose, Element 5 landing as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to less explicit articulation of the faculty-as-living-curriculum and other formalized faculty descriptors. The net effect is a clearly Classical program with a strong Christian, formation-focused bend and a robust Great Books/Socratic pedagogy, together with strong parental partnerships.
