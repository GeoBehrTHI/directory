## Executive Summary

The Bridge Christian School (Natural Bridge, VA, 24579) appears to publish public materials that do not openly align with the classical Christian education rubric as defined by the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School. An initial scan of the school’s crawl data (collected from the school’s site) yields content that is largely focused on standard school administration and general Christian-school framing, with no explicit articulation of classical curricular/theological commitments (e.g., Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, scholé, liturgical learning, or a clearly defined Great Conversation). This absence of explicit classical markers in the public-facing materials supports rating the school as NOT CLASSICAL across the six rubric categories. The classical criteria, including references to truth, goodness, beauty as transcendentals; formation of virtue; liberal arts; Great Books; Socratic pedagogy; and a live, living curriculum, are not evidenced in the publicly surfaced content we analyzed. The knowledge-base lexicon (Six Essential Elements) provides a concrete set of positive/negative indicators that we apply to each category; in this case, the school’s materials do not demonstrate these indicators in the required directions. See the keyword-alignment discussion and the rubric-driven scoring notes below, anchored in the Six Essential Elements framework and its lexicon. 

Given the available content, the aggregate assessment is:
- Overall classification: NOT CLASSICAL
- Most categories show little to no evidence of classical markers (Religious Foundations, Purpose, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community)

What would move bridges toward CLASSICAL is substantial, explicit alignment with the Classical School rubric: articulating a theological/metaphysical foundation; presenting a formation-focused, virtue-centered aim; codifying a liberal-arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with Great Books and history as a through-line; adopting Socratic/Scholé-based pedagogy appropriate to K–12; defining a faculty living the living-curriculum; and building a robust, parent-partner community that is explicitly engaged in classical formation. If the school were to adopt these elements and document them publicly (on-site statements, curriculum maps, faculty profiles, parent-learning opportunities), it would begin to move toward a CLASSICAL rating.

## What Would Move the Needle

- If The Bridge Christian School (Natural Bridge, VA, 24579) were to aim for CLASSICAL, it would need to publicly articulate and implement a coherent classical-education program, including:
 - A religious/metaphysical foundation that explicitly grounds character formation in transcendentals (truth, goodness, beauty) and a liturgical worldview; include a publicly stated theology/catechesis approach and liturgical practices.
 - A clear school purpose and goal centered on formation and virtue — not just information or job-readiness; articulate the telos of education (pursuit of true, good, beautiful; formation into human excellence).
 - A documented liberal-arts curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; primary historical sources; literacy in classical languages where appropriate; history as Western civilization; history through primary sources; cultivation of wonder).
 - Pedagogy that includes contemplative/restful learning (scholé), singing/chanting in early years, and Socratic seminars/debates in upper grades; explicit and sustained use of narrated/recited learning, not exclusively project-based approaches.
 - A faculty culture that presents teachers as virtuous mentors and living exemplars, with a clearly defined teacher-portrait and professional-development path aligned to classical pedagogy.
 - A robust parental partnership with formal parent-education opportunities, open-campus access, and family liturgies or family-learning practices aligned to classical formation.
- Concrete steps include publishing a publicly accessible “Classical Alignment” page (mission, telos, curriculum map, teaching-methodology brief, and faculty profiles), instituting a Great-Books-based curriculum map, and launching parent-education initiatives (book studies, lectures, and family-learning guides).

- Metrics and evidence to publish publicly once implemented: a published curriculum map showing the Trivium/Quadrivium progression, a documented adoption of scholé/restful learning across grades, evidence of Socratic/monologue-based pedagogy in upper grades, and a parent-partner program with structured formation offerings.
