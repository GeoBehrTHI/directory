## Executive Summary

Classical Charter Schools of Southport NC 28422 (CCS-Southport) presents a public, tuition-free K–8 program that markets itself as a classical charter school with a strong emphasis on direct instruction, structured classrooms, and a “premier performance-driven year-round” approach. Public-facing materials emphasize a replicated network of model schools, open enrollment, a year-round schedule, direct instruction, and a focus on discipline, routines, and a high-quality teaching staff. While the school clearly foregrounds formation of character and moral growth in places (e.g., “moral growth” in statements about what the school hopes to accomplish), the publicly visible materials do not consistently articulate the core classical lexicon (great books, Great Conversation, Seven Liberal Arts, Socratic dialogue, scholé, or explicit emphasis on mastery of transcendentals). Taken as a whole, the evaluation falls between SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL and NOT CLASSICAL in certain categories, with Category 2 (School Purpose and Goal) and Category 5 (Faculty) showing clearer alignments to classical aims, while Categories 1, 3, and 4 show substantial gaps relative to a fully classical program. The aggregate classification is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Evidence of classical orientation exists in spirit: a replication model with a long-standing network, emphasis on moral growth, and a focus on rigorous teacher quality. This aligns with some classical aims around formation and a serious, discipline-led pedagogy (Categories 2 and 5). 
- There is limited explicit public articulation of core classical curriculum elements (Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts, Great Conversation) and of Socratic pedagogy, which constrains a full CLASSICAL rating in Category 3 (Curriculum) and Category 4 (Pedagogy). 
- The school’s explicit DI (Direct Instruction) approach, while robust and evidence-based for certain outcomes, diverges from the traditional classical pedagogy emphasis on Socratic dialogue, memory work, and contemplative study in several grade bands. 
- Faculty rhetoric around “moral growth” and “highest quality teaching staff” is consistent with classical aims around mentorship and virtue, placing Category 5 toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, though the site stops short of a fully developed “living curriculum” portrait. 
- Parental involvement and community partnership are present, but the public materials do not consistently foreground parents as co-educators or provide structured pathways (books, gatherings, or explicit family formation) in the way classical lexicons describe. This yields a category rating toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL for Category 6.

### Category 2: School Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize formation of character and moral growth in service of education, with phrases like “moral growth” and “formation” within the school’s narrative. The Overview mentions “moral growth” as a guiding outcome, indicating orientation toward virtues beyond rote information. 
- Public materials also emphasize a replicated, tradition-like model (Leland/Whiteville) and a focus on a “premier performance-driven year-round school,” which aligns with some classical aims but is framed in a modern performance context. 
- The NC School Report Card materials note literacy in Latin, Cursive, and Grammar as part of the network’s program (a nod to traditional classical elements), even though this is framed within the Roger Bacon network rather than a verbatim Great Books program. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords aligned with formation and virtue: formation, moral growth, moral imagination (concepts from the classical lexicon). The site uses “moral growth” and “formation” language, which maps onto Positive Keywords in the Six Elements lexicon (Formation of Affections; Cultivation of Wisdom and Virtue). 
- No substantive Negative keywords indicating a lack of formation are present in the public materials.

Why this rating
- The school clearly prioritizes formation and moral development (formation/moral growth) rather than purely information-transfer; however, the explicit articulation of virtue-centered aims (and the depth of that formation) is not as robust as canonical classical-language statements (e.g., “pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty” with full articulation of telos). Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is warranted.

What would move the needle
- More explicit articulation of a classical telos (truth, goodness, beauty) as the core aim of education, with direct statements about formation beyond information, and a clearly stated aim toward human flourishing and liberal-arts-informed formation.
- Add explicit reference to Great Books, classical canons, and a stated objective of the liberal-arts-based formation to further elevate toward CLASSICAL.

Citations
- Public materials referencing moral growth and formation. 
- Latin, Cursive, Grammar as part of the network’s curriculum (supporting traditional elements) in the NC School Card materials. 
- Classical-education formation framework.

### Category 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — NOT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize Direct Instruction as the core pedagogy (e.g., “small, direct instruction setting,” “frequent assessment to ensure mastery”). This DI emphasis diverges from the Socratic dialogue and scholé-centric practices central to classical pedagogy. 
- The school’s public materials discuss a year-round schedule and structured environments but do not showcase the typical classical-pedagogy features like Socratic seminars, contemplation, or memorization/recitation as core methods in lower or upper grades. The blog content and admissions copy emphasize DI and direct instruction rather than classical seminar formats. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive/Classical Pedagogy keywords (e.g., Socratic dialogue, drilling, memorization, recitation, scholé, memory work) are not evidenced in the public materials; DI-based language is predominant.
- Negative keywords indicating DI dominance (e.g., "lecture-based instruction," "rote learning," "teacher-centered instruction") are not explicitly stated as negative in the materials; however, the absence of classical-pedagogy markers counts against a CLASSICAL rating.

Why this rating
- The explicit use of Direct Instruction and absence of public-facing Socratic, recitation, or memory-work pedagogy positions Category 4 in NOT CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle
- Introduce classical pedagogy components publicly: Socratic discussions in suitable grade bands, recitation/memorization routines (chanting or memory work), copywork or imitation-based exercises, and a policy that foregrounds contemplative learning (scholé) in appropriate cycles.
- Provide teacher professional development in classical pedagogy and publish a public outline of the pedagogical approach across K–8.

Citations
- Direct Instruction emphasis in admissions/overview materials. 
- Public-facing reference to classical pedagogy elements not present (lexicon context).
