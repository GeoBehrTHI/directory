# Classicality Audit: St. Ambrose Academy

Executive Summary
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
- Why: The school’s public materials consistently foreground a Catholic theological vision (Mass, sacraments, chaplain), articulate formation in virtue and wisdom as the telos of education, and describe a liberal‑arts curriculum anchored in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin, primary sources, and Socratic pedagogy. Evidence includes explicit references to “Christ-filled community,” “regular access to Mass and the Sacraments,” “form the whole person in virtue,” “primary sources,” “Socratic teaching,” and a course of study listing Latin, Logic, and Rhetoric by name     .
- School website used: https://ambroseacademy.org/ 

Method note: This audit applies the positive/negative keyword indicators and six-category framework from Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and the companion lexicon, comparing St. Ambrose’s site language to confirm or negate classical identity across each category  .

------------------------------------------------------------

CATEGORY 1: RELIGIOUS AND METAPHYSICAL FOUNDATIONS
- What we looked for (per “Six Elements” and lexicon): transcendental moral realism (truth, goodness, beauty, virtue, wisdom), theological grounding (theology, faith and reason), liturgical and communal practices (liturgy, Mass, chapel, prayer)  .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - “Christ‑filled community”; “Catholic Faith”; “form the human person” 
  - “Regular access to Mass and the Sacraments … mentored by a chaplain … deep love of Jesus Christ” 
  - “Oratory for worship” at the new campus; “daily prayer” appears in school communications and events  
  - “Truth” repeatedly connected to learning (e.g., Socratic leading to truth; Blessed Sacrament called “the ultimate primary source”) 
- Keyword comparison to “Six Elements” positives: theology/Christ-centered, liturgy/Mass/chapel/prayer, virtue/formation, truth—all strong matches. No “values‑neutral” or “relativism” signals were found (negatives) .
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 10 (Christ‑filled, Catholic Faith, virtue, truth, Mass, Sacraments, chaplain, oratory/worship, prayer, Christ-centered)
  - Negative hits: 0
  - Percent positive: 100%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria (moral realism, theology, liturgy): 100% (3/3)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

CATEGORY 2: SCHOOL PURPOSE AND GOAL
- What we looked for: formation and the cultivation of wisdom/virtue (not mere job prep), pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty, education as formation not information .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - “Education … good in itself,” “form the whole person in virtue—intellectual, moral, and theological” 
  - “Return to classical methodology … to form the human person” 
  - The “great conversation” and Western heritage orientation implied via “great ideas,” integrated history sequence by era 
- Comparison: Matches the “wisdom/virtue,” “formation,” and “true/good/beautiful” thrust of the positive indicators. The site also mentions students being “well prepared for post‑high school college study,” which is acceptable but can echo a negative signal when it dominates; here, it does not dominate the mission framing  .
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 7 (formation, virtue, purpose beyond utility, truth, great conversation/Western heritage, integrated vision, human flourishing)
  - Negative hits: 2 (college‑prep language appears but not dominant)
  - Percent positive: 78%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria: 100% (3/3)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

CATEGORY 3: SCHOOL CURRICULUM
- What we looked for: the liberal arts (Trivium/Quadrivium), history as history (not “social studies”), Great Books/primary sources/Great Conversation, integrated studies/wonder .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - Trivium explicitly named: “grammar, logic, and rhetoric” (tools and courses) 
  - Course of study lists Latin, Logic, Rhetoric, Philosophy, History (by era), Religion across grades 6–12; no “social studies” labeling found  
  - “Primary sources,” “great ideas,” students join a conversation “taking place for millennia” (Great Books proxy language)  
- Comparison: Strong alignment with positive indicators (Trivium, Latin, Great Books via primary sources, integrated historical sequence). Occasional STEM language is framed as subordinate to and strengthened by the liberal arts (not a STEM‑first orientation) .
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 10 (Trivium terms x3, Latin, Logic, Rhetoric, Philosophy, primary sources, great ideas/great conversation, integrated history)
  - Negative hits: 1 (minor “hands‑on experiments” mention—acceptable in moderation; not “project‑based learning dominance”) 
  - Percent positive: 91%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria: 75% (3/4; explicit “wonder” language not observed)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

CATEGORY 4: SCHOOL PEDAGOGY OR TEACHING METHODS
- What we looked for: restful/cholé learning, memory/chant in lower grades, Socratic discussion/dialectic in upper grades, rhetoric/speeches/orations opportunities  .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - “Socratic teaching … ‘soul leading’ … lead students to discover the truth,” distinction from open‑ended seminars; multiple mentions of “discussion‑based classroom”  
  - Rhetoric in the junior year and a Senior Seminar named in the graduation sequence (rhetorical formation implied) 
- Comparison: Strong on Socratic/dialectic and rhetoric trajectory for 6–12. No explicit “scholé” or “restful learning” terminology found; memory/chant keywords are not advertised (the school begins at grade 6, where chant is less central). One mild experiential phrase (“hands‑on experiments”) appears but is integrated within a classical framework, not as constructivist pedagogy dominance .
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 3 (Socratic, discussion‑based, rhetoric/Senior Seminar)
  - Negative hits: 1 (hands‑on experiments—minor)
  - Percent positive: 75%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria: 50% (2/4; clear Socratic and rhetoric focus; no explicit scholé/memory‑chant claims)
- Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

CATEGORY 5: SCHOOL FACULTY
- What we looked for: teachers as models/mentors, collegial culture; “living curriculum,” portrait/profile of teacher/student .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - “Role Models for the Next Generation,” “witnesses to a life‑long love of learning,” and “collaborative and collegial work environment” for faculty  
  - “Mentored by a chaplain and teachers,” emphasizing imitation of virtue; faculty/staff annually affirm a Profession of Faith (virtue/formation, faith and reason)  
- Comparison: Strong matches to positive indicators of teachers as virtuous mentors and collegial culture .
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 5 (role models, mentorship, virtue modeling, collegiality, profession of faith)
  - Negative hits: 0
  - Percent positive: 100%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria: 100% (2/2)
- Rating: CLASSICAL

CATEGORY 6: SCHOOL PARENTS AND COMMUNITY
- What we looked for: partnership with parents as co‑educators, parent formation, open campus/ongoing hospitality/community life .
- Site keywords/evidence found:
  - Repeated open invitations to “Visit,” tours, and Open House classroom observations; robust community calendar (Benefit Dinner, community events)  
  - Community life around feasts (e.g., All Saints’ Party), litany/bonfire, school‑wide celebrations 
  - History page stresses a “broader community of families” building a “culture of virtue” together 
- Comparison: Clear hospitality/community life and open‑door culture; however, we found no explicit “parent as co‑teacher/parent formation” language typical of K–8 hybrids. Evidence of partnership is present but not articulated as parent formation programming.
- Quantitative estimate:
  - Positive hits: 4 (visits/open house, community calendar, feasts/events, community of families)
  - Negative hits: 0
  - Percent positive: 100%
  - Coverage of sub-criteria: 33% (1/3 explicitly; events/hospitality are strong, but parent‑formation/co‑teaching not explicit)
- Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

------------------------------------------------------------

Aggregate Assessment
- Strength of evidence across six elements: Robust and coherent. The school directly states it is a Catholic classical junior/senior high school; the curriculum and pedagogy align with classical hallmarks (Trivium, Latin, primary sources, Socratic method; rhetoric and senior‑year seminar; integrated history)   . Religious and metaphysical foundations are especially strong, with Mass/sacramental life and a chaplain integrated into school life, matching liturgical/communal best practices in the “Six Elements” framework . Faculty culture and mentoring ethos are also explicitly emphasized .
- Weaker signals: While grades 6–12 appropriately emphasize Socratic/rhetoric, published materials don’t highlight scholé/restful learning, or lower‑school memory/chant (not applicable to K–5 since the school starts at grade 6). Parent partnership/formation could be made more explicit in public materials (though hospitality/community life is evident).
- Overall classification: CLASSICAL
