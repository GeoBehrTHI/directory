## Executive Summary

Sycamore Christian Academy (SCA) presents as a clearly classical Christian school in Hamilton, Ohio, with a robust integration of faith and classical pedagogy. The website and related firecrawl content emphasize a Trivium-based curriculum (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a Western classical inheritance, formation of virtue and wisdom, and active partnership with parents and the local church community. Public materials repeatedly foreground a Christ-centered, biblical worldview, soul formation, and a philosophy of education that prioritizes truth, goodness, and beauty over mere information or job-preparation.

Overall, the six-classical categories yield five strong CLASSICAL placements and one SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL placement, producing an aggregate CLASSICAL rating with a few categories that could be strengthened to achieve full classical alignment. Areas with the strongest classical alignment include Religious/Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, Curriculum (Trivium and Western classical emphasis), Faculty, and Parents/Community. Pedagogy shows substantial classical elements (restful Scholé-style practices and memory-ritual components) but lacks explicit documentation of full Socratic or high-school-level seminar usage on public materials, hence a single category rated SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The aggregate rating remains CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Lower-grade pedagogy features restful, contemplative elements, singing/chanting, memory work, and multi-sensory learning (Scholé-like and mnemonic practices) in Kindergarten and Jr-K. 
  - There are indications of more rigorous, discussion-based elements in upper grades (Socratic-style discussion or seminars are referenced in classical education literature; however, explicit public evidence of regular Socratic seminars at SCA’s upper levels is not clearly documented in the site content). The materials emphasize classical dialogue through the Trivium framework and rhetorical training, which implies some level of spoken reasoning and argumentation. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: scholé (restful learning), memory/recitation/chanting, singing, wonder, contemplation, unified pedagogy; memory-focused learning; classical rhetoric and speech training.
  - Negative keywords present: few explicit references in public materials to exclusive use of experiential/project-based, student-directed-only approaches, or to fully absent contemplative rest.
- Why this rating
  - The public materials reveal strong contemplative and mnemonic pedagogy consistent with classical aims, plus some evidence of higher-level Socratic/seminar-like practices in the curriculum, but explicit, consistent documentation of upper-grade Socratic seminars or seminars with a formal Harkness-style approach is not uniformly evidenced across all public materials. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL for Pedagogy.

What Would Move the Needle

- For Pedagogy to reach full CLASSICAL status, strengthen explicit, scalable documentation of upper-grade Socratic seminars, Harkness-style discussions, and formal rhetoric-based assessments in all publicly accessible content (and provide sample syllabi or classroom rubrics showing these practices in practice).
- Expand public references to Great Books and the Great Conversation as a formal curricular pillar with explicit examples across grades, including a stated list of canonical works and how they are integrated with Trivium stages.
- Explicitly articulate a liturgical/cultic rhythm (prayer, worship, sacred music) as a standard, recurring element across all grade bands (with time-tabled formats or syllabi) to strengthen Liturgical and Communal Practices signals in Element 1.
