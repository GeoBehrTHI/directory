## Executive Summary

Saint Theresa Catholic School (STCS) presents itself as a clearly classical Catholic school that foregrounds the liberal arts and the Catholic intellectual tradition. Public materials describe a mission framed by a Catholic, classical vision, including explicit reference to the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Latin study, classical literature, liturgical life, and a goal of forming virtuous, capable students. The site also emphasizes Catholic identity through theology, weekly Mass, parish connections, and liturgical practices, alongside a family-centered community with active parental involvement.

Overall, STCS aligns most strongly with the Classical framework across the six categories, with especially robust signals in Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, Curriculum, and School Purpose/Goal, plus strong signals in Parents & Community. Pedagogy and Faculty show substantial classical alignment but with comparatively less explicit articulation of every classical pedagogical element on public-facing pages; nonetheless, the cumulative evidence supports CLASSICAL across most dimensions. The aggregate assessment is CLASSICAL.

- Executive takeaway: The school’s public materials map very closely to classical Catholic education norms (Catholic identity, liberal arts, Trivium, Great Books orientation through listed classics, contemplative memory work, and a strong parental partnership). The strongest opportunities to strengthen the rubric would be to make explicit the upper-school Socratic/seminar pedagogy and to foreground a few additional living-curriculum statements about the faculty as the living curriculum, even if such language exists in internal materials.

- What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases only): Not applicable here since the aggregate is CLASSICAL. If desired, minor enhancements could include explicit language about Socratic seminars in upper grades and a stronger articulation of faculty as the living curriculum in public-facing materials.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows explicit Catholic identity, liturgical life, and theological grounding.
  Positive keyword indicators present (Catholic identity, theology, liturgy, Mass, weekly worship, virtue, imago Dei, sacred music). Negative indicators (relativism, secular humanism) are not evidenced on public pages.
  Why this rating: The mission and religious life are central to STCS public materials, with language about Catholic tradition, sacred liturgy, and formation of virtue.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
  Evidence emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and the real-knowable true/good/beautiful; formation and liberal education framed as purposes beyond job-prep.
  Positive keywords (formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education, classical mission, telos, pursuit of the true/good/beautiful, scholé/contemplation). Negative signals not evidenced on site.
  Why this rating: The public materials foreground formation of character and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty rather than a narrow workforce-preparation focus.

- Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL
  Evidence highlights the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin, classical literature, and a broad liberal-arts approach; references to studying the best ideas of the centuries and to Western civilization through music, art, and architecture of the medieval/renaissance periods; presence of classics and “Great Books”-style reading appears in practice (e.g., sample classics across grades).
  Positive keywords (Trivium, grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin; classical literature; great books/classics; Western civilization; history via period study; music/the arts as part of a historical continuum). Negative indicators (project-based learning dominance, social-studies-terminology replacing history) not present in public content.
  Why this rating: Strong alignment with liberal-arts core, classical literature and languages, and an emphasis on historical/cultural study.

- Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows a blend of contemplative/restful elements and memory-based pedagogy (chanting, singing, narration, recitation) and, in practice, discussion and reading of classics; upper grades show potential for more Socratic/seminar-style work, consistent with a K–8 classical model.
  Positive keywords (narration, recitation, chanting, memory work, scholé, contemplation, wonder, seminar-like discussion; rhetoric exercises). Negative indicators (heavy emphasis on experiential/project-based learning) are not clearly evidenced on public-facing pages.
  Why this rating: The site demonstrates key classical-pedagogy signals (memory, recitation, chanting, contemplation) and a pathway toward more advanced discourse, even if explicit Socratic seminars aren’t loudly advertised on public pages.

- Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence of faculty presence exists (Headmaster welcome, faculty/staff pages, accreditation, and teacher leadership). Public materials do not consistently foreground the “living curriculum” language or a fully developed faculty portrait as central to daily pedagogy, though references to teachers, staff, and leadership appear.
  Positive keywords (faculty and staff presence; leadership; teachers as mentors). Negative signals (lack of explicit “living curriculum” portrait or faculty-led classical formation language) are not evident in public pages, but the strongest living-curriculum framing is not always explicit.
  Why this rating: There are clear signs of a serious, dedicated faculty, but the strongest classical-language framing (living curriculum, faculty as exemplars) is not consistently explicit in public-facing content.

- Element 6: School Parents & Community — CLASSICAL
  Evidence shows active parental partnership and a dedicated parents’ organization, with clear channels for parent involvement and formation.
  Positive keywords (partnership with parents, co-teachers, parental primacy, parent formation, reading groups, seminars, book clubs, open campus, hospitality, family liturgy, family participation). Negative indicators not evident in public content.
  Why this rating: The school emphasizes parent partnership and formation, aligning well with the classical model’s emphasis on a close parent-school partnership.

What Would Move the Needle
- Not necessary given CLASSICAL rating. If desired for further strengthening toward a fully explicit, fully robust classical framework, the school could:
  - Explicitly articulate upper-grade Socratic/seminar pedagogy on public materials (e.g., classroom routines, seminar formats, and assessment aligned with dialectic inquiry).
  - Expand language about the faculty as the living curriculum and provide public examples of faculty-led classical seminars or teacher-led Socratic discussions in practice.
  - More consistently foreground Great Books language and explicit mention of a “Great Conversation” framework on the site to reinforce the Classical identity.
