## ABSTRACT

Saint Theresa Catholic School (STCS) presents a robust CLASSICAL profile across most of the classical-school rubric. The public materials consistently emphasize Catholic identity, formation in virtue, and a liberal-arts curriculum grounded in the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric) with Latin and classical literature. There is strong evidence of a liturgical and communal Catholic life (Mass and Emmaus Chapel) and a clear parental-partnership orientation (STAP and open-campus language). The curriculum shows explicit classical elements (Trivium, Latin, classics, and periods of Western civilization education). Pedagogy signals include memory-based and contemplative practices (narration, recitation, chanting) alongside discussion of classics, with the potential for more explicit Socratic practice in upper grades. Faculty presence and leadership, while evident, do not consistently foreground the explicit “living curriculum” framing on public pages, though conversations about faculty/staff exist. Overall, STCS is CLASSICAL with a few opportunities to sharpen explicit classical-language framing of pedagogy and faculty in public communications.
