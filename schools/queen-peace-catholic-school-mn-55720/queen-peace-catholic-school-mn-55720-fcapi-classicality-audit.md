## Executive Summary

Queen of Peace Catholic School (Cloquet, MN) presents a robust Classical-Catholic profile across most core elements. Public materials emphasize a Catholic liberal-arts framework framed by liturgical life, formation of character, and a Trivium-oriented curriculum. The school foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty as real and knowable, grounds study in Catholic theology and catechesis, and integrates liturgical practice and sacramental life into daily schooling. There is strong evidence of a Trivium-based curriculum, a commitment to Great Books-style engagement with classic texts, and a living faculty culture deeply tied to the school’s mission. Parental involvement and community partnership are clearly prioritized, with explicit emphasis on collaboration between parents and teachers, and open campus norms.

Overall, the school is CLASSICAL with notable strengths in religious/metaphysical foundations, school purpose and goal, curriculum, faculty, and parent/community engagement. The Pedagogy category shows meaningful classical traits (Trivium, contemplative/restful elements, and liturgical memory), but there is less explicit mention of Socratic seminars or sustained high-school-level Paideia-style seminars in the public materials, placing Pedagogy as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. The six-category aggregate leans CLASSICAL.

Key takeaways by category:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: Strong CLASSICAL indicators (truth/goodness/beauty; liturgy; Catholic theology; communal worship; sacred music; sacramental worldview).
- Purpose and Goal: Strong CLASSICAL indicators (formation, virtue, wisdom; true/beautiful/good; classical telos).
- Curriculum: Strong CLASSICAL indicators (Trivium; liberal arts; great books-style engagement; classical texts; memory and contemplation).
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL (evidence of contemplative/restful pedagogy and liturgical/memory-based approaches; less explicit evidence of Socratic seminars across grades in public materials).
- Faculty: Strong CLASSICAL indicators (model teachers; living curriculum; mentorship; collegial, mission-driven culture).
- Parents & Community: Strong CLASSICAL indicators (partnering with parents; parent formation opportunities; open-campus visitation).

What would move the needle (for SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL cases)
- Explicitly foreground sustained Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy across grades (especially middle/high school).
- More explicit articulation of Great Conversations/Great Books scope and frequency (e.g., a defined Great Books sequence for K-12 and regular Paideia-style seminars).
- Clearer reference to long-term classical-language study (Latin/Greek) as a formal, programmatic element across grades.
- Additional explicit statements about the “living curriculum” in faculty training and assessment to strengthen the Faculty category beyond general evidence.

What Would Move the Needle
