# ABSTRACT

Elijah House Academy (EHA) earns a CLASSICAL classification across six criteria: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose and Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community. The school’s public materials articulate a Christ-centered worldview, a formation-focused aim, a liberal-arts curriculum with grammar/logic/rhetoric and engagement with Great Books/“living books,” a pedagogy featuring Socratic seminars, memory/recitation, and oratory, and a culture of mentors and family partnership. These signals align with the Positive Keyword Indicators in the Six Essential Elements framework, as detailed in the classical lexicon. Evidence includes explicit statements of faith and theology, mission and values centered on formation, curriculum that foregrounds the Trivium and Great Books, pedagogical practices in scholé and dialogue, the living-curriculum portrayal of faculty, and a clear home–school partnership ethos. The school’s public materials consistently emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty in a way that supports a classical telos and a culture of virtue.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: The Bible as inspired Word of God and a Trinitarian framework are explicitly stated; liturgical/community life is embedded in formation; reverence and wonder are foregrounded via worship and doctrinal grounding.
- Purpose and Goal: Formation in virtue and wisdom; teleology oriented toward true, good, beautiful; emphasis on Christian flourishing and communal purpose.
- Curriculum: Liberal arts orientation with grammar/logic/rhetoric; history embedded in Christian narrative; engagement with classic literature; “living books” and Great Books sensibilities; an integrated view of history across grades.
- Pedagogy: Socratic seminars; restfulness (Scholé); memory/chanting; public speaking and rhetoric; formation through imitation and practice.
- Faculty: Mentorship and living curriculum; portrait of teacher; team leadership and trust; vocation-centered formation.
- Parents & Community: Strong partnership with families; parent formation and home–school collaboration; open visitation.
