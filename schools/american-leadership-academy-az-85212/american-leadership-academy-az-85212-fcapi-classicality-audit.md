## Executive Summary

American Leadership Academy (AZ 85212) exhibits a blend of classical-oriented formation and contemporary schooling structures. The public-facing materials emphasize character formation through a values-based, leadership-centered program (R.A.I.S.E. values: Respect, Accountability, Integrity, Service, Excellence) and a time-proven, core-curriculum approach, rather than a strict Great Books, rigorous grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence typical of some classical programs. While there are clear indications of virtue, moral formation, and family partnership, the materials offer limited explicit references to canonical classical elements such as Great Books, a grammar-logic-rhetoric curriculum, or Socratic pedagogy in the full high-school spectrum. Taken together, the school demonstrates meaningful classical-influenced aspects but does not comprehensively meet the full classical rubric across all six dimensions. Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key contextual notes drawn from the school’s public materials include:
- ALA emphasizes R.A.I.S.E. leadership values and a moral, wholesome environment that underpins the educational program (evidence of moral formation and virtue-oriented language) .
- The school frames its approach as “time-proven” and values-based, with an emphasis on forming leaders and partnering with families (evidence of formation-oriented mission and parental partnership) .
- Curricular emphasis centers on Core Knowledge (not Common Core) and a traditional, time-proven academic structure, rather than explicit Great Books or liberal-arts triad (grammar, logic, rhetoric), which is a hallmark of classical pedagogy in many programs (evidence of Core Knowledge and traditional curriculum stance) .
- There is strong emphasis on parent involvement and community engagement (evidence of partnership with families) .

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly integrate Great Books and a Great Conversation framework into the curriculum, with explicit references to Great Books selections and guided Socratic discussion in appropriate grade levels.
- Add a formal grammar-logic-rhetoric sequence (mini-lexicon and program) within the early-middle grades, plus explicit rhetoric training (speech, declamation, public speaking) in upper grades.
- Incorporate classical pedagogical methods (narration/recitation, memory work, chanting) alongside Core Knowledge where possible, with documented implementation details on the website.
- Include explicit theological/metaphysical framing and liturgical/community practices (where appropriate to the school’s mission) to strengthen religious foundations and liturgical life if aligned with the school’s identity.
- Develop a visible “living curriculum” portrait of the teacher and a formal teacher-mentor framework, including faculty professional development specifically in classical pedagogy.
- Expand publicly available evidence of parental formation programming (book studies, lectures, and family-liturgy aligned events) to reinforce the parents-as-co-teachers model.
