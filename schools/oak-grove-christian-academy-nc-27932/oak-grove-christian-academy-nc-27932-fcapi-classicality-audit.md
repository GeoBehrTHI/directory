## Executive Summary

Oak Grove Christian Academy (NC 27932) presents a classical-Christian orientation with explicit emphasis on a Trivium-based curriculum, Christian theological grounding, and parent partnership within a hybrid schooling model. Public-facing materials describe a classically-minded program that centers on grammar, logic, and rhetoric, with explicit attention to virtue, character, and the formation of the whole person within a Christ-centered worldview. Evidence from the school’s site shows a deliberate Christian framing (Christ-centered values, theology-informed formation) and a pedagogy that blends traditional classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric, memory work) with a hybrid home-school component and parent partnership. While the program emphasizes important classical markers (grammar/logic/rhetoric, history integration, virtue formation, and a living Christianity in daily practice), explicit mentions of Great Books, a formal Great Conversation, and a sustained Socratic high-school pedagogy are less clear in the public materials. Overall, the school aligns strongly with classical aims and Christian formation, falling into the CLASSICAL category with some room for expansion into certain classical touchstones (e.g., explicit Great Books emphasis, broader Socratic-seminar pedagogy) to reach a fully classical standard across all six categories.

Key highlights from materials:
- Classical framework stated explicitly; “classically minded” and a focus on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) are described on the site.  
- Christian/theological commitments are central (non-denominational Christian grounding; Christ-centered education; theology integrated into the education approach).  
- Pedagogical elements referenced include memory work, chanting, and a rest-and-repetition rhythm in lower grades, with more advanced dialogue-like inquiry in upper grades (Socratic/seminal discussion patterns are implied via classical practice rather than explicitly described in every public page). 
- Family/parent partnership and hybrid schooling model are emphasized (parents as partners, home education components, and shared leadership in the educational process).
