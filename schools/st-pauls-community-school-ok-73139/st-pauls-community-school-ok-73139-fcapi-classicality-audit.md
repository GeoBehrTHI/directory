## Executive Summary

St. Paul’s Community School (South Oklahoma City, OK) presents a robust classical Christian program, with clear emphasis on the Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), Great Books culture, Latin study, and a pedagogy that blends contemplative moments with Socratic dialogue and rhetorical training. Public-facing materials consistently frame education as formation of character and virtue in a Christian, truth-seeking context, rather than solely information delivery or job preparation. Across six categories, the school demonstrates strong alignment with classical education principles, yielding an overall CLASSICAL designation.

Key strengths across categories include explicit statements of formation toward truth, goodness, and beauty; a Trivium-centered curriculum with emphasis on Great Books and liberal arts; a pedagogy that blends singing, memory work, contemplative practice, and Socratic/seminar style inquiry; and a family-centric community that partners with parents and invites ongoing involvement. The school’s own materials repeatedly foreground theological foundations, communal worship or liturgical rhythms, and a vision of education as formation within a Christian frame. These elements are consistently reinforced by multiple public-facing pages (About, Philosophy of Education, Get Involved, Admissions, and specific faculty pages). Citations from the school’s public content illustrate these points, including explicit references to the Trivium, Great Books, and Christ-centered mission, as well as the strong parental partnership and open-Door approach to engagement.

Aggregate Assessment

All six categories are CLASSICAL based on the evidence and keyword analysis. The school demonstrates comprehensive alignment with the classical education rubric across religious/metaphysical foundations, purposeful formation of character and wisdom, classical curriculum, traditional pedagogy, model faculty, and engaged parents/community.

What Would Move the Needle

- If any category shifted toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, concrete steps to move the needle would include:
 - Explicitly codifying and publishing a concise “Great Books” curriculum map by grade level, including primary texts and how they interrelate across disciplines.
 - Adding a transparent, shared-language summary of the “Scholé” or contemplative time within daily schedules to demonstrate sustained restfulness as a formal pedagogical practice.
 - Providing public-facing examples of student work that showcase high-level Socratic seminars or public speaking (e.g., student speeches, debates, or oratorical exercises) with assessment rubrics.
 - Expanding parental-formation resources (e.g., a regular parent book club or liturgical calendar alignment resources) to deepen parent engagement in the classical formation beyond visits and general communications.
