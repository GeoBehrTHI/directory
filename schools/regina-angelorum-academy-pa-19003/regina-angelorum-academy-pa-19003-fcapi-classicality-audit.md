## Executive Summary

Regina Angelorum Academy (RAA) in Ardmore, PA presents a coherent classical-Catholic profile across its public-facing materials. The school foregrounds a Catholic theological and metaphysical frame, a virtue- and wisdom-centered purpose, a classical liberal arts curriculum, and pedagogy that blends contemplative/memory-building activities with Socratic and rhetoric-oriented experiences. Public materials emphasize liturgy-informed community life, a low-tech learning environment, explicit fidelity to the Magisterium, Latin/Greek exposure, and explicit attention to Great Books/Great Conversation themes. The faculty are described as living mentors within a “living curriculum,” and the community emphasizes strong partnerships with parents who are treated as co-educators. Collectively, these signals align Regina Angelorum Academy with a CLASSICAL (or strongly classic) rubric, with no obvious negative indicators in the available public materials.

- Overall classification by category: CLASSICAL for all six categories.
- Aggregate ranking: CLASSICAL.
- Note: No category required a needle-moving “move the needle” section since all six categories meet the CLASSICAL benchmark.

Evidence for this assessment is drawn from the school’s mission/vision statements and curriculum landmarks, including explicit Catholic-theological foundations, classical curriculum affirmation, and pedagogy that blends contemplative elements with Socratic/seminar-style practice. Key sources show: an unapologetically Catholic identity tethered to the Magisterium, Scripture and Tradition in the curriculum, and a programmatic emphasis on Truth, Beauty, and Goodness; the Ruah Woods Theology of the Body curriculum; a classical liberal arts framework (grammar, logic, rhetoric; trivium/quadrivium references); a low-tech, memory- and chant-based lower-school approach, and upper-school Socratic/seminar formats; and a strong emphasis on parental partnership as co-educators.

- Core Catholic/metaphysical framing and liturgical community practices are documented in multiple public materials. 
- The school describes its mission as forming students and families in Catholic faith formation, classical wisdom, and virtue in action. 
- The Theory-of-the-Body curriculum (Ruah Woods) is explicitly adopted to ground Catholic anthropology in the classroom. 
- Truth, Beauty, Goodness as a cross-cutting curricular motif is repeatedly named. 
- The school frames its approach as classical liberal arts, including Latin/Greek exposure and a time-tested curriculum. 
- A low-tech, mindful, memory-forward pedagogy (lower grades) and Socratic/rhetoric-oriented upper grades are described. 
- Parents as co-educators and a robust partnership with families are emphasized.
