## Executive Summary

Acts Community School TX 77536 appears to be a church‑aligned educational initiative rather than a classical school in the strict sense. Public material from the Acts Community Church-side crawl emphasizes theology, gospel formation, and church life (mission to Reach/Train/Send, faith in Jesus, grace, God, etc.), rather than the standard classical curriculum components (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; liberal arts canon; Socratic pedagogy; Great Conversation). Consequently, the overall assessment is NOT CLASSICAL, with some indicators that align to a religious formation model rather than a full classical program. This divergence is most evident in Curriculum and Pedagogy, where explicit Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, or Socratic pedagogy are not evident in the publicly cited materials. The site does show a clear religious/theological orientation and community emphasis, which touches Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) and Element 6 (Parents & Community) in a limited way, but does not robustly meet the six classical elements across all six categories.

Key evidentiary notes:

- The site presents a strong theological orientation (Jesus, faith, grace, God, church) and mission framing (Reach/Train/Send), with explicit statements about following Jesus and modeling His life. This is cited in mission statements and church messaging.
- Public materials lack explicit references to classical curriculum components (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Great Conversation; scholé; Socratic seminars; Great Books canon). The absence of these explicit elements supports a NOT CLASSICAL designation for Curriculum and Pedagogy. See the cited church materials for the religious framing, rather than classical pedagogy content.
- The public-facing materials highlight a multi‑generational, multi‑ethnic, church‑community focus, which touches community/community-formation language but does not demonstrate a structured classical parent partnership or formal “living curriculum” framing.

Bottom line: NOT CLASSICAL overall, with Element 1 and Element 2 showing partial alignment to a religious formation mindset, and Categories 3–5 not meeting classical criteria as evidenced by the available public materials.
