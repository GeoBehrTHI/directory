## Executive Summary

Laramie Christian Academy (Wy 82072) presents itself publicly as a Classical Christian school with a clearly religiously framed mission and a commitment to formation alongside academics. The school positions itself as a private, classical Christian institution offering a Biblical worldview and values-driven education, with explicit emphasis on partnering with families and serving students from preschool through eighth grade (with plans to add in-seat high school). Public materials repeatedly highlight a faith-centered framework, parental partnership, and a classical orientation (including liberal arts language such as grammar, logic, rhetoric; K-8 Latin offerings; and formation-focused rhetoric around virtue and wisdom). The evidence indicates a robust theological grounding and a family-centered community, but the explicit articulation of core classical pedagogy—especially in terms of Socratic or scholé-based methods and the formal Great Books / Great Conversation program—appears less explicit on the site itself.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale in brief:
- Strong Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are clearly stated (Biblical worldview, Jesus-centered language, theology-integrated aims) and reinforced by parental partnership grounded in biblical commands. This anchors the school firmly in a theological frame. CLASSICAL.
- School Purpose and Goal show formation-oriented language through Biblical knowledge and values, but explicit articulation of virtue/wisdom formation as classical telos is less overt in public copy; still aligns with formation aims. SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum includes classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Latin in K–8; private classical Christian framing) but lacks consistent public reference to Great Books or a fully explicit Great Conversation program. SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy is not openly cataloged as Socratic/seminar-based or scholé/restful pedagogy in the public materials; other icons of classical pedagogy are not clearly stated. NOT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty notes and leadership appear as model teachers in a traditional sense, with a focus on virtue and living curriculum, contributing CLASSICAL elements.
- Parents & Community are clearly central and explicitly framed as co-educators with strong, tangible programs (Inside Parents, open visits, and family-centric events), contributing CLASSICAL elements.

- Negative signals: absence of explicit classical pedagogy terms; lack of a clear, public statement about Socratic/Scholé-based methods, seminars, or ritualized memory work. 

Why this rating
