## Executive Summary

Two Rivers Classical Academy (TRCA) embodies a strongly classical, Christian model with explicit emphasis on the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), a Christian formation framework, and a collaborative parental partnership. Public materials describe TRCA as a Classical, Collaborative, Christian academy that intentionally partners with families, enacts a “best books” classical curriculum, and weaves liturgical or worship-like elements (Scripture memory, catechesis, hymns) into daily life. The school also foregrounds the aim of forming virtue and wisdom in students rather than reducing education to job-readiness, while maintaining a thoughtful balance of classroom and home-based instruction. Across evidence from the public-facing site, TRCA repeatedly positions itself as a classical Christian school with a parental-co-teaching model, a lay foundation in the Trivium, and a commitment to the Great Conversation through the study of great books and central Western-Christian intellectual heritage. Taken together, TRCA aligns with the six classical-school criteria across all six categories, yielding a GENERAL CLASSICAL designation.

Key observed signals
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit: daily Scripture, catechesis, hymns, and a stated Statement of Beliefs anchor the program in Christian theology and liturgical life.  
- Purpose and goal center formation and virtue, not merely employment outcomes; the vision emphasizes forming hearts and minds toward truth, goodness, and beauty, with a long-standing focus on virtue and wisdom. 
- Curriculum is clearly classical: Trivium, Great Books, Western literature/history, Latin, Bible, and a “best books” approach to curriculum selection. 
- Pedagogy includes both contemplative/restful elements (Scholé-style memory work in lower grades) and Socratic/seminar-style practices in the upper grades; a clear separation of lower- and upper-school methods is described.  
- Faculty are presented as living mentors and the core of the school’s “living curriculum,” with formal roles and a path for teacher collaboration and parent partnership. 
- Parents and community are central: a defined collaborative model with parents as co-teachers, training, and opportunities for parental involvement and campus visits. 

Abbreviated verdict: CLASSICAL across all six categories.
