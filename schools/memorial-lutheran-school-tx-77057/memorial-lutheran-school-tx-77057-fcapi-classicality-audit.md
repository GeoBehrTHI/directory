## Executive Summary

Memorial Lutheran School (MLS) in Houston, TX presents a robust alignment with classical education principles across religious foundations, purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement. Public materials depict MLS as a classical Lutheran K–12 school with daily chapel, a Gospel-centered formation, and a mission to cultivate virtue, wisdom, and love of neighbor. The curriculum emphasizes humane letters, Latin, history, logic, rhetoric, and primary-source study, accompanied by Socratic discussion and memorization practices in lower and upper grades. Faculty are described as enthusiastic scholar-teachers, and the school explicitly frames parents as partners through structures such as a Parent Hub and PTL. Across categories, MLS consistently foregrounds truth, goodness, beauty, worship, and formation—core tenets of classical education as defined in the Six Essential Elements framework.

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are clearly articulated through daily Chapel, a Gospel-centered worldview, and Lutheran foundations that permeate instruction. Evidence: daily chapel, gospel-based formation, theology-grounded instruction, and liturgical practices.  
- Purpose and Goal center on formation, virtue, and mastery of language with a grounding in truth, goodness, and beauty, rather than training for a specific career path. Evidence: mission emphasis on foundation in Gospel; five-fold educational goals; articulation of truth, goodness, beauty in the classical vision. 
- Curriculum emphasizes humane letters, Latin, history, logic, rhetoric, and primary-source study; indicates a liberal-arts orientation with Socratic inquiry. Evidence: Humane Letters, Latin, History credits, Logic & Rhetoric, primary-source reading, and Socratic discussion in humanities. 
- Pedagogy includes contemplative elements (formation through songs, recitation, catechesis) alongside rigorous Socratic discussion, seminars, and rhetoric training at the upper levels. Evidence: lower-school singing/chanting and memory work; upper-school Socratic discussion/seminars; rhetoric and public speaking opportunities. 
- Faculty are described as model teachers and mentors, with a living-curriculum ethos and a focus on virtue, skill, and knowledge; composition includes high-degree-credential data. Evidence: enthusiastic scholar-teachers; high share of advanced degrees; living curriculum framing. 
- Parents and Community are positioned as co-educators and partners, with structured avenues for parental involvement (Parent Hub, PTL, family resources, campus visits). Evidence: Parent Hub, PTL references, family resources, calendar, and campus engagement channels.
