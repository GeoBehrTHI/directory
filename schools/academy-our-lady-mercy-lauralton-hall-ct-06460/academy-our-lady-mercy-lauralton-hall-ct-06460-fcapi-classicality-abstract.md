## ABSTRACT

The Academy of Our Lady of Mercy Lauralton Hall demonstrates meaningful classical-leaning signals anchored in Catholic Mercy identity, formation-oriented aims, and a governance model that places faculty at the center of student development. The public materials emphasize Mercy values (faith, service, courage, leadership) and a mission focused on forming virtuous, capable young women, consistent with classical aims in Element 2. The school’s curriculum and signature programs reflect strong college-preparatory and STEM/Arts components but do not foreground a classical Trivium-based framework or Great Books canon, which limits Element 3 designation to NOT CLASSICAL. Pedagogical signals show memory-based, mentorship-driven practices (and music-based memory elements) that align with some classical practices, yet explicit Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy is not clearly documented in public materials, placing Element 4 at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. Faculty signals—centered on mentors and a living curriculum—align well with classical expectations (Element 5: CLASSICAL). Parent and community engagement is evident through admissions and family stories, yet there is less public documentation of formal parent formation as co-teachers (Element 6: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL). Aggregate assessment places the school as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall, with clear needle-moving opportunities in articulating and publicly publishing classical core elements (Great Books/Great Conversation, Trivium, scholé-based pedagogy) and expanding explicit liturgical and parent-formation programs.

- Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The school’s public materials reveal a strength in religious identity, character formation, and mentorship culture, with notable opportunities to deepen classical elements in curriculum, pedagogy, and parent-community alignment.
