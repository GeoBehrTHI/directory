## Executive Summary

St. Therese Classical Academy (Chester, NY, 10918) presents a largely classical profile with strong religious/theological framing, a formation-centered mission, and a pedagogy oriented toward Socratic discourse within a liberal-arts, Catholic context. Public materials regularly emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty; a faith-centered environment; and a commitment to classical methods (e.g., Socratic seminars, “great conversation,” and references to a Great Books–style tradition within the Chesterton network). There are explicit signs of classical aims (incarnational environment, formation through virtue, emphasis on memory work and liturgical cadence in some classical pedagogy materials) but the public-facing content also leaves several classical elements less explicit (e.g., granular statements about grammar/logic/rhetoric as a curricular core; full articulation of a systematic Great Books program; and fully detailed evidence of laid-out “memory/recitation” practices). Taken together, the school overall reads as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with clear CLASSICAL signals in several areas and more contingent signals in others.

Key takeaways:

- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strongly present (Christ-centered, faith-based formation, incaranational imagery) and supported by mission language and Chesterton-network references. This category is CLASSICAL.
- School Purpose and Goal is formation-oriented (virtue, wisdom, truth/beauty) with a clear “formation over information” stance cited in public materials. This category is CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum signals are present (liberal arts framing, Great Books/Great Conversation linkage via Chesterton network, and emphasis on wonder), but explicit curricular specifics (grammar/logic/rhetoric, full Great Books roster) are not consistently spelled out on the public pages. This category is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy highlights Socratic dialogue and a contemplative (Scholé-like) rhythm in some materials, but concrete evidence of a comprehensive restful pace and explicit memorization/recitation practices is limited in public content. This category is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty signals include founder-led instruction and a culture of mentorship implied by “teacher-led” mission and the founder’s biography, but explicit statements about the faculty as “living curriculum” or a formal teacher portrait are not clearly articulated in public pages. This category is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Parents & Community emphasis shows family orientation and opportunities for engagement (open houses, tours, and family-centered messaging), consistent with a partnership model; however, explicit, recurring statements about parents as co-teachers and a formal parent-formation program are less overt in the public content. This category is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
