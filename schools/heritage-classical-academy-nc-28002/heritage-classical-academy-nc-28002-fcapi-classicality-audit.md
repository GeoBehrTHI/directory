## Executive Summary

Heritage Classical Academy (Albemarle, NC 28002) presents a clearly classical-leaning profile rooted in a Christ-centered, classical-education philosophy. Public-facing materials emphasize a biblical worldview, a commitment to virtue and wisdom, and a traditional liberal arts orientation framed by a “classical model.” Evidence across the school’s site shows explicit statements about Christ-centered education, a classical-model approach, family/parental partnership, and an emphasis on formation through memory, virtue, and a faith-inflected understanding of history and learning. Several sources highlight core values such as Christ-centered education, a classical model that centers truth, goodness, and beauty, and partnerships with families. These elements align Heritage Classical Academy with the classical tradition in meaningful ways, though some pedagogical specifics (e.g., explicit, high-frequency Socratic seminars in the upper grades or formal Great Books sequences) are less clearly documented on the site. Overall, the school earns a CLASSICAL designation, with a couple of areas that are clearly CLASSICAL but could be strengthened to reach full classical fidelity.

Executive synthesis by category (category-by-category snapshot) and summarized evidence:

  - Evidence: The school presents itself as Christ-centered and biblically grounded. Core values identify Christ-centered learning and a biblical worldview; mission statements repeatedly speak of Christ-centered classical education and Latin-scriptural grounding. Public materials describe curriculum and community shaped by Jesus Christ and God’s Word, and emphasize the sacred dimension of education, virtue, and truth. See Christ-centered language and biblical worldview emphasis in core materials.   
  - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include Christ-centered, truth, goodness, beauty (core values and mission), virtue, and biblical worldview; these map to Positive Keywords in the classical lexicon. The site also references theology and sacred text in its framing. Negative indicators (secularism, relativism) are notably absent in public-facing materials. Overall, a preponderance of positive religious/metaphysical indicators is present.    
  - Why this rating: The school explicitly grounds education in a Christian framework and a biblically informed worldview, with language about truth, goodness, and beauty as transcendent ideals. The alignment with theological grounding and liturgical/communal sensibilities is strong in public materials. This justifies CLASSICAL rather than merely SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: The mission repeatedly frames education as formation toward virtue and human excellence, not simply job preparation. Phrases like “excellent Christ-centered classical education” and references to cultivation of virtue, wisdom, and human flourishing appear in public materials. The school clearly positions education as formation toward human excellence, grounded in true/good/beautiful telos.

Negative indicators (e.g., “workforce preparation” emphasis) are not evident in public materials. The presence of “formation” and “virtue/wisdom” aligns with classical aims.

- Element 3: Curriculum — CLASSICAL
  - Evidence: Curriculum framing is anchored in classical education rhetoric: explicit references to a classical model, and in-admission materials noting the use of classical methodologies. The site describes a reading program that incorporates traditional resources (e.g., LOE and Memoria Press resources) and uses a curriculum map to guide study. There are clear signals of a grammar-logic-rhetoric orientation (public references to classical foundations and core materials). The school emphasizes history throughout the curriculum and a classical approach to literature/history via read-alouds and historically grounded content.   
  - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Great Books/Great Conversation (implied through classical framing and reading choices), history throughout the curriculum, and a focus on wonder. The presence of LOE (Logic of English) and Memoria Press materials further anchors classical methods. Negative indicators from the school’s public materials are not evident.  
  - Why this rating: The school coherently promotes a curriculum consistent with classical education, including grammar-logic-rhetoric orientation, engagement with history and classical literature/philosophy through a read-aloud and “great conversation” ethos, and a strong emphasis on wonder in learning. CLASSICAL is warranted.

There is explicit mention of memory-focused, contemplative approaches (Scholé-style) and singing/chanting to memorize, which align with classical pedagogy. There are also mentions of more dialogic, Socratic-style practices in the six-element lexicon as a model, but explicit evidence of systematic Socratic seminars, debates, or upper-school rhetoric programs on the Heritage site is less direct. Jennifer’s Story describes memorization of Bible verses and catechism, indicating memory-based pedagogy in early grades. 
  - Positive/Negative keyword analysis: Positive indicators include Scholé/restful learning, memory work (recitation/chanting), and contemplative elements, plus memorization and catechesis; these map to the Positive Keywords in the classical lexicon. The site does not consistently show explicit, school-wide Socratic seminars or a sustained, formal upper-school rhetoric program in published content, which would be stronger evidence of classic high-school pedagogy. Therefore, some classical elements are documented, but not uniformly across all grades, yielding a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL designation. 
  - Why this rating: The school demonstrates classical pedagogy components (restful learning, memory work, recitation, catechesis) especially in lower grades, and signals a classical approach elsewhere, but the absence of consistently documented, grade-wide Socratic seminars and formal upper-school rhetoric programs reduces the certainty of a fully classical pedagogy.

Negative indicators are not evident on the site. Overall, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL is warranted.
  - Why this rating: The school portrays a faculty culture leaning toward virtue, mentorship, and professional development, but lacks explicit articulation of a formal classical-faculty framework (e.g., “portrait of a teacher,” formal living-curriculum role). This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

  - Why this rating: Heritage Classical Academy situates parents as co-teachers, emphasizes parental formation and partnership, and maintains open-visit policies, which align with classical expectations for engaged parent/community collaboration.

What would move the needle
- Pedagogy (Element 4): Increase explicit, publicly documented deployment of Socratic seminars and high-school-level Great Conversation in upper grades; publish a clear, provincially visible outline of upper-grade pedagogy (seminars, debates, and rhetoric training) to complement lower-grade memory/Scholé practices. Consider publishing exemplars of upper-grade seminars or student-led discourse, along with a formal description of upper-school rhetoric initiatives.
- Faculty (Element 5): Publish a formalized “Portrait of a Classical Teacher” and “Living Curriculum” articulation that describes how faculty embody the living curriculum and how teacher-teacher and teacher-student mentorship examples are integrated into daily practice. Expand professional-development commitments with publicly visible cycles that align with classical formation (e.g., seminars on Great Books, classical pedagogy, and curriculum design).
- History/Great Books emphasis (Element 3): If not already, articulate a formal Great Books sequence or a “Great Conversation” read list and schedule, and show cross-disciplinary integration of the Great Books canon across grades to strengthen the liberal-arts thesis.
- Liturgical/communal practices (Element 1): If aligned with the school’s ecclesial environment, publish explicit mention of liturgical or communal-prayer practices, devotional rhythms, or chapel/mass-type gatherings within the school day to strengthen liturgical grounding (where appropriate to the school’s theology and culture). This would further strengthen the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations category.

ABSTRACT
Heritage Classical Academy NC 28002 demonstrates a strong CLASSICAL alignment across the majority of the classical-education rubric. The school’s public materials emphasize Christ-centered, biblical-worldview framing, a classical model focused on truth, goodness, and beauty, and a formation-centric purpose oriented toward virtue and wisdom. The curriculum is anchored in classical methods, with evidence of liberal-arts orientation and historical-literary engagement, and a robust emphasis on family/parent partnerships and community involvement. Pedagogy and faculty elements show a mixture of lower-grade contemplative/memory-based practices and upper-grade dialogic potential, but publicly available materials do not consistently document a full, school-wide Socratic/seminar-based pedagogy or a formal living-curriculum portrait of the faculty. The aggregate picture supports a CLASSICAL designation, with two categories (Pedagogy and Faculty) rated SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL due to partial documentation of the classical-pedagogy and explicit “living curriculum” faculty framing. The school’s strong parental-partnership model and liturgical/theological grounding further reinforce its classical orientation.
