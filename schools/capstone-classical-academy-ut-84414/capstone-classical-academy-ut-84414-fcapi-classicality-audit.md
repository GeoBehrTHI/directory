# Classicality Audit: Candeo Schools (AZ)
Website used: https://candeoschools.com/

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Candeo Schools explicitly frames its program around the classical liberal arts with repeated emphasis on forming wisdom and virtue and on the transcendentals—truth, goodness, and beauty. The site names the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), Latin (grades 4–8), formal logic and rhetoric in middle school, and practices like memorization and recitation in the lower grades. It partners with families and articulates a formative, mission‑driven culture. As a public charter, however, it does not ground its philosophy in theology or liturgical life; this limits Category 1 (Religious & Metaphysical) under the six‑element framework you provided. Result: strong classical alignment in Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Parents/Community; partial alignment in Faculty and Religious & Metaphysical Foundations. Key pages include statements such as “grow wisdom and virtue for a life well lived” and “pursue truth, beauty, and goodness,” and outline trivium‑based study with Latin, logic, rhetoric, history (not “social studies”), and wonder‑oriented learning.    

Method note: This audit uses the “Six Essential Elements of a Classical School” keyword framework (positive/negative indicators) across six categories, with quantitative keyword counts and coverage estimates before assigning ratings.

## Aggregate Rating: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Rationale: Strong performance in Purpose/Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Parents/Community; partial in Faculty; constrained in Religious & Metaphysical due to non‑theological, non‑liturgical character of a public charter. Using the Six Elements lexicon, the overall language and structure are substantially classical but not fully aligned with the theological and liturgical dimensions of the framework.  

---

## What would move the needle
- Religious & Metaphysical: Within legal constraints for public charters, further articulate (in philosophical/secular terms) objective moral order/natural law foundations that underwrite the school’s virtue and transcendentals language; clarify how these norms anchor curricular and disciplinary practices. This would strengthen the metaphysical grounding dimension of the framework. 
- Faculty: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and/or “Portrait of a Graduate,” and describe faculty formation (e.g., seminars on classical pedagogy, great‑books reading for faculty). This directly hits the “teachers as mentors/living curriculum” markers. 
- Curriculum: Publicly list literature by grade and identify primary texts/Great Books used to more clearly signal participation in the Great Conversation. 
- Pedagogy: Name specific practices already implied—e.g., “chanting and singing” in K–3 memory work; “seminar/disputation” elements in grades 7–8—to make the classical pedagogy more legible on the site. 
- Parents & Community: Add parent‑formation touchpoints (book clubs, talks on classical education, recommended reading lists) to deepen alignment with the parent partnership ideal.
