## Executive Summary

Point Christian Academy (PCA) in Chattanooga, TN, presents a coherent classical Christian profile across most criteria, with strong religious/metaphysical foundations, a purpose grounded in formation and virtue, a curriculum aligned to core classical principles, and robust parental/community partnership. Public materials consistently foreground a Biblical worldview, Christ-centered mission, and classical education, including explicit references to grammar, logic, and rhetoric, as well as memory-based pedagogy and liturgical/communal language in some sections. The school references classical pedagogy (Socratic style in upper grades is implied by dialogue-based methods in the classical rubric) and emphasizes family partnerships, witness to faith, and a living faculty model.

Overall, PCA demonstrates CLASSICAL orientation in four categories (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations; Purpose and Goal; Faculty; Parents & Community) and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Curriculum and Pedagogy, with evidence of authentic classical indicators (grammar/logical/rhetorical focus, formation over information, liturgical/communal sensibilities, and a strong faith framework). The public materials do not show explicit, universal Great Books reading mandates or a fully articulated Great Conversation across all grade levels, which is why Curriculum and Pedagogy land in the SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL band rather than fully CLASSICAL. The aggregate classification is therefore CLASSICAL, with targeted needle-moving opportunities identified for Curriculum and Pedagogy to reach full alignment.

Key insights:
- Strong religious/metaphysical grounding is explicit (biblical truth, Christian worldview, Jesus/Christ-centered framing) and reinforced by mission statements and preschool materials. This supports Element 1 as CLASSICAL.
- The school’s stated goals emphasize formation, virtue, and spiritual growth alongside intellectual development, aligning Element 2 with CLASSICAL.
- Curriculum shows core Trivium elements (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and a history-focused framing, but lacks explicit, universal Great Books/Great Conversation mandates in public materials, supporting Element 3 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Pedagogy includes memory-based singing/chanting in the lower grades and Socratic-style discussion/reasoned discourse in instruction in line with classical modes, though explicit, widespread articulation of upper-grade Socratic seminars is not consistently demonstrated in the public materials, supporting Element 4 as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.
- Faculty portrayals emphasize virtue, mentorship, and living curriculum; parent/community partnerships are actively described, supporting Element 5 and 6 as CLASSICAL.

- The school frames its mission around nurturing spirituality and a Christian educational community, including references to bearing the light of Christ and partnership with families in faith-formation. 
- Public “What is classical Christian education?” materials articulate a biblical/theological framework and a classical approach (grammar/logics/rhetoric) as foundational to the enterprise. 

Positive Keyword Analysis
- Present positive indicators: biblical truth, Christian, Christ, gospel, biblical worldview, theology (implicitly in the classical Christian framing), liturgy/worship (through communal faith life language in materials), sacred tradition.
- Frequency/strength: High presence of explicit religious framing and Christian formation language; moderate explicit use of liturgical language (e.g., communal/faith life references) and theology in public-facing pages.

Why this rating
- The materials clearly position PCA within a religious-metaphysical frame aligned with classical Christian education, including explicit references to Biblical truth, Christian pedagogy, and Christ-centered formation. This aligns strongly with Positive Keywords in Element 1 and with the “Grounded in Theology” and “Liturgical/Communal” cues in the Six Essential Elements lexicon. The absence of secular or relativistic signals in the public materials reinforces CLASSICAL rather than NOT CLASSICAL.

The Great Books/Great Conversation is a hallmark indicator in the Six Essential Elements framework that is not explicitly codified in PCA’s public-facing materials. 

Positive Keyword Analysis
- Present keywords: grammar, logic, rhetoric (explicit in the public materials); Great Books/Great Conversation (not explicitly mandated by PCA materials; mentions of classical canon appear in the general classical education lexicon, not PCA-specific pages); history/ Western civilization framing (some public content mentions a classical approach though not always labeled as Great Books).
- Frequency/strength: Grammar/logic/rhetoric are clearly present; explicit Great Books/Great Conversation is not clearly mandated on PCA pages, and history-integration is implied rather than spelled out. This yields a moderate positive signal with a missing explicit Great Books point.

Why this rating
- Element 3 criteria lean toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL because PCA demonstrates core classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric; history-focused pedagogy implied) but does not consistently publish explicit Great Books/Great Conversation mandates across its public materials. The absence of explicit, universal Great Books integration reduces the category to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

Citations: What is classical Christian education (turn0file7); Our Mission & Vision (turn0file2); Preschool/Academics/program pages with classical content (turn0file4/turn0file6).

### Element 4: Pedagogy — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Lower-school pedagogy includes memory-based learning traditions such as singing, chanting, and recitation, which align with the scholé/restful/remembering aspects in the classical rubric. PCA materials describe teaching that emphasizes memory and wonder in younger grades (e.g., Solfège, music, drawing exercises). 
- Public materials reference Socratic discussion and rhetoric in upper grades as part of classical education, though explicit, school-wide orchestration of Socratic seminars across grades is not consistently stated in PCA’s public content. The public-facing material emphasizes classical education with an emphasis on dialogue, but concrete evidence of a broad, consistent Socratic pedagogy across all grades is not always explicit. 
- The school emphasizes a balance of contemplative/restful learning and rigorous inquiry, including opportunities for memory work, and upper-level rhetoric training (e.g., speeches) in the public content. This aligns with classical pedagogy but the explicit, published schedule of Socratic seminars across K-12 is not uniformly documented in the accessible pages. 

Positive Keyword Analysis
- Present keywords: scholé, memory work, chanting, recitation, relaxation/restful learning; Socratic discussion/seminars; rhetoric training; debates; memory-based pedagogy; classical dialogic pedagogy.
- Frequency/strength: Strong for lower grades (memory/chanting; holistic/wonderful learning); moderate for upper grades (Socratic/dialogue rhetoric). The explicit, consistent nationwide application of Socratic seminars is not uniformly visible in PCA’s public content.

Why this rating
- Because PCA clearly demonstrates both memory-based, contemplative pedagogy in early grades and discourse-based, rhetoric-oriented pedagogy in upper grades, but the explicit, school-wide, consistent implementation of Socratic pedagogy across all grades is not always explicit in the public pages, the Pedagogy category lands in SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Citations: Lower-grade pedagogy (turn0file6); Classical education explanation with Socratic/dialogue (turn0file7); Mission/vision (turn0file2).

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curriculum: Make Great Books/Great Conversation explicit and universal across grade levels. Public materials should clearly articulate a school-wide Great Books program and the integration of Great Conversation into the curriculum, with explicit grade-by-grade expectations and sample readings. This would elevate Element 3 to CLASSICAL by making Great Books/Great Conversation a published, consistent pillar across all grades. (References to Great Books appear in the Six Essential Elements lexicon as a core positive indicator; PCA currently demonstrates core Trivium-based elements but does not consistently publish Great Books mandates on public pages.) 
- Pedagogy: Expand explicit articulation of upper-grade Socratic seminars and formal rhetorical pedagogy across more grades, including teacher training documentation and a published pedagogy framework. The current public materials show memory-based lower-grade methods and some upper-grade Socratic/ rhetoric signals, but a school-wide, published pedagogy framework would strengthen alignment with the Pedagogy criteria. 
- History/Great Conversation: Strengthen explicit history-integration across the curriculum and ensure “history as history” language is used consistently (rather than social studies framing). The Six Essential Elements emphasize history/Western civilization and integrated study; PCA could amplify these signals in public materials to strengthen Element 3. 
- Public-facing language around formation: Increase explicit use of terms like “formation,” “soul formation,” and “moral imagination” in mission statements and curriculum pages to reinforce the formation-centered ethos across all categories. This would further bolster Element 2 (Purpose and Goal) to CLASSICAL.
