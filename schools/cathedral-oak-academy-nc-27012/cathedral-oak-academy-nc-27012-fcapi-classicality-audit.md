## Executive Summary

Cathedral Oak Academy NC 27012 presents a robust classical-education profile embedded in a Christian (Biblical) worldview. Public materials consistently frame education as formation in wisdom and virtue through a Great Books-oriented curriculum, the Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric), and a liberal-arts foundation. The school foregrounds liturgical and communal practices (Chapel, worship), a deliberate partnership with parents (University-Model blended approach), and a staff that models Christian character. Together these elements align strongly with the six-element classical rubric across all categories, yielding a primary classification of CLASSICAL overall.

Key evidence includes: a stated commitment to a Great Books-based education and the Great Conversation, emphasis on the seven liberal arts and the Trivium, explicit incorporation of worship and Biblical worldview into curriculum, and a family-partnered University-Model pedagogy that remains grounded in formation and virtue rather than mere information transfer. Evidence of liturgical life (Chapel), character formation (virtue, wisdom, discipleship), and a clearly articulated mission (Christ-centered, Biblical worldview, mission beliefs) further reinforces the classical alignment. Public statements about pedagogy (memorizations and chant in lower grades; Socratic-style discussion and rhetoric in upper grades) dovetail with the traditional classical pedagogy framework.

Aggregate Assessment

All six categories strongly align with CLASSICAL. The public content across COA’s site emphasizes a classical curriculum (Great Books, Trivium, liberal arts), classical pedagogy (Socratic-influenced inquiry, memorization, rhetoric), liturgical life (Chapel, worship), virtuous character formation (wisdom, virtue, discipleship), faculty as mentors, and active parental partnership. The positive keyword indicators from the Six Essential Elements document are consistently reflected in COA’s public materials, and negative indicators are not evident in the school’s messaging. Therefore, the overall ranking is CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- If aiming for a fully explicit alignment in Pedagogy, the school could annotate and publicly showcase explicit “Socratic seminars,” “Scholé-based rhythms,” and direct examples of upper-school seminars or debates to demonstrate formal engagement with Socratic pedagogy in grades 9–12.
- Consider publishing a concise mapping of Great Books titles and the Trivium progression across grades to strengthen the explicit connection between curriculum content and pedagogical method for prospective families.
- While parental partnership is strong, additional public-facing materials that demonstrate specific parent training content (workshops, book studies, or reading guides on classical education) could further reinforce the Parents & Community category.
