## ABSTRACT

Tyndale Christian Academy Florida (33527) demonstrates a predominantly classical orientation grounded in Christian theology and virtue formation. The school’s religious/metaphysical foundations are strong, anchored in Scripture, a Baptist Statement of Faith, and a Christ-centered mission. The school’s purpose emphasizes formation, virtue, wisdom, and leadership, aligning with classical aims. There is clear evidence of a grammar-stage curriculum through admissions language and a library emphasis on truth, goodness, and beauty. Parental involvement and In Loco Parentis policies are robust, signaling a strong community partnership.

However, the curriculum and pedagogy show only partial alignment with classical norms. A documented Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis is not explicitly present beyond implied literature engagement, and explicit logic, dialectic, or Socratic pedagogy for upper grades is not consistently evidenced. The faculty portrayal exists but lacks a clearly articulated “living-curriculum” model in public materials. Overall, the school is CLASSICAL in its foundational commitments and community goals but is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in its full-spectrum implementation of classical education across all six categories.

Key category-by-category notes:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations: CLASSICAL — evidence of theology-grounded education, truth/goodness/beauty emphasis, and liturgical/theological framing; strong faith-based formation. 
- School Purpose and Goal: CLASSICAL — formation through virtue and wisdom, leadership through biblical study, and a telos beyond mere workforce preparation. 
- Curriculum: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — grammar-stage is present; Great-Books/Great Conversation and full logic/rhetoric emphasis are not clearly documented in public materials. 
- Pedagogy: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — contemplative/restful aims and memory-oriented practices hinted; limited explicit Socratic/seminar emphasis in public materials. 
- Faculty: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL — visible faculty presence and leadership, but lack of explicit “living curriculum” faculty framework in public materials. 
- Parents & Community: CLASSICAL — strong parental partnership, open campus, in loco parentis, and formal parent-involvement policies.
