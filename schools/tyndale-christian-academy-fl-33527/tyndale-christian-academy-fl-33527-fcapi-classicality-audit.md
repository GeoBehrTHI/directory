## Executive Summary

Tyndale Christian Academy (Dover, FL 33527) presents a distinctly Christian, virtue- and wisdom-centered approach to education with several hallmarks of classical education, particularly a clear emphasis on formation through truth, goodness, and beauty, a grammar-stage orientation in early grades, and a governance/parent-partnership model anchored in a strong Statement of Faith and Biblical framework. The public materials show strong religious foundations, a stated telos of forming leaders through Biblical study and virtue, and explicit parental involvement policies. However, while the school signals several classical traits (notably grammar-stage framing and virtue-centered aims), there is less explicit evidence of a full Great-Books orientation, robust logic/rhetoric pedagogy, or a comprehensive, formal Great Conversation/Liberal-Arts curriculum across all grades. On balance, the school aligns with classical education in meaningful ways but falls short of a complete, all-six-category CLASSICAL alignment. Therefore, overall assessment: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are strongly present: Christ-centered mission, theology integrated with curriculum, and explicit statements of faith and Scripture-based education. This supports a CLASSICAL rating in Element 1. Evidence includes Mission Statements, Philosophy of Education emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty, and a Statement of Faith.  
- School Purpose and Goal centers on formation, virtue, wisdom, and leadership through biblical study, aligning with CLASSICAL aims though not exhaustively framed as liberal-arts formation; evidence supports CLASSICAL in Element 2. 
- Curriculum includes a clear Grammar-stage reference (3.2.1 Grammar) and a library emphasizing truth, goodness, and beauty, but lacks explicit, repeated Great-Books/Great-Conversation language across the curriculum; thus Element 3 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.  
- Pedagogy shows a mix: grammar-stage memory/recitation emphasis and contemplative aims, with limited explicit documentation of Socratic seminars or full rhetoric training in upper grades; this yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Element 4. 
- Faculty presentation is present (meet-the-team), but there is no explicit, fully articulated “living curriculum” portrait in public materials; thus Element 5 is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Parents & Community are strongly engaged through In Loco Parentis, parental involvement requirements, and campus-visit openness; this supports CLASSICAL in Element 6.  

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The school presents a Christ-centered, Biblical framework and theology-driven mission (e.g., “Educating to Glorify God,” Christ-centered education, and Scripture-based formation). 
  - Philosophy of Education emphasizes nourishing the soul on truth, goodness, and beauty according to God’s Word, and notes General and Special Revelation. This aligns with transcendental moral realism and metaphysical grounding in theology. 
  - Statement of Faith and Baptist theological commitments surface in governance and policy documents. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Present positive keywords: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; liturgy/worship (theology-centered, Scripture-based). Also “wonder” appears in materials describing cultivation of wisdom. 
  - Negative indicators from the lexicon are not overtly present in the public materials; there is no clear signaling of secular relativism or moral neutralism.
- Why this rating
  - The school robustly embeds religious/metaphysical aims (God’s Word, theology, virtue, and shaping character) but does not present a liturgical program with explicit communal worship formats in every context. The Foundations are clear and strong yet not fully dominated by liturgical practice in public-facing materials. This yields a CLASSICAL alignment leaning toward CLASSICAL in conviction, but given the limited liturgical/communal detail, it sits at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully Classical. 

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Public materials frame education as formation: formation of virtue and leadership for wisdom, not merely job-prep. Mission statements emphasize character and human flourishing. 
  - Explicit aim to cultivate truth, goodness, and beauty; formation of leaders through Biblical study and virtue. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, human flourishing, liberal education (implied through formation and virtue), the true/good/beautiful (truth/goodness/beauty). 
  - Negative keywords from the lexicon (e.g., “workforce preparation,” “21st-century skills”) are not emphasized or foregrounded in public materials.
- Why this rating
  - The school foregrounds formation and virtue as core telos, consistent with classical aims of human excellence and moral formation, rather than primarily job-preparation. The evidence supports a CLASSICAL orientation in purpose/goal.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - There is a clear Grammar stage framing in admissions/grammar language (3.2.1 Grammar). This signals classical structuring for early grades. 
  - Library materials emphasize literature and the moral aims of truth, goodness, and beauty. Publics show intentional cultivation of reading and moral discernment. 
  - Philosophy indicates scripture-based education and integration of general revelation; not explicit Great Books programs, or explicit “Great Conversation” or triadic liberal-arts curriculum in the public materials. This absence reduces the strength of a full classical curriculum signal.
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Present positive keywords: grammar; classics/literature (via library and philosophy), history (through curriculum implications), memory/recitation in pedagogy (not heavily documented but implied in core classical practices).
  - Notable gaps: explicit mention of Great Books, Great Conversation, logic and rhetoric as uniform, required elements of the curriculum; explicit history through Western civilization framing beyond general references is not clearly documented.
- Why this rating
  - While grammar-stage is present and literature-focused library aims exist, explicit triad of liberal-arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the explicit Great Books/Great Conversation tradition are not strongly evidenced in the public materials. Thus, SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Grammar-stage emphasis and memory-oriented practice (narration/recitation) is suggested by admissions materials and library emphasis; the school discusses cultivating wonder and a contemplative stance (scholé-like language) in pedagogy.  
  - Mentions of “cultivating wisdom” and wonder align with restfulness and contemplation as parts of schooling; there is some sense of structured dialogue in higher grades implied by “Reason, Discern, & Lead” messaging. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Present positive keywords: contemplation/restful learning (scholé), memory work/recitation, wonder, rhetoric training (upper-grade potential) though explicit instances of Socratic seminars or formal debates are not clearly documented.
  - Negative indicators (from lexicon) such as exclusive emphasis on experiential or project-based learning are not strongly signaled; however, explicit Socratic seminars are not strongly documented either.
- Why this rating
  - The pedagogy shows classical underpinnings (grammar-stage, memorization/recitation, wonder as a pedagogical focus) but lacks robust, well-documented Socratic seminars, full upper-grade rhetoric training, or explicit, widespread use of classical dialectic pedagogy across the curriculum. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating.

### Element 5: School Faculty — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - Public materials show a "Meet The Team" page with named teachers and administrator leadership; the principal and teachers are described in professional terms, suggesting teachers as mentors. 
  - While not explicitly framed as a “living curriculum” portrait or a formal faculty profile, the presence of a dedicated leadership structure and teacher personnel indicates professional credentialing and mentorship roles.
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Present positive keywords: mentors, teachers, leadership, faculty. The materials describe teachers and leaders but do not deeply articulate a “living curriculum” or a formal faculty profile in the classical sense.
  - No explicit negative indicators in the public materials.
- Why this rating
  - The school demonstrates professional faculty presence and mentorship culture but does not explicitly present the “living curriculum” or highly formal classical-faculty descriptors found in some classical schools. The absence of those explicit classical-faculty signals places Element 5 at SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

### Element 6: School Parents and Community — CLASSICAL

- Evidence
  - The materials emphasize In Loco Parentis, ongoing parental partnership, and daily communication with parents; there are explicit statements about parental involvement and home-school alignment. 
  - There are documented policies on parental involvement (service hours), admissions, and campus visits; there is a public-facing Parent Handbook and policies designed to engage parents in formation and governance.  
  - The Step Up For Students program indicates public-facing openness to family support and parental access to school resources. 
- Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
  - Present positive keywords: partnership with parents, co-teaching, parental formation, open campus, parent formation; families engaged in home-school alignment; visits welcomed; volunteering and service hours.  
  - Negative indicators: None evident in public materials.
- Why this rating
  - The school demonstrates a robust and explicit commitment to parental involvement and community partnership, a hallmark of classical education models, leading to a CLASSICAL rating for Element 6.

- Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Rationale: The strongest signals are Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose/Goal, and Parents & Community, each clearly aligning with classical aims. The Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Faculty show foundational classical elements but are not consistently explicit across all grades and core practices (e.g., no consistent, explicit Great Books/Great Conversation emphasis, limited explicit Socratic pedagogy, and only partial articulation of a living, classical faculty framework). Taken together, the school demonstrates meaningful classical alignment but does not meet every criterion at full strength across all six categories.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly articulate and implement a comprehensive Great Books/Great Conversation framework across applicable grade levels, including a documented list of canonical works, discussion protocols, and assessment methods.
- Strengthen explicit pedagogy in logic, rhetoric, and dialectic across the upper grades, including documented Socratic seminars, debates, and structured rhetoric projects (speeches, orations, and persuasive writing) aligned with classical pedagogy.
- Codify and publicly publish a “Living Curriculum” portrait of the faculty, including teacher development in classical methods, ongoing teacher seminars on classical pedagogy, and evident alignment between teacher professional practice and classical aims.
- Expand explicit liturgical or communal practices in public communications (where appropriate) to reflect liturgical disciplines or formal communal prayer/music components as a regular facet of school life (if consistent with the school’s theology and governance structure).
- Increase explicit cross-curricular integration of the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with cross-referenced historical contexts and primary sources to strengthen the perception and practice of classical education beyond grammar-stage emphasis.
- Document and showcase a more explicit Great-Books-centric program in the school’s curriculum pages, including sample syllabi, assessment rubrics, and rationale for each core work.
