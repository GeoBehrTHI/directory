## Executive Summary

Coram Deo Academy (Kennewick, WA 99336) presents a clearly Christian, classical-oriented program anchored in the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with explicit mission, vision, and curricular framing that align with core classical-school indicators. Public materials emphasize Christ-centered formation, truth-goodness-beauty as real and knowable, and a disciplined, Gemeinschaft-oriented approach to learning. Notable strengths include a stated mission to partner with Christian families, a public articulation of the Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric progression, and visible evidence of school-wide efforts to integrate religious formation with intellectual formation (e.g., senior thesis in rhetoric, Shakespeare/Great-Books-style offerings, and a structured campus culture). Several elements that are commonly associated with a more robust classical program—explicit Great Books reading, an explicit, liturgical communal life, and a fully articulated Quadrivium or Scholé-centered pedagogy—are present but less explicit in the public-facing materials. Overall, the school lands between CLASSICAL and SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, leaning toward CLASSICAL due to its explicit Christian-theological framing, Trivium-focused curriculum, and strong partnerships with families, while showing room for fuller articulation of some classical markers (e.g., explicit reference to Great Books as a sustained curricular throughline beyond Shakespeare-based events, and broader liturgical practices).

- Aggregate rating: CLASSICAL
- Key takeaway: CDA demonstrates a coherent classical Christian orientation with a clearly enacted Trivium pathway, theology-integrated mission, and active parent/community engagement. The strongest opportunities to “move the needle” would involve more explicit articulation of a Great Books approach across disciplines, a broadened sense of liturgical/community practices, and clearer identification of upper-level pedagogical practices (e.g., Socratic seminars and formal rhetoric work) as a systemic, school-wide habit rather than primarily in limited program pages. However, the current public materials already reflect a robust classical-Christian formation ecology.
