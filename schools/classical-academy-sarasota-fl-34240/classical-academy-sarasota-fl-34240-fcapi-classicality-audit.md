## Executive Summary

The Classical Academy of Sarasota (TCA) presents a publicly accessible program and culture that is strongly aligned with classical education principles across all six rubric categories. Core elements such as the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric); a history-centered, canon-based curriculum; explicit cultivation of virtue and character; Socratic and rhetoric-informed pedagogy; a living, model-centered faculty ethos; and a proactive partnership with parents and the community are evident in the website content. The school’s motto Do the Good. Learn the True. Love the Beautiful. explicitly foregrounds truth, goodness, and beauty, which anchors Element 1 (Religious and Metaphysical Foundations) in a CLASSICAL orientation. The Seven Hills of TCA and Virtues & Habits pages reinforce a coherent classical mission focused on formation, virtue, and a communal life that includes parent engagement and small-community dynamics. The Lower and Upper School materials, Teaching the Timeline, and College/Career counseling pages collectively demonstrate curriculum breadth (including Latin, canonical literature, primary sources, and history in a Trivium-informed structure) as well as pedagogical practices that include memory work, chanting, and Socratic discussions. Across categories, positive keyword indicators (truth, virtue, Great Books, history, Latin, scholé, memory work, etc.) substantially outweigh negative indicators, with minimal reliance on contemporary “workforce-ready” framing or project-based/directed constructs that would signal a tension with classical aims. The aggregate rating is CLASSICAL.

Key evidentiary highlights:

- Motto invoking the transcendentals: truth, goodness, beauty (Do the Good. Learn the True. Love the Beautiful.)
- Canonical, history- and virtue-centered curriculum guided by Western classics; explicit emphasis on the Great Books/first-source documents and history as the backbone of learning (e.g., “first-source documents,” “great works of literature,” historical chronology across the trivium) ,
- Trivium-centric curriculum with Latin instruction starting early; history instruction tied to chronological study; emphasis on mastery and rhetoric in upper grades 
- Pedagogy includes singing/chanting and memory work in the lower grades; Socratic/seminar-style discourse and rhetorical training in upper grades ,
- Faculty as living curriculum and a culture of mentorship and virtuous exemplars; explicit statements about teacher roles and small-community mentoring
- Strong parent partnership orientation and visible parent-facing engagement (parent talks, book-study/formations, open campus ethos) ,

- Supporting lower- and middle-school pedagogy emphasizes wonder and reverence in learning, aligning with a metaphysical frame (wonder as a gateway to the transcendent in learning).

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

The absence of explicit theology texts or liturgical worship in the public-facing materials does not negate the metaphysical orientation evidenced by transcendentals and virtue-centric framing. Overall alignment is CLASSICAL.
