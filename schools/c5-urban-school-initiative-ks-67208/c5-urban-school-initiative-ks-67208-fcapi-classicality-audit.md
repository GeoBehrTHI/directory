## Executive Summary

C5 Urban School Initiative Kansas (C5 USI KS 67208) presents a clearly classical, Christian educational model anchored in the liberal arts, formation, and community partnerships. Public materials emphasize a Christian identity, the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric), the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty, and formation of character through communal practices, faculty as mentors, and robust parent/community engagement. The District framing positions Hope Academy Wichita as the prototype and outlines a district-wide, mission-aligned, church-linked network intended to scale classical Christian education across urban Kansas. Evidence from the crawl also shows explicit commitments to prayer, partnerships with families, and a philanthropy-enabled structure to support students and teachers.

Overall classification (aggregate): CLASSICAL

- Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): CLASSICAL
- Element 2 (Purpose & Goal): CLASSICAL
- Element 3 (Curriculum): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Element 5 (Faculty): CLASSICAL
- Element 6 (Parents & Community): CLASSICAL

The score pattern reflects a strong religious grounding, clear formation goals, and explicit parent/community partnerships. The curriculum and pedagogy sections show robust classical themes, but with less explicit detail on some high-school pedagogical practices (e.g., direct Socratic seminars) in the public-facing materials, which is why some aspects are designated somewhat classical in those categories. The school’s own materials also push toward scalable district-level implementation and funding models, which is well-aligned with classical aims but introduces organizational and financial scaffolds beyond the core pedagogical elements.

What would move the needle: If this school aims to reach full CLASSICAL in all six categories, the most impactful steps would be to explicitly foreground and document: (a) Socratic/seminal classical pedagogy in upper grades (explicit description of seminars, debates, and public speaking opportunities), (b) direct mention and integration of Great Books and Great Conversation into curriculum documentation, and (c) a public articulation of the lower- and middle-school contemplative/restful aspects alongside rigorous, Socratic elements to demonstrate a balanced schrödinger-like memory of scholé. Additionally, a public-facing “Living Curriculum” statement that details teacher formation as the living embodiment of the curriculum would further strengthen the categories on Faculty and Pedagogy.

- Core values and mission statements articulate a Christian life informed by theology, piety, and liturgical-like communal practices (e.g., prayer as a structural activity).

- Keywords not evident (negative): secularism, atheism, relativism, value-neutral ethics (no explicit presence in public materials).

## What Would Move the Needle

- Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly foreground explicit Great Books/Great Conversation language and evidence in curriculum documents (e.g., Great Books in reading lists, Great Conversation in seminar formats). This would strengthen the alignment with the classical curriculum rubric and push toward CLASSICAL rather than SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 
- Element 4 (Pedagogy): Provide a detailed public articulation of high-school pedagogical practices (explicit Socratic seminars, peer-led seminars, debates, public speaking opportunities) across all grades, including lower- and middle-school adaptations, to demonstrate a concrete continuum of schooling from K-12 aligned with classical pedagogy.
- Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a formal portrait or living-document description of the ideal teacher and student profile, including mentoring expectations, teacher development paths, and exemplar classrooms, to strengthen the “living curriculum” and “model teachers” criteria.

These needle-mover items would bolster explicit documentation and public-facing articulation of practices that currently exist in principle or within specific pages but are not always described in formal policy language. Evidence in the crawl already indicates strong alignment in many areas; formalizing documentation will push all six categories toward strong CLASSICAL.

- Evidence and citations: The district’s Christian identity and formation commitments are documented, including hiring of Christ-centered faculty and a mission-aligned staff, plus the Great-Book-like liberal arts framing. The public materials show explicit parent partnerships and a district/network model.
