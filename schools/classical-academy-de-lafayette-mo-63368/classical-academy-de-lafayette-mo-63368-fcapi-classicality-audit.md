## Executive Summary

Classical Academy De Lafayette Mo 63368 (Lafayette Academy) demonstrates a robust classical-education orientation across its public-facing materials. The school presents a mission rooted in forming virtuous character through a content-rich classical liberal-arts program, with explicit emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty, and the cultivation of wisdom. Its curriculum foregrounds the liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Latin), Great Books/readings from Western canon, and a rhetoric-focused upper school, all signaling a strong Classical alignment. While the school foregrounds formation, virtue, and the Great Books tradition, it shows less explicit, overt theology-led pedagogy or liturgical practices in public materials. Taken together, the school rates CLASSICAL overall, with Category-specific notes discussed below.

Key anchors across sources include: mission statements that tie academic rigor to virtue and true wisdom; explicit reference to reading Great Books and a “great conversation” approach; emphasis on the trivium/quadrivium through grammar and rhetoric, with a rhetoric stage in high school; and an emphasis on teacher leadership, low-tech classrooms, and parent engagement as part of a classical formation.   

Overall classification: CLASSICAL.

- What stands out: Consistent emphasis on virtue, truth, beauty, and goodness; great-books orientation; explicit rhetoric and logic/Latin through the grade levels; strong teacher-leadership and formation through a “living curriculum” ethos; active parent engagement through open houses, tours, and community events.

- What would move the needle (if needed): The public materials could further explicitly articulate theology-grounded integration, liturgical/community practices, and a more explicit articulation of scholé/restful-learning principles in lower and middle grades to strengthen the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations and Pedagogy categories. Notably, the current materials already include many of the core classical elements and strong formation language, which justifies the CLASSICAL classification at present. No major needle-moving changes are required at this time.
