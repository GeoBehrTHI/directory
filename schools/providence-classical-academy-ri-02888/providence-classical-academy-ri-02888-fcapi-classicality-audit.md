## Executive Summary

Providence Classical Academy (PCA) in Rhode Island presents a clearly recognisable classical Christian school model. Across public-facing materials, PCA emphasizes formation of character through a classical curriculum and pedagogy, explicit theological/themetical anchors, and a collaboration with parents as co-educators. The school's messaging repeatedly foregrounds truth, wisdom, virtue, and God’s Word as real and knowable, and it consistently ties academic aims to the formation of well-rounded, virtuous citizens rather than solely to workforce preparation. The public materials show concrete articulation of Trivium-based pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric), engagement with living books and Great Books-like study (including biblical texts as foundational “Great Books” in all subjects), liturgical and communal practices, and a collaborative, parent-inclusive model of schooling. Overall, PCA aligns strongly with the classical rubric, and it would be classified as CLASSICAL across all six rubric categories.

Key observations driving the overall CLASSICAL rating:
- Religious and Metaphysical Foundations are explicit and central (Bible-centered curriculum, Statement of Faith, worship, liturgical elements, and a theologically grounded worldview) .
- Purpose and Goal foreground formation, virtue, and wisdom (core virtues, Portrait of a Graduate, formation-oriented language) (evidence in mission and virtues materials) .
- Curriculum centers the Trivium, Great Books/primaries, and liberal arts integration with a strong emphasis on living books and biblical materials (grammar/logic/rhetoric, classical history/literature, and biblical texts) as core content .
- Pedagogy emphasizes contemplative, memory-based and liturgical elements in the lower grades, with Socratic/seminar-style and rhetorical work in the upper grades, consistent with classical pedagogy .
- Faculty messaging highlights Christian teachers, “living curriculum,” and a collaborative culture with explicit expectations about faith and scholarly formation .
- Parents & Community emphasis is pronounced, with explicit division of responsibilities between school and families, ongoing parent formation, and open-campus/community engagement, including training for co-teaching .
