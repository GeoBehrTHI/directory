## Executive Summary

Providence Academy (Providence Classical Christian Academy) in Saint Louis, MO 63125 presents a robust, textually explicit orientation to classical Christian education. Based on the public materials in its crawl, the school foregrounds a Christ-centered worldview, devotion to virtue and wisdom, engagement with great books and classical languages, and a deliberately formed pedagogy that includes both contemplative/memory-based practices and Socratic/seminar-style inquiry in upper grades. The school also emphasizes parental partnership, transparency about curriculum, and a public-facing articulation of its classical mission (often described as “classical” and “Christian”). Across all six classical-category dimensions, Providence demonstrates strong alignment with classical criteria, with some areas emphasizing formation, worship, and a Christian community rather than purely secular or job-focused aims.

Key evidentiary signals from Providence’s public materials include: explicit statements that Providence is “Christ-centered” and uses the classical model to equip students for wisdom and virtue; repeated references to great books, classical languages (Latin/Greek), and Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric); pedagogy that blends memory/chanting and recitation in the lower grades with Socratic discussion and rhetoric in the upper grades; a described living curriculum where faculty model virtue; and a stated partnership with parents as co-teachers and open access to curriculum through documents like the Providence Academic Guide. These signals come from multiple Providence pages, including Our History, A Classical School, The Classical Christian Alternative to Public Education, and related blog and faculty pages.

Because the six categories of the classical rubric are best grounded in the keyword indicators from the classical lexicon, Providence’s materials yield a nearly uniform CLASSICAL rating across all categories. The public signals emphasize truth, goodness, beauty through a Christian lens; the Trivium and Great Books; contemplative and Socratic pedagogy; a living, character-forming faculty; and a robust, ongoing parental partnership. There are few or no explicit negative indicators in the public text that would push any category toward NOT CLASSICAL; where some categories emphasize formation, faith, and community over strictly secular or job-oriented aims, those are consistent with classical education as defined in the traditional rubric.
