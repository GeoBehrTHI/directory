## Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL

Evidence

The model emphasizes faith and reason meeting in every class, and the catechetical content (Old/New Testament, Cathechism) is braided into the curriculum. The network materials describe an incarnational environment and liturgical elements such as Mass within the Catholic framework.  
- The site explicitly highlights the transcendentals (good, beautiful, true) as a curricular aim and uses Catholic theology as the backbone of formation. 

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: incarna­tional, Catholic, theology, Cathechism, Mass, liturgy, sacred, Incarnation, faith/reason, transcendentals (good/beautiful/true). 
- Negative keywords (relativism, secularism, etc.) are not evident in the public messaging. 

Why this rating
- The school’s public materials foreground a Catholic, incarnational metaphysical framework and treat theology and liturgy as integral to education, aligning strongly with the Positive indicators for Element 1 and showing a clear absence of the Negative indicators in public messaging. Verdict: CLASSICAL.

### Element 2: School Purpose and Goal — CLASSICAL

Evidence
- The school frames education as formation of virtue and wisdom, not merely preparation for work, with explicit emphasis on forming character and loves, and on “life” preparation beyond college admissions. The Three Pillars model (Intellect, Character, Spirituality) foregrounds formation. The materials emphasize that truth, goodness, and beauty are real and knowable, and that education is formation rather than information. The mission notes “formation” and “human flourishing,” and the program includes “the greater good and glory to God,” aligning with paideia and classical teleology.  

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords present: formation, virtue, wisdom, liberal education, classical mission, great conversation, telos, scholé, contemplation. 
- Negative keywords present: none explicit (the messaging tends toward formation and virtue rather than career/preparation-only language). The public messaging sometimes notes college preparation, but it is framed as part of a broader life formation rather than a sole aim. 

Why this rating
- The messaging strongly supports formation-focused aims, with virtuous-education language and a telos oriented toward truth/goodness/beauty and human flourishing. CLASSICAL.

Why this rating

### Element 6: School Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- There is clear evidence of parent involvement in admissions and community life (shadow days, parent questionnaires to teachers as part of admissions, family interviews, etc.). Public materials show parent-facing processes (Shadow Day, teacher questionnaires involving parents, records/shadow processes), and school-hosted events (Annual Gala) and donor materials. While these demonstrate community engagement, explicit public messaging about parents as co-teachers, parent formation programs, or open-campus partnerships is less explicit than in the strongest classical statements. The school does host community/donor events (Higher Purpose Gala) and admissions events that invite family participation.   

Positive/Negative keyword analysis
- Positive keywords found: partnership with families (not always phrased as such, but family involvement in admissions, shadow days), parent gatherings/donor events (Gala), open-house/admissions events; community engagement and hospitality in the event ecosystem.  
- Negative keywords present: minimal explicit indication of “parents as co-teachers” or formal family formation programs; the messaging tends to present families as participants in school life rather than co-educators in a formal sense. This aligns with the Negative indicators in the Six Elements lexicon (consumer language and minimal parent formation). The current messaging indicates more involvement in events and fundraising than explicit school-as-co-educator partnerships. 

Why this rating
- Evidence shows strong community events and parent-admission involvement, but fewer explicit, ongoing, structured parent-formation/co-teaching programs in public messaging. This warrants SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than full CLASSICAL for Element 6.
