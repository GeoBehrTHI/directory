## Executive Summary

Veritas Collegiate Academy VA 23320 presents a confident classical Christian posture in its public materials. The school clearly identifies itself as a classical Christian preparatory school and structures its program around the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) with grades K-12 implicit in its site navigation (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric tracks) and a stated mission to educate within a biblical, theologically grounded framework. Public content emphasizes formation of character within a Christian worldview, a strong sense of community, and a global-leadership orientation. Taken together, the school demonstrates a classical orientation with a measurable Christian foundation, yielding an aggregate assessment of SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than fully CLASSICAL across all six categories (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community). Evidence of explicit classical components is strongest in Religious Foundations and Curriculum (Trivium-focused structure), with less explicit articulation of some classical pedagogical methods, Great Books engagement, and a living-faculty narrative in public-facing materials. See category-by-category notes below for detailed keyword analysis and scoring rationale. Key sources include the school’s own site content describing its classical Christian identity and Trivium structure, historical statements about the school’s mission, and content referencing biblical worldview and spiritual life.

- Make Great Books and Great Conversation explicit: Add a clearly stated program or cadence for Great Books reading, primary sources, and a “Great Conversation” across grades (explicitly linking to canonical texts and primary sources). This would push Curriculum (Element 3) toward CLASSICAL.
- Elevate explicit Socratic pedagogy and scholé: Publicly document and demonstrate the use of Socratic seminars, memory work, and contemplative, restful learning (Scholé) in daily practice. This would raise Pedagogy (Element 4) and potentially Faculty (Element 5) if coupled with a living curriculum narrative.
- Strengthen the living curriculum narrative: Publish explicit statements about faculty as mentors, the “portrait of a teacher,” and the faculty as the core of the school’s living curriculum. This would lift Element 5 toward CLASSICAL.
- Expand liturgical and liturgical-life integration: If not already present, articulate formal liturgical practices, sacred music, and a defined liturgical calendar to strengthen Religious Foundations (Element 1) and tie to the school’s spiritual life.
- Deepen parental formation and co-teaching language: Explicitly position parents as co-educators and provide structured parent formation opportunities (reading groups, book studies, parent covenant, etc.) to elevate Element 6 to CLASSICAL. 
- Increase explicit historical/philosophical breadth in Curriculum: More explicit inclusion of Western civilization curricula, primary-source history, and cross-disciplinary integration (interdisciplinary curriculum) would reinforce the classical stance in Element 3.
