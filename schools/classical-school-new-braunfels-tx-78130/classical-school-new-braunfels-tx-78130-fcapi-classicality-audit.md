## Executive Summary

The Classical School of New Braunfels (The Classical School NB, TX 78130) presents a thoroughly classical, faith-informed program consistent with core classical education principles. Public materials (the school’s website content captured in the crawl) emphasize formation over information, a biblically grounded worldview, fidelity to great books, and a trinity of liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) as the spine of the curriculum. The school highlights a pedagogical structure that includes memory work and singing in lower grades, Socratic/logic-based work in later years, and rhetoric-oriented expression as students mature. They explicitly describe parent partnership in education, a living-faculty-as-formation approach, and a two-campus, home/classroom collaborative model.

Key signals aligning NB with classical criteria:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations anchored in a biblical worldview, Jesus-centered purpose, and God-centered education. Evidence: explicit Biblical worldview, Jesus-centered aims, integration of biblical framework across curriculum, and references to God’s rule in education.  
- Purpose and goals oriented toward formation, virtue, wisdom, and the love of truth, goodness, and beauty rather than solely career preparation. Evidence: formation-first language, virtue/wisdom emphasis, and references to truth, goodness, and beauty as real.  
- Curriculum built around grammar, logic, and rhetoric; explicit mention of Great Books and Latin; history integrated across subjects. Evidence: grammar/logic/rhetoric naming, Great Books references, Latin in early grades, and history-focused content across disciplines.  
- Pedagogy including memory work, singing, and contemplative elements in lower grades, plus Socratic/seminar-like elements and rhetoric development in upper grades. Evidence: lower-school singing/recitation; emphasis on open dialogue and critical thinking; Latin/logic/rhetoric progression; rhetoric opportunities in upper grades.  
- Faculty described as virtuous, mentoring, and forming the curriculum; living curriculum; emphasis on teacher formation, partnership, and character. Evidence: teacher partnership with co-teachers; teachers shaped by great books; living curriculum language present in materials. 
- Parents and community as co-teachers; strong emphasis on partnership with families and open access/engagement opportunities. Evidence: parent co-teachers, home/school partnership, and invitations for parent involvement.
