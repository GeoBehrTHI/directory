## Executive Summary

New Covenant Christian Academy (NCCA) in Hardin, KY presents a notably classical Christian framework across its public materials. The school foregrounds Christ-centered formation, biblical worldview, and the liberal-arts orientation, anchored by explicit references to classical pedagogy (grammar, logic, rhetoric), the Trivium, and elements of the Great Conversation. Public materials emphasize liturgical aspects (weekly chapel/worship), a partnership with families, and a structured progression from grammar through rhetoric (K-12), along with a culture that values memory work, recitation, singing, and Socratic-style inquiry at the upper levels. Taken together with explicit statements about being “Classically Informed” and about the liberal-arts curriculum, these cues align strongly with the Six Essential Elements framework for classical education. The school also demonstrates collaboration with parents and clear engagement with the community, further reinforcing the classical formation model.

Overall classification by category (based on keyword analysis against the Six Essential Elements keyword indicators) is CLASSICAL across all six categories. The school’s public materials consistently reflect transcendental aims (truth, goodness, beauty) through a biblically grounded, virtue-centered approach; curricular structure includes grammar, logic, and rhetoric; pedagogy includes memorization, chant, and Socratic discussion at appropriate levels; faculty and leadership are portrayed as mentors and living exemplars; and parents/community partnerships are foregrounded.

## What Would Move the Needle

Given the SCHOOL is classified CLASSICAL across all six categories, no “needle-moving” changes are required to reach a more classical status. If the aim were to further strengthen CLASSICAL alignment, possible enhancements could include:
- Expand explicit Great Books/Great Conversation commitments beyond implied canon references, with a published list of primary-sources and assigned readings across grade levels.
- Increase explicit articulation of the Seven Liberal Arts in curriculum documents, mapping each grade/year to Grammar-Logic-Rhetoric milestones and Quadrivium topics.
- Provide more documented evidence of the living-curriculum concept in faculty descriptions and governance materials (e.g., explicit statements showing teachers as mentors who embody classical aims).

However, these are additive rather than corrective steps given the current evidence.
