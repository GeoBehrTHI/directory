# Executive Summary

Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Arise Classical Academy (https://www.ariseclassicalacademy.com) presents strong textual evidence of a classical Christian identity: it explicitly centers education on Christ and Scripture, affirms the transcendentals of truth, goodness, and beauty, commits to formation (wisdom and virtue) over mere information, and names the trivium and quadrivium as curricular pillars. Pedagogically, the school references chants/singing in the grammar stage and rich discussion in upper grades, though it lacks explicit “Socratic”/“scholé” language. Faculty language is emerging but thin. Parent partnership and formation are notably strong via its University-Model structure and an EQUIP discipleship/parent training program.

Most impactful signals:
- Religious-metaphysical and purpose statements repeatedly invoke truth, goodness, beauty; Christ-centered integration across subjects; and worship practices (choir, hymns) as part of school life   .
- Curriculum affirmations include the trivium and quadrivium; Latin; Western heritage; and a classical text set (Humanitas, Lost Tools of Writing, Canon Press Logic, Apologia) .
- Pedagogical references include chants/songs (grammar stage) and rich discussion; choir/hymnody also featured, though “Socratic” and “scholé” terms are not yet used  .
- Strong parent partnership (parents as primary educators in a University-Model), with explicit parent formation (book clubs, worship, training) via EQUIP  .

Scoring framework and lexicon: All keyword analyses below reference the positive/negative indicator sets from Six Essential Elements of a Classical School and the classicality lexicon organized by the six elements (e.g., transcendental terms; theology/liturgy; formation; trivium/quadrivium; Socratic/chant/scholé; faculty-as-mentors; partnership with parents)    .

- Faculty formation: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” and a brief note on faculty classical formation (mentoring, great books seminars, collegial culture), matching the faculty indicators  .
- Great Books list: Consider a public “selected readings”/Great Books overview to emphasize primary-source engagement and the Great Conversation .
