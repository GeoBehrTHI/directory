## Executive Summary

Veritas Classical Academy in Marietta, Ohio (45750) presents a robust orientation toward classical education, with clear commitments to virtue formation, a liberal-arts curriculum, and a staged approach (grammar/logic/rhetoric). Public-facing materials emphasize truth, goodness, and beauty, along with a Christian/theological framing, a three-part curriculum (grammar-logic-rhetoric), and integrated moral formation. The school also foregrounds parental involvement (admissions process includes parent/board interviews) and a community-rich environment (house system, dress code, fine arts, and Character/Virtue emphasis).

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Rationale at a glance:
- Strengths align with classical ideals in Rel/Metaphysical Foundations and School Purpose/Goal (emphasis on truth, goodness, beauty; formation of character; liberty as civic virtue).
- Curriculum and pedagogy show clear classical scaffolding (grammar/logic/rhetoric; history and Western tradition; emphasis on virtue and memory through repetition and recitation motifs, though with limited explicit mention of classic Great-Books language on public materials).
- Faculty and parental/community signals exist (staff bios; parent-interaction through admissions; community features), but the materials are less explicit about a fully articulated “living curriculum” or deeply codified master-teacher portrait in the public narrative.
- Some pedagogical signals are not explicitly aligned with classic methods (e.g., Socratic seminars and scholé are not overtly described on the site), which drags the pedagogy rating toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL.

What would move the needle if the school wanted a fuller CLASSICAL rating is to explicitly foreground Great Books/Great Conversation, Socratic seminars or dialectic in upper grades, and explicit Scholé/restful learning language, plus more explicit articulation of teachers as living exemplars and a deeper parent-formation program on classical pedagogy.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

### Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

For example, the site describes Veritas as a private Christian classical school devoted to traditional learning and character development, with theological framing evident in mission language and references to virtue, truth, and beauty as goods. 
- The vision states education in the truest sense by fostering wonder and love for what is true, good, and beautiful, tying moral realism to educational aims. This aligns with transcendental/virtue-oriented foundations. 
- Reference to God’s endowment and formation of character appears in leadership messaging (Headmaster letters and mission statements). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: truth, goodness, beauty; virtue; theology; Christian formation; worship/worship-like community signals (though explicit liturgical practices are not foregrounded in the site’s public content).
- Negative keywords: Not evident (no explicit signals of secular relativism or value-neutral ethics in the public materials).

Why this rating
- The school clearly anchors itself in a theologically oriented, virtue-formation frame and uses the transcendental triad (truth, goodness, beauty) as a core educational aim, consistent with Classical lexicon expectations. The absence of explicit liturgical practice but strong theology and moral formation arguments still supports CLASSICAL-leaning foundations; however, explicit liturgical practices or a more formal “the Mistress Science” theology integration across disciplines would push this to CLASSICAL. Given public materials, the current read is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, leaning toward CLASSICAL in intent but not uniformly explicit in all liturgical/metaphysical signals.

### Element 3: Curriculum — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence

The “Academic Curriculum” page emphasizes liberal-arts education, with references to classical education and stages. 
- The site highlights fine arts, current extracurriculars, and a reading of Western tradition via references to great minds and classical canon in ethos (Socrates, Euclid, Shakespeare, etc.). This aligns with Great Books/Great Conversation orientation implicitly in public messaging (great western works are referenced in notable figures). 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar, logic, rhetoric; liberal arts; history (Western civilization); great books/great conversation (implied through mention of Western greats and classical aims); wonder; curriculum integration across disciplines; classical pedagogy references (even if some are implicit).
- Negative keywords: The Montessori element in early education is not inherently classical and could be seen as divergent if interpreted strictly; the site does not consistently foreground explicit Great Books titles or an overt “Great Conversation” program in the public content, though the tone and references strongly lean classical. There is partial ambiguity about a full Great Books program in public materials.

Why this rating
- The core Trivium structure and historically oriented framing are present, meeting key curriculum elements. However, explicit public signaling of a formal Great Books program and a fully integrated Great Conversation approach is less overt in the public-facing content, and Montessori in early years may temper the strict classical curriculum signal for some evaluators. This yields SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rather than CLASSICAL. 

### Element 4: Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

Evidence
- Public materials emphasize a staged curriculum (grammar/logic/rhetoric), which implies a structured pedagogy aligned with classical aims. The rhetoric stage and senior thesis concept are described in the curriculum. 
- The site stresses virtue formation and a disciplined, memory-oriented approach through public-facing descriptions such as dress code, house system, and long-standing traditions. This signals a traditional, classroom-centered pedagogy rather than a solely student-led or project-based model. 
- There is no explicit, detailed public description of Socratic seminars, scholé (restful learning), or direct recitation/memorization as a formal pedagogy for all grades, though the Trivium framework and “formation through imitation and practice” language exist in the knowledge-base lexicon. The lack of explicit, public, classroom-level signals for Socratic/dialogic pedagogy and scholé reduces the rating from CLASSICAL to SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL. 

Positive/Negative Keyword Analysis
- Positive keywords present: grammar/logic/rhetoric; rhetorical training in rhetoric stage; senior thesis defense; disciplined learning; memory work signals (via classical pedagogy vocabulary in the knowledge-base lexicon, though not explicitly described on-site as a daily method).
- Negative keywords/signals: lack of explicit Socratic seminars; absence of explicit scholé/restful learning language in public content; limited direct mention of chanting, dictation, or memorization as formal pedagogy in public pages.

Why this rating
- The curriculum structure and emphasis on the Trivium are clear, but explicit, publicly stated pedagogical practices like Socratic dialogue, scholé, and chant/memory work are not prominently described in the school’s public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL rating for Pedagogy.
