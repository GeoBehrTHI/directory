## Executive Summary

Yellowstone Schools TX 77004 presents as a hybrid private-Christian and public-charter model operating on a single campus in Houston’s Third Ward. Public materials emphasize a faith-informed, formation-oriented approach with a strong emphasis on moral development, character formation, and college-preparatory opportunities (e.g., dual enrollment and early college credits). The website highlights small class sizes, family partnerships, and a mission to prepare students for life beyond high school, including college and career pathways. While the school foregrounds formation, virtue, and faith, explicit Great Books, classical grammar-logic-rhetoric curriculum, and SrOSc-based Socratic pedagogy are not clearly documented on the site. Taken together, Yellowstone demonstrates meaningful classical-adjacent signals (formation, virtue, theology, community partnership) but lacks consistent, explicit articulation of core classical curriculum and pedagogy beyond formation and college-prep framing.

Overall classification (aggregate): SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- Core strengths (signals of classical orientation): explicit theology and moral formation; formation-focused mission; partnership with families; emphasis on small class sizes and teacher dedication; aspiration to prepare students for college and life beyond high school.
- Gaps relative to a fully classical model: absence of clearly stated Great Books/Great Conversation reading programs, explicit Trivium-based curriculum (grammar-logic-rhetoric), and explicit Socratic pedagogy in upper grades within public-facing materials; limited public mentions of liturgical practices or formal catechesis in the classroom context.

What would move the needle: to shift toward CLASSICAL, Yellowstone could articulate and expand explicit classical elements such as a described Great Books program and a Trivium-based curriculum, embed Socratic seminars in upper grades, highlight a more explicit “living curriculum” approach by faculty, and more clearly connect liturgical or sacred-text-based inquiry across disciplines. Strengthening parent education and public-facing documentation of these elements would also advance perceived classical alignment.
