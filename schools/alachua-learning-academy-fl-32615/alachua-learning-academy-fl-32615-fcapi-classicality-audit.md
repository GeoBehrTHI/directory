## Executive Summary

Alachua Learning Academy (ALA) presents as a tuition-free public charter K–8 school with a clearly stated emphasis on a nurturing, values-based, character-formation approach within a small-school setting. Public materials emphasize virtues via the school’s H.E.A.R.T. framework, a family-oriented environment, personalized, hands-on programs, and strong academic performance. However, the site does not explicitly articulate the classical-tradition markers most central to traditional Classical Education (e.g., liberal-arts curriculum built on the Trivium/Quadrivium, Great Books, Socratic pedagogy, and liturgical or theological formation). Public materials describe advanced coursework and experiential programs, but there is limited explicit reference to the canonical Great Books tradition, direct Socratic seminars in upper grades, or a history-centered, literature-rich curriculum typical of a fully Classical program.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

Key insights:
- Strengths aligned with Classical sensibilities in formation, virtue, and a focus on humane education (moral character, lifelong learning; values-based life). Evidence: statements about character formation and the small-family environment, plus a family-centered approach and emphasis on virtues (H.E.A.R.T.). 
- Evidence of classical curriculum and pedagogy is present but not explicit or comprehensive (no clear Great Books emphasis, no explicit Trivium/Quadrivium framing, few direct references to Scholé or Socratic seminars in the public materials). Evidence of curriculum includes core subjects, hands-on and experiential learning, and a program like ACCEL, but not the classical lexicon. 
- Religious/metaphysical foundations are not present; the school states it is nonsectarian. This challenges the Religious & Metaphysical Foundations criteria in the classical rubric, though it aligns with some secular approaches to classical education. 
- Parent and community engagement is present (families reported as valuing the school, and there are enrollment options and family involvement themes), but explicit “co-teacher” partnership language is less obvious in public materials.

Aggregate assessment
- Based on keyword signals and explicit statements in the six categories, the school exhibits partial alignment with classical education (formation, virtue, small community). However, it does not consistently demonstrate core Classical Education hallmarks (Great Books, Trivium/Quadrivium, Scholé pedagogy, liturgical/theological foundations, explicit classical teacher portraits). Therefore, the aggregate rating is SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

What Would Move the Needle

- Explicitly articulate a Great Books/Great Conversation component within the curriculum and clearly connect it to a liberal-arts framework (Grammar–Logic–Rhetoric progression) across K–8 with planned teacher-led seminars.
- Introduce formal Socratic pedagogy in the upper grades (Socratic seminars, dialectic discussions) and a clear pathway from K–8 that transitions to a high-school classical program.
- Adopt explicit Scholé/restful-learning practices as a daily or weekly rhythm (quiet study, contemplative reading periods, and structured memory work) with documentation in the curriculum guide.
- Develop a formal “Living Curriculum” portrait of the teacher and a “portrait of a student” aligned with classical ideals, including ongoing teacher training in classical pedagogy (logical reasoning, classical rhetoric, and memorization).
- Expand the curriculum to include the liberal arts core (grammar, logic, rhetoric) along with a study of Western civilization/history from a classical perspective and the canon of classical authors (with opportunities for Latin/Greek exposure as appropriate).
- Strengthen explicit religious or metaphysical foundations (if chosen) in a nonsectarian way (e.g., a shared language around truth, goodness, beauty that respects pluralism) or, if remaining nonsectarian, explicitly articulate how classical aims can be pursued within a pluralistic framework without theological instruction.
- Increase explicit parent-education programming that positions parents as co-teachers and aligns family life with classical formation (reading groups, seminars, family liturgies if aligned with the school’s values).
