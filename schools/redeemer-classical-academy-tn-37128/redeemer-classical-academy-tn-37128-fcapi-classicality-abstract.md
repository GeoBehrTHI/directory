## ABSTRACT

Redeemer Classical Academy (RCA) TN 37128 demonstrates strong alignment with classical education principles, anchored in a Christ-centered, liturgical framework, the grammar–logic–rhetoric Trivium, extensive Great Books study, and Latin/argumentation-focused coursework. Public materials emphasize virtue formation, theology, weekly chapel, and a collaborative approach with parents, with explicit signals of classical curriculum (grammar–logic–rhetoric; Omnibus/Great Books; Latin; Senior Thesis). Overall, RCA is CLASSICAL, with Element 4 (Pedagogy) and Element 5 (Faculty) showing SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL signaling due to less explicit articulation of Socratic pedagogy and a formal living-curriculum philosophy, respectively. Element 1, Element 2, Element 3, and Element 6 show clear CLASSICAL alignment, while Element 4 and Element 5 show partial signals.

- Element 1: CLASSICAL – Evidence of theology, liturgy (weekly chapel), Christ-centered mission, and virtue formation; strong positive signals for transcendental and communal rootedness. 
- Element 2: CLASSICAL – Formation and virtue-centered aims, Great Books engagement, and a teleology toward wisdom and truth. 
- Element 3: CLASSICAL – Trivium-informed curriculum with grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books canon; Latin; Omnibus program; senior rhetoric work. 
- Element 4: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL – Strong classical content, but explicit Socratic/Paideia-style pedagogy is not clearly documented in public materials. 
- Element 5: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL – Qualified faculty and collaborative culture are evident, but explicit public signaling of the faculty as the “living curriculum” or a formal archetype is not consistently present. 
- Element 6: CLASSICAL – Strong parent partnership signals (parents as co-teachers, home formation, opportunities for involvement). 

Overall, RCA’s public-facing materials align strongly with Classical education norms, with concrete evidence across curriculum, Great Books, theology, and parental partnership. The most pronounced growth opportunities lie in making pedagogical methods (Socratic/seminal seminars) and the faculty-as-living-curriculum concepts more explicit in public communications. The aggregate assessment remains CLASSICAL.
