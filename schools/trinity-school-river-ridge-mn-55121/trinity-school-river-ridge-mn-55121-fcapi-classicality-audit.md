## Executive Summary

Trinity School at River Ridge, MN (River Ridge campus) demonstrates a cohesive classical Christian framework across religious foundations, purpose, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty, and parent/community engagement. Public materials consistently describe the school as offering a rigorous classical Christian education, grounded in a Christian theological vocabulary, with explicit attention to formation of character, virtue, and a long-standing liberal-arts orientation. The school highlights engagement with great texts, a humane-letters oriented curriculum, and governance by a faculty trained in classical pedagogy. There are clear signals of liturgical and communal practices (e.g., Nine Lessons and Carols), a strong parent community, and broad family involvement. By most measures aligned to the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric, Trinity River Ridge qualifies as CLASSICAL.

Key drivers include: explicit Christian/theological framing (Nicene Creed; People of Praise origin), formation-focused language (truth, goodness, beauty; formation of character; serving God and neighbor), a curriculum featuring humane letters, literature, and great-text study (e.g., humane letters seminar; interviews with faculty), and pedagogical signals consistent with scholé, Socratic inquiry in upper levels, and a living-teacher model. Public evidence also shows robust faculty engagement, accreditation and recognition, and a broad program emphasizing the liberal arts within a historically grounded tradition. - Liturgical and communal practices surface through events such as A Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, indicating liturgical life as part of the educational experience.

- The materials consistently affirm a transcendental, theologically grounded orientation (truth/goodness/beauty; Christian formation; liturgical life). The explicit naming of Nicene Creed and People of Praise roots, plus liturgical events, are strong markers of the religious/metaphysical foundation the rubric expects. This aligns squarely with CLASSICAL.

### Element 3: School Curriculum — CLASSICAL

- The site emphasizes a liberal-arts cadence (grammar/logic/rhetoric in later grades implied by the classical framework; humane-letters emphasis; college prep). Some of these curricular details appear in the “Humane Letters Seminar” and “Humane Letters” references across the curriculum pages.

Why this rating

### Element 4: School Pedagogy or Teaching Methods — CLASSICAL

Evidence

- Pedagogical signals align with Scholé/restful learning and Socratic/seminar-type engagement in upper levels, along with opportunities for rhetoric and oration. The Mortimer Adler/Paideia reference on the Academics page signals a structured, dialogic, teacher-guided approach at core.
- The 12th-grade Humane Letters Seminar indicates upper-level engagement with texts through discussion and analysis, consistent with Socratic/dialogic pedagogy and seminar-based learning.
- Lower-division pedagogy is described as deeply engaged with primary texts and original documents, with a model of teachers guiding learning and fostering a loving memory of core texts, which aligns with chanting/recitation memory work and disciplined discussion. While explicit lower-element chanting is not shown in surface materials, the emphasis on Latin/Western canonical memory and memorization-style pedagogy is consistent with the classical approach described in the lexicon.

Positive/Negative keyword analysis

- Positive keywords observed: Socratic dialogue/seminar; Dialectic; Scholé; memorization/recitation (memory work, recitation, chant); formation through imitation and practice; seminar/tutorial style; great conversation; guided inquiry.
- Negative keywords: The public materials do not emphasize project-based learning as the sole pedagogy; however, there is a broad-curriculum with integrated humanities—no explicit signals of dismissing Socratic, restfulness, or memory-based pedagogy. The explicit references to Paideia and humane-letters seminars align with classical pedagogy rather than non-classical methods.

Why this rating

- The combination of upper-level Socratic/seminar work, Paideia-style pedagogy references, and humane-letters lower/upper-level pedagogy signals strong alignment with CLASSICAL pedagogy.

Citations

- Mortimer Adler/Paideia framing: Mortimer Adler quote on Paideia approach.
- Humane Letters Seminar (rhetorical/dialogic practice): 12th-grade humanities seminar.
