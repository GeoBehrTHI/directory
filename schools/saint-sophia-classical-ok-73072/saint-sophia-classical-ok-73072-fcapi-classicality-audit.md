## Executive Summary

Saint Sophia Classical Academy (Norman, OK 73072) presents a clearly classical orientation anchored in Christian theological formation, a articulated commitment to virtue and wisdom, and a deliberately liberal-arts–minded curriculum. Across materials drawn from the school’s site,Memoria Press curriculum references, and public-facing statements, the school emphasizes formation over mere information, the study of Latin and classical languages, engagement with the Great Books/Great Conversation, and a pedagogy that includes Socratic discussion in the upper grades and traditional, teacher-led instruction in the lower grades. The religious foundations are explicit, with Gospel-centered partnership with families, Christian/Orthodox framing, and adherence to creedal statements. Public materials also emphasize small class sizes, a professional faculty, accreditation, and parental engagement through events and campus visits.

Overall classification by category:

This results in an aggregate CLASSICAL assessment for the school, with a single category (Faculty) identified as somewhat less explicitly aligned to the full classical rubric as defined by the Six Essential Elements and its Positive/Negative Keyword indicators. The “needle” opportunities center on making the living-faculty dimension and explicit Great-Books/Great-Conversation expressions more visible, and aligning faculty portraiture and formation language more directly with classical norms.

What would move the needle (applies because one category is deemed SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL):
- Make the living-curriculum concept and a concrete “portrait of a classical teacher” explicit on the site (to align with the Lexicon’s Faculty indicators).
- Strengthen explicit articulation of Great Books, Great Conversation, and a clearly stated plan for Socratic study in upper grades (to reinforce Curriculum and Pedagogy alignment).
- Expand parent-formation activities and explicit “parents as co-educators” language with visible offerings (book studies, seminars) to fully realize the Parents & Community indicators.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 5 (Faculty) to reach CLASSICAL: Make explicit the living-curriculum concept in public materials and provide a formal “portrait of the teacher” and “core teacher attributes” that align with classical expectations (e.g., model virtue, mastery of the liberal arts, mentorship role).
- For Element 3 (Curriculum) and Element 4 (Pedagogy): Add explicit, public-facing Great-Books lists and a clearly stated plan for regular Socratic seminars in specific grade bands, with sample seminar prompts and assessment rubrics to demonstrate how the Great Conversation is integrated across the curriculum.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand and publicize formal parent-education offerings (book studies, lectures, and workshops) that align with classical formation and provide more explicit language about parents as co-teachers beyond open-campus events.
- For Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Maintain and deepen explicit liturgical/communal practices and ensure ongoing articulation of theological foundations (e.g., creedal statements) within the broader curriculum to further align with liturgical and communal dimensions.

These changes would help move the school from SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in Faculty toward CLASSICAL across that dimension, and would further deepen the alignment in Curriculum and Pedagogy with the Great-Books-centered, contemplative, and virtuous formation ethos.
