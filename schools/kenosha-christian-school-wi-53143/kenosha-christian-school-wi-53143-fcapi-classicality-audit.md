## Executive Summary

Kenosha Christian School (KCA) presents a Christian, relationally-centered approach to education with clear religious foundations and a community-oriented ethos. Based on public-facing materials from the school and applying the Six Essential Elements rubric (as captured in the classical education knowledge lexicon), the school demonstrates a recognizable Christian orientation and some classical features, but lacks explicit, orthogonal evidence of a fully classical curriculum and pedagogy (e.g., explicit Seven Liberal Arts, Great Books/Great Conversation, or sustained Socratic pedagogy across grades). Overall, the school is best described as SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, with a strong religious and community formation component and room to deepen classical elements in curriculum and pedagogy.

Executive Summary — Element-by-Element Snapshot

- Element 1: Religious and Metaphysical Foundations — CLASSICAL
  Evidence: Public materials emphasize a Christian framework with “Spiritual Vibrancy” and “God at the center,” signaling a theologically grounded, transcendental orientation. The lexicon for classical education identifies theology, liturgy, worship, and sacred orientation as core signals of classical religious foundations. 

- Element 2: Purpose and Goal — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The site foregrounds relational formation, community and spiritual aims, and the idea of educating for a larger good (church/community) beyond mere information. While not explicit in terms like “virtue/wisdom” in the public text, formation and affections are suggested through relational language and spiritual aims. Positive signals exist in the framework of formation and teleology, but explicit statements about virtue/wisdom are not foregrounded in the site content. 

- Element 3: Curriculum — NOT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The site materials emphasize relational, spiritual, and academic excellence without explicit mention of liberal arts core components (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books; Great Conversation; history as Western civilization; Latin/Greek) that define a classical curriculum. The classical lexicon includes those curriculum signals; they are not visibly foregrounded in KCA’s public materials. 

- Element 4: Pedagogy — NOT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: Pedagogical signals in KCA’s public content center on individualized interaction and team-building, with no explicit evidence of Socratic seminars, rigorous rhetoric training, or Scholé-based contemplative pedagogy in the text. The classical pedagogy signals emphasize Socratic dialogue, memorization, and rest/Scholé; these are not foregrounded in the available site content. 

- Element 5: Faculty — NOT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The public materials do not present a fully developed “living curriculum” or explicit faculty portraits as described in classical rubrics. There is a focus on relationship and mentorship in a general sense, but specific classical faculty signaling (model teachers, living curriculum, professional collegiality as explicit “portrait of a teacher”) is not clearly evidenced in the site text. 

- Element 6: Parents & Community — SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
  Evidence: The school emphasizes family orientation and relational partnering (“KCA is a family”) and community engagement, which aligns with the classical emphasis on partnership with families and formation of affections. However, explicit parent formation programs, open campus practices, or formalized “co-teaching with parents” language are not clearly enumerated in the public text. Positive signals exist, but the strongest explicit classical signals (e.g., formal parent formation, households catechesis) are not fully demonstrated in the public materials. 

Overall Aggregate Ranking: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL

- The school clearly embeds Christian religious foundations and community formation, which are central to classical education for many families. However, the explicit, canonical classical elements (Great Books, Seven Liberal Arts, rigorous Socratic pedagogy, living faculty as curriculum, formalized parent formation) are not consistently foregrounded in public materials. This yields a SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL overall assessment.

What would move the needle

- Make Great Books and Great Conversation explicit: Publicly articulate a liberal arts foundation (grammar/logic/rhetoric, Trivium/Quadrivium, Western canon, primary sources) and show how they integrate into history, literature, and philosophy units.
- Adopt and publicize classical pedagogy signals: Implement and showcase Socratic seminars, memoria/study practices (recitation/memory work), and opportunities for upper-school rhetoric and oratory; document a Scholé-based rhythm (deliberate, contemplative pace) throughout the program.
- Clarify and publish a formal "formation" framework: Explicitly map virtue formation, moral realism, and the pursuit of truth/goodness/beauty, with measurable signals (character education, ethical reasoning, mentorship models, and living-curriculum portraits of teachers).
- Expand faculty signals: Provide a clear "portrait of a teacher" and a "faculty living curriculum" narrative, including ongoing classical pedagogy training and faculty collaboration.
- Strengthen parent/community alignment: Publicly present a robust parent-formation program (book studies, seminars, family catechesis), plus open-campus visits and partnerships with families as co-educators.
