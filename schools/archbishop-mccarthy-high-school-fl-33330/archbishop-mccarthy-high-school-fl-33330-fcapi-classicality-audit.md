## Executive Summary

Archbishop Edward A. McCarthy High School (Southwest Ranches, FL, 33330) presents a clearly Catholic, formation-centered program with strong religious anchor points (Mass, liturgy, campus ministry, theology, sacraments) and a robust college-preparatory framework (AP courses, college advisement, Naviance, 1:1 technology). Public materials emphasize Catholic identity, character formation, and a faith-infused campus culture, alongside modern amenities (Apple Distinguished School designation, 1:1 iPad program, advanced facilities). However, the materials largely lack explicit Great Books, liberal arts Trivium/Quadrivium pedagogy, or explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy typical of classical models. The school thus sits firmly within a Catholic, college-prep tradition with some elements aligned to classical aims (formation, virtue, reverence, community), but does not fully meet the core curricular/pedagogical levers of classical education as defined by the Six Essential Elements rubric.

Overall classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL.

- Strengths: Strong religious/metaphysical foundations; explicit formation goals; faculty depicted as experienced and devoted; robust Catholic identity; active parent/community engagement; 1:1 technology and college-advisement infrastructure.

- N needles to move toward full CLASSICAL: Integrate explicit liberal arts elements (Great Books, grammar/logic/rhetoric in a defined Trivium framework), introduce Socratic pedagogy more consistently (especially in upper grades), and articulate a clearer Great Conversation orientation within the curriculum.

- What would move the needle: (1) Add a clearly defined classical core (grammar-logic-rhetoric, great books selections, classical history strands) and a graded Socratic/seminar program from middle school through high school; (2) incorporate more contemplative Scholé-minded days and memory/chant practices in line with classical pedagogy; (3) weave explicit language learning (Latin/Greek) and primary-source study into core courses; (4) deepen explicit Great Books reading across curricula; (5) maintain and expand strong Catholic identity while foregrounding classical educational aims in communications with parents.

Executive summary category-by-category snapshots and the detailed keyword analyses follow, grounded in the public materials and aligned to the Six Essential Elements.

## What Would Move the Needle

- Curricular enrichment: Introduce a clearly articulated classical core (grammar-logic-rhetoric) and a defined Great Books program with a fall-spring reading sequence and accompanying seminars from middle school through high school.
- Pedagogical shift: Implement regular Socratic seminars, memory-work traditions, and chant/narration practices where appropriate to cultivate the classical discipline of mind and memory.
- Language and primary sources: Integrate Latin (or Greek) as a core language offering at least in foundational levels, with primary-source history/literature to support a “Great Conversation” approach.
- History and humanities emphasis: Reframe history as a Western civilization-centered liberal arts discipline with primary-source readings and interpretive writing; clearly distinguish history from social studies.
- Explicit articulation of telos: Emphasize the classical telos (true, good, beautiful) as an educational aim beyond college-prep outcomes in mission statements and program documentation.
- Faculty development: Expand formal classical pedagogy training and create living-syllabus exemplars for teachers, plus faculty seminars on classical teaching methods.
- Communication with families: Increase explicit, public-facing documentation of the classical elements (e.g., a classical-aim policy, curriculum maps, and reading lists) to help parents understand how classical education is integrated with the Catholic mission.
