## Executive Summary

Mineral Christian School (MCS) in Mineral, Virginia presents a predominantly CLASSICAL profile across the school’s public materials and mission language. The site foregrounds a Classical Christian formation that centers on truth, goodness, and beauty, grounded in Christian theology and biblical horizons. Evidence includes explicit statements about being a Classical Christian School, alignment with Mineral Baptist Church, and language emphasizing wisdom, worship, and wonder (pursuit of wisdom and a biblical worldview) along with strong liberal-arts content (grammar, logic, rhetoric; Great Books; Latin). The Great Books emphasis is explicit, and the curriculum highlights the liberal arts as foundational, including structured memory work and liturgical-influenced practices. While core classical elements (grammar/logic/rhetoric; Great Books; Latin) are clearly present, more explicit pedagogical modalities commonly associated with classical schools (such as Socratic seminars across the upper grades or Scholé/meditative pedagogy described in detail) are less directly documented on the site. Taken together, the school’s materials place it firmly in CLASSICAL, with some elements more robustly evidenced than others (notably Element 4 and Element 5 show partial alignment).

Key evidence points include:
- The school identifies itself as a Classical Christian School and as a ministry of Mineral Baptist Church, signaling a theological foundation embedded in the school’s life. 
- Public statements emphasize Christ-centered formation, worship, wisdom, and wonder. 
- The Great Books and Liberal Arts framework is explicit (Great Books, Liberating Arts, grammar/logic/rhetoric, Latin).  
- Evidence of religious-liturgical orientation appears via statements about Biblical worldview and worship-oriented language in teacher profiles and school materials.

## What Would Move the Needle

- For a school already classified as CLASSICAL, targeted enhancements could further strengthen the Pedagogy and Faculty dimensions (Categories 4 and 5) toward full classical alignment:
  - Provide explicit, public documentation of Socratic or Paideia-style pedagogy across upper grades (Element 4), including sample seminars, rubrics, and exemplars of student-led inquiry and dialectic work.
  - Expand public-facing faculty profiles to articulate a living-curriculum approach (Element 5), including explicit statements about teacher as mentor, collaboration among faculty, and ongoing classical teacher training or seminars.
  - More explicit articulation of the historical breadth of the curriculum, with direct references to primary sources, sacred texts, and canonical histories beyond liturgical/worship language (Element 3).
