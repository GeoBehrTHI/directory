# Classicality Audit: Artesia Classical Academy

School URL audited: https://artesiaclassicalacademy.org/

## Executive Summary
Overall classification: CLASSICAL

Artesia Classical Academy presents as a distinctly classical Christian school. The website explicitly anchors learning in a Christian worldview across all subjects and asserts moral and metaphysical claims about truth and the absence of neutrality. It highlights the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Latin, great works of Western civilization, integrated studies, and Socratic/debate-centered pedagogy, with memory work via songs and chants in the lower grades. The school also emphasizes partnership with parents and provides parent-facing formation resources. Two areas show thinner evidence: explicit articulation of the school’s philosophical goal using the hallmark “truth, goodness, beauty/virtue” language, and an explicit faculty philosophy describing teachers as mentors and moral exemplars.

Key strengths:
- Clear theological integration throughout the curriculum with explicit “no neutrality” language and “biblical truth” claims and Christ-centered framing .
- Robust curriculum markers of classical education: Trivium, Latin, Western Civilization, great books, integration, and Socratic/discussion pedagogy with debate and memory work via “songs, chants, and movement” fileciteturn1file6turn1file2turn1file3.
- Partnering with parents and promoting parent formation through recommended readings and explicit “In Partnership with Parents” language .

Primary gaps to strengthen:
- Make the telos of education more explicit in classical terms: “cultivation of wisdom and virtue,” and the transcendentals “truth, goodness, beauty,” while avoiding college/career-first framing that appears on one page (standardized tests; college and vocation success) .
- Publish a succinct “portrait of a teacher”/faculty philosophy highlighting teachers as mentors and models of virtue.

## What would move the needle
Because some categories scored SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL, the following concrete updates would likely elevate those areas:

- Purpose/Goal: Add a concise mission statement that foregrounds “the cultivation of wisdom and virtue” and the pursuit of “truth, goodness, and beauty” as real and knowable ends of education. Reduce or context-balance references to standardized testing and college/career outcomes so they are clearly subordinate to formation aims (aligned to Six Elements positive indicators) fileciteturn1file10turn1file5.
- Faculty: Publish a “Portrait of a Teacher” outlining teachers as mentors and models of virtue who embody prudence, justice, temperance, fortitude, and intellectual humility; include notes on collegiality/faculty formation (reading seminars, great books for faculty) .
- Religious/Liturgical life: If applicable, add references to communal worship rhythms (e.g., morning prayer/chapel, hymnody) to make the liturgical dimension visible in public materials (a positive signal in the Six Elements lexicon) .
- Pedagogy: Consider articulating the scholé/restful learning dimension and “wonder” explicitly (e.g., language about contemplation, delight, and unhurried depth) .
