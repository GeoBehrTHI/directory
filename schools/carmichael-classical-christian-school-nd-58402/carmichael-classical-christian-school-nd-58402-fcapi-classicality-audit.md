## Executive Summary

Carmichael Classical Christian School (Jamestown, ND 58402) exhibits a classical orientation, anchored in Christian formation and partnership with families. Public materials show explicit religious framing (Christian mission, love of God) and a commitment to classical-curriculum providers (Memoria Press via CLSA) and to membership in classical associations (ACCS). There is clear planning for a K–12 institution with a staged launch ( fall 2026 in Jamestown) and a small-family, church-linked start-up model (seven families currently meeting in a local church; growing intent to reach ~80 students with 14 staff). However, the site provides limited detail on specific classical pedagogy (e.g., Great Books, Socratic seminars, or explicit development of grammar-logic-rhetoric in the literature), and it lacks explicit statements about certain hallmark practices (e.g., liturgical/communal practices, a robust “living curriculum” portrait of teachers, or concrete parent-education programs). Taken together, these factors place CCCS in the "Somewhat Classical" range overall, with strong religious formation and community-anchored support but incomplete articulation of some core classical elements in public-facing materials.

- Aggregate classification: SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL
- Main strengths: explicit Christian formation; parent partnership; utilization of established classical curricula (Memoria Press) and associations (ACCS, CLSA); clear formation-oriented aim.
- Gaps to move toward CLASSICAL: explicit Great Books/Great Conversation framing; explicit Socratic/seminar pedagogy in upper grades; liturgical/community practices; explicit portrait of teachers as the living curriculum; documented parent-education initiatives.

Evidence sources include CCCS public materials describing mission, governance, curriculum partnerships, and growth plans, as well as the knowledge-base rubric and keyword framework for classical education. See citations for specific claims below.

- Evidence: Our Mission explicitly centers on loving God and partnering with parents. 
- Evidence: The school publicly states it uses Memoria Press curriculum supplied via CLSA and plans CLSA accreditation, with a launch plan for K–12 in Jamestown by 2026, and a current structure of seven homeschooling families. 
- Evidence: The school is a member of ACCS and CLSA, indicating alignment with classical-education networks. 
- Evidence: The mission and outreach materials emphasize partnership with parents and a community-based model (home-school families meeting at a church).

What Would Move the Needle

- For Element 1 (Religious & Metaphysical Foundations): Add explicit language and documentation of liturgical or communal practices (e.g., morning prayers, chapel schedules, sacred music) and a formal articulation of theology integrated across disciplines to transition toward CLASSICAL.
- For Element 2 (Purpose & Goal): Articulate a clear, public statement about the cultivation of virtue and wisdom (truth, goodness, beauty) as real goods in the curriculum, with measurable outcomes or devotional practices tied to formation (to move toward CLASSICAL).
- For Element 3 (Curriculum): Publicly list Great Books/Great Conversation commitments (even if via Memoria Press materials), and spell out how liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric) are sequenced and integrated across K–12. Include examples of canonical readings and how Wonder is cultivated in the curriculum.
- For Element 4 (Pedagogy): Describe explicit pedagogy practices such as Socratic discussions in upper grades, seminars, and opportunities for student rhetoric and oration; publish a pedagogy map showing how grammar, logic, and rhetoric are operationalized in classroom practice.
- For Element 5 (Faculty): Publish a living-curriculum profile of teachers, including expectations, mentorship roles, and a teacher portrait that illustrates how faculty model virtue, knowledge, and skill; provide examples of faculty involvement in shaping curriculum and student formation.
- For Element 6 (Parents & Community): Expand on parent-education initiatives (book studies, parent evenings, community lectures) and demonstrate more opportunities for visits, volunteering, and deeper co-teaching roles on campus; publish a formal “parents as co-teachers” framework with examples.
