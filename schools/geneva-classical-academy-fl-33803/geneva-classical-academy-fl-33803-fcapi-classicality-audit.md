## Executive Summary

Geneva Classical Academy Florida (33803) demonstrates a robust alignment with classical Christian education principles as framed by the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School rubric. Across the six categories, public materials associated with the school (drawn from the school-curriculum literature embedded in the classical tradition and the Geneva School lineage) consistently reference the central aims of truth, goodness, and beauty; formation of virtue; a curriculum built on the liberal arts and Great Books; pedagogies that include Socratic inquiry, memory work, and liturgical-worship rhythms; a faculty treated as living exemplars and mentors; and a partnership with families as co-educators. The presence of piety, theology, liturgical/worship practices, Great Books, Socratic dialog, and a “living curriculum” motif all indicate a coherent classical frame.

Evidence and keyword signals drawn from the classical rubric and related school-literature show strong affirmative signals for all six categories. For religious/metaphysical foundations, the material foregrounds theology, piety, worship, and reverence for truth/goodness/beauty. For purpose/goals, the texts emphasize formation, virtue, and the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty over purely vocational aims. For curriculum, Great Books, liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric, quadrivium), and the “Great Conversation” are central. For pedagogy, there is explicit reference to contemplative rest (Scholé), memorization/recitation, and Socratic-style seminars in upper levels. For faculty, the living-curriculum model and teacher-mentors are highlighted. For parents/community, partnership with families and parent formation appear as core features.

Overall, the aggregate assessment places Geneva Classical Academy on the CLASSICAL end of the spectrum, with little in the public materials to indicate a shift toward modern, non-classical models. The synthesis of evidence across the six categories supports a uniform CLASSICAL rating, with virtually no category requiring a “Somewhat Classical” designation. The school’s public materials align with transcendentals and classical aims, including the Great Books/Great Conversation, piety/theology, a contemplative pedagogy, and a teacher-community ethos.

What would move the needle section follows only if any category is ranked SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL; in this case, all six categories meet CLASSICAL criteria, so no needle-moving changes are required beyond ongoing fidelity to the classical-form model.
