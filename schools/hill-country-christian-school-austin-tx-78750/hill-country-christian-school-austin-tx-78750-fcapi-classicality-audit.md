## Executive Summary

Hill Country Christian School of Austin (Hill Country Christian School Tx 78750) presents a robust Classical Christian program that aligns strongly with the Six Essential Elements of a Classical School. The publicly available materials consistently frame the school as a classical covenantal institution centered on Christ, the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric), Great Texts/Great Books, and a biblically informed worldview permeating all subjects. The campus materials emphasize weekly chapel, Scripture memory, liturgical and communal practices, and a three-stage Trivium curriculum, all of which map clearly to Classical expectations. The school also foregrounds virtue formation, character, leadership, and partnership with families in a covenantal framework, and it showcases a college-preparatory, even engineering-pathway approach in high school, including AP/dual-credit options and accreditation. Taken together, the six-category analysis yields a CLASSICAL classification across Religious & Metaphysical Foundations, School Purpose & Goal, Curriculum, Pedagogy, Faculty, and Parents & Community.

Key observations:
- Religious and metaphysical foundations are explicit and integral (Christ-centered, Scripture memory, weekly chapels, covenantal community) and are reinforced by formal theology within the curriculum. This demonstrates a transcendent moral framework with liturgical practices as part of daily life on campus. Evidence: covenantal language, weekly chapels, Scripture memory, and biblical worldview across subjects. 
- The school’s stated purpose and goals center on virtue, wisdom, formation, and a biblically grounded understanding of truth, goodness, and beauty, rather than mere job preparation. Evidence: mission statements emphasize truth, character, service, and thinking logically, not just employment outcomes. 
- The curriculum foregrounds the liberal arts through the Trivium (Grammar/Logic/Rhetoric), Great Texts/Great Books, and a covenantal-biblical worldview across grades, with explicit references to Great Books/Great Texts and Socratic inquiry. Evidence: Trivium table; Great Texts; Socratic method; engineering/college-prep pathways. 
- Pedagogy integrates contemplative/restful elements with rigorous discourse: lower grades include memory, singing, and review; upper grades emphasize Socratic discussion, seminars, and public rhetoric. Evidence: lower/upper school pedagogy notes and classroom approaches. 
- Faculty are portrayed as mentors and living curriculum, with leadership from a clearly identified head of school and a “faculty stories” emphasis on relationships and mentorship. Evidence: Head of School profile, faculty-staff storytelling. 
- Parents & Community are treated as partners, with covenantal expectations, parental involvement, and parent-led gatherings, along with resources for parents about classical education. Evidence: covenantal language, parent-partner materials, and parent resources.
