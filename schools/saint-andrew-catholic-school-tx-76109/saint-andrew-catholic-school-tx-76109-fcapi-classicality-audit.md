## Executive Summary

Saint Andrew Catholic School (Fort Worth, TX 76109) presents a compelling case as a CLASSICAL Catholic school embedded in a deeply spiritual, formation-first mission. The website and school materials emphasize a Christ-centered community, partnership with families, a strong Catholic identity, and a curriculum transformation toward Catholic classical education, including partnerships with the University of Dallas for a Catholic classical approach and explicit reference to Great Books, Latin instruction, and classical pedagogy such as Socratic seminars in the upper grades. This combination yields a robust alignment with the Six Essential Elements of Classical Education, with particular strength in religious foundations, formation-oriented purpose, pedagogy that includes Socratic inquiry and memory work, and a strong sense of faculty as living mentors within a living curriculum. Some aspects of curricular implementation (notably the Early Childhood Montessori track) introduce non-classical elements at the K–8 level, creating a partial deviation that places this school in the Somewhat Classical spectrum for Curriculum, while other categories remain strongly Classical.

Overall, the school’s six-category assessment places Saint Andrew Catholic School predominantly in CLASSICAL territory, with Curriculum being the modest exception that drags the aggregate slightly toward SOMEWHAT CLASSICAL in that category alone. The aggregate classification is CLASSICAL.

Aggregate Assessment: CLASSICAL

- The school demonstrates a predominantly CLASSICAL orientation across five categories (Religious Foundations, Purpose & Goal, Pedagogy, Faculty, Parents & Community) with Curriculum showing a strong but not pure classical alignment due to Montessori elements in Early Childhood.

What Would Move the Needle

- If Curriculum were to reach a fully classical, PK–12 consistency (eliminate Montessori in Early Childhood or clearly integrate it within a unified classical framework), the school would achieve a stronger, seamless classical curriculum footprint from PK–12.
- Increase breadth and depth of Great Books exposure and explicitly codify a K–12 Great Books sequence aligned to the UD Catholic Classical program, including more explicit “read alouds” and primary-source engagement across all grade bands.
- Expand the lower grades’ engagement with classical pedagogy (memory work, recitation, and narrative forms) to a fuller K–8 continuity, ensuring that memory and structured recitation are consistent and integrated with Socratic inquiry as students advance.
- Strengthen explicit public articulation of a long-term, formal teacher formation program modeled on a classical educator profile (portrait of a classical teacher) with ongoing professional development in classical pedagogy.
- Broaden parent education offerings (e.g., regular classical education book studies and lectures) and formalize home/household liturgy alignment to deepen the parents’ role as co-teachers.
